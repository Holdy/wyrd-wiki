#title
The Curse on Te Waru's New House

#summary
When chief Te Waru violated sacred *tapu* laws by smoking inside his newly commissioned ceremonial house at the foot of Paeroa Mountain, a deadly curse was triggered that claimed the lives of multiple wives and children whenever construction resumed. Despite attempts to finish the magnificent timber structure over decades, the wrath of the unseen spirits repeatedly struck until elaborate purification rituals were eventually performed.

#details
Te Waru, a prominent warrior and master carver of the Ngati-Whaoa tribe, set out to build a magnificent carved house at the foot of Paeroa Mountain to honor his young wife. However, he absentmindedly entered the unfinished building while smoking his pipe, transgressing the ancient law of *tapu* which strictly forbids food or smoking in sacred spaces before neutralizing rites are performed. Warned by a local *tohunga* (priest) that the gods would retaliate, Te Waru ignored the warning, and shortly after, his young wife died. Years later, when he attempted to resume carving the house with a second wife, she also died. A third attempt after marrying again resulted in the deaths of his new wife and children. Recognizing the undeniable power of the curse, the unfinished carvings were abandoned under a strict spiritual ban until the white *tohunga* Tāré and expert Arawa carvers finally acquired the slabs, completed the monumental "Rauru" meeting house, and performed ancient cleansing ceremonies to lift the curse.

#location
The story is centered at the foot of Paeroa Mountain within the Paeroa Range in the central North Island of New Zealand, located between Rotorua and the Waikato River.

#title
The Wainuiomata Mākutu Lifting

#summary
During a family ritual to lift a suspected curse or possession, a young woman tragically drowned while her relatives attempted to flush out perceived malevolent entities. The improvised exorcism became a landmark real-world account of spirit possession belief in New Zealand history.

#details
In October 2007, 22-year-old Janet Moses began exhibiting erratic behavior following personal hardships and stress. Convinced that she was afflicted by a malignant curse or possessed by dark spirits, members of her extended family gathered at a residential flat in Wainuiomata to perform an improvised mākutu lifting (exorcism). Over the course of the ritual, family members forcibly poured large amounts of water into her mouth and eyes and physically attempted to expel the entities they saw in her. Tragically, the ordeal resulted in Janet's death by drowning on October 12, 2007, leading to a landmark police investigation and manslaughter convictions for several relatives who genuinely believed they were saving her life.

#location
The events took place at a residential flat in the Wellington suburb of Wainuiomata, New Zealand.
