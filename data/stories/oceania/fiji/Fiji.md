#title
The Curse of the Sacred Red Prawns

#summary
A scorned chief's daughter caused a rejected suitor and his delicacy of red prawns to be cast into a pool on the island of Vatulele, establishing a strict taboo. Anyone who dares to touch or remove the sacred red prawns from their pool is cursed with a disastrous shipwreck.

#details
Long ago on the island of Vatulele, a chief's daughter named Yalewan-ni-Cagi-Bula—the Maiden-of-the-Fair-Wind—was renowned for her beauty but possessed a harsh and scornful heart. When a young mainland chief traveled across the sea to court her, he brought a prized gift of rare red prawns cooked in coconut milk. Rejecting both the suitor and his offering, the haughty maiden ordered her attendants to cast him over the cliffside. The young chief fell into the sea below, and his gift of prawns plunged into a secluded rocky pool, where they miraculously came back to life and turned bright scarlet. From that day onward, a strict sacred taboo (*tabu*) was placed upon the pool, declaring that the red prawns must never be disturbed or harmed. Anyone foolish enough to steal or interfere with them faces a delayed and dreaded curse: a violent shipwreck on the surrounding seas.

#location
The sacred pool containing the red prawns is located in the coastal cave system of Vatulele Island, south of Fiji's main island of Viti Levu.

#title
The Spirit Possession at Nacavanadi Church

#summary
During a prayer service in an island village, a congregant became possessed by a spirit that falsely accused a local homeowner of practicing witchcraft. Prompted by the entity's claims, the minister and villagers destroyed the targeted house, leading to a landmark court ruling against the church.

#details
In April 2007, a religious prayer session was held at the Nacavanadi Methodist Church on Gau Island, Fiji. During the service, a member of the congregation became possessed by an entity. Speaking through the possessed individual, the spirit declared that a villager named Ravuama Vonu practiced witchcraft and kept a worshipped skull inside his nearby house. Driven by the spirit's claims, the local Methodist minister, church steward, and members of the congregation broke into the home, smashing the concrete floor and destroying personal property in search of the alleged relics. The homeowners subsequently took legal action against the church officials, and the court found the minister and the Methodist Church vicariously liable for the destruction, ordering financial compensation.

#location
The Nacavanadi Methodist Church and the adjacent village home located in Nacavanadi Village on Gau Island, Fiji.
