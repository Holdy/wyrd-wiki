#title
The Pathway to Heaven

#summary
A British colonial officer walking along a remote island path encounters a local villager who ignores his greetings, only to discover upon reaching his destination that the man had died the previous day.

#details
While stationed in the Gilbert Islands (now Kiribati), Arthur Grimble heard of a death in a distant village and wished to attend the funeral to observe local traditions. When the villagers refused to guide him because it was an intensely private matter, Grimble set out alone along a secluded trail known locally as "the pathway to heaven". Along this isolated track, he passed an acquaintance walking in the opposite direction, but despite calling out a greeting by name, the man walked past in complete silence without a backward glance. Upon arriving at the village, Grimble mentioned the strange encounter, eliciting only fearful silence from the locals before the chilling truth was revealed: the man he had met and passed on the trail was the very person whose funeral he had come to attend, having died the day before.

#location
The events took place along a remote path on Makin Meang (Little Makin) in the Gilbert Islands, Kiribati.

#upstream-source
A Pattern of Islands by Arthur Grimble (John Murray, 1952).

#title
The Possessed Girl of Abaiang

#summary
A thirteen-year-old girl in the Gilbert Islands became possessed by a malevolent ancestral spirit, exhibiting an unnaturally swollen abdomen and superhuman strength, until a missionary successfully performed an exorcism at the village church.

#details
During the 1930s in the Gilbert Islands (now Kiribati), traditional beliefs regarding involuntary spirit possession were documented by local clergy. Father Ernest Sabatier recorded a striking account of a thirteen-year-old girl who fell victim to a hostile ancestral spirit (*anti*). The entity invaded her body, lodging within her hard, swollen abdomen and causing violent convulsions. Despite her young age, the possessed girl displayed terrifying, unnatural strength during the ordeal. When the missionary intervened at the mission church, he engaged in a dramatic spiritual struggle, performing an exorcism to cast out the possessing entity and free the girl from the torment.

#location
The Roman Catholic mission church and compound on the atoll of Abaiang in Kiribati.

#upstream-source
Father Ernest Sabatier, *Astride the Equator: An Account of the Gilbert Islands* (*Sous l'équateur du Pacifique: Les Isles Gilbert et la Mission Catholique*, 1939; translated by Ursula Nixon, Melbourne: Oxford University Press, 1977).
