#title
Surech and Dulei

#summary
Two young lovers from rival Palauan villages defied the bitter social enmity between their clans, which they considered a curse, and chose to leap into the sea together rather than be separated.

#details
Long ago in Palau, two neighboring clans lived in deep hostility, divided by ancient pride and conflict. Surech, the son of a chief from one village, and Dulei, the daughter of a matriarch from the neighboring village, met by chance near the mangroves and fell deeply in love. Bound by strict cultural laws that forbade unions between warring communities, their secret meetings were eventually discovered by elders who threatened them with exile. Cornered one night by pursuing guards and realizing their love was condemned as a curse by their people's rigid hostility, Surech and Dulei ran to a coastal cliff and plunged into the sea together. Their tragic fate is commemorated in traditional Palauan wood-carved storyboards, serving as an enduring reminder of the delicate balance between custom and compassion.

#location
The jungle and coastal areas situated between the villages of Ngiwal and Ngkeklau on Babeldaob Island, Palau.

#upstream-source
Palauan Storyboards: Art, Narrative, and Heritage by the Belau National Museum (2010).

#title
The Voice of the Chelid

#summary
Traditional Palauan lore recounts how human mediums known as kerong were overtaken and possessed by divine spirits, speaking in altered voices and conveying profound oracles from behind mats.

#details
In traditional Palauan folklore and historical accounts, certain individuals were chosen by supernatural entities known as *chelid* (gods or ancestral spirits) to serve as their earthly mouthpieces. When the spirit descended, the medium—referred to as a *kerong*—would undergo a violent physical transformation, often stimulated by chewing betelnut. Seated inside a dedicated spirit house (*chelid blai*) or behind a privacy screen of woven mats near the village community meeting house (*bai*), the host would tremble, shake, and experience facial contortions. The possessing entity would take total control of the medium's faculties, speaking in a shrill, altered voice for hours at a time to deliver political prophecies, divine the causes of illnesses, and offer guidance to village chiefs.

#location
The traditional village meeting house (*bai*) and adjacent spirit shrine situated in a village settlement on Babeldaob Island in the Republic of Palau.

#upstream-source
Jan Kubary, "Die Religion der Palauer" (1888); Karl Semper, *The Palau Islands in the Pacific Ocean* (1873); cited in Jay D. Dobbin and Francis X. Hezel, "Distribution of Spirit Possession and Trance in Micronesia" (1996).
