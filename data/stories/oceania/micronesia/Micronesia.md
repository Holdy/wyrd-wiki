#title
The Curse of the Royal Tombs of Nan Madol

#summary
When a foreign governor broke into the sealed ancient tombs of Nan Madol in defiance of local taboos, he was struck down by a deadly curse manifested in supernatural storms and sudden death. Local tradition warns that anyone who violates these sacred ruins will face divine retribution.

#details
Nan Madol, an ancient megalithic city constructed on artificial islets off the eastern coast of Pohnpei in Micronesia, has long been protected by strict oral taboos and local laws forbidding anyone from disturbing its sacred ruins and royal tombs. Disregarding these warnings and the local king's prohibitions, German Governor Victor Berg entered a sealed tomb on the island and opened the coffin of the ancient rulers. That very night, the island was wracked by violent spirit activity, torrential rains, and lightning, accompanied by the haunting sound of a conch shell. Governor Berg fell into delirium and died the following morning under mysterious circumstances, which the native inhabitants attributed entirely to the divine curse placed upon violators of the sacred land.

#location
The royal tombs on the artificial islets of Nan Madol, located just off the eastern coast of Pohnpei in the Federated States of Micronesia.

#title
The Spirit Vehicle of Fefan Island

#summary
A young girl in Chuuk State is suddenly seized and possessed by the spirits of her deceased relatives during a family gathering, exhibiting preternatural strength and speaking in their exact voices to resolve family disputes.

#details
Documented in ethnographic records of Micronesian folklore and real-world accounts collected by researchers Francis X. Hezel and Jay D. Dobbin, the event occurred within a family home in the islands of Chuuk. A young woman suddenly fell back from her seat and lost consciousness after complaining of a severe headache. Upon awakening, she began screaming and thrashing violently. When male relatives attempted to intervene, she displayed astonishing, preternatural physical strength that easily overpowered grown men. Adopting the exact timbre, voice characteristics, facial expressions, and mannerisms of her deceased mother and aunt, the possessing entity harangued the household regarding unresolved family tensions and a bitter land dispute. After hours of upheaval, the spirits departed, leaving the girl exhausted and with total amnesia of the possession episode, while the family promptly resolved their disputes.

#location
A residential family house in the village on Fefan Island within the Chuuk Lagoon, Federated States of Micronesia.

#upstream-source
Francis X. Hezel and Jay D. Dobbin, "Spirit Possession in Chuuk" and "The Distribution of Spirit Possession and Trance in Micronesia" (*Pacific Studies*, Vol. 19, No. 2).

#reference
https://micsem.org/article/spirit-possession-in-chuuk-a-socio-cultural-interpretation/

#title
The White Lady of Fonté Bridge

#summary
A grieving spirit of a woman mistreated by her husband haunts a village bridge in Guam, appearing at dusk to travelers passing by. Her tragic death in the nearby river led to generations of sightings by villagers and churchgoers.

#details
In the late 1600s, a Spanish officer married a prominent Chamorro woman and settled in the village of Maina. Over time, the officer grew abusive and demanding, forcing his wife to make daily treks to fetch fresh water from the nearby Fonté River. One stormy night, during a violent downpour, he ordered her to fetch water again. While walking in the dark, she slipped into the turbulent, swollen river and was swept away to her death, her body never found. Stricken with remorse, her husband died lonely shortly afterward. Since then, a sorrowful apparition known as the White Lady—dressed in a flowing white gown with long silvery hair and teary eyes—has been frequently seen near the river bridge in Maina at dusk before fading away as the moon rises.

#location
The bridge crossing the Fonté River in the village of Maina on the island of Guam.
