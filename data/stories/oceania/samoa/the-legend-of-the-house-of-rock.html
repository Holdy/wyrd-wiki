The Legend of the House of Rock

Summary

When a labor competition between village men and women to thatch a chief's house resulted in the men's abysmal failure, the infuriated chief cursed the structure, turning it permanently into solid stone. Today, this volcanic cave formation stands as a celebrated cultural landmark and the origin of a famous Samoan proverb.

Details

In ancient Samoa, a chief named Tautunu commanded the people of Falealupo to build a grand communal house, with the men and women tasked with thatching opposite halves of the roof. While the women worked diligently and completed their side ahead of schedule, the men lagged far behind in their performance. Enraged by the men's abysmal failure and incompetence, Chief Tautunu placed a powerful curse upon the building, transforming the entire structure—along with its poles and thatched roof—into permanent rock. Known as the Fale o le Ma'a (House of Rock), this lava tube cave became a permanent monument and inspired the traditional Samoan proverb honoring the determination and reliability of women.
