#title
The Witch of Saguazú

#summary
Renowned as the ultimate sorceress of Yaguarón, Micaela Yaharí wielded dark hexes and a forbidden magical book that cemented a permanent curse upon her estate. Upon her death in 1917, her unredeemed sins made her coffin so immovably heavy that it had to be dragged by oxen to the church, where her restless spirit still haunts the grounds.

#details
Micaela Yaharí was a legendary payesera (sorceress) who lived in the rural Saguazú district of Yaguarón. Famed for her mastery of the occult, she owned an ancient grimorio filled with powerful spells and maledictions, and locals whispered of her ability to fly across the valleys and detach her head from her body. Her magic was both feared and potent; when a wealthy, haughty young woman cruelly rejected a poor suitor, Micaela cast a harsh hex upon her as punishment. Years later, during the 1947 revolution, a marine stole her sacred book of dark arts, prompting Micaela's unseen influence to ensure that his political faction suffered total defeat. When she died in 1917, her heavy karma and unabsolved sins manifested physically: her coffin became so impossibly dense that ten men could not lift it, requiring oxen to drag her remains toward the parish church. Because the local priest refused to allow her body entry into holy ground, a spectral curse settled over her property—now known as the Castillo de Saguazú—where visitors and residents still report hearing her echoing laughter and witnessing her phantom presence.

#location
The Castillo de Saguazú is located in the Saguazú district of Yaguarón, approximately 50 kilometers southeast of Asunción, Paraguay.
