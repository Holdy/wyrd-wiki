#title
The Drenched Hitchhiker of Route 11

#summary
Motorists driving along the coastal highway between Mar del Plata and Miramar occasionally encounter a soaked young man hitchhiking who mysteriously vanishes inside their vehicles.

#details
According to local folklore along Provincial Route 11 in Buenos Aires Province, drivers traveling the coastal stretch between Mar del Plata and Miramar frequently encounter a young man standing by the roadside. Completely drenched as if he had just emerged from the sea and wearing an anguished expression, he raises his hand to flag down passing cars for a ride to Mar del Plata. Those who stop report that he climbs in silently, muttering in a low voice about a water accident or getting lost on the beach. Shortly afterward, amidst a moment of driver distraction or thick fog, he completely disappears, leaving behind only an empty, damp seat.

#location
The stretch of Provincial Route 11 located between Mar del Plata and Miramar in the Buenos Aires Province of Argentina.

#title
The Possession of María Laura in Villaguay

#summary
In 1993, a teenage girl in Entre Ríos, Argentina, was reportedly possessed by a demonic entity, exhibiting superhuman strength and speaking fluent English without ever having learned it.

#details
On October 31, 1993, Father Spahn and parish priest Máximo Hergenreder were called to a family home in Villaguay, Entre Ríos, after a young teenager named María Laura began exhibiting terrifying signs of demonic possession. When the priests arrived, they found her lying in bed, spitting holy water and mocking crucifixes with a deep voice. Despite having only attended a local state school where English was never taught, María Laura spoke fluently in English and demonstrated sudden, superhuman strength that allowed her to violently shake the clergy. Father Spahn maintained regular visits with her throughout her ordeal until she eventually made a full recovery and resumed her normal life. The harrowing event later inspired musician Fito Páez to write his song "Las tardes del sol, las noches del agua".

#location
The family home of María Laura located in Villaguay, Entre Ríos Province, Argentina.

#title
The Seven Buried Cats of Avellaneda

#summary
Following a bitter football rivalry, seven black cats were allegedly buried beneath a stadium goal to curse a major sports club with decades of failure. The prolonged hex was only lifted decades later when the missing seventh carcass was finally excavated.

#details
In 1967, Racing Club became the first Argentine football team to lift the Intercontinental Cup, a triumph that filled supporters of their fierce cross-town rivals, Independiente, with jealous fury. Seeking esoteric revenge, a group of Independiente fans enlisted a friendly night watchman and broke into the rival stadium under the cover of night. There, they buried seven dead black cats around the grounds—six beneath one of the goalposts and one in a secret location—casting a potent jinx over the club. What followed was a 35-year drought of domestic championships marked by bankruptcy, heartbreaks, missed penalties, and even a historic relegation in 1983. 

Over the years, desperate managers and club officials attempted to undo the curse by digging up the pitch. While they uncovered six skeletal remains, the elusive seventh cat remained hidden beneath concrete, allowing the misfortune to persist. It was not until 2001, when incoming coach Reinaldo "Mostaza" Merlo—renowned for his superstitious rituals—insisted on tearing up the specific concrete-covered area behind the goal to extract the final cat, that the dark cloud was finally lifted and Racing Club was crowned league champions once again.

#location
Presidente Perón Stadium (El Cilindro), located in Avellaneda, Greater Buenos Aires, Argentina.
