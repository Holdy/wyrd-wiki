#title
The Exorcism of the Student at the Monastery of Saint Gerasimos

#summary
A seventeen-year-old student afflicted by an unclean spirit was brought to a historic island monastery in Greece where he was successfully exorcised before the relics of a revered ascetic. The account was documented in a mid-twentieth-century Greek newspaper.

#details
In late September 1949, a seventeen-year-old boy from the village of Vathy who attended high school in Lefkada became severely troubled by an unclean spirit. Suffering from the possession, he was taken to the Monastery of Saint Gerasimos on the island of Kefalonia, Greece, which is renowned for healing the afflicted and demon-possessed. While attending the Divine Liturgy chanted by Father Spyridon Linardatos, the boy suddenly began screaming in terror, crying out that he was "Legion" and questioning where the saint would cast him. As the priest attempted to perform an exorcism using the Honorable Cross, the young man bit down on the crucifix, declaring it to be a powerful weapon. The following day, as he knelt before the coffin holding the incorrupt relics of Saint Gerasimos, the boy shouted that he had been silenced and defeated before suddenly rising completely cured and healthy.

#location
The Monastery of Saint Gerasimos is located in the Omala valley on the island of Kefalonia, Greece.

#upstream-source
The newspaper *Peloponnisos* (October 16, 1949 issue, article titled "The Healing of a Demonically Possessed At St. Gerasimos").

#title
The Guardian of the Divided Road

#summary
Fearing a lingering curse and the release of trapped spirits, road builders chose to split a Guyana highway into two lanes rather than cut down an ancient silk cotton tree. Locals believe the tree acts as a prison for malevolent entities, ensuring that severe misfortune or illness befalls anyone who dares to harm or disturb it.

#details
Deep in Guyanese folklore, the giant silk cotton tree is widely known as a "Jumbie Tree" or "Dutchman tree," rumored to be haunted by vengeful spirits and ancient entities left behind from the colonial era. According to tradition, these trees serve as vessels or prisons for supernatural forces. The folklore dictates that anyone who attempts to fell or damage the tree will unleash these trapped jumbies, bringing a severe curse and madness upon themselves and the surrounding community. 

So deeply ingrained is this belief that when the main public highway was being constructed through Mahaicony, planners and builders refused to remove the massive silk cotton tree standing directly in the path. Rather than risk the wrath of the curse, the highway was explicitly engineered to split into two separate lanes, allowing the sacred tree to remain entirely undisturbed in the middle of the divide to this day.

#location
The silk cotton tree stands on the public highway at Perseverance Village in Mahaicony, East Coast Demerara, Guyana, where the road splits into dual lanes around it.
