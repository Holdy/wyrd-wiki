The Demonic Possessions of Santa Clara

Summary

In 1674, between 23 and 26 nuns at the Santa Clara convent in Trujillo, Peru, suffered a terrifying wave of demonic possessions characterized by violent trances and supernatural visions. Despite prolonged exorcisms, the outbreak sparked widespread panic and drew the scrutiny of the Lima Inquisition.

Details

In November 1674, the commissioner of Trujillo reported to the Tribunal of the Holy Office of the Inquisition in Lima that dark entities had taken possession of the bodies of between 23 and 26 Poor Clare nuns at the Santa Clara convent. Afflicted nuns, most notably Luisa Bénitez and Ana Núñez, suffered from severe trances, altered voices, and terrifying visions of winged serpents, toads, worms, and dark figures. Confessors performed prolonged exorcisms, but the possessions persisted, fueling widespread colonial anxiety. During the Inquisition's investigation, suspicions fell upon a local healer who had recently treated an ill nun inside the convent, as well as indigenous and Afro-descendant servants. Ultimately, the Holy Office concluded the women were deluded rather than truly possessed, resulting in their imprisonment and expulsion from the religious order.
