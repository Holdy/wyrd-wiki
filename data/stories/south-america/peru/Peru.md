#title
The Curse of Casa Matusita

#summary
A notorious building in downtown Lima is plagued by centuries-old legends of witchcraft, mysterious tragedies, and a hex that drives anyone who stays on its upper floor to madness. According to lore, the property was originally cursed by a Persian woman condemned by the Spanish Inquisition.

#details
Standing at a busy intersection in the historic center of Lima, Casa Matusita has inspired generations of urban legends regarding supernatural occurrences and dark sorcery. One prominent legend traces the building's curse back to the mid-18th century, when a Persian immigrant named Parvaneh Dervaspa settled on the site, was accused of witchcraft by the Holy Inquisition, and was burned at the stake after confessing under torture. Before her death, or as a consequence of her dark pacts, she allegedly cast an enduring curse upon the land and any structure built upon it. Over the decades, other sinister stories accumulated around the house, including tales of tragic mass poisonings, familicides, and psychological breakdowns. Most famously, television personalities and daredevils who bet they could survive a night on the building's second floor allegedly fled within hours, suffering severe mental derangement or psychiatric hospitalization.

#location
Casa Matusita is located at the intersection of Avenida Inca Garcilaso de la Vega and Avenida España in the Historic Centre of Lima, Peru.

#title
The Demonic Possessions of Santa Clara

#summary
In 1674, between 23 and 26 nuns at the Santa Clara convent in Trujillo, Peru, suffered a terrifying wave of demonic possessions characterized by violent trances and supernatural visions. Despite prolonged exorcisms, the outbreak sparked widespread panic and drew the scrutiny of the Lima Inquisition.

#details
In November 1674, the commissioner of Trujillo reported to the Tribunal of the Holy Office of the Inquisition in Lima that dark entities had taken possession of the bodies of between 23 and 26 Poor Clare nuns at the Santa Clara convent. Afflicted nuns, most notably Luisa Bénitez and Ana Núñez, suffered from severe trances, altered voices, and terrifying visions of winged serpents, toads, worms, and dark figures. Confessors performed prolonged exorcisms, but the possessions persisted, fueling widespread colonial anxiety. During the Inquisition's investigation, suspicions fell upon a local healer who had recently treated an ill nun inside the convent, as well as indigenous and Afro-descendant servants. Ultimately, the Holy Office concluded the women were deluded rather than truly possessed, resulting in their imprisonment and expulsion from the religious order.

#location
The Santa Clara Convent is located in the historic center of the city of Trujillo in northwestern Peru.

#title
The Procession of Death

#summary
A gossiping widow witnessing a midnight funeral procession of skeletal figures through the streets of Cajamarca receives a terrifying premonition of her own imminent demise.

#details
In colonial Cajamarca around 1620, an inquisitive and gossipy widow named Doña Jordana looked out her window late at night to observe a solemn funeral procession moving through the dark streets. The figures were not living citizens, but terrifying skeletons covered in white shrouds who carried burning bones like candles and chanted funeral psalms. As the spectral parade passed, one of the skeletal figures stepped forward, warned Doña Jordana to repent for her gossiping tongue, and handed her a burning bone from the afterlife, prophesying that she would join the procession within two days. True to the warning, Doña Jordana was found dead two days later with the burning bone beside her.

#location
The historic streets of Cajamarca in northern Peru.
