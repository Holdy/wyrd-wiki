The Moving Coffins of the Chase Vault

Summary

Following multiple tragic family interments within a heavily sealed marble tomb, heavy lead coffins were repeatedly discovered violently displaced and tossed about without any visible disturbance to the surrounding sand or masonry.

Details

Constructed in the early 18th century and later acquired by the wealthy and formidable Chase family, a heavy underground burial vault in a southern parish churchyard became the site of a bizarre and enduring legend. Beginning with the entombment of little Ann Maria Chase in 1808, followed by her sister Dorcas and eventually their tyrannical father Colonel Thomas Chase, every subsequent time the massive stone door was unsealed—requiring multiple men to move and cemented shut with fresh mortar—the heavy lead coffins inside were found in complete disarray. Despite the floor being covered in a layer of fine sand that would capture any footprint, no tracks were ever found, and no water had leaked inside. The phenomenon repeated across successive openings until the vault was completely emptied and permanently abandoned.
