#title
McCabe's Curse on Nassau

#summary
A bitter prisoner named McCabe cast a vengeful, generation-spanning curse upon the town of Nassau in 1814, decreeing that its citizens' graves would forever be desecrated and their town plagued. This poetic malediction was preserved and later unearthed by journalists, etching itself into local lore.

#details
In the year 1814, a prisoner named McCabe was held in Nassau, New Providence. Deeply aggrieved and filled with malice toward the authorities and townspeople, he penned a fierce and poetic curse upon the settlement before his demise. The curse decreed that the inhabitants would never rest in peace, wishing that their graves would be rooted up by hogs and their community subjected to continuous misfortune across generations. Long after McCabe's time, the text of the curse was brought to light by an American journalist named William Drysdale, who lived in Nassau, cementing the legend within Bahamian historical folklore.

#location
Nassau, New Providence Island, The Bahamas.

#title
The Ghost of Hatchet Bay

#summary
Following her tragic murder by villagers, a young mother's spirit haunts a roadway in Eleuthera. She routinely flags down passing drivers for a ride before mysteriously vanishing between two speed bumps.

#details
In the early 1960s in Rock Sound, Eleuthera, a hardworking young woman became pregnant by a married man, sparking a severe local scandal. Enraged villagers dragged her to an old silk cotton tree near a road curve and stoned her to death. Ever since, drivers passing by late at night have reported seeing a barefoot woman in old-fashioned clothes standing under the tree, often cradling a baby. When motorists stop and offer her a ride, she accepts silently and then vanishes without a trace between the first and second speed bumps before reaching the settlement.

#location
The haunting takes place along the road near the old silk cotton tree between the first and second speed bumps in Rock Sound, Eleuthera, The Bahamas.
