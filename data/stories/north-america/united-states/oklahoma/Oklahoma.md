#title
The Ghost of the Skirvin Hotel

#summary
Built in 1910, the historic Skirvin Hotel in Oklahoma City is famously haunted by a tragic chambermaid named Effie who supposedly jumped from a top-floor window during Prohibition.

#details
Opened in 1911 by oilman W.B. Skirvin, the luxury hotel became a high-society hub and a Prohibition-era speakeasy. According to local legend, Skirvin had an affair with a maid named Effie, who became pregnant and was locked away on the top floor to avoid scandal. Despondent after childbirth, Effie reportedly threw herself and her infant out of a top-floor window. Ever since, guests, staff, and visiting professional sports teams have reported eerie occurrences, including disembodied crying, a female voice whispering or propositioning guests, and objects moving on their own.

#location
The Skirvin Hotel is located at 1 Park Avenue in Oklahoma City, Oklahoma.

#title
The Haunting of Fort Washita and the Legend of Aunt Jane

#summary
Built in 1842 and abandoned after the Civil War, the ruins of Fort Washita near Durant, Oklahoma, are said to be haunted by the spirit of a mysterious woman known as Aunt Jane.

#details
Constructed in 1842 by federal forces to protect the Chickasaw and Choctaw tribes from invaders, Fort Washita became a bustling outpost for westbound settlers during the California Gold Rush. On the brink of the Civil War, the U.S. military abandoned the fort, and Confederate troops subsequently used it as a supply depot until 1865, when they set fire to it upon departure. In the decades since, the charred and ruined remnants of the fort have gained a reputation for intense paranormal activity. Central to local folklore is "Aunt Jane," the apparition of a woman reportedly murdered at the site. Although her true identity has never been officially verified, several legends attempt to explain her presence: one claims she was a freed slave and Union spy killed by Confederate soldiers; another suggests she was an officer's wife murdered by bandits en route; and a third proposes she was an officer's wife caught up in a fatal love triangle.

#location
The ruins of Fort Washita Historic Site and Museum are located near Durant, Oklahoma.

#title
The Haunting of the Stone Lion Inn

#summary
A grand 1907 Victorian mansion in Guthrie, Oklahoma, serves as a bed and breakfast haunted by the spirit of a young girl who tragically died of whooping cough.

#details
Originally built as a lavish family home for merchant T.E. Houghton and his twelve children, the 8,000-square-foot mansion later served as a boarding house and a funeral parlor before becoming Oklahoma's first bed and breakfast. Local lore states that the hauntings began after an eight-year-old Houghton daughter named Augusta fell ill and died of whooping cough following a dosing mistake by a nurse. Guests and owners have frequently reported waking up to a little girl standing by their bed, toys mysteriously strewn across the floor despite locked closets, the scent of tobacco from Mr. Houghton, and the heavy sensation of someone sitting on the edge of the bed.

#location
The Stone Lion Inn is located in historic Guthrie, Oklahoma.

#title
The Hex House of Tulsa

#summary
In 1944, a Tulsa duplex became the center of a bizarre police investigation after two women were discovered living under the psychological and occult control of their landlord. Dubbed the Hex House by media and locals, the property inspired decades of dark urban folklore.

#details
In 1944, police in Tulsa, Oklahoma, investigated a suspicious rationing fraud case involving Carolann Smith, who had applied for ration books using the names of others, including her dog. Upon searching her duplex, authorities uncovered a disturbing situation: two young women, Virginia Evans and Willetta Horner, had been living in the cold basement under Smith's absolute psychological and occult control for seven years, surrendering their paychecks, wearing tattered clothes, and enduring severe abuse under the belief of religious purification. Police also discovered books on witchcraft, mesmerism, and willpower, alongside a backyard grave containing the remains of dogs buried in caskets. The bizarre true-crime case immediately captured public imagination, earning the residence the moniker "Hex House" and cementing it in Oklahoma folklore for generations.

#location
The original Hex House was located at a residential duplex in Tulsa, Oklahoma.

#title
The Lady in White of the Overholser Mansion

#summary
Staff and visitors at the historic 1903 Overholser Mansion in Oklahoma City frequently report sighting the apparition of Anna Overholser, a 19th-century society queen, wandering the halls and gazing from the locked third-floor windows of her former home.

#details
Built in 1903 by pioneer entrepreneur Henry Overholser and his wife Anna, the Chateauesque mansion located at 405 NW 15th Street in Oklahoma City served as the epicenter of local high society for decades. Following the deaths of Henry in 1915 and Anna in 1940, the historic home eventually became a preserved museum. Over the years, numerous accounts from museum staff, guests, and passersby have described uncanny phenomena within the house. Visitors and docents have reported catching fleeting glimpses of a petite woman standing about 5 feet 2 inches tall, dressed in a lacy white gown with her dark hair piled in a bun—matching Anna Overholser's appearance in historic photographs. Passersby and construction workers have frequently watched curtains move on the locked third floor when no one is inside, and night security alarms are regularly triggered in empty rooms. Former staff members even established a habit of playing dinner chimes or speaking respectfully upon arrival to acknowledge the enduring presence of the home's former residents.

#location
The Henry and Anna Overholser Mansion is located at 405 NW 15th Street in Oklahoma City, Oklahoma.

#title
The Phantom Hitchhiker of Highway 20

#summary
A motorist driving along Highway 20 in the winter of 1965 picked up a young boy hitchhiking near Claremore, only for him to mysteriously vanish from the backseat before reaching Pryor.

#details
In the winter of 1965, a woman named Mae Doria was driving along Highway 20 just east of Claremore, Oklahoma, when she noticed a young boy hitchhiking by the roadside and stopped to offer him a lift. As they traveled toward Pryor, the boy engaged in conversation about sports and his school, but upon nearing a desolate stretch near their destination, he grew abruptly distressed and asked to be let out. When Mrs. Doria pulled over and turned to check on her passenger, the boy had completely vanished from the vehicle without a trace.

#location
The sighting occurred along Highway 20 on the stretch of road running east from Claremore toward Pryor in Oklahoma.

During the Prohibition era, legend states that Skirvin had an affair with a hotel maid who subsequently became pregnant. To protect his reputation, Skirvin had her locked away on the tenth floor of the hotel. Even after giving birth, the woman remained imprisoned. Distraught and mentally unhinged, she took her infant child and threw herself out of the window. In the decades since, the hotel has gained widespread notoriety—particularly among visiting NBA players and guests—for unexplained paranormal phenomena. Reports include phantom crying of a baby, doors opening and closing independently, objects moving without assistance, and encounters with a spectral presence.

#title
The Restless Spirit of Fort Gibson

#summary
Dating back to the post-Civil War era, Fort Gibson's military cemetery is said to be haunted by Vivia Thomas, a woman who disguised herself as a soldier to find her beloved.

#details
Following the Civil War, a young woman named Vivia Thomas from Boston allegedly disguised herself in soldier's clothing to cross the frontier and locate her fiancé stationed at Fort Gibson in the Indian Territory. Upon arriving, she discovered that the soldier had either transferred or died, leaving her completely heartbroken. She lingered near the fort before her untimely death and burial in the Fort Gibson Military Cemetery. Ever since, visitors and caretakers have reported seeing the phantom figure of a woman wandering quietly among the headstones at dusk, searching in vain for her lost soldier.

#location
The Fort Gibson National Cemetery is located in Fort Gibson, Oklahoma.

#title
The Restless Spirits of Historic Fort Reno

#summary
Established in 1875 on the Oklahoma frontier, Historic Fort Reno is widely reported to be haunted by soldiers, prisoners of war, and a sorrowful "Lady in White." Visitors and paranormal investigators frequently encounter disembodied voices, ghostly faces at windows, and phantom cavalry sounds across the grounds.

#details
Fort Reno served as a vital military post through the Indian Wars, the Land Run of 1889, and as a prisoner of war camp during World War II. Over the decades, the heavy toll of disease, isolation, and military conflict left a lingering spiritual residue across the post. Among the most enduring legends is the "Lady in White," a sorrowful apparition seen wandering near the officers' quarters and chapel, often accompanied by the scent of lavender. Other recurring phenomena include a mysterious face pressed against the upper windows of the former visitor center and the phantom sounds of galloping horses and shouted commands echoing across the parade grounds. 

#location
Historic Fort Reno is located at 7107 W. Cheyenne Street just west of El Reno, Oklahoma.
