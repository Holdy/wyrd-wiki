#title
Lydia of the Red Onion Saloon

#summary
A preserved 1898 gold rush bordello in Skagway is said to be haunted by the spirit of a former sex worker named Lydia, who wanders the upstairs crib rooms leaving the scent of fresh lilacs.

#details
Built in 1898 during the Klondike Gold Rush, the Red Onion Saloon operated as a high-end dance hall and bordello featuring ten crib rooms upstairs where miners paid for the company of women selected via dolls displayed at the bar. According to local lore and employee accounts, the ghost of Lydia—a former prostitute who allegedly suffered from syphilis and died by suicide—haunts the second floor. Staff and visitors report sudden cold spots, white blurs, mysterious photographs, and the distinct fragrance of freshly cut lilacs as Lydia wanders the historic rooms.

#location
The Red Onion Saloon is located at 205 Broadway St in Skagway, Alaska.

#title
The Curse of Portlock

#summary
Settlers and cannery workers in the remote Alaskan bay of Portlock were driven away by a decades-long curse of violent attacks, mysterious disappearances, and a monstrous presence haunting the settlement. Following years of mutilated bodies, eerie nocturnal screams, and a devastating fire at the local cannery, the entire community was forced to abandon the area by 1950.

#details
Originally visited by explorers in the 18th century who found abandoned native settlements and fled from sickness and terrifying mountain cries, Portlock Bay on the Kenai Peninsula later became home to a bustling salmon cannery and community of Russian-Alutiiq settlers in 1921. Despite strict rules, curfews, and armed guards established to protect against Nantiinaq—a legendary cannibalistic giant said to rule the surrounding forest—workers began turning up mutilated or disappearing entirely. After multiple violent incursions into the cannery building, including a devastating fire, and over fifteen recorded disappearances, the remaining residents fled overnight in 1950, leaving the area a desolate ghost town widely regarded as cursed ground.

#location
The historic Portlock cannery site is located on the southern edge of the Kenai Peninsula along the shores of Port Chatham bay in Alaska.

#title
The Phantom Bride of Baranof Castle

#summary
Following the 1867 Alaska purchase, a heartbroken Russian governor's daughter who allegedly died on her wedding night became a legendary apparition haunting a historic rocky promontory in Sitka.

#details
Following the transfer of Alaska to the United States in 1867, a persistent ghost story emerged regarding Castle Hill in Sitka, where a Russian governor's two-story wooden and brick building known as the castle once stood before burning down in 1894. According to local folklore, a young woman—variously described as a governor's daughter or a Russian princess—was forced into an unwanted arranged marriage. On her wedding night, amidst the celebrations, she vanished and was later found dead in a drawing-room. Visitors and soldiers stationed at the abandoned site reported encountering her sorrowful apparition draped in a dark robe, wandering the halls and cupola while leaving the faint scent of wild roses in her wake.

#location
Baranof Castle State Historical Site (Castle Hill) overlooking Sitka Harbor in Sitka, Alaska.

#title
The Phantom of the Hotel Captain Cook's Restroom

#summary
Following a tragic suicide in 1972, the spirit of a young woman took up residence in the women's restroom of Tower 2 at the Hotel Captain Cook in Anchorage, becoming particularly possessive of the final toilet stall.

#details
Constructed by former Alaska governor Walter J. Hickel, the Hotel Captain Cook opened its first tower in 1965 and expanded with the addition of Tower 2 in 1972. According to local legend, following the grand opening festivities of the second tower, a distressed young woman tragically took her own life inside the public women's restroom. Over the decades, visitors and hotel staff have reported unsettling paranormal occurrences in the room, including sudden feelings of intense unease, fainting spells, and more aggressive manifestations such as stall walls shaking violently or invisible hands grabbing users by the ankles. To appease the spirit and mitigate disturbances, hotel management ultimately ordered the final stall on the left to be permanently bolted shut.

#location
The women's restroom in Tower 2 of the Hotel Captain Cook, located at 939 W 5th Ave in Anchorage, Alaska.

#title
The Phantoms of McCarthy Road

#summary
Travelers and workers along the McCarthy Road near Chitina frequently report encountering ghostly figures, phantom voices, and mysterious tombstones materializing along the roadside.

#details
After the shutdown of the historic Kennecott copper mines and the Copper River & Northwestern Railway, the surrounding Alaskan wilderness became steeped in local folklore. Motorists driving along the McCarthy Road—which closely follows the abandoned railway grade near Chitina—have repeatedly encountered paranormal phenomena. Travelers and construction crews have reported seeing phantom tombstones appear by the roadside only to vanish into thin air, alongside disembodied voices and the spectral figures of long-dead miners and children haunting the remote corridor.

#location
The hauntings occur along the McCarthy Road near Chitina, Alaska, specifically where the dirt route parallels the abandoned Copper River & Northwestern Railway grade.

#title
The Specter of Room 315

#summary
Juneau's historic Alaskan Hotel harbors a notorious reputation for hauntings centered around Room 315, where eerie phenomena culminated in a dramatic incident involving a visiting Navy sailor.

#details
Opened in 1913, the Alaskan Hotel in downtown Juneau is widely believed by staff and visitors to be haunted, with the epicenter of its ghostly lore located in Room 315 on the third floor. In May 2007, a U.S. Navy sailor aboard a docked missile cruiser specifically requested to stay in the hotel's famously "haunted room." Later that night, police and hotel staff were summoned after glass shattered and the sailor crashed through a third-story window, falling between adjacent buildings before miraculously surviving his injuries. Responding officers discovered the walls of Room 315 covered in blood, cementing the room's chilling reputation among employees and guests.

#title
The Spectral Madam of the Red Onion Saloon

#summary
A historic 1898 brothel and saloon in Skagway is said to be haunted by the spirit of a former madam who still tends to the building and its rooms.

#details
Opening its doors in 1898 to serve miners during the gold rush, the Red Onion Saloon in Skagway operated both as a saloon on the ground floor and a ten-room brothel upstairs, where madams used miniature dolls to indicate whether the rooms were occupied. Over the years, staff and visitors have reported numerous paranormal phenomena on the upper floor, most notably the ghost of a former resident named Lydia. Witnesses have described hearing heavy footsteps on the stairs, smelling strong waves of vintage perfume accompanied by sudden drops in temperature, and seeing a full apparition running down the hallway into the former madam's room. Witnesses also claim the spirit occasionally waters the indoor plants and maintains a watchful presence over the historical museum.

#location
The Red Onion Saloon at 201 Broadway in Skagway, Alaska.

#title
The Waiting Bride of the Golden North Hotel

#summary
During the Klondike Gold Rush, a young woman named Mary reportedly locked herself away on the top floor of a Skagway hotel waiting for her fiancé who never returned, becoming a spectral fixture of the building.

#details
Built in 1898 during the height of the Klondike Gold Rush, the Golden North Hotel in Skagway became the setting for one of Alaska's most famous ghost tales. Local legend recounts that a prospector known as "Klondike Ike" arrived at the hotel with his fiancée, Mary. The next morning, Ike set off into the surrounding mountains to stake a gold claim and never came back. Heartbroken, Mary refused to leave her third-floor room, where she eventually passed away. Patrons and staff over the decades have claimed to see her shadowy apparition peering out of the third-floor windows or wandering the hotel's corridors, perpetually searching for her lost fiancé.

#location
The Golden North Hotel located at 3rd Avenue and Broadway in Skagway, Alaska.
