#title
The Legend of Grand Lake

#summary
An old indigenous legend recounts a devastating battle on the shores of Grand Lake where women and children perished on an overturned raft, leaving their ghostly echoes to haunt the waters.

#details
According to local folklore recorded in the 19th century by early pioneer Joseph L. Wescott, Grand Lake was once the campsite of a band of Ute Indians led by Chief Chekiwow. During a surprise attack by rival Cheyenne and Arapaho warriors, the Ute women and children were placed onto a raft and pushed onto the lake for safety. Amidst a violent storm and fierce combat that wiped out the Ute warriors, a giant wave capsized the raft, drowning every woman and child aboard. The surviving Ute chief declared the lake haunted ground. To this day, locals and visitors report hearing unearthly wails and seeing ghostly forms rising from the deep, mist-covered waters of Grand Lake.

#location
Grand Lake, located in Grand County, Colorado, at the western edge of Rocky Mountain National Park.
