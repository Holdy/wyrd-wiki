Niwot's Curse

Summary

When gold prospectors encroached upon the Boulder Valley in 1858, Arapaho leader Chief Niwot prophesied that everyone captivated by the region's beauty would ultimately destroy it by staying. Despite initial threats and tensions, a shaman's dream of an overwhelming flood convinced the chief to pursue a path of peaceful coexistence.

Details

In the autumn of 1858, a band of gold-seeking prospectors led by Captain Thomas Aikins established a camp where Boulder Creek emerges from the Front Range onto the Great Plains. Chief Niwot of the Southern Arapaho rode out from nearby Valmont Butte with his elder braves to greet the intruders, warning them that the valley was cursed. He declared that anyone who saw the beauty of the valley would want to stay, and their presence would ultimately be the undoing of that very beauty. Although the confrontation nearly escalated into violence—with braves preparing a war party while settlers plied Niwot with food and drink—an Arapaho shaman experienced a vision of a great flood covering the land. Recognizing that an influx of white settlers was inevitable, Niwot chose a path of peace to protect his people from being swept away.
