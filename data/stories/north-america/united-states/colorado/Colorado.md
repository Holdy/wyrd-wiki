#title
Niwot's Curse

#summary
When gold prospectors encroached upon the Boulder Valley in 1858, Arapaho leader Chief Niwot prophesied that everyone captivated by the region's beauty would ultimately destroy it by staying. Despite initial threats and tensions, a shaman's dream of an overwhelming flood convinced the chief to pursue a path of peaceful coexistence.

#details
In the autumn of 1858, a band of gold-seeking prospectors led by Captain Thomas Aikins established a camp where Boulder Creek emerges from the Front Range onto the Great Plains. Chief Niwot of the Southern Arapaho rode out from nearby Valmont Butte with his elder braves to greet the intruders, warning them that the valley was cursed. He declared that anyone who saw the beauty of the valley would want to stay, and their presence would ultimately be the undoing of that very beauty. Although the confrontation nearly escalated into violence—with braves preparing a war party while settlers plied Niwot with food and drink—an Arapaho shaman experienced a vision of a great flood covering the land. Recognizing that an influx of white settlers was inevitable, Niwot chose a path of peace to protect his people from being swept away.

#location
Valmont Butte is located in Boulder County, Colorado, approximately four miles northeast of central Boulder.

#upstream-source
Morley, Judy Mattivi, Ph.D. ("OUR PEOPLE: Southern Arapahos Are Part of Boulder's Spirit", Boulder Magazine, 2005).

#title
The Ghost Lights of Silver Cliff Cemetery

#summary
Visitors to a historic cemetery in Custer County report witnessing mysterious, dancing orbs of light that float among the weathered headstones and follow people through the grounds. These phantom lights have been documented since the late 19th century, long before electricity was introduced to the area.

#details
Since the early days of the mining boom in Silver Cliff and Westcliffe, locals and visitors have shared accounts of strange, glowing orbs appearing within the historic Silver Cliff Cemetery after dark. Eyewitnesses describe small, colorful lights—ranging from the size of a fingernail to several inches across—darting between headstones, floating lazily in the grass, and occasionally gathering around a central white marble cross or a larger, campfire-like glow. Observers have reported that the lights actively follow individuals across the cemetery grounds and remain unresponsive to headlights, weather conditions, or nearby town lights, sustaining a legend of ghostly phenomena that predates the arrival of electricity in the region.

#location
The Silver Cliff Cemetery is located just outside the town of Silver Cliff in Custer County, southern Colorado.

#title
The Ghostly Performer of the Central City Opera House

#summary
The historic Central City Opera House is reportedly haunted by the spirit of Mike Dougherty, a 19th-century miner and stage performer who startles backstage visitors with sudden cold spots, hair-ruffling, and the lingering scent of alcohol.

#details
Built in 1878 in the historic mining hub of Central City, the Central City Opera House hosted countless entertainment troupes during Colorado's gold rush. Among them was Mike Dougherty, a miner who became a beloved local stage performer in 1865 before tragically drinking himself to death. Decades later, long after the theater went dark or during quiet hours, backstage visitors and staff have repeatedly reported being startled by a strong, fleeting odor of alcohol, stout nudges on the shoulder, and their hair being lightly ruffled from behind in empty corridors. Strange flickering orbs of light and ghostly footsteps on the balconies have also been documented by visitors exploring the historic landmark.

#location
The Central City Opera House located at 124 Eureka Street in Central City, Colorado.

#title
The Haunting of Room 320 and the Cruise Room Postman

#summary
Room 320 of Denver's Oxford Hotel is said to be haunted by a woman who committed a tragic murder-suicide there in 1898, while the hotel's adjacent Cruise Room bar is visited by a phantom postal worker.

#details
In 1898, a woman named Florence Montague shot and killed her lover before taking her own life in Room 320 of the Oxford Hotel in downtown Denver. Ever since, single male guests staying in the room have reported feeling invisible hands pulling their arms and having their bedsheets violently ripped away. Downstairs in the hotel's Art Deco Cruise Room bar—a former Prohibition-era speakeasy—bartenders and patrons frequently report seeing a ghostly postal worker in a vintage uniform. He orders a beer, mutters about delivering Christmas gifts to the children, and leaves behind a bottle that inexplicably remains completely full.

#location
Room 320 of the Oxford Hotel located at 1600 17th Street in Denver, Colorado.

#title
The Legend of Grand Lake

#summary
An old indigenous legend recounts a devastating battle on the shores of Grand Lake where women and children perished on an overturned raft, leaving their ghostly echoes to haunt the waters.

#details
According to local folklore recorded in the 19th century by early pioneer Joseph L. Wescott, Grand Lake was once the campsite of a band of Ute Indians led by Chief Chekiwow. During a surprise attack by rival Cheyenne and Arapaho warriors, the Ute women and children were placed onto a raft and pushed onto the lake for safety. Amidst a violent storm and fierce combat that wiped out the Ute warriors, a giant wave capsized the raft, drowning every woman and child aboard. The surviving Ute chief declared the lake haunted ground. To this day, locals and visitors report hearing unearthly wails and seeing ghostly forms rising from the deep, mist-covered waters of Grand Lake.

#location
Grand Lake, located in Grand County, Colorado, at the western edge of Rocky Mountain National Park.

#upstream-source
Joseph L. Wescott and Fred H. Quincy (pamphlet published January 26, 1926, reprinted from the Grand Lake Prospector, July 29, 1882).

#title
The Phantom Lady of the Cripple Creek Palace Hotel

#summary
The historic Palace Hotel in Cripple Creek is said to be haunted by an apparition in a white nightgown who mystically lights candles and turns on lights in empty rooms.

#details
Dating back to the gold rush era of 1893, the Palace Hotel (or Palace Block) in Cripple Creek has long been associated with local ghost lore. For decades, owners, family members, and visitors have reported sightings of an ethereal woman with dark hair, dressed in a long white nightgown with a lace-neck collar. Known locally by the name of Kitty Chambers (though historians note the true spirit may be a former manager named Mary Hedges), the phantom is famous for inexplicable paranormal activity. Over the years, witnesses have reported lights turning on in vacant rooms, bedsheets turning down by an unseen hand, and mysterious lit candles appearing in specific patterns on dining room and bar tables when no one else is in the building.

#location
The Palace Hotel located at the intersection of Bennett Avenue and Second Street in Cripple Creek, Colorado.

#title
The Phantom Prisoner of Phantom Canyon

#summary
Train passengers traveling through a rugged mountain gorge in the 1890s repeatedly reported seeing an executed convict walking along the tracks. Even after the railway was converted into a public road, spectral figures continued to haunt the route.

#details
During the 1890s, when the narrow-gauge Florence and Cripple Creek Railroad was operating through a remote mountain pass, train passengers were frequently startled by a grim apparition. Along the tracks, travelers would spot a man wearing a prison uniform, walking as though nothing was amiss. The deeply unnerving part of these encounters was that the man had been executed at the state prison in Cañon City just days prior to being seen wandering the canyon. Today, as motorists traverse the winding route known as Phantom Canyon Road, reports persist of ghostly encounters involving not only the executed prisoner but also the spirits of past miners and railroad workers.

#location
Phantom Canyon Road runs for 30 miles between Cañon City and Victor in Colorado, following the path of the historic Florence and Cripple Creek Railroad.

#title
The Restless Spirits of the Hotel Colorado

#summary
Established in 1893 in Glenwood Springs, the Hotel Colorado served as a naval hospital during World War II and is now reputed to be haunted by a wartime nurse and other historic phantoms.

#details
Styled after European Italian resorts, the grand hotel hosted presidents and notable figures before functioning as a naval hospital in WWII, with its basement serving as a temporary morgue. Among its most well-known hauntings is "Bobbie," a nurse reportedly killed by a jealous officer during the war, whose presence is signaled by the scent of vintage gardenia perfume. Other reported phenomena include the spirit of a woman in tower suite room 661 who repeatedly closes windows against drafts, and a ghostly presence smelling of cigar smoke lingering in the hallways.

#location
The Hotel Colorado is located at 526 Pine St. in Glenwood Springs, Colorado.

#title
The Spectral Echoes of the Lumber Baron Inn

#summary
Built in 1890 by Scottish immigrant John Mouat, this historic Denver mansion became the site of a tragic 1970 double murder that local lore links to persistent paranormal activity.

#details
Originally constructed as a grand Queen Anne-style Victorian mansion showcasing various rare woods, the building fell into decay and served as a tenement by 1970. During that year, a 17-year-old runaway girl living there was raped and strangled, and her 18-year-old friend was shot after discovering the crime. Although the murders remain unsolved, guests and paranormal investigators have since reported sightings of a young woman's apparition, phantom footsteps, and electronic voice phenomena echoing through the restored halls.

#location
The Lumber Baron Inn is located at 2555 W. 37th Ave. in Denver, Colorado.

#title
The Terrors of Room 320 at the Oxford Hotel

#summary
Opened in 1891 near Union Station in downtown Denver, the historic Oxford Hotel is famously haunted by the violent spirit of a woman murdered in Room 320.

#details
The Oxford Hotel was designed as Denver's premier luxury Victorian hotel, but behind its elegant façade lies a dark history. According to hotel lore, a woman was strangled in Room 320 in the late 1890s by a jealous lover. Today, male guests staying in Room 320 frequently report physical assaults, including scratches and feeling as though they are being strangled, as well as seeing an enraged female apparition in the room's mirrors. Additionally, a bearded apparition has been sighted staring at women in the hotel's restrooms.

#location
The Oxford Hotel is located at 1600 17th St. in downtown Denver, Colorado.
