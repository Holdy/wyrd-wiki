Melancholy Mary of North Head

Summary

Perched on the stormy Washington coast, the historic North Head Lighthouse is haunted by Mary Pesonen, the wife of the station's very first keeper who died tragically near the cliffs in 1923. Park staff and visitors have reported inexplicable electrical surges, moving objects, and phantom footsteps echoing through the keeper's quarters.

Details

Put into service in 1898 to protect vessels navigating the treacherous waters of the Columbia River's mouth, the lighthouse was managed by Alexander "Ikey" Pesonen and his wife Mary. Following a diagnosis of severe depression in 1923, Mary tragically fell from the steep ocean cliffs near the station. Ever since, local newspapers and oral histories have maintained that her sorrowful spirit, often called "Melancholy Mary," lingers around the lightkeeper's house, where visitors and volunteers continue to experience unexplained electrical glitches and phantom noises.
