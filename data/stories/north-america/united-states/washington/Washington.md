#title
Melancholy Mary of North Head

#summary
Perched on the stormy Washington coast, the historic North Head Lighthouse is haunted by Mary Pesonen, the wife of the station's very first keeper who died tragically near the cliffs in 1923. Park staff and visitors have reported inexplicable electrical surges, moving objects, and phantom footsteps echoing through the keeper's quarters.

#details
Put into service in 1898 to protect vessels navigating the treacherous waters of the Columbia River's mouth, the lighthouse was managed by Alexander "Ikey" Pesonen and his wife Mary. Following a diagnosis of severe depression in 1923, Mary tragically fell from the steep ocean cliffs near the station. Ever since, local newspapers and oral histories have maintained that her sorrowful spirit, often called "Melancholy Mary," lingers around the lightkeeper's house, where visitors and volunteers continue to experience unexplained electrical glitches and phantom noises.

#location
North Head Lighthouse is situated at Cape Disappointment State Park near Ilwaco, Washington.

#title
The Ghosts of Thornewood Castle

#summary
Constructed from 15th-century English materials on the shores of American Lake in Lakewood, Washington, Thornewood Castle is widely reported to be haunted by its original builders and other lingering spirits.

#details
Commissioned by wealthy banker Chester Thorne and designed with his wife Anna, Thornewood Castle was built between 1908 and 1911 using authentic 400-year-old bricks, oak timbers, and stained glass shipped around Cape Horn from England. After the Thornes passed away, generations of visitors, guests, and owners reported ghostly phenomena across the sprawling 54-room Tudor Gothic manor. Most notably, lightbulbs in Chester's former second-floor bedroom are frequently found unscrewed, Anna’s reflection has been seen in her original bedroom mirror overlooking the sunken English garden, and visitors near American Lake have reported glimpses of a small child standing silently by the shore.

#location
Thornewood Castle is located on the eastern shore of American Lake in Lakewood, Washington.

#title
The Historic Spirits of Thornewood Castle

#summary
Built in 1911 using 400-year-old English bricks, Lakewood's Thornewood Castle is haunted by its original wealthy owner, his wife, and a child said to have drowned in the nearby lake.

#details
Commissioned by businessman Chester Thorne and completed in 1911, Thornewood Castle is a 27,000-square-foot English Tudor and Gothic mansion built with genuine antique materials imported from England. After Chester Thorne's death in his second-floor bedroom in 1927 and his wife Anna's passing in 1954, their presences reportedly lingered on the estate. Guests and staff have reported light bulbs unscrewing themselves in Chester's former room, Anna's reflection appearing in an antique mirror in the bridal suite, and the phantom sighting of a small child standing near the edge of American Lake where a grandchild of a former owner tragically drowned.

#location
Thornewood Castle is located at 8601 Gravelly Lake Drive SW in Lakewood, Washington.

#title
The Monk of Manresa Castle

#summary
Built in the late 19th century as a grand residence in Port Townsend, Manresa Castle is haunted by the spirit of a distraught monk who reportedly took his own life in the attic. Guests and staff have frequently reported eerie occurrences, including disembodied footsteps, mysterious cold spots, and doors opening on their own.

#details
Originally constructed in 1892 by Port Townsend's first mayor, Charles Eisenbeis, the building later became a Jesuit college before operating as a historic hotel. Local folklore centers on a priest or monk who allegedly hanged himself in the bell tower or attic out of despair. Over the decades, guests staying in rooms such as 302, 304, and 306 have reported hearing heavy footsteps pacing the empty attic above them, witnessing lights flicker without explanation, and feeling sudden, unnerving drops in temperature.

#location
Manresa Castle is located at 651 Cleveland Street in Port Townsend, Washington.

#title
The Prophet of Highway 12

#summary
Following the 1980 eruption of Mount St. Helens, drivers along Highway 12 reported picking up a mysterious female hitchhiker who warned of an impending second catastrophe before abruptly vanishing from moving vehicles.

#details
In the wake of the devastating May 1980 eruption of Mount St. Helens, motorists traveling along Highway 12 about 80 miles north of the volcano reported picking up a beautiful woman with haunting eyes. She prophesied a second massive volcanic eruption scheduled between October 12 and 14 that would devastate the surrounding region. Before bewildered drivers could process her words or check on her, she vanished into thin air from the passenger seat or back seat while vehicles were traveling at highway speeds. Although subsequent seismic activity in October was milder and slightly delayed, multiple independent accounts of the phantom woman circulated among local police departments.

#location
This paranormal encounter takes place along Highway 12, located roughly 80 miles north of Mount St. Helens near Castle Rock, Washington.

#title
The Restless Patrons of the Oxford Saloon

#summary
Operating since 1910 in a historic Snohomish building with a violent past, the Oxford Saloon is haunted by former bouncers, murdered bordello workers, and past owners.

#details
Established in 1910 in a building originally constructed as a dry goods store in 1890, the Oxford Saloon served as a bustling gambling den and brothel where numerous violent confrontations and murders occurred. Among its resident ghosts is Henry, a moonlighting police officer who was stabbed to death on the stairs while breaking up a bar fight and now allegedly sneaks into the ladies' restroom. Another famous spirit is Katherine, a brothel manager who was found decapitated in a bathtub on the second floor, alongside Amelia, a sex worker whose spirit is said to reach out from the walls. Additionally, former owner Nels Peterson is said to leave behind the scent of cigar smoke and appear mysteriously in photographs.

#location
The Oxford Saloon is located at 913 1st Street in Snohomish, Washington.

#title
The Spectral Governess of Roche Harbor

#summary
Operating continuously since the late 19th century on San Juan Island, the historic Hotel de Haro is haunted by the ghost of Adah Beeny, a former governess and secretary. Staff members have reported doors opening unprompted, appliances activating on their own, and phantom sounds of rustling clothing in empty rooms.

#details
Built in 1886 at Roche Harbor Resort, the Hotel de Haro served visiting dignitaries, businessmen, and luminaries like Theodore Roosevelt. According to local lore and staff accounts, the spirit of Adah Beeny—who served the founding McMillin family—still wanders the aged wooden corridors. Employees have repeatedly experienced inexplicable paranormal phenomena, such as storehouse doors unlocking themselves, items mysteriously shifting positions, and chilling sensations that make entering certain areas nearly impossible.

#location
Hotel de Haro is located within the Roche Harbor Resort on San Juan Island, Washington.

#title
The Spirits of Thornewood Castle

#summary
The historic English Gothic mansion in Lakewood, Washington, is said to be haunted by the spirits of its original builders and other apparitions tied to its storied past.

#details
Built between 1908 and 1911 by industrialist Chester Thorne on the shores of American Lake, Thornewood Castle was constructed using centuries-old bricks and materials shipped around Cape Horn from Europe. Following Thorne's death in 1927 and subsequent family tragedies on the estate, the 40-room mansion became the subject of numerous local ghost legends. Guests and staff have reported encountering Chester Thorne in his former bedroom—where lightbulbs are frequently found mysteriously unscrewed—and sighting his wife, Anna, looking out over the gardens or appearing as a reflection in an antique mirror. Additionally, visitors have occasionally glimpsed a spectral child standing by the edge of the lake.

#location
Thornewood Castle is located on the shores of American Lake in Lakewood, Pierce County, Washington.

None.
