#title
Lydia of the Jamestown Bridge

#summary
On rainy and foggy nights, motorists near Jamestown encounter a young woman in a white dress requesting a ride, only to vanish into thin air upon arrival at her destination. Her spectral appearance is said to mark the site of a tragic automobile accident.

#details
In 1924, a university student named Burke Hardison was driving home to High Point on a foggy, rainy night when he spotted a young woman standing beside the road near an overpass. Dressed in a white gown, she signaled for him to stop and asked for a ride to High Point. Hardison obliged and drove her to her destination, but when he stepped out of his car, she had completely vanished. Upon knocking on the door of the house, her grieving mother informed him that her daughter had tragically died in a car accident at that exact overpass the previous year. Over the decades, numerous other drivers have reported similar sightings of the phantom hitchhiker near the abandoned bridge and railroad underpass.

#location
The ghost is sighted near the railroad bridge underpass and abandoned bridge on E. Main Street in Jamestown, North Carolina.

Nancy Roberts, *An Illustrated Guide to Ghosts & Mysterious Occurrences in the Old North State*.

#title
The Burial of Samuel Jocelyn

#summary
A grieving friend in Wilmington was visited by the apparition of a recently buried companion, who insisted he had been buried alive and proved it when his coffin was exhumed.

#details
In March 1810, Samuel R. Jocelyn died unexpectedly in Wilmington and was interred in the graveyard at St. James Episcopal Church. Prior to his death, Jocelyn and his friend Alexander Hostler had debated whether the dead could return to earth, agreeing that whoever died first would attempt to reveal themselves. A few days after the funeral, Hostler was startled in his room by the sudden appearance of Jocelyn, who pleaded, "How could you let me be buried when I was not dead?" Jocelyn instructed Hostler to open his coffin, warning that he would be found lying face downward instead of in his original position. Disturbed and skeptical, Hostler enlisted the help of Lewis Toomer to exhume the grave by night. Upon removing the coffin lid and shining a dark lantern inside, they discovered the horrifying truth: Jocelyn's body had indeed turned face downward, confirming the ghastly mistake.

#location
The events center on the cemetery at St. James Episcopal Church, situated at the corner of Fourth and Market Streets in Wilmington, New Hanover County, North Carolina.

Alfred Moore Waddell, A History of New Hanover Co., and the Lower Cape Fear Region 1723-1800 (1909).

#title
The Devil's Tramping Ground

#summary
Located in the pine woods of Chatham County, North Carolina, a mysterious 40-foot barren circle of ground is said to be paced nightly by Satan as he contemplates his evil deeds. Any objects left inside the circle overnight are invariably cleared away by morning.

#details
In western Chatham County, approximately ten miles from Siler City, lies a perfectly round, 40-foot-wide path where normal vegetation refuses to grow. Regional legend maintains that the Devil frequents this location on his nightly walks, pacing the circle while contemplating his mischief. Locals and visitors have tested the legend over the decades by placing logs, sticks, and other obstacles inside the barren path, only to find them completely cleared away by morning. Alternative folklore attributes the circle to ancient Native American tribal dances or a battle memorial site tied to the Lost Colony of Roanoke, while scientific soil studies have also pointed to extreme salt content and sterility as natural factors.

#location
The Devil's Tramping Ground is situated in the pine woods roughly ten miles from Siler City in western Chatham County, North Carolina.

#details
For generations, locals near Harper's Crossroads have whispered about a mysterious forty-foot circle where no grass, weeds, or trees will ever sprout. According to local folklore, the barren earth is scorched by the cloven hooves of the Devil himself, who spends his nights pacing around the perimeter plotting the damnation of human souls. Dogs whimper and refuse to enter the clearing, and men who attempted overnight vigils allegedly fled or lost their sanity from whatever terror they witnessed. #title
The Ghost of Jamestown Bridge

#summary
A young woman in a white dress known as "Lydia" has reportedly haunted the bridge and railway underpass area in Jamestown, North Carolina since the 1920s, vanishing after being picked up by motorists on rainy nights.

#details
Dating back to initial accounts collected around 1924, travelers driving through Jamestown on wet, foggy nights have encountered a young woman in a formal white dress flagging down cars for assistance to get to High Point. Motorists who give her a ride have reported that she mysteriously vanishes from the back or passenger seat of the car right before arriving at her destination, or vanishes the moment they step out to help. Local folklore connects the apparition to a fatal automobile accident from the 1920s, with later historians suggesting a link to a tragic 1920 road accident involving a young woman named Annie Jackson near the same stretch of highway.

#location
The old abandoned bridge and railway underpass near E. Main Street in Jamestown, North Carolina (35°59'50.0"N 79°55'33.1"W).

#upstream-source
An Illustrated Guide to Ghosts & Mysterious Occurrences in the Old North State by Nancy Roberts (1950) / North Carolina Legends by Richard Walser.

#title
The Ghost of Maco Station

#summary
A flickering lantern light has been sighted for over a century along old railway tracks in Brunswick County, said to be carried by the headless ghost of a railroad conductor killed in an 1867 wreck.

#details
In 1867, a wood-burning train suffered a tragic accident near a rural rail stop known as Farmer's Turnout (later Maco Station). Conductor Joe Baldwin, riding in the rear car, realized his carriage had uncoupled and waved a lantern in a desperate attempt to stop an oncoming train. The resulting collision decapitated him, and witnesses claimed his lantern flew into the adjacent swamp and continued to burn. In the decades that followed, railroad workers, travelers, and even President Grover Cleveland reported seeing a mysterious, flickering light moving along the tracks. Some workers even mistook the phantom light for a signal and brought their trains to a halt. Although the tracks were removed around 1980, the legend of old Joe Baldwin searching for his head remains a famous piece of regional lore.

#location
The apparition was historically sighted along the former railroad tracks near the old Maco Station site in Brunswick County, North Carolina.

#upstream-source
John Harden, Tar Heel Ghosts (1954).

#reference
https://www.ncpedia.org/maco-light

#title
The Ghost of St. James Cemetery

#summary
Mistakenly pronounced dead following an argument in 1810, Samuel Jocelyn was buried alive at a Wilmington church cemetery, where his grieving ghost is said to still linger near his grave.

#details
In 1810, following a violent argument with his wife at a hunting lodge, a young man named Samuel Jocelyn rode out into the Honey Island Swamp in the middle of winter, where he was found cold, stiff, and unresponsive the next day. Believed dead, he was quickly buried in the historic cemetery behind St. James Episcopal Church in Wilmington. Three days later, Jocelyn reportedly appeared in the dreams of two friends, begging them to dig him up. When his friends finally relented and exhumed the body, they discovered him lying in a dried pool of blood with his fingers completely clawed down to the bone from desperately trying to escape his casket. Local accounts and ghost tour guides state that his shadowy apparition continues to haunt the church grounds where he met his tragic fate.

#location
St. James Episcopal Church Cemetery located at 25 S. 3rd Street in Wilmington, North Carolina.
