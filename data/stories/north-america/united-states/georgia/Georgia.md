#title
Echoes of the Winecoff Hotel Fire

#summary
Decades after the deadliest hotel fire in U.S. history, current occupants and passersby in downtown Atlanta report hearing phantom screams, smelling smoke, and seeing trapped faces in the windows.

#details
Opened in 1913 as the Winecoff Hotel at 176 Peachtree Street NW, the building was advertised as completely fireproof. However, on December 7, 1946, a catastrophic fire swept through the structure, claiming more than 100 lives. Although the building has since been renovated and renamed the Ellis Hotel, the tragedy continues to echo through time. Current guests and staff report that fire alarms routinely trigger at 2:48 a.m.—the exact moment the blaze began—while visitors outside occasionally spot terror-stricken faces pressed against the upper-story windows crying for help.

#location
The Ellis Hotel (formerly the Winecoff Hotel) is located at 176 Peachtree Street NW in Atlanta, Georgia.

#title
Lorenzo Dow's Curse

#summary
When a rowdy frontier town in Georgia violently rejected an eccentric traveling preacher, he placed a devastating curse upon the settlement that caused it to wither away. While fires, storms, and economic ruin eventually erased the town from the map, the home of the one resident who showed him kindness was miraculously spared.

#details
In the 1800s, the rough frontier town of Jacksonboro, Georgia, was known for its lawlessness and rowdy saloons. When the eccentric evangelist Lorenzo Dow arrived to preach against alcohol, drunken townspeople pelted him with rotten vegetables and attempted to lynch him. Only the intervention of a pious resident, Seaborn Goodall, saved Dow and provided him shelter for the night. The next morning, Dow rode to the town limits, shook the dust from his shoes, and cursed Jacksonboro to wither and die from its wickedness, while proclaiming that the Goodall house would remain untouched. Over the following years, a series of unexplainable fires, destructive storms, and the decline of the local lumber industry caused the town to empty completely and disappear into history, leaving the Seaborn Goodall House as the only structure left standing.

#location
The historic Seaborn Goodall House is situated at the end of Dell Goodall Road, approximately five miles outside of Sylvania in Screven County, Georgia.

#title
The Apparitions of Heritage Hall

#summary
Visitors and staff touring a historic 19th-century Greek Revival home in Madison frequently report unexplained sounds, shadow figures, and the tragic lingering presence of past residents who died within its walls.

#details
Built in 1811 and later embellished with grand white columns in the 1840s, Heritage Hall served as the home of Dr. Elijah Jones before becoming a historic house museum. Local tradition notes that at least four individuals took their final breaths in the house, including Virginia Nisbet, who tragically passed away in the master bedroom in 1851 during childbirth. Visitors touring the home report hearing the eerie sound of children laughing in empty upstairs rooms, seeing unusual shadow figures, and encountering lingering paranormal activity commemorated today with Victorian hair-wreath mourning displays.

#location
Heritage Hall is located along Main Street in Madison, Georgia.

Morgan County Historical Society local archives and oral traditions.

#title
The Ghost of Smith Hall

#summary
Mortally wounded during the Civil War, a young soldier named John J. Griffen, Jr. died while seeking refuge at LaGrange College, and his restless spirit continues to haunt the building's halls.

#details
During the Civil War, John J. Griffen, Jr. suffered a battle wound near Newnan, Georgia. Deciding to bypass an overcrowded military hospital, he traveled to LaGrange to seek medical care and shelter from his sister, Indiana, who was a student at the academy. Despite her nursing, his injuries proved fatal, and he passed away in the building's guest room. In the years since, students and college officials have reported frequent paranormal phenomena inside Smith Hall, including sudden freezing temperatures, being tripped on staircases, and hearing a soft voice echoing through the corridors, calling out the name "Indiana."

#location
Smith Hall stands on the campus of LaGrange College in LaGrange, Georgia.

#title
The Haunted Pillar of Broad Street

#summary
A stone pillar standing in downtown Augusta is said to be cursed by a traveling preacher, bringing destruction and death to anyone who dares to move or damage it.

#details
The pillar is the sole surviving remnant of the old city market that straddled Broad Street from 1830 until a winter tornado destroyed the building in February 1878. According to local folklore, a traveling preacher was denied permission to evangelize at the market and placed a curse upon it, prophesying that a great storm would destroy the market except for a single pillar, and that anyone who attempted to move or destroy that remaining pillar would be struck dead. Over the decades, the pillar has been struck by automobiles and toppled by cotton bales, yet the legend persists as a cornerstone of Augusta folklore.

#location
The Haunted Pillar stands on the southeast corner of Fifth and Broad Streets in Augusta, Georgia.

#title
The Haunted Tales and Curses of Euharlee

#summary
In the historic northwest Georgia town of Euharlee, visitors and landowners report chilling encounters with restless spirits at Mount Zion Cemetery's 19th-century graves and the town's 1886 covered bridge.

#details
Nestled along Euharlee Creek, the rural town of Euharlee carries scars from the Civil War when General William T. Sherman's troops camped on the land now known as Tilley Mill. Adjacent to the mill ruins lies Mount Zion Cemetery, where property owners and surveyors have repeatedly experienced a strange, unsettling phenomenon: an invisible force—described as a white cloth sweeping across their brows—whenever they tend to the tombstone of Leddy Rogers. A short distance away in the center of town stands the 1886 Euharlee Covered Bridge, which local folklore ties to an ancient witch's curse. Legends recount tragic figures haunting the bridge, including the sounds of a groaning hangman's rope and phantom footsteps, phenomena reinforced during overnight paranormal vigils when inexplicable boot and hoof prints appeared across guarded sand-covered floors.

#location
Mount Zion Cemetery at Tilley Mill and the Euharlee Covered Bridge in Euharlee, Georgia.

#upstream-source

#title
The Phantom Sailors of The Pirate's House

#summary
Dating back to the 18th century, Savannah's legendary Pirate's House tavern is haunted by the spectral remnants of drunken sailors, ruthless buccaneers, and men who were shanghaied through underground tunnels.

#details
Situated in Savannah's historic district, the Pirate's House incorporates the Herb House, built in 1754 as the oldest existing building in Georgia. In its early days, the tavern was a notorious gathering place for international seamen and pirates associated with Blackbeard's crew. Local folklore and historical accounts maintain that intoxicated patrons in the rum cellar were frequently shanghaied—unwittingly hauled through secret underground tunnels directly to the Savannah River and forced onto outbound ships. Today, staff and diners report heavy boot steps on wooden floorboards, disembodied moans echoing from the sealed tunnels, and phantom seamen watching from the corners before fading into nothingness.

#location
The Pirate's House is located at 20 E Broad St, Savannah, GA 31401.

#title
The Roll Call of Oakland Cemetery

#summary
Atlanta's historic Oakland Cemetery is haunted by the restless echoes of Civil War soldiers and phantom roll calls echoing through its Victorian grounds.

#details
Chartered in 1850 and sprawling across nearly 50 acres, Oakland Cemetery is Atlanta's oldest public burial ground and holds the graves of thousands of unknown Confederate and Union soldiers. Visitors exploring the Confederate section after dark have experienced bone-chilling drops in temperature, phantom drumming, and eerie roll calls where disembodied voices call out lists of names, with unseen entities responding "Here!" Others have reported terrifying visions of ghostly soldiers hanging from ancient oak branches or stone monuments coming to life in the mist.

#location
Oakland Cemetery is located at 248 Oakland Ave SE, Atlanta, GA 30312.

#title
The Specters of Old Ghost Road

#summary
Motorists navigating a dark, tree-canopied dirt road in rural Georgia report encountering mysterious floating orbs and the wandering spirits of Civil War soldiers. Local drivers warn against stopping along the route, where disembodied footsteps and sudden electronic failures plague the superstitious track.

#details
Old Ghost Road in Brooklet, Georgia, is a secluded dirt lane infamous for its eerie atmosphere and paranormal encounters. According to local lore, visitors who park along the road at midnight and flash their headlights three times will witness mysterious glowing orbs drifting through the heavy oak canopy. The rhythmic crunch of gravel often mimics footsteps following closely behind cars, while witnesses report shadowy figures and the lost spirits of Civil War soldiers lingering in the dark tree line. Additionally, vehicles traversing the road frequently experience inexplicable electrical malfunctions and power losses.

#location
Old Ghost Road is located in the rural town of Brooklet, Bulloch County, Georgia.

#title
The Spirits of The Marshall House

#summary
Savannah’s oldest operating hotel, which served as a Civil War field hospital, is haunted by the lingering spirits of amputee soldiers, playful ghost children, and its original owner, Mary Marshall.

#details
Built in 1851 by Mary Magdalene Leaver Marshall, the Marshall House on East Broughton Street was occupied by the Union Army under General William Tecumseh Sherman and used as a field hospital during the Civil War. Visitors and staff have reported numerous paranormal occurrences over the decades, including phantom sounds of a typewriter from the room where author Joel Chandler Harris once stayed, occasional whiffs of rotting flesh in rooms tied to the hospital era, phantom children playing and bouncing balls in the hallways, and sightings of an amputee soldier walking the halls while holding his missing arm.

#location
The Marshall House is located at 123 E Broughton St, Savannah, GA 31401.
