Lorenzo Dow's Curse

Summary

When a rowdy frontier town in Georgia violently rejected an eccentric traveling preacher, he placed a devastating curse upon the settlement that caused it to wither away. While fires, storms, and economic ruin eventually erased the town from the map, the home of the one resident who showed him kindness was miraculously spared.

Details

In the 1800s, the rough frontier town of Jacksonboro, Georgia, was known for its lawlessness and rowdy saloons. When the eccentric evangelist Lorenzo Dow arrived to preach against alcohol, drunken townspeople pelted him with rotten vegetables and attempted to lynch him. Only the intervention of a pious resident, Seaborn Goodall, saved Dow and provided him shelter for the night. The next morning, Dow rode to the town limits, shook the dust from his shoes, and cursed Jacksonboro to wither and die from its wickedness, while proclaiming that the Goodall house would remain untouched. Over the following years, a series of unexplainable fires, destructive storms, and the decline of the local lumber industry caused the town to empty completely and disappear into history, leaving the Seaborn Goodall House as the only structure left standing.
