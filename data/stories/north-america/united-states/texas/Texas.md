#title
The Big Thicket Light of Bragg Road

#summary
A mysterious ghostly light periodically appears at night along Bragg Road in Hardin County, Texas, with local folklore attributing the phenomenon to headless railroad workers, murdered laborers, or lost travelers.

#details
The Big Thicket light, also known as the Saratoga light, is a nocturnal ghostly phenomenon reported along Bragg Road—a seven-mile dirt road built on an old Santa Fe railway bed running through the dense woods of the Big Thicket in Hardin County, Texas. Originally constructed in 1901 and later dismantled in 1934, the old railway line became a breeding ground for eerie legends after newspaper reports in 1960 popularized sightings of mysterious orbs and floating lights. Popular local lore attributes the lights to various tragic figures: a railroad employee decapitated in a train wreck who walks the road holding a lantern searching for his head, a crew of Mexican laborers murdered by their foreman for their accumulated wages and hastily buried in the woods, or a lost night hunter wandering the muddy briars forever. While skeptics attribute the lights to swamp gas, foxfire, or vehicle headlights from nearby roads, the eerie reputation of Bragg Road persists among visitors and locals alike.

#location
Bragg Road, running between Bragg Station (Farm Road 1293) and Saratoga in Hardin County, Texas.

#upstream-source
Francis E. Abernethy, ed., Tales from the Big Thicket (Austin: University of Texas Press, 1966).

#reference
https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/big-thicket-light

#title
The Big Thicket Light of Old Bragg Road

#summary
A mysterious, spectral light is said to wander up and down Old Bragg Road in the Big Thicket of Hardin County, Texas, appearing to night travelers as a glowing lantern or wandering flame.

#details
Old Bragg Road is a remote, seven-mile dirt road running through the dense woods of the Big Thicket in Hardin County, Texas, constructed over the abandoned bed of a Santa Fe railway branchline that operated from 1901 to 1934. While the tracks were still active and long after they were removed, motorists and local residents frequently reported seeing a strange, unexplained orb of light moving across the darkened right-of-way. Over the decades, various folkloric explanations emerged to account for the phenomenon, attributing the light to a decapitated railroad brakeman searching for his lost head with a lantern, a lost night hunter wandering the briars, or a railroad work crew murdered for their wages and buried nearby. The legend gained widespread notoriety in the summer of 1960 when local newspaper accounts brought it to regional attention, prompting hundreds of curious visitors to drive out to the isolated road in search of the ghostly light.

#location
Old Bragg Road runs through the Big Thicket between Farm Road 1293 near Bragg Station and the community of Saratoga in Hardin County, Texas.

#upstream-source
Francis E. Abernethy, ed., *Tales from the Big Thicket* (Austin: University of Texas Press, 1966).

#reference

#title
The Ghost Bride of Hotel Galvez

#summary
Room 501 of a historic Texas coastal hotel is said to be haunted by a heartbroken woman who took her own life after learning her fiancé perished at sea.

#details
Built in 1911 to welcome tourists back to Galveston following the devastating 1900 hurricane, the Hotel Galvez has accumulated numerous local legends over more than a century of operation. The most famous ghost story centers on a young woman named Audra, who stayed in Room 501 during the mid-1950s while awaiting her mariner fiancé to return from sea. Each day, she walked up to one of the hotel's rooftop turrets to watch for his ship on the horizon. When word finally reached her that her fiancé's ship had been lost, Audra succumbed to grief and hanged herself from the turret. Since that time, guests staying in Room 501 have frequently reported inexplicable disturbances, including water faucets turning on by themselves, lights flickering, and phantom reflections appearing in mirrors.

#location
Hotel Galvez is located at 2024 Seawall Boulevard in Galveston, Texas, with Room 501 situated on the top floor of the historic building.

#title
The Lady of the Seventh Floor at the Baker Hotel

#summary
The landmark Baker Hotel in Mineral Wells is said to be haunted by the spirit of a former manager's mistress who tragically plunged to her death from the upper floors.

#details
Dominating downtown Mineral Wells, Texas, the grand Baker Hotel opened in 1929 and became a renowned luxury resort before closing in 1970. Long before and after its closure, stories of paranormal phenomena circulated around the building. The most famous resident ghost is a woman said to be the mistress of the hotel's manager, who stayed in a suite on the southeast corner of the seventh floor. Distraught over their turbulent affair, she reportedly jumped to her death from the top of the building. Over the decades, staff and caretakers have reported eerie encounters on the seventh floor, including the distinct scent of her perfume, lipstick stains appearing on empty glasses in unrented rooms, and gentle flirtations directed at male workers.

#location
The Baker Hotel, located at 200 E. Park Avenue in Mineral Wells, Texas.

#title
The Light of Saratoga

#summary
A mysterious glowing orb periodically appears at night along an old timber road in the Big Thicket, inspiring enduring local legends of headless railroad workers and restless pioneers.

#details
Bragg Road is an unpaved, seven-mile corridor running through the dense woods of Hardin County, Texas, originally laid as a railroad bed in 1901 and abandoned after the tracks were removed in 1934. Even while the rail line was still active, travelers reported seeing a strange, unaccompanied light drifting along the right-of-way. Over the generations, the local community developed several tragic folk explanations for the phenomenon. The most popular legend claims the light is the lantern of a railroad employee who was decapitated in a train wreck and wanders the dark woods searching for his missing head. Other versions attribute the ghostly glow to a Mexican work crew supposedly murdered by a greedy foreman, a lost night hunter carrying a fire pan, or a troubled brakeman who died of grief after losing his livelihood. 

#location
Bragg Road runs through the Big Thicket in Hardin County, Texas, connecting the former site of Bragg Station near Farm Road 1293 to Saratoga.

#upstream-source
Francis E. Abernethy, ed., Tales from the Big Thicket (Austin: University of Texas Press, 1966).

#title
The Phantom of Green Elm Bridge

#summary
In 1948, four Texas cattlemen crossing an isolated bridge over the Trinity River witnessed a screaming, phantom woman floating through the air before vanishing into thin air.

#details
In October 1948, four cattlemen driving back to Chico, Texas, stopped their car on the Green Elm Cemetery Bridge—a wooden and iron bridge spanning the west fork of the Trinity River between Wizard Wells and Chico—to take a break at dusk. Suddenly, they were startled by a blood-curdling feminine scream originating from upstream. Looking up, they watched in terror as the apparition of a woman wearing a white dress floated about twenty feet above the river, thrashing and screaming as if drowning in unseen floodwaters. The ghostly woman drifted right over the top of the bridge before disappearing like a misty vapor into the river bend downstream, leaving the men profoundly shaken. Years later, locals and oilfield workers continued to report hearing or seeing the spectral woman near the bridge and the nearby 19th-century cemetery.

#location
Green Elm Cemetery Bridge spans the west fork of the Trinity River on the Jack-Wise County lines south of Texas FM 1810.

#title
The Restless Spirit of Colonel Driskill

#summary
Built in 1886, Austin's historic Driskill Hotel is said to be haunted by its original owner, Colonel Jesse Driskill, who lingers in the lobby and upper floors.

#details
Opened in December 1886, the Driskill Hotel was the grand vision of cattle baron and civic leader Colonel Jesse Driskill, designed to rival the finest hotels in the country. However, after financial devastation from harsh winters and droughts forced him to sell the property shortly after its completion, Driskill died in 1890 before fully enjoying its enduring success. Guests and staff at the Romanesque-style hotel have long reported encounters with a gentlemanly apparition believed to be the Colonel himself. Visitors frequently report smelling cigar smoke and hearing phantom footsteps in the lobby and bar area, as if the original owner is still patrolling and watching over his creation.

#location
The Driskill Hotel, located at 604 Brazos Street in Austin, Texas.

#title
The Spectral Maid of the Menger Hotel

#summary
The historic Menger Hotel in San Antonio is haunted by several spirits, most notably Sallie White, a Victorian-era maid who was tragically murdered by her husband.

#details
Opened in 1857 on the original battlegrounds of the Alamo in San Antonio, the Menger Hotel is regarded as one of the most haunted historic hotels in Texas. Among its many reported apparitions—including former President Teddy Roosevelt and cattle baron Captain Richard King—the most frequently sighted is Sallie White. Sallie worked as a beloved maid at the hotel during its early days, known for her friendly demeanor and dedication to keeping the premises tidy. Tragically, her life was cut short when she was killed by her abusive husband. Today, guests and hotel staff frequently report seeing her ghost dressed in an old-fashioned maid's outfit, dusting furniture or folding towels before mysteriously disappearing into thin air.

#location
The Menger Hotel, located at 203 Alamo Plaza in San Antonio, Texas.
