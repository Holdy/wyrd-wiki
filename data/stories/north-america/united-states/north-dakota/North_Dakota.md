#title
The Cursed Rock Faces of Wind Canyon

#summary
After a trapper and his family refused to abandon their home in the Dakota Badlands, an indigenous shaman cursed them to remain there forever. Consequently, their likenesses were etched into the rocky bluffs overlooking the Little Missouri River for eternity.

#details
In the early 1800s, a solitary trapper living along the Little Missouri River fell in love with a French widow, brought her to his isolated homestead, and started a family in what is now the North Dakota Badlands. As tensions mounted between encroaching settlers and local indigenous tribes, a shaman visited the family and demanded they leave their land. When the trapper firmly refused, declaring they would never leave their home, the shaman chanted a curse ensuring the white family would be trapped there forever. Shortly thereafter, a smallpox outbreak swept the area, claiming the lives of the wife, son, and trapper. Over the years, wind and water sculpted the soft rock bluffs along Wind Canyon into human-like faces that watch the sunset, fulfilling the shaman's curse.

#location
The rock faces are located along the Wind Canyon trail in the South Unit of Theodore Roosevelt National Park in western North Dakota, overlooking the Little Missouri River.

#title
The Ghost of Governor Frank Briggs at the Former Governor's Mansion

#summary
Staff and visitors at the historic Victorian mansion have reported unexplained footsteps, moving curtains, and self-operating doors in the master bedroom where Governor Frank Briggs passed away from tuberculosis in 1898.

#details
Built in 1884, the Victorian mansion served as the official residence for North Dakota governors until 1960 before becoming a museum managed by the State Historical Society. In 1898, Governor Frank Briggs died in the master bedroom from tuberculosis before completing his term or achieving his political goals. Local folklore and museum accounts suggest that his unfulfilled ambitions tethered him to the home. Staff and visitors have repeatedly reported paranormal activity concentrated around his former death room, including mysterious footsteps, curtains swaying without a draft, and doors opening and closing on their own accord.

#location
The Former Governor's Mansion at 320 E Avenue B in Bismarck, North Dakota.

#title
The Ghosts of the Custer House

#summary
Staff and tour guides at the reconstructed Custer House in Fort Abraham Lincoln report persistent paranormal phenomena, which local lore attributes to General Custer returning to his former home.

#details
At the reconstructed mansion of General George and Libbie Custer—located on the Missouri River bluffs at Fort Abraham Lincoln near Mandan—tour guides and park workers have frequently reported experiencing a distinct, unseen "presence." Witnesses have described feeling physical taps on the shoulder, observing objects such as dishes and candlesticks move on their own, and discovering valuable household linens wiped clean by invisible hands. According to local legend, spirits of individuals who meet a traumatic end return to the earthly location where they were happiest, which for General Custer was his spacious frontier residence prior to the Battle of Little Bighorn.

#location
The reconstructed Custer House at Fort Abraham Lincoln State Park, situated on the Missouri River bluffs just south of Mandan, North Dakota.

#title
The Gray Lady of Sims

#summary
For over a century, the ghost of a former pastor's wife known as the "Gray Lady" has been reported haunting the historic Scandinavian Evangelical Lutheran Church and parsonage in the ghost town of Sims, North Dakota.

#details
Bertha Dordal moved to Sims, North Dakota, in 1916 with her husband, Reverend Lars Dordal, and their three young children, hoping the dry western climate might help cure her tuberculosis. Tragically, her health declined, and she passed away in 1917 at just 36 years old. Following her death, parishioners and residents began reporting unusual phenomena inside the town's newly built Scandinavian Evangelical Lutheran Church and parsonage. Witnesses heard phantom organ music and unexplained footsteps, while others reported seeing a gray-toned apparition walking upstairs. Affectionately dubbed the "Gray Lady" due to the pallor common to tuberculosis sufferers, Bertha's spirit is remembered as a comforting and benevolent presence who would adjust windows, tidy cupboards, and lay blankets over cold visitors during chilly nights. 

#location
The Scandinavian Evangelical Lutheran Church and parsonage are located in the historic ghost town of Sims, North Dakota, situated approximately 47 miles southwest of Bismarck.

#title
The Haunting of Chateau de Mores

#summary
Built in 1883 as a summer hunting lodge, the historic Chateau de Mores in Medora is said to be haunted by the apparition and lingering presence of Medora de Mores.

#details
Constructed in 1883 by French diplomat and entrepreneur Marquis de Mores for his wife Medora, the 26-room hunting lodge and summer home overlooks the Badlands of Medora, North Dakota. Though Medora died while traveling and never returned to live permanently in the home, visitors, tour guides, and locals have long reported paranormal occurrences within its walls. Witnesses frequently report seeing the apparition of an elegant woman moving through the rooms, the indentation of a body on Medora's side of the bed, inexplicable cold spots, mysterious laughter, and lights turning on by themselves when the building is entirely empty.

#location
Chateau de Mores is located atop a bluff overlooking the town of Medora and Theodore Roosevelt National Park in western North Dakota.

#title
The Haunting of the Custer House

#summary
Tour guides and visitors at the historic Custer House have reported eerie phenomena, including the phantom sound of a billiard ball break and the apparition of a woman in a dark gown looking out an upper window.

#details
Located at Fort Abraham Lincoln, the Custer House is a reconstructed replica of the home once occupied by Lieutenant Colonel George Armstrong Custer and his wife, Libbie. Over the years, interpreters and visitors have reported several paranormal occurrences. Park tour guides have frequently reported hearing the sharp, distinct sound of a billiard ball break echoing from the game room, only to investigate and find no one there and no balls disturbed. Additionally, both guests and staff members in the early morning and late evening hours have sighted the ghostly figure of a woman in a dark gown standing at the second-floor window, gazing out over the fort grounds.

#location
The Custer House at Fort Abraham Lincoln State Park near Mandan, North Dakota.

#title
The Legend of White Lady's Lane

#summary
The ghost of a young woman named Anna Story wanders a rural North Dakota lane in a white nightgown every Halloween night. Murdered by a rejected suitor, her restless spirit searches eternally for peace.

#details
Long ago near Leyden and Walhalla, North Dakota, a young woman named Anna Story lived with her family. A peddler named Sam Kalil sought to marry Anna, but after a dispute over a prior bargain, Kalil forced his way into their home and shot Anna dead in her nightclothes. Though Kalil was eventually apprehended and sentenced to life imprisonment, Anna's spirit refused to rest. Today, her ghost—known as the White Lady—is said to haunt a misty rural stretch of road known as White Lady's Lane near Eddie's Bridge, wandering in her flowing white nightgown every Halloween night in search of the peace she never found in life.

#location
The hauntings occur along White Lady's Lane near Eddie's Bridge in Walhalla, North Dakota.

#title
The Specters of the Fargo Theatre

#summary
Opened in downtown Fargo in 1926, the historic Fargo Theatre is said to be haunted by a lady in white, phantom projection noises, and shadowy basement figures.

#details
Designed in the Art Deco style and opened on March 15, 1926, the Fargo Theatre served as a silent film palace and community hub before being restored in the late 20th century. Over the years, patrons, staff, and paranormal investigators have documented eerie phenomena throughout the building. The most famous spirit is the "Lady in White," a spectral figure reportedly seen lingering near the balcony or upper rows of the auditorium, accompanied by faint humming or sudden chills. In the projection booth, staff have reported knocking sounds, flickering lights, and the ghostly sound of film reels spinning when no equipment is running. Additionally, the maze-like basement is notorious for oppressive atmospheres, shadowy darting figures, and disembodied voices telling visitors to be quiet.

#location
The Fargo Theatre is located on Broadway Street in downtown Fargo, North Dakota.

#title
The Spectral Echoes of Fort Abraham Lincoln

#summary
Established in 1872 along the Missouri River, Fort Abraham Lincoln and its reconstructed Custer House are haunted by phantom footsteps, disembodied voices, and the spirit of Libbie Custer.

#details
Located near Mandan, North Dakota, Fort Abraham Lincoln served as a major military outpost in the 1870s and was the departure point for Lt. Colonel George Armstrong Custer's ill-fated 7th Cavalry before the Battle of the Little Bighorn. Today, the fort has been partially reconstructed—most notably the Victorian-style Custer House. Visitors and staff report an overwhelming sense of being watched, persistent cold spots, phantom footsteps echoing through empty halls, and the apparition of a woman in period dress believed to be Libbie Custer, who endlessly waits for her husband's return. Similar paranormal reports of disembodied voices, marching boots, and doors opening on their own emanate from the old barracks and storage buildings.

#location
Fort Abraham Lincoln State Park is situated on bluffs overlooking the Missouri River just south of Mandan, North Dakota.

#title
The Stack Monster of the Liberty Memorial Building

#summary
State Historical Society employees in the late 1960s and early 1970s frequently heard unexplained footsteps, coughing, and throat-clearing in the library stacks, leading them to dub the unseen presence the "Stack Monster."

#details
During the late 1960s and early 1970s, staff members working in the State Historical Society offices within the Liberty Memorial Building began noticing they were not alone after hours. Unexplained noises—including shuffling footsteps, coughing, and the sound of someone clearing their throat—echoed through the library stacks where no one else was present. While some jokingly blamed a large tomcat named Bruiser that occasionally snuck into the building, staff noted the cat arrived during the day, whereas the noises occurred at night. The friendly spirit became such a fixture of local lore that when the society eventually moved to the Heritage Center, both the Stack Monster and Bruiser were humorously granted security passes.

#location
The Liberty Memorial Building on the grounds of the State Capitol in Bismarck, North Dakota.
