#title
The Girl of Lorance Creek

#summary
Following oil drilling near a historic Saline County cemetery in 1920, workers reported hearing the screams and cries of a young girl who was murdered and buried near the creek during the Civil War.

#details
Lorance Creek in Saline County is associated with the legend of an unnamed girl who was murdered by being pushed into the creek and buried at Cockman Cemetery on December 24, 1863. In 1920, when oil workers began drilling near the creek and cemetery, they reported hearing a woman's scream and crying out "Why?", followed by sightings of her walking in a white burial dress toward the creek before vanishing.

#location
Lorance Creek is located in Saline County, Arkansas, near Cockman Cemetery.

#title
The Gurdon Light

#summary
An unexplained, glowing light has been intermittently sighted hovering along the railroad tracks outside Gurdon, Arkansas, since the 1930s. Local legend attributes the phenomenon to the lantern of a murdered railroad foreman's ghost searching for his head.

#details
Beginning in the 1930s, residents and visitors near the town of Gurdon, Arkansas, began reporting sightings of a mysterious, floating orb of light hovering above a desolate stretch of railroad tracks. Over the decades, various scientific explanations have been proposed to account for the phenomenon, ranging from car headlight reflections on nearby Interstate 30 to swamp gas and piezoelectric effects from subterranean quartz crystals. However, the most enduring local legend ties the eerie light to a tragic real-world event: the December 1931 murder of railway foreman William McClain, who was beaten to death near the tracks by an employee during a dispute over work hours. According to folklore, the haunting illumination is the ghostly lantern carried by McClain's spirit as he endlessly searches for his severed head along the rails where he died.

#location
The light appears along a wooded stretch of railroad tracks just outside the city of Gurdon in Clark County, Arkansas.

#title
The Haunting of the Allen House

#summary
Built in 1905, this historic Monticello residence is said to be haunted by the family member who took her own life there and by her young son, with tenants reporting phantom footsteps, moans, and invisible forces pushing back against closed doors.

#details
The Allen House in Monticello was built in 1905 by Joe Lee Allen and lived in by his family. After his death, his daughter Ladelle tragically committed suicide in the south bedroom by drinking potassium cyanide, and the room remained sealed off until 1986. Since the 1950s, residents and tenants have heard unexplained moans and footsteps, and experienced paranormal encounters such as closet doors pushing back when someone tries to shut them.

#location
The Allen House is located in Monticello, Drew County, Arkansas.

#title
The Hauntings of the 1886 Crescent Hotel

#summary
Built in Eureka Springs in 1886, the luxury resort-turned-hospital is widely reputed to be one of the most haunted locations in Arkansas, inhabited by the spirits of former patients, staff, and builders.

#details
Opened in May 1886 as an opulent resort in Eureka Springs, the stone-built Crescent Hotel has undergone multiple transformations, most notably serving as a cancer hospital run by con artist Norman Baker in the late 1930s. Following decades of shifting uses and eventual restoration, guests and staff have reported numerous paranormal encounters throughout the historic building. Among the frequently reported spirits are a nurse pushing a gurney down fourth-floor hallways, a stone mason named Michael who fell to his death during the hotel's initial construction, and former patients associated with Baker's fraudulent medical facility. Sightings and eerie experiences led to widespread national attention, cementing the hotel's reputation as a premier haunted landmark.

#location
The 1886 Crescent Hotel located at 75 Prospect St, Eureka Springs, Arkansas.

Alan Brown, *Haunted Places in the American South*.

#title
The Phantom Hitchhiker of Highway 365

#summary
A motorist picks up a soaked young woman hitchhiking on a lonely stretch of highway. Upon reaching her purported home, the driver learns she died years prior and finds the passenger seat empty.

#details
On stormy nights along Arkansas Highway 365 between Woodson and Redfield, drivers frequently report encountering a distressed young woman standing by the roadside seeking a ride. In the classic retelling, a driver named Josh picks up the wet, quiet hitchhiker and drives her to the address she provides in Redfield. When he knocks on the door and asks the homeowner if her daughter needs assistance, the shocked resident reveals that her daughter died in a car crash exactly four years prior. Rushing back to his vehicle, the driver finds the passenger seat completely empty. Other variations of the legend describe the hitchhiker vanishing mid-ride, or motorists finding her coat hanging upon her headstone.

#location
Arkansas Highway 365 between the communities of Woodson and Redfield, Arkansas.

#title
The Spectral Masons and Quack Doctors of the Crescent Hotel

#summary
Built in 1886 as a luxury mountain resort and later operated as a fraudulent cancer hospital, this historic Arkansas hotel is haunted by a fallen stonemason, a phantom nurse pushing a gurney, and the ghost of its former quack owner.

#details
The 1886 Crescent Hotel in Eureka Springs was constructed by Irish stonemasons, one of whom—a red-haired man named Michael—reportedly fell to his death near what is now Room 218 and continues to haunt the room with flickering lights and loud noises. Decades later, con man Norman Baker turned the hotel into a fraudulent cancer hospital during the 1930s, and guests and staff now report seeing his ghost in the basement, alongside a ghostly nurse pushing a gurney down the third-floor hallway at night and a patient named Theodora in Room 419.

#location
The 1886 Crescent Hotel & Spa is located atop West Mountain overlooking Eureka Springs in Carroll County, Arkansas.

#title
The Spirits of the Hornibrook Mansion

#summary
Constructed in Little Rock in 1888, the historic Queen Anne Victorian mansion—now operating as a bed and breakfast—is said to be haunted by its original builder, who died tragically shortly after its completion.

#details
The Hornibrook House, completed in 1888 as an elaborate Queen Anne Victorian residence in Little Rock, was built for businessman James H. Hornibrook. Tragically, just two years after the mansion's completion, Hornibrook suffered a fatal stroke at the front gate after hosting an evening gathering at his saloon. Over subsequent decades, as the grand home passed through various owners and functions—including a women's college, rooming house, and nursing home—occupants and visitors reported unexplained occurrences, including flickering lights, strange sounds, and the persistent presence of a well-dressed gentlemanly spirit believed to be Hornibrook himself.

#location
The Hornibrook House (The Empress of Little Rock) located at 2120 Louisiana Street in Little Rock, Arkansas.

#title
The Surgeon Who Never Left

#summary
Staff members at a former rural hospital building in Baxter County have reported spontaneous lights, phantom cigar smoke, and the lingering presence of a beloved longtime surgeon.

#details
Dr. John Guenthner operated a hospital in Gasville, Arkansas, starting in the 1930s and 1940s, serving as a pillar of the local medical community until his retirement. After the building later housed the Baxter County Heritage Museum, curators and staff reported supernatural occurrences, including lights turning on by themselves and the distinct scent and wisps of cigar smoke in the hallway, attributed to the spirit of Dr. Guenthner.

#location
The former hospital building is located in Gasville, Baxter County, Arkansas.
