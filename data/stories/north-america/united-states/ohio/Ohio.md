#title
The I-71 Hitchhiker

#summary
A motorist driving northbound on Interstate 71 late at night encountered a faceless hitchhiker who abruptly faded into thin air. The terrifying apparition left the driver shaken and convinced of a supernatural presence on the lonely stretch of highway.

#details
Rich Heileman, a Cleveland-area newspaper reporter, was driving home to Berea from Medina along Interstate 71 at approximately 1:00 a.m. Noticing a pale figure in a light tan raincoat walking along the unlit shoulder, he considered whether the person needed assistance. As he drew closer and the man turned to extend his thumb, Heileman saw that the figure lacked any facial features, appearing completely dark where a face should be. Before the car reached him, the hitchhiker turned transparent and vanished completely.

#location
The encounter took place along the northbound shoulder of Interstate 71, north of Medina, Ohio.

#title
The Legend of Gore Orphanage

#summary
According to local folklore, a massive fire once engulfed an orphanage on Gore Orphanage Road in Vermilion, Ohio, killing dozens of children whose ghostly screams can still be heard echoing through the ruins. In reality, the legend conflates a historic surveyor's gore, a real nineteenth-century mansion fire, and distant highway traffic sounds.

#details
For over a century, travelers and teenagers visiting Gore Orphanage Road in Vermilion, Ohio, have shared terrifying accounts of glowing lights, ghostly apparitions, and the wailing cries of children. The legend claims that in the late 1800s or early 1900s, a horrific fire broke out at an orphanage, trapping children behind blocked doors and burning them alive. Over time, local folklore fused this myth with the history of the Swift Mansion (Swift's Hollow), the actual Light of Hope Orphanage operated by Reverend Johann Sprunger (which closed in 1916), and the devastating 1908 Collinwood school fire. Although historians have verified that no orphanage fire ever occurred on Gore Road—and that the eerie "cries" heard near the ruins are actually the high-pitched hum of truck traffic carrying across the distant Ohio Turnpike bridges—the legend of Gore Orphanage remains one of Ohio's most enduring and popular local ghost stories.

#location
The ruins of the Swift Mansion and the infamous Gore Orphanage Road are located just south of Vermilion, Ohio, near Sperry Road and the Vermilion River in Lorain County.

#title
The Legends of Gore Orphanage Road

#summary
Woven from historical tragedies and accounts of religious isolation, Gore Orphanage Road in Vermilion is famous for legends of a devastating orphanage fire and the haunting cries of lost children.

#details
Local folklore tells of a horrific nineteenth-century fire that purportedly engulfed an orphanage on Gore Road, trapping and killing dozens of children. While historical records show that the site was actually home to the Light of Hope Orphanage established by Reverend Johann Sprunger in 1902—which closed amid allegations of severe neglect and financial trouble rather than a catastrophic fire—the legend merged with memories of a real school fire in nearby Collinwood. Today, visitors to the road and the nearby Swift Mansion ruins frequently report glowing lights, ghostly apparitions, and the phantom cries of children.

#location
Gore Orphanage Road is located approximately a quarter-mile north of Rosedale near the Sperry Road intersection in Vermilion, Ohio.

#title
The Melonheads of Wisner Road

#summary
Generations of locals and teenagers have reported encountering deformed, large-headed humanoids lurking in the wooded stretches along Wisner Road. According to legend, these creatures are the descendants of mutated children and their mad doctor creator who escaped into the forests after destroying his laboratory.

#details
For decades, local folklore in Northeast Ohio has spoken of the "Melonheads"—small humanoid creatures with oversized, bulbous heads, hairless skin, and glowing red eyes. Legend traces their origin back to a mysterious physician known as Dr. Crow, who either took in hydrocephalic children or subjected them to bizarre medical experiments in his woodland home near Kirtland and Chardon Township. According to the darker version of the tale, the abused children eventually revolted, killed the doctor, burned down his facility, and fled into the surrounding woods. Over generations, the survivors inbred and grew feral, fiercely hostile to outsiders. Countless drivers and teenagers traversing the winding, densely wooded stretches of Wisner Road and nearby Mitchell's Mill Road have reported terrifying encounters, ranging from strange shadows darting through the trees to small, deformed figures chasing cars or lurking near the old river bridge.

#location
Wisner Road winds through a densely wooded portion of Kirtland and Chardon Township in Northeast Ohio, running alongside the East Branch of the Chagrin River.

#title
The Phantom Brakeman of Moonville Tunnel

#summary
Built in 1856 to transport coal through southeastern Ohio, the abandoned Moonville Tunnel is famously haunted by the ghost of a railroad worker and a mysterious lantern-wielding light.

#details
Located in the dense woods of Vinton County, the Moonville Tunnel was part of the Marietta and Cincinnati Railroad, serving the mining community of Moonville and the nearby Hope Furnace. According to local legend and historical records, a brakeman was fatally injured on the tracks on March 31, 1859, after being crushed by an oncoming train. Over the following decades, several other tragic fatalities occurred along the rails near the tunnel. Visitors and locals have long reported seeing a hovering orb of light or a ghostly figure carrying a lantern walking through the dark brick archway where the railway once ran.

#location
Moonville Tunnel is located along the multi-use trail in Zaleski State Forest in Vinton County, Ohio.

#title
The Spectral Keepers of Moonville Tunnel

#summary
Deep within the forests of Vinton County, the historic Moonville Tunnel is said to be haunted by former miners, railroad workers, and mysterious figures like the Engineer and the Lavender Lady.

#details
Built in 1856 as part of the Marietta and Cincinnati Railroad, the Moonville Tunnel in Zaleski State Forest saw numerous fatal accidents over more than a century of train operations before closing in 1987. Local folklore and legends quickly grew around the dark, isolated brick tunnel, with visitors and locals reporting sightings of a ghostly engineer holding a swinging lantern, the lingering scent of lavender associated with a female specter, and menacing encounters with a spirit known as "The Bully."

#location
Moonville Tunnel is located along the former rail trail off Hope-Moonville Road in Vinton County, Ohio.

#title
The Spirited Guests of the Historic Buxton Inn

#summary
Established in 1812, Granville's Buxton Inn is said to be spiritually active, hosting the benevolent ghosts of former owners, a phantom cigar-smoking major, and a spectral cat.

#details
Operating continuously since the early nineteenth century as a tavern, stagecoach stop, and safe haven on the Underground Railroad, the Historic Buxton Inn in Granville has accumulated a rich legacy of paranormal accounts. Current inn management describes the building as "spiritually active" rather than haunted, noting that former owners and caretakers returned after death to watch over the property. Notable resident spirits include Ethel "Bonnie" Houston (known as the "Lady in Blue" and accompanied by the scent of gardenias), Major Buxton (signaled by the smell of cigar smoke), and a ghostly cat that reportedly jumps onto beds to cuddle guests staying in Room 9.

#location
The Historic Buxton Inn is located at 313 E. Broadway in Granville, Ohio.

#title
The Spirits of the Columbian House

#summary
Built in 1828 as a frontier stagecoach stop and inn in Waterville, Ohio, the historic Columbian House is said to be haunted by the souls of early pioneers and travelers who passed through its doors.

#details
Constructed by town founder John Pray along North River Road, the prominent yellow building has served as a hotel, jail, and restaurant over its nearly two-century history. Its storied past even attracted industrialist Henry Ford, who held a grand ball on the third floor and reportedly attempted to purchase the building. For more than a century, residents and visitors have shared ghost stories about unexplained phenomena, doors locking on their own, and the distinct feeling of unseen souls roaming the halls of the former stagecoach stop.

#location
The Columbian House is located at 4302 River Road in Waterville, Ohio.

#title
The Tragedy and Specters of Franklin Castle

#summary
Built by German immigrant banker Hannes Tiedemann in the late 19th century, Cleveland's ornate Franklin Castle is dogged by rumors of family curses, secret passages, and ghostly apparitions including a crying girl and a woman in black.

#details
Constructed between 1881 and 1883 for banker Hannes Tiedemann, the imposing Victorian-style mansion on Franklin Boulevard became associated with tragedy after multiple family members, including four of Tiedemann's children and his mother, died within a short span of years. Over subsequent decades, successive occupants and visitors reported paranormal activity ranging from flickering lights and mysterious slamming doors to the sounds of a sad, crying girl and sightings of a spectral woman dressed in black, alleged to be a former maid.

#location
Franklin Castle is located at 4308 Franklin Boulevard in Cleveland, Ohio.

#title
The Woman in Gray of Camp Chase Cemetery

#summary
Camp Chase Confederate Cemetery in Columbus, Ohio, where over 2,000 Confederate prisoners died during the Civil War, is haunted by a sorrowful apparition known as the Woman in Gray who mourns over specific graves.

#details
Serving as a prison camp during the Civil War, Camp Chase saw thousands of deaths due to disease and malnutrition. While thousands of soldiers rest within the cemetery grounds, its most famous specter is not a soldier, but a young woman dressed in a Civil War-era gray traveling suit. Witnesses have reported seeing her with her head bowed in grief, walking among the headstones, passing effortlessly through locked iron gates, and pausing to weep over the grave of the Unknown Soldier and the grave of Private Benjamin F. Allen. Fresh flowers have frequently been found left at these tombstones following her reported appearances, and reenactors have even recorded hearing mournful sobbing in the empty night air.

#location
Camp Chase Confederate Cemetery is located at 2923 Sullivant Avenue in Columbus, Ohio.
