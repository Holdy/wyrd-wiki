#title
The Curse of Dudleytown

#summary
Originating from a sixteenth-century English execution, a generational hex supposedly followed the Dudley family to an isolated and rocky Connecticut settlement. Over the following centuries, the village was abandoned as successive residents suffered ruin, madness, and mysterious deaths.

#details
According to lore, when Edmund Dudley was beheaded in England in 1510 for plotting against King Henry VIII, a curse was placed on his bloodline, dooming all his descendants to horror and death. Decades later, members of the Dudley family immigrated to America and established a small settlement in the heavily shadowed hills of Cornwall, Connecticut, known as Dudleytown. Although never an incorporated town, the rugged community grew around iron ore production and timber, but it was continually plagued by bizarre tragedies. Residents and descendants experienced uncharacteristic bouts of insanity, fatal lightning strikes, mysterious disappearances, and devastating fires. As hardships mounted and the local iron industry shifted elsewhere, the settlement was completely deserted by the early twentieth century, leaving behind only stone foundations, cellar holes, and enduring legends of a dark, lingering curse.

#location
Dudleytown is an abandoned historical settlement located within the Dark Entry Forest in the town of Cornwall, Litchfield County, Connecticut.

#title
The Exorcism of Anna Ecklund

#summary
An American woman named Emma Schmidt, known under the pseudonym Anna Ecklund, endured a decades-long demonic possession that culminated in an intensive series of exorcisms at an Iowa convent in 1928. During the harrowing rituals, she reportedly levitated, spoke in languages she had never learned, and displayed violent physical reactions to holy objects.

#details
Beginning in her youth, Emma Schmidt exhibited a severe physical and psychological aversion to religious items, holy water, and churches. After years of escalating disturbances and failed medical explanations, the Catholic Church formally determined she was possessed by multiple entities—identified as Beelzebub, Judas Iscariot, Lucifer, her abusive father, and her aunt. In 1928, Father Theophilus Riesinger undertook a prolonged exorcism at the Franciscan convent in Earling, Iowa, spanning three separate sessions across August, September, and December. Throughout the ordeal, Schmidt's body swelled and distorted, she vomited foreign debris, spoke in Latin and German, and exhibited terrifying strength and levitation. On December 23, 1928, Father Riesinger commanded the entities to depart for the final time, allowing Schmidt to return to a peaceful life of faith until her later years.

#location
The Franciscan convent located in the small town of Earling, Iowa.

#title
The Ghost Boy of Dead Man's Curve

#summary
Drivers who leave a coin on a specific bridge along Clinton Road at midnight may have it returned by the spirit of a young boy who drowned nearby. In more chilling accounts, the phantom attempts to push unsuspecting visitors into the water.

#details
According to local folklore popularized in New Jersey, a young boy tragically lost his life after falling or drowning in Clinton Brook near a treacherous stretch known as Dead Man's Curve on Clinton Road. Legend maintains that his spirit continues to haunt the bridge. If a traveler places a coin—such as a quarter—on the middle of the road at midnight, the coin will be mysteriously returned. However, encountering the spectral boy can be perilous, as some witnesses claim the ghost will attempt to push onlookers over the side of the bridge into the rushing waters below.

#location
The bridge over Clinton Brook at Dead Man's Curve on Clinton Road in West Milford, New Jersey.
