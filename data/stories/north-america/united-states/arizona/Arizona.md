#title
Julia Lowell of the Copper Queen Hotel

#summary
Guests staying in Room 315 of Bisbee's historic Copper Queen Hotel frequently report feeling the phantom presence and whispered pranks of a former resident known as Julia Lowell.

#details
Completed in 1902 by the Phelps Dodge Mining Company, the Copper Queen Hotel in Bisbee is Arizona's oldest continuously operating hotel. Local folklore maintains that several spirits roam its halls, the most famous being Julia Lowell, a woman who reportedly worked as a prostitute at the hotel and took her own life there. According to recurring accounts from guests—particularly men staying in Room 315—Julia makes her presence known late at night by whispering in guests' ears, playfully tugging on bedsheets, or brushing against their feet.

#location
The Copper Queen Hotel located at 11 Howell Avenue in Bisbee, Arizona.

#title
The Curse of the Lost Dutchman's Mine

#summary
Generations of prospectors and explorers seeking a legendary hidden gold mine in the rugged desert mountains have met with mysterious deaths and disappearances, cementing a belief in an ancient curse.

#details
According to legend, Spanish conquistadors first explored the sacred Apache mountain range in search of gold and were warned by the indigenous people that their Thunder God would punish trespassers. After numerous men vanished or were found decapitated, the Spaniards fled. Later, Mexican miners, the German prospector Jacob Waltz, and dozens of subsequent treasure hunters met with similar violent ends, mysterious vanishings, and fatal accidents while hunting for the fabulously wealthy lost mine near Weaver's Needle.

#location
Weaver's Needle is a prominent rock pinnacle located within the Superstition Mountains east of Apache Junction, Arizona.

#title
The Ghosts of the Historic Oatman Hotel

#summary
Built in 1902 in the mining town of Oatman, Arizona, the historic hotel is said to be haunted by a mischievous Irish miner named "Oatie" as well as the enduring spirits of famous guests Clark Gable and Carole Lombard.

#details
The Oatman Hotel, originally constructed in 1902 as the Durlin Hotel during a massive gold rush in the Black Mountains of Arizona, witnessed the boom and bust of a vibrant mining camp along Route 66. Over the decades, local lore grew around the historic building, centered on several resident spirits. The most famous legend claims that Hollywood icons Clark Gable and Carole Lombard spent their wedding night at the hotel in 1939 and continued to return for its peaceful solitude; today, staff and guests report hearing the couple whispering and laughing in empty rooms, and photographs of their former room have occasionally revealed unexplained apparitions. Another prominent spirit is "Oatie," believed to be an Irish miner who drank heavily after losing his family and died behind the hotel in 1930. According to local accounts, Oatie haunts his old room by playing bagpipes, opening windows, and yanking blankets off beds on scorching desert nights. Additional ghostly phenomena reported throughout the hotel include footsteps on freshly cleaned floors, self-flushing toilets, and items moving on their own in the saloon.

#location
The Oatman Hotel is located at 181 Main Street in the historic village of Oatman, Arizona.

#title
The Phantom Bellboy of the Hotel Monte Vista

#summary
Opened on New Year's Day in 1927 along Route 66, the historic Hotel Monte Vista in Flagstaff is famously haunted by a phantom bellboy who knocks on guest doors in the middle of the night.

#details
Built entirely through public municipal bonds and opening in 1927, the Hotel Monte Vista in Flagstaff, Arizona, hosted numerous notable figures over the decades, including John Wayne. Over the years, the hotel accumulated a reputation for heavy paranormal activity, attributed to bootlegging days, wild brawls, and tragic deaths. The most enduring ghost story centers on Room 210, known as the Zane Grey room, where guests are frequently awakened by a phantom bellboy knocking on the door to announce room service, only to reveal an empty hallway upon opening. Additionally, local lore notes that dogs placed in this room routinely exhibit extreme fear and agitation.

#location
Hotel Monte Vista, located at 100 N. San Francisco Street in Flagstaff, Arizona.

#title
The Phantom Bride of the Hassayampa Inn

#summary
Following her husband's mysterious disappearance on their wedding night in 1927, a heartbroken bride named Faith took her own life at the hotel, where her ghost has been seen wandering ever since.

#details
In 1927, a young woman named Faith and her much older husband checked into the historic Hassayampa Inn in Prescott, Arizona, to spend their wedding night. Shortly after arriving, the husband stepped out to buy cigarettes and never returned. Stricken with grief, Faith wandered the hotel lobby for three days before going back upstairs. According to local lore, she hanged herself either from the bell tower or a flagpole on the roof. Over the years, employees and guests have frequently reported encountering her apparition dressed in a long white gown as she roams the lobby and corridors.

#location
The Hassayampa Inn is located at 122 East Gurley Street in Prescott, Arizona.

#title
The Phantom Lady of the San Carlos Hotel

#summary
Decades after a guest tragically jumped from the roof of the newly opened Phoenix hotel, visitors continue to be awakened by the silent apparition of an elegant woman in an evening gown. Standing at the foot of their beds, she calmly stares at them before floating effortlessly through closed doors.

#details
Constructed in 1928 on the site of Phoenix's first school, the San Carlos Hotel became the site of tragedy just five weeks after opening when a distressed guest named Leone Jensen jumped to her death from the roof. Ever since, guests staying in rooms across the hotel have reported being awakened by a strange feeling in the middle of the night to see a well-dressed woman wearing a flowing evening gown, hat, gloves, and scarf standing at the foot of their bed. She does not cause fear, but rather a calming sensation, staring silently for several seconds before floating out of the room without opening or closing the door.

#location
The San Carlos Hotel is situated at 202 North Central Avenue in downtown Phoenix, Arizona.

#title
The Phantom Maintenance Worker of the Jerome Grand Hotel

#summary
Once operating as the United Verde Hospital, the Jerome Grand Hotel is haunted by the restless spirit of a maintenance worker who died in the elevator shaft.

#details
Perched high upon Cleopatra Hill in Jerome, Arizona, the Jerome Grand Hotel originally opened in 1927 as the United Verde Hospital before closing in 1950 and reopening as a hotel in 1996. Among its many reported ghosts—including a bearded miner and a small child—the most famous is Claude "Scotty" Harvey, a hospital maintenance worker whose body was found pinned under the elevator in 1935 under mysterious circumstances. Though officially ruled an accident, many suspected foul play. Ever since his death, unexplained phenomena have plagued the elevator and basement areas, including self-moving elevator cars, phantom lights in the shaft, and an angry shadowy presence.

#location
Jerome Grand Hotel, located at 200 Hill Street in Jerome, Arizona.

#title
The Resident Handyman of Hotel Congress

#summary
A long-term resident handyman who lived at Tucson's Hotel Congress for over three decades reportedly continues to play practical jokes from beyond the grave.

#details
Operating in downtown Tucson since 1919, Hotel Congress is renowned both for its 1934 connection to outlaw John Dillinger and its enduring resident ghosts. Among the most famous is Vince Szuda, a maintenance worker who lived in Room 220 for 36 years and was known in life for collecting and repurposing butter knives as screwdrivers. Following his death in 2001, hotel staff and guests have repeatedly reported finding butter knives mysteriously laid out in plain sight in his former room, along with other playful paranormal disturbances throughout the historic building.

The Specter of Claude Harvey at the Jerome Grand Hotel

#summary
A former hospital maintenance worker whose mysterious death was ruled an accident is said to still haunt the elevator shaft and basement of the historic Jerome Grand Hotel.

#details
Built in 1927 as the United Verde Hospital on Cleopatra Hill, the five-story structure served the bustling mining community of Jerome until closing in 1950. In 1935, maintenance worker Claude "Scotty" Harvey was found crushed to death beneath the hospital's elevator. Though officially ruled an accident, many locals suspected foul play because the elevator was found in perfect working order. Ever since his death—and continuing after the building was restored and reopened as the Jerome Grand Hotel in 1996—staff and guests have reported eerie sounds, phantom elevator activity, and sightings of a shadowy, angry figure in the basement and stairwells.

#title
The Vanishing Passenger of Route 66

#summary
A truck driver on an isolated Arizona highway picks up a young woman in a white dress who silently disappears from the locked cab of his truck mid-journey.

#details
Late one winter night, a long-haul truck driver traveling along the Arizona stretch of Route 66 between Seligman and Kingman noticed a young woman standing alone by the roadside wearing a thin white dress despite the freezing cold. Concerned for her safety miles from any town, he pulled over and offered her a ride. The woman silently climbed into the cab and maintained a distant, quiet demeanor as the truck continued down the highway, offering only brief, monosyllabic answers when spoken to. A few miles later, when the driver glanced back over to the passenger seat, she had completely vanished without a sound or any movement of the cab door. When he later described the mysterious encounter to locals in a nearby town, they noted that she strongly resembled a young woman who had tragically died in a car accident on that exact stretch of highway years prior.

#location
The Arizona stretch of U.S. Route 66 between the towns of Seligman and Kingman.
