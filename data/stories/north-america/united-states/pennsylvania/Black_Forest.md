#title
Mark McCoy's skull

#summary
The highwayman Mark McCoy hanged himself from a pine in Pennsylvania's Black Forest, and when a dog brought his skull into a hunter's shack and it was hung over the door, screams echoed across the mountain. The haunting only stopped when Horatio Nelson and his wife reburied the skull in McCoy's grave under a cover of stones.

#details
Mark McCoy was a highwayman on the Jersey Shore-Coudersport Turnpike, now Route 44, whom Henry Shoemaker called a regional Robin Hood who only robbed from the rich. When he shot and killed a young Smethport attorney carrying land-tax money, the first time he had fired on a victim, he was badly shaken. Ava Burkheiser, whose family had sheltered him, then turned down his proposal, and McCoy hanged himself from a pine near the hunter's shack where he had made his first robbery. He was buried at the foot of the tree. That autumn, the shack's owner, Levi Trexler, arrived for deer season, and a dog brought McCoy's skull inside. Trexler hung it over the door, screams echoed across the mountain, and the skull fell and knocked him out cold. Each time it was reburied, dogs dug it up and the screaming returned, until Horatio Nelson and his wife buried it in McCoy's grave and covered the grave with stones. The site of the grave has since been lost.
