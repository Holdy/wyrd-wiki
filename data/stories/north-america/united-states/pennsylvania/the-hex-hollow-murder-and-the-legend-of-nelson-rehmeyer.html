The Hex Hollow Murder and the Legend of Nelson Rehmeyer

Summary

In 1928, the brutal murder of a Pennsylvania "powwow" healer in his remote York County farmhouse sparked enduring local legends of dark magic, curses, and an unburnable house.

Details

In late November 1928, a superstitious Pennsylvania man named John Blymire, convinced that he had been cursed with crippling health problems and bad luck by a rival faith healer, sought guidance from a local witch named Emma Knopp. Knopp confirmed that Blymire was indeed hexed and identified local farmer and "powwower" Nelson Rehmeyer as the sorcerer responsible. To break the curse, Knopp instructed Blymire to steal Rehmeyer’s copy of the traditional folk-magic spellbook, Long Lost Friend, take a lock of his hair, and bury them both deep in the earth.

On the night of November 27, 1928, Blymire, accompanied by two teenagers named John Curry and Wilbert Hess, broke into Rehmeyer’s weathered, two-story clapboard farmhouse in rural Winterstown, Pennsylvania. When the 60-year-old Rehmeyer refused to surrender his spellbook, a violent confrontation ensued. Blymire choked Rehmeyer while Curry struck him in the head with a block of wood, killing him. Seeking to destroy evidence and lift the curse permanently, the men poured kerosene over Rehmeyer's body and set it on fire before fleeing.

However, despite the heavy application of fuel, the house and Rehmeyer's body miraculously failed to burn, only smoldering slightly—a phenomenon locals immediately attributed to Rehmeyer's powerful lingering magical protection. The three men were quickly captured, tried, and sentenced to prison, but the farmhouse survived. Today, Rehmeyer's Hollow remains a focal point of Pennsylvania folklore, with locals whispering that the spirit of the murdered healer still haunts his isolated home, where a clock above the stove remains frozen at the exact minute of his death.
