#title
The Black Angel of Oakland Cemetery

#summary
An imposing bronze statue in Iowa City turned mysteriously black after its installation, giving rise to persistent folklore that anyone who touches, kisses, or vandalizes it is doomed by a fatal curse. Over the years, numerous local legends claimed that crossing the monument brings about sudden death or supernatural retribution.

#details
Commissioned in 1913 by Teresa Feldevert to honor her late son and husband, a towering bronze angel statue shocked its creator and townspeople by turning a deep, eerie black shortly after arriving at Oakland Cemetery. As oxidation transformed its appearance, dark rumors took root that the monument carried a malignant curse. Over the decades, local lore warned that kissing the statue would stop a person's heart, touching it on Halloween would bring death within seven years, and any attempt to vandalize or deface the figure would result in violent, mysterious retribution.

#location
The statue stands over a gravesite on the north side of Oakland Cemetery in Iowa City, Iowa.

#title
The Black Angel of Oakland Cemetery

#summary
An 8.5-foot-tall bronze angel statue erected in an Iowa City graveyard in the early 20th century became the center of dark local folklore due to its eerie, oxidized black discoloration and tragic origins. According to local superstition, touching or kissing the monument brings swift death or terrible curses.

#details
In 1912, Teresa Dolezal Feldevert commissioned a monumental bronze angel from artist Mario Korbel to stand watch over the graves of her son, Edward, and her late husband, Nicholas, in Oakland Cemetery in Iowa City, Iowa. Originally cast in bronze, the statue gradually oxidized over the decades into a dark, greenish-black hue. As the monument darkened, generations of local students and thrill-seekers spun sinister legends to explain its morbid appearance, claiming that Teresa had been a witch or that the statue turned black due to hidden sins. Numerous taboos and curses arose around the monument, with local folklore warning that anyone who kisses or touches the statue will be struck dead, that virgins kissed before it will break its curse, and that young women who approach it in the moonlight will die within months.

#location
Oakland Cemetery is located at 1000 Brown Street in Iowa City, Iowa, where the Black Angel monument stands atop the Feldevert family gravesite.

#summary
Erected in 1912 in Iowa City's Oakland Cemetery, a towering bronze memorial statue known as the Black Angel has inspired decades of local folklore and curses concerning anyone who dares touch or kiss it.

#details

Over time, exposure to the elements caused the bronze to oxidize into a dark, eerie, greenish-black patina. This striking visual transformation fueled local legends and ghost stories throughout Iowa City. Other variations of the legend claim that the statue's color darkens each year to reflect those it has claimed, or that anyone foolish enough to touch the angel at midnight on Halloween will die within seven years.

#title
The Legend of the Marsh Rainbow Arch Bridge

#summary
A historic arched bridge south of Lake City is said to be haunted by an invisible entity with an insatiable appetite for chocolate.

#details
Spanning the North Raccoon River, the Marsh Rainbow Arch Bridge dates back to 1914 and features a distinctive arched profile. According to local folklore, the bridge is visited by a spirit that loves chocolate; local legend holds that if an unopened bar of chocolate is left in the middle of the bridge at midnight, the chocolate will vanish within five minutes while leaving the outer wrapper completely intact.

#location
The Marsh Rainbow Arch Bridge spans the North Raccoon River just south of Lake City, Iowa.

#title
The Phantom of Stony Hollow Road

#summary
A heartbroken young woman who jumped to her death from a roadside cliff in the 19th century continues to haunt a secluded rural road in Burlington, Iowa. Travelers who call her name after dark may see her spectral figure or receive a fatal warning.

#details
In the 1800s, a young woman named Lucinda arranged to meet her lover on Stony Hollow Road so they could run away together. When her lover was delayed because his wagon became stuck in the mud, Lucinda mistakenly believed he had abandoned her. Overwhelmed by despair, she climbed to the top of the towering cliff alongside the road and leaped to her death, tragically arriving moments before her lover showed up. Today, motorists and ghost hunters visit the road at night. According to local legend, shouting Lucinda's name three times causes her faint apparition to appear near the cliffside, and if she drops a rose at a visitor's feet, it portends their tragic death within days.

#location
Stony Hollow Road is a rural road located approximately eight miles north of Burlington, Iowa, featuring a steep cliff bordering the roadway.

#title
The Spirits of the Squirrel Cage Jail

#summary
A historic rotary jail in Council Bluffs is said to be haunted by the spirits of former jailers, prisoners, and a mournful little girl.

#details
Built in 1885 and operating until 1969, the unique three-story revolving cell structure in Council Bluffs has long been reported to host strange paranormal phenomena. Staff and visitors have reported disembodied footsteps, doors opening on their own, cold spots, and the apparition of a mournful little girl locked inside empty cells. Some believe the hauntings stem from the building's site on an old church morgue, or from former caretakers and inmates who left behind a lingering presence.

#location
The Historic Squirrel Cage Jail is located at 226 Pearl Street in Council Bluffs, Iowa.
