The Black Angel of Oakland Cemetery

Summary

Erected in 1912 in Iowa City's Oakland Cemetery, a towering bronze memorial statue known as the Black Angel has inspired decades of local folklore and curses concerning anyone who dares touch or kiss it.

Details

The nine-foot-tall bronze statue was commissioned by Theresa Feldevert to stand guard over her family's burial plot. Over time, exposure to the elements caused the bronze to oxidize into a dark, eerie, greenish-black patina. This striking visual transformation fueled local legends and ghost stories throughout Iowa City. Popular folklore warns that touching or kissing the angel will result in immediate death unless the person is a virgin. Other variations of the legend claim that the statue's color darkens each year to reflect those it has claimed, or that anyone foolish enough to touch the angel at midnight on Halloween will die within seven years.
