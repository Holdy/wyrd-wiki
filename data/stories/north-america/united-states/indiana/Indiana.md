#title
The Blue Lady of the Story Inn

#summary
Guests staying in the historic upstairs room of the Story Inn frequently report encounters with a spectral woman in white known as the Blue Lady, whose appearances are accompanied by the scent of cherry tobacco.

#details
Founded in 1851 by Dr. George Story, the remote village of Story, Indiana, dwindled over the decades into near-obscurity. After the village's general store and surrounding buildings were converted into a bed and breakfast in the late 20th century, visitors occupying the upstairs room began reporting paranormal phenomena. According to local legend, when a specific blue bedside light is illuminated, a ghostly woman appears in a flowing white gown with icy blue eyes and blue ribbons, while the air fills with the aroma of cherry tobacco—allegedly the spirit of Jane Story, wife of the village founder, who had a strong preference for cherry tobacco. Numerous guests and investigators have recorded unexplained manifestations, including moving objects and self-opening doors.

#location
The Story Inn is located at 6404 State Road 135 S, in the unincorporated village of Story, Indiana, within Brown County.

#title
The Blue Lady of the Story Inn

#summary
Guests staying at Indiana's historic Story Inn frequently report sightings of a spectral woman dressed in blue who haunts the second-story room above the old general store.

#details
Located in the tiny timber village of Story, the Story Inn is the state's oldest country inn. The second-story room situated above the historic general store is named the Blue Lady Room after a lingering local legend. Guests and inn staff have documented encounters with an apparition known as the Blue Lady, who is said to materialize in the room, leaving behind traces of her presence and appearing in guest logs dating back decades.

#location
The Story Inn is located in the historic village of Story in Brown County, Indiana.

#title
The House of Blue Lights

#summary
Local legend long claimed that an eccentric Indianapolis millionaire kept his deceased wife's body in a glass coffin surrounded by blue Christmas lights in his secluded estate. In truth, the wealthy inventor Skiles Test simply loved the color blue and used blue bug-zapping and holiday lights, though trespassers and urban explorers propagated the macabre folklore for decades.

#details
Skiles Edward Test, a wealthy inventor and businessman, lived on a large, heavily wooded farmstead off Fall Creek Road on the northeast side of Indianapolis. Fuelled by his technical ingenuity and vast inheritance from his father (president of National Motor Vehicle Company), Test built private rail systems, his own power plant, and an elaborate swimming pool on the estate. Because Test was fond of the color blue—using blue holiday lights and blue bug-zapping lights around his property—local teenagers and outsiders spun wild rumors between the world wars. The core legend insisted that his wife had died in a tragic accident and that he kept her corpse preserved in a glass casket illuminated by blue lights. Despite no such tragedy occurring, the property became plagued by trespassers and vandals throughout the 1950s and 1960s, leading Test to erect tall fences and eventually stay elsewhere at night. Test passed away in 1964, and though the estate was later razed and turned into Skiles Test Nature Park, local folklore persists that blue lights can still be seen flickering through the woods at night.

#location
Skiles Test Nature Park is located on Fall Creek Road on the northeast side of Indianapolis, Indiana, just west of Shadeland Avenue and Interstate 465.

#upstream-source
The 1975 book _The House of Blue Lights_ by Kay J. Miclot.

#title
The Legend of Stepp Cemetery

#summary
Situated deep within the Morgan-Monroe State Forest, Stepp Cemetery is said to be haunted by a ghostly woman in black who mourns over an infant's grave, surrounded by decades of chilling Hoosier folklore.

#details
Dating back to at least the mid-nineteenth century, Stepp Cemetery in Indiana's Morgan-Monroe State Forest gained a dark reputation beginning around the turn of the 20th century, when a fringe religious sect known as the Crabbites reportedly held unusual gatherings on the grounds. As the cemetery became increasingly remote following its incorporation into the state forest in 1929 and popular among local teenagers in the mid-20th century, the folklore rapidly expanded. Local legends describe a ghostly woman in black who wanders the grounds and visits the grave of an infant, alongside tales of a mysterious tree stump known as the "Warlock's chair" associated with local curses. 

#location
Stepp Cemetery is located in a remote wooded section of the Morgan-Monroe State Forest in Indiana.

#title
The Legend of the Avon Haunted Bridge

#summary
Motorists driving under a historic 1906 railroad span in Avon, Indiana, are traditionally expected to honk their horns to muffle the phantom screams of a grieving mother or a construction worker buried in wet cement. The soaring masonry arch bridge has collected numerous tragic folklore tales since its early-twentieth-century construction.

#details
Built in 1906 for the Big Four Railroad (and still utilized by CSX today), the massive double-tracked masonry arch bridge on County Road 625 East in Avon, Hendricks County, has inspired persistent ghost stories for over a century. Several distinct legends attempt to explain the haunting presence: one tale tells of a drunken railroad worker who slipped during construction and was accidentally buried alive in the wet cement, whose moans can allegedly be heard whenever a train crosses overhead. Another widely repeated local legend claims that a young mother walking the railroad tracks with her sick baby slipped and fell from the bridge to their deaths, with her wailing cries for her infant echoing at night. A third account attributes the noises to four workers who fell into White Lick Creek below. To this day, local drivers customarily honk their horns when passing beneath the bridge's stone arches to quiet the restless spirits.

#location
The Avon Haunted Bridge is situated over County Road 625 East, approximately half a mile south of U.S. Route 36 in Avon, Indiana, adjacent to Washington Township Park.

The Phantoms and Traps of Primrose Road

#summary
Generations of motorists visiting a remote rural road northwest of South Bend have shared local legends of vanishing farmhouses, ghostly warnings, and unexplained vehicle malfunctions.

#details
Northwest of South Bend, Primrose Road has long been the subject of local ghost lore. Generations of curious locals and teenagers have driven down the remote stretch at night, passing near the historic Portage Prairie Dunkard Cemetery. Popular legend holds that driving along the unlit road at specific speeds causes cars to mysteriously stall or tires to go flat, while phantom farmhouses and apparitions emerge from the adjacent cornfields to warn travelers away.

#title
The Tragic Haunting of Hannah House

#summary
Built in 1858 by Alexander Hannah, this historic Indianapolis home is legendarily haunted by the spirits of fleeing slaves who perished in a cellar fire while hidden on the Underground Railroad.

#details
Alexander Hannah served as a prominent civic leader and purportedly operated a sanctuary for escaping slaves on the Underground Railroad. According to local folklore, a group of slaves sheltering in the cellar accidentally knocked over an oil lamp, causing a fatal fire. Unable to escape, several occupants died from smoke inhalation and burns, and were hastily buried in the cellar dirt. Decades later, visitors and paranormal investigators reported intense paranormal phenomena, including disembodied whispers, foul smells, scratching noises in the cellar, and the ghostly presence of an infant in the upper bedrooms.

#location
The Hannah House is located at 3801 Madison Avenue in Indianapolis, Indiana.

#title
The Woman in Black of Stepp Cemetery

#summary
Stepp Cemetery, a remote graveyard hidden within the Morgan-Monroe State Forest, is said to be haunted by a grieving woman in black who guards a child's grave and a cursed tree stump.

#details
Dating back to the mid-19th century—with graves including War of 1812 veteran Isaac Heartstock who died in 1851—Stepp Cemetery became even more isolated when it was enveloped by the Morgan-Monroe State Forest in 1929. Around the turn of the 20th century, a fringe religious sect known as the Crabbites reportedly used the area for rituals, contributing to the site's eerie reputation. As generations of teenagers began visiting the remote clearing in the mid-20th century, local legends flourished. The most prominent tale surrounds a ghostly woman dressed in black who wanders the grounds, often seen in the back corner of the cemetery tenderly holding her infant child from a historic grave adorned with toys. Another famous legend involved a nearby tree stump dubbed the "Warlock's chair," which was said to carry dark curses for anyone who sat upon it.

#location
Stepp Cemetery, located within the Morgan-Monroe State Forest near Martinsville, Indiana.

#upstream-source
Indiana Folklore, Volume 5.
