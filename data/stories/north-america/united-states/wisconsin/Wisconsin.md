#title
Boy Scout Lane

#summary
Boy Scout Lane is an isolated dead-end road near Stevens Point, Wisconsin, that has inspired decades of urban legends about a doomed troop of Boy Scouts.

#details
Stretching about 2,500 feet between Cemetery Road and Little Chicago Road in the Town of Linwood near Stevens Point, Boy Scout Lane is famous for chilling folklore. Countless versions of the legend claim that a troop of Boy Scouts met a tragic end there in the 1950s or 1960s—whether murdered by their Scoutmaster, killed in a forest fire, trapped in a bus accident, or simply vanishing without a trace. Despite historical records showing no such tragedy ever occurred, generations of visitors have reported eerie phenomena along the road, including glowing lantern lights moving through the woods after dark, ghostly apparitions of scouts, mysterious child-sized handprints appearing on vehicles, and an overwhelming sense of dread.

#location
Boy Scout Lane is located in the Town of Linwood near Stevens Point, Wisconsin, stretching between Cemetery Road and Little Chicago Road.

#title
The Accursed Gravestone of Martha Jane Coplan

#summary
An 1861 gravestone bearing a stern warning against desecration inspired local folklore claiming that a buried woman was a witch whose curse brings physical harm to anyone who disturbs her tomb. Visitors report that those who vandalize or disrespect the gravesite suffer immediate misfortune, such as broken limbs.

#details
In Riverview Public Cemetery in Kewaunee, Wisconsin, the flat headstone of Martha Jane Coplan, who died in 1861 at age 38, features the chilling inscription: *"Accursed be the man who molests this grave or stone."* Over time, local legend distorted this historical grave-robbery deterrent into a supernatural warning, claiming Martha was a witch buried standing up so she could not escape. Folklore maintains that anyone who disrespects or disturbs her grave will face terrible luck and accidents, with local tales recounting instances of teenagers who vandalized the marker and suffered broken arms later that night. While historians note the inscription was a standard 19th-century defense against body-snatchers seeking medical cadavers, the eerie legend endures.

#location
The key location is Martha Jane Coplan's gravestone situated in the corner of Riverview Public Cemetery in Kewaunee, Wisconsin.

#title
The Apparition of the Mabel Tainter Theater

#summary
Erected in 1889 as a memorial to a prominent lumber baron's daughter, this historic Menomonie theater is famously haunted by the ghost of a woman in white and mysterious late-night activity.

#details
The Mabel Tainter Memorial Theater was built in 1889 on Main Street in Menomonie, Wisconsin, as a tribute to Captain Andrew Tainter’s 19-year-old daughter, Mabel, who died following a severe illness (or, according to local folklore, a tragic pregnancy complication). Over the years, actors, staff, and passersby have reported eerie occurrences inside the Victorian-era building, including lights turning on by themselves, disembodied footsteps in empty halls, and sightings of a spectral woman in a white dress wandering the auditorium. Local police have even received calls from pedestrians seeing figures inside the locked building long after hours.

#location
The Mabel Tainter Theater situated on Main Street in Menomonie, Wisconsin.

#title
The Apparitions of Buffalo Ridge Road

#summary
Drivers traveling along the isolated northern dirt stretch of Buffalo Ridge Road at night have reported encountering a tragic female apparition and mysterious running silhouettes. Local folklore attributes these hauntings to a dark family tragedy that occurred at the site.

#details
According to local legend, the northern unpaved end of Buffalo Ridge Road is haunted following a tragic sequence of events where a man took his life, and his grief-stricken wife subsequently killed their three children before ending her own life. Motorists navigating the dark, bumpy road after dark frequently experience unsettling paranormal phenomena. Witnesses have reported seeing the apparition of a woman with dark hair wearing a dress, as well as bizarre silhouettes of people running on all fours. Others have noted eerie white mists drifting through the trees, ghostly footsteps, and low growling sounds coming from the surrounding woods.

#location
The hauntings take place along the northern dirt-road section of Buffalo Ridge Road in Wisconsin.

#title
The Haunted Bunk Beds of Horicon

#summary
The purchase of a second-hand set of bunk beds plunged a Wisconsin family into a terrifying ordeal involving demonic apparitions, poltergeist activity, and unexplainable fires.

#details
In February 1987, Allen and Debbie Tallman purchased second-hand bunk beds for their young daughters at a local shop in Horicon, Wisconsin, storing them temporarily in the basement where family members began experiencing mysterious illnesses. Once moved into the children's bedroom, strange paranormal occurrences escalated rapidly: a radio clock dial moved on its own, children reported seeing a witch-like figure with glowing red eyes amidst flames, and Allen experienced dramatic personality changes accompanied by disembodied voices and flying furniture. Even after a reverend blessed the home and the bunk beds were banished back to the basement, the terrifying manifestations continued until the family was forced to flee the house in the middle of the night. The bed was later featured on national television before being permanently destroyed.

#location
A residential house located in Horicon, Wisconsin.

#title
The Haunting of Summerwind Mansion

#summary
Built as a secluded fishing lodge on the shores of West Bay Lake, Summerwind became the subject of intense supernatural legends after successive owners and residents reported violent apparitions, moving walls, and haunting disturbances. The abandoned estate eventually burned down following a lightning strike in 1988.

#details
Originally constructed in the early 20th century, the grand home—later known as Summerwind or the Lamont Mansion—became infamous for eerie occurrences spanning decades. When Robert Patterson Lamont and his family lived there in the early 1900s, Lamont reportedly fired his pistol at a ghostly figure appearing in the kitchen before abandoning the property in terror. Years later, in the 1970s, subsequent resident Arnold Hinshaw and his family experienced continuous paranormal disturbances, including disembodied voices, shifting room dimensions, cold gusts of wind, and an alleged demonic presence that drove Arnold to a nervous breakdown. Though locals and historians noted that many of the most dramatic haunting claims were popularized by later books and media rather than initial residents, the mansion cemented its reputation as Wisconsin's most haunted house before being struck by lightning and destroyed by fire in June 1988.

#location
The ruins of Summerwind Mansion are located on the shores of West Bay Lake in the Town of Land O' Lakes, Vilas County, Wisconsin.

#title
The Phantom Boy Scouts of Boy Scout Lane

#summary
An isolated dead-end road in rural Wisconsin is famously haunted by the spirits of Boy Scouts who reportedly met a tragic end in the mid-twentieth century.

#details
Located in Linwood Township near Stevens Point, Boy Scout Lane is a remote, unpaved road where a planned scout camp was never built. Over the decades, local folklore and urban legends grew around the area, claiming that an entire troop of Boy Scouts died there under mysterious circumstances—ranging from a murderous scoutmaster or bus driver to a tragic forest fire started by a dropped lantern. Visitors and paranormal investigators over the years have reported eerie phenomena along the lane, including the sounds of breaking branches and unseen footsteps in the woods, glowing red or white lights resembling swinging lanterns, and ghostly child-sized handprints appearing on vehicles.

#location
Boy Scout Lane is an isolated 2,500-foot unpaved road located in Linwood Township near Stevens Point, Portage County, Wisconsin.

#title
The Restless Founder of the Pfister Hotel

#summary
A historic luxury hotel in downtown Milwaukee is widely reported to be haunted by its founder, regularly startling visiting professional baseball players with unexplained electrical and physical disturbances.

#details
Opened in 1893 by Charles Pfister as the "Grand Hotel of the West," the Pfister Hotel at 424 East Wisconsin Avenue in Milwaukee is renowned not only for its Victorian art collection but also for its active paranormal reputation. Over the years, numerous visiting Major League Baseball players and hotel guests have reported unsettling nocturnal encounters, including heavy footsteps stomping across locked rooms, doorknobs turning, air conditioning units and televisions powering on and off by themselves, and disembodied voices or knocking. Staff and guests frequently attribute these spectral pranks and electrical anomalies to the lingering spirit of founder Charles Pfister, who reportedly still roams the historic corridors.

#location
The Pfister Hotel is located at 424 East Wisconsin Avenue in downtown Milwaukee, Wisconsin.

#title
The Secrets of the Octagon House

#summary
Built in 1856 by Fond du Lac's first mayor, the historic Octagon House features hidden passages, a secret basement tunnel, and enduring legends of the paranormal tied to a Civil War tragedy.

#details
Constructed in 1856 by Isaac Brown, the eight-sided home was given to his son Edwin and his wife Ruth as a wedding gift. When the Civil War broke out, Edwin enlisted in the Union Army and was killed at the Battle of Antietam in 1862. Over the decades, the house gained a reputation for its unusual architectural quirks—including secret rooms, a false fireplace leading to a basement tunnel, and a crawl space with a cryptic 1888 message. Longtime owner Marlene Hansen and countless visitors reported unexplained phenomena, such as the sounds of children playing in empty rooms, cold spots, and sightings of apparitions believed to be grieving family members who never fully came to terms with Edwin's death.

#location
The historic Octagon House located in the historic district of Fond du Lac, Wisconsin.
