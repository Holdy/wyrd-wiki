The Tragedy and Haunting of Houghton Mansion

Summary

Following a horrific automobile accident and subsequent suicides and deaths in 1914, the grand Houghton Mansion in North Adams became plagued by paranormal phenomena, including shadow figures, disembodied voices, and lingering sorrow.

Details

In August 1914, wealthy industrialist and first mayor of North Adams, Albert Charles (A.C.) Houghton, set out on a drive in his Pierce-Arrow touring car with his daughter Mary, friends, and their long-time chauffeur, John Widders. While navigating a hill under repair, the car plunged down an embankment, instantly killing their friend Sybil Hutton and fatally injuring Mary Houghton. Stricken with intense grief and guilt, the chauffeur John Widders shot himself in the mansion's stable just hours later. A.C. Houghton himself passed away nine days after the accident, deeply broken by the tragedy. Since these events, visitors and paranormal investigators have reported frequent haunting phenomena throughout the 17-room neoclassical estate, including shadow figures, weeping apparitions of Mary and A.C., ghostly footsteps on the stairs, and disembodied voices demanding intruders leave.
