#title
The Phantoms of the Hoosac Tunnel

#summary
Carved through the Berkshire Mountains under deadly conditions that claimed nearly two hundred lives, the Hoosac Tunnel became notorious for ghostly moans, blue lights, and headless apparitions.

#details
Construction on the 4.75-mile Hoosac Tunnel began in 1851 and quickly became known as the "Bloody Pit" due to dangerous explosives, cave-ins, and toxic gases that killed approximately 190 workers. In 1865, a nitroglycerin explosion under suspicious circumstances killed two workers, after which a third explosives expert, Ringo Kelley, was found strangled to death deep within the shaft. Following these tragedies, laborers refused to work after dark, reporting chilling cries of agony and phantom sounds echoing through the dark tunnel. In 1868 and later in 1872, investigators and engineers—including a doctor who diagnosed the tunnel with "poltergeist-itis"—reported witnessing a glowing, blue, headless apparition floating along the tunnel floor before vanishing into thin air. Even today, as an active railroad tunnel, travelers and workers continue to report strange presences and muffled voices in the dark.

#location
The Hoosac Tunnel cuts through the Hoosac Mountain Range in Berkshire County, Massachusetts, with its East Portal located in Florida, Massachusetts, and its West Portal in North Adams, Massachusetts.
