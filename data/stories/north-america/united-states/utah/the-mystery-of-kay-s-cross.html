The Mystery of Kay's Cross

Summary

For decades, a towering stone and cement cross hidden in a Kaysville hollow became the focus of dark urban legends about a murderous polygamist and cult rituals. In reality, the mysterious monument was built in the 1940s at the urging of a wandering religious zealot who claimed to be the reincarnation of Jesus Christ.

Details

Standing twenty feet tall in a secluded wooded ravine on the northern outskirts of Kaysville, Utah, a crudely constructed stone and cement cross marked with a large letter "K" became a fixture of local folklore. Generations of teenagers and children snuck onto private property—owned by the secretive Kingston family—to seek out the monument, giving rise to wild stories. According to local lore, the cross was built by a mad polygamist who murdered his seven wives, buried them around its base, and entombed a heart inside the structure before hanging himself. Other rumors whispered of glowing stones during full moons, witches, werewolves, and animal sacrifices.

For decades, the true origins of the cross remained shrouded in secrecy. However, historical journals and records later revealed that the monument was actually constructed in 1946 by members of the Kingston clan at the behest of Francis Pencovic, a charismatic self-proclaimed messiah who went by the name Krishna Venta. Pencovic had convinced the group to build the monument as a tribute before moving on to California, where he later founded a movement known as WKFL. In February 1992, an 80-pound dynamite blast destroyed the cross, though the ruins and the dark legends surrounding the hollow persist to this day.
