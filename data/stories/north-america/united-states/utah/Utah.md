#title
Kay's Cross

#summary
Looming in a remote hollow in Davis County, an imposing twenty-foot stone and mortar cross is surrounded by dark folklore of murderous polygamists and supernatural hauntings.

#details
Constructed in the mid-twentieth century, Kay's Cross in Kaysville, Utah, became the subject of intense local legend. According to popular myth, the cross was built by a deranged polygamist who murdered six of his wives and buried them around its base, or encased his seventh wife's heart within the stone. In reality, the land was once inhabited by Elden Kingston and his polygamous followers, who built the cross at the behest of cult leader Krishna Venta. Over the years, the site attracted rumors of occult rituals, animal sacrifices, and the ghost of the alleged seventh wife glowing under a full moon, before the structure was heavily damaged by a mysterious explosion in 1992.

#location
Kay's Cross is located in a secluded hollow off US-89 in Kaysville, Davis County, Utah.

#title
The Ghosts of the Benson Grist Mill

#summary
Restored nineteenth-century pioneer milling grounds in Tooele County are said to be haunted by disembodied voices, phantom grinding sounds, and the spirit of a former miller.

#details
Established in 1854 under the direction of LDS leader Ezra Taft Benson, the Benson Grist Mill in Tooele Valley served as a crucial agricultural hub for early pioneers. After falling into disuse and later being restored as a historical heritage site, visitors and paranormal investigators reported intense ghostly activity on the grounds. Accounts include shadowy figures lurking near the old millstones, cold spots, disembodied voices, children's laughter echoing through the halls at night, and the phantom sound of grain being ground as though machinery was operating in an empty mill.

#location
The Benson Grist Mill is located at 3258 Stansbury Road in Stansbury Park, Tooele County, Utah.

#title
The Haunting of the Ben Lomond Hotel

#summary
The historic Ben Lomond Hotel in Ogden is said to be haunted by a tragic bride who drowned in her bathtub and her grief-stricken son who took his own life in the adjacent room.

#details
Constructed in 1927 along Washington Boulevard in downtown Ogden, the grand Ben Lomond Hotel gained a dark reputation through local folklore surrounding its upper floors. According to legend, a newlywed bride drowned in the bathtub of Room 1102 just days after her wedding. Guests and visitors over the decades have reported unexplained sounds of running water and unearthly chills when the room is empty. Furthermore, lore states that her grief-stricken son took his life in the adjacent Room 1101, where visitors frequently describe sudden cold spots, oppressive sensations, and sightings of a somber male figure.

#location
The Ben Lomond Hotel is located along Washington Boulevard in downtown Ogden, Utah.

#title
The Mystery of Kay's Cross

#summary
For decades, a towering stone and cement cross hidden in a Kaysville hollow became the focus of dark urban legends about a murderous polygamist and cult rituals. In reality, the mysterious monument was built in the 1940s at the urging of a wandering religious zealot who claimed to be the reincarnation of Jesus Christ.

#details
Standing twenty feet tall in a secluded wooded ravine on the northern outskirts of Kaysville, Utah, a crudely constructed stone and cement cross marked with a large letter "K" became a fixture of local folklore. Generations of teenagers and children snuck onto private property—owned by the secretive Kingston family—to seek out the monument, giving rise to wild stories. According to local lore, the cross was built by a mad polygamist who murdered his seven wives, buried them around its base, and entombed a heart inside the structure before hanging himself. Other rumors whispered of glowing stones during full moons, witches, werewolves, and animal sacrifices. 

For decades, the true origins of the cross remained shrouded in secrecy. However, historical journals and records later revealed that the monument was actually constructed in 1946 by members of the Kingston clan at the behest of Francis Pencovic, a charismatic self-proclaimed messiah who went by the name Krishna Venta. Pencovic had convinced the group to build the monument as a tribute before moving on to California, where he later founded a movement known as WKFL. In February 1992, an 80-pound dynamite blast destroyed the cross, though the ruins and the dark legends surrounding the hollow persist to this day.

#location
Kay's Cross stood in a secluded wooded ravine on the northern outskirts of Kaysville, Utah.

#title
The Phantom Nun of St. Anne's Retreat

#summary
According to local legend, a pregnant nun at a secluded mountain camp in Logan Canyon had her baby drowned by the Mother Superior before she herself was pushed into the swimming pool and killed. Today, visitors and legend-trippers report sightings of the grieving nun wandering the grounds and floating in the pool.

#details
Deep in Logan Canyon, the former retreat property known as St. Anne's Retreat became the center of a persistent local legend cluster. Collected widely by students and archived in the Fife Folklore Archives at Utah State University, the tale tells of a young nun who became pregnant while staying at the property. When she decided against giving her child up for adoption, the Mother Superior allegedly drowned the infant in the camp's swimming pool. Devastated and searching the grounds for her baby, the young nun was subsequently pushed into the pool by the Mother Superior and drowned as well. Generations of teenagers and thrill-seekers visiting the site have reported eerie encounters, including sightings of the spectral nun wandering among the abandoned buildings and floating face-down in the camp's pool.

#location
St. Anne's Retreat is located off Logan Canyon (State Route 89) east of Logan, Utah.

#title
The Phantoms of Big Cottonwood Canyon

#summary
Motorists traveling along Big Cottonwood Canyon Road after dark frequently encounter a distressed, dripping-wet woman standing by the roadside who vanishes when approached. Drivers have also reported picking up mysterious hitchhikers who disappear mid-ride.

#details
Stretching roughly 15 miles from the canyon's mouth, Big Cottonwood Canyon Road is reputed to be one of the most haunted drives in Utah. After sunset, travelers have reported seeing a young woman standing distressed and drenched by the asphalt, only for her to completely vanish when motorists pull over to help. Other drivers have encountered a lone male hitchhiker near the canyon entrance who seemingly fades into thin air, leaving behind only the echoing sound of clinking carabiners. Another frequent apparition involves a hitchhiker and his dog who climb into cars before inexplicably vanishing from the rearview mirror as the vehicle approaches the canyon floor.

#location
Big Cottonwood Canyon Road begins at the mouth of the canyon in Salt Lake County, Utah.

#title
The Purple Lady of the Rio Grande Depot

#summary
A woman wearing all purple who was struck and killed by a train after an argument with her fiancé is said to haunt the halls and restrooms of the historic Rio Grande Depot in Salt Lake City.

#details
Built in 1910 by the Denver and Rio Grande Western Railroad, the Rio Grande Depot in downtown Salt Lake City has long been associated with the ghost known as the "Purple Lady." According to local legend, a young woman dressed entirely in purple—including a violet suit, hat, and veil—met her fiancé at the station when he returned as a soldier. During a heated argument, the soldier threw her engagement ring onto the railroad tracks. When she ran out to retrieve it, she was struck by an oncoming train and killed instantly. For over eighty years, staff and visitors have reported paranormal activity tied to her spirit, including flickering lights, phantom footsteps, a woman singing near the restrooms, and emergency calls originating from empty elevators.

#location
The Rio Grande Depot is located at 300 South Rio Grande Street in Salt Lake City, Utah.
