#title
The Apparition at the Norwich Inn

#summary
The historic Norwich Inn, dating back to 1797, is said to be haunted by the spirit of a former innkeeper who ran a bootleg liquor operation during Prohibition and now wanders the upper floors and dining room in a black formal gown.

#details
Established in 1797 on land owned by Colonel Jasper Murdock, the Norwich Inn in Norwich, Vermont, carries a rich history that includes entertaining President James Monroe in 1817. In 1920, the property was purchased by Charles and Mary "Ma" Walker. According to local legend, after her husband's death, Ma Walker continued operating the tavern by selling bootleg liquor from the basement throughout Prohibition until the mid-1930s. Following her passing, her spirit has reportedly been seen gliding along the upper floors and wandering the parlor or dining room dressed in a black formal gown, before disappearing into the library. Guests staying in room 20 and other areas have also reported unexplained phenomena such as self-flushing toilets, faucets turning on and off by themselves, and rocking chairs moving without anyone sitting in them.

#location
The Norwich Inn located at the corner of Main Street and Beaver Meadow Road in Norwich, Vermont.

#title
The Curse of the Hayden Family

#summary
A dying woman's curse predicted that the Hayden name would perish in poverty by the third generation, a fate apparently fulfilled when the family's once-grand brick mansion in Vermont fell into total financial ruin and abandonment.

#details
In 1801, William Hayden settled in the town of Albany, Vermont, where he established a frame house and a business but reportedly failed to repay substantial debts owed to his mother-in-law, Mercie Dale. As Mercie grew seriously ill, she suspected William of poisoning her and pronounced a hex: "The Hayden name shall die in the third generation, and the last to bear the name shall die in poverty." Initially, the prediction seemed improbable as William's son amassed great wealth through railroad construction and built an impressive brick mansion in 1854 complete with massive columns, granite details, and a spring-floored ballroom. However, decades later, a combination of contested wills, debt, illness, and legal disputes unraveled the estate. The mansion was eventually abandoned, and the last surviving family member to bear the name, Armenia "Mamie" Hayden, died in poverty in 1927.

#location
The historic Hayden mansion situated in the town of Albany in Vermont's Northeast Kingdom.

#title
The Dog-Loving Ghost of Brantview House

#summary
Brantview House, a historic mansion on the campus of St. Johnsbury Academy in Vermont, is haunted by a polite, dog-loving phantom who interacts with household pets and startles residents.

#details
Once a grand private residence and now part of St. Johnsbury Academy, Brantview House carries a longstanding local reputation for paranormal activity. On one notable evening recounted in Vermont folklore, the house mother was reading in the parlor while her Afghan hound rested at her feet. Suddenly, the dog began snarling as its fur was ruffled by an invisible hand. Recognizing the presence, the dog quickly relaxed and watched as an unseen visitor walked across the room and politely closed the door behind them before departing.

#location
Brantview House is located on the campus of St. Johnsbury Academy in St. Johnsbury, Vermont.

#title
The Ghost of Emily's Bridge

#summary
A jilted lover is said to have taken her life inside a 19th-century covered bridge in Stowe, Vermont, where her angry spirit now haunts visitors.

#details
In the mid-19th century, a young woman named Emily was scheduled to meet her fiancé at the Gold Brook Bridge in Stowe to elope. When her lover failed to show up, the heartbroken Emily allegedly hanged herself from the rafters of the wooden structure. In the decades that followed, the site—commonly known as Emily's Bridge—became the center of local ghostly lore. Visitors and superstitious travelers have reported hearing strange, disembodied voices echoing inside the dark tunnel, feeling sudden unnatural cold spots, and even finding mysterious, claw-like gouges scored into the sides of cars parked near or driven through the bridge.

#location
Gold Brook Covered Bridge (Emily's Bridge) crossing Gold Brook on Covered Bridge Road in Stowe, Vermont.

The New England Grimpendium by J.W. Ocker.

#title
The Ghost of the Old Stagecoach Inn

#summary
Built in 1826 as a private residence and later operating as an inn in Waterbury, the historic building is said to be haunted by former residents whose lingering presence manifests as flickering lights, self-activating room fixtures, and odd nocturnal noises.

#details
The Old Stagecoach Inn, located at 19 N. Main St. in Waterbury, Vermont, dates back to 1826 when it was constructed as a private home. Over the generations, the building transitioned into an inn, but former residents and guests have reportedly left an enduring imprint. Guests and staff have recounted numerous inexplicable occurrences over the years, such as rocking chairs moving on their own, cabinet doors swinging open, window shades shifting, and lights flickering or turning on spontaneously in empty rooms. While modern innkeepers remain skeptical, visitors continue to report feeling an unseen presence and experiencing unexplained paranormal phenomena throughout the historic building.

The Old Stagecoach Inn located at 19 N. Main St. in Waterbury, Vermont.

#title
The Ghostly Guests of the Equinox Hotel

#summary
Standing since 1769 in Manchester, Vermont, the historic Equinox Hotel is said to be haunted by former guests and staff, including First Lady Mary Todd Lincoln and a former owner who met a tragic end on the property.

#details
Operating in various forms since Marsh Tavern opened in 1769, the Equinox Hotel in Manchester has hosted numerous U.S. presidents and notable figures. Among its most famous spectral residents is First Lady Mary Todd Lincoln, who stayed at the hotel in the summer of 1864 with her sons. She loved her stay so much that guests and staff claim her spirit—along with the plaintive sounds of her young son Tad—still lingers in the historic halls. Additionally, the hotel's long-time owner George Orvis, who tragically drowned in the nearby Equinox Pond after hitting his head in 1917, is believed by staff to haunt the building and grounds he cherished in life.

#location
The Equinox Golf Resort & Spa is located at 355 Equinox Pond Road in Manchester Village, Vermont.

#title
The Hauntings of Wilson Castle

#summary
Built in 1885 in Proctor, Vermont, Wilson Castle is widely reputed to be haunted by the spirits of its former residents and visitors who encounter disembodied voices, cold spots, and phantom footsteps throughout its thirty-two rooms.

#details
Constructed in 1885 by Dr. John Johnson and later purchased in 1939 by radio engineer Herbert Lee Wilson, Wilson Castle is a sprawling 32-room estate combining Dutch neo-renaissance, Scottish baronial, and Queen Anne architectural styles. Over the decades, residents, tourists, and professional paranormal investigation teams have reported frequent eerie occurrences within the manor. Classic signs of haunting include cold drafts in the tower, objects moving independently in the basement, phantom footsteps on the grand staircase, and spectral apparitions roaming the halls. Local folklore and whispers also suggest hidden passageways and mysterious burials beneath the castle's stone floors, cementing its reputation as one of Vermont's premier haunted landmarks.

#location
Wilson Castle is located at 297 West Proctor Road in Proctor, Vermont.

#title
The Windowed Tomb of Dr. Timothy Clark Smith

#summary
Plagued by a paralyzing fear of premature burial, a globe-trotting Vermont doctor had a custom underground crypt built with a glass viewing window so he could monitor his status in the afterlife.

#details
Dr. Timothy Clark Smith (1821–1893) was a worldly physician who served as a surgeon in the Russian army and a U.S. Consul in Odessa. Living during an era before modern embalming when mistaken death and premature burials were legitimate concerns, Smith developed an intense phobia of waking up inside a coffin under the earth. Following his sudden death at a hotel in Middlebury, Vermont, in February 1893, his son Harrison supervised the construction of a bizarre and specialized burial vault at Evergreen Cemetery in New Haven, Vermont. The brick crypt was built beneath a grassy mound, featuring stone steps leading to a subterranean chamber and a vertical cement shaft rising from the doctor's face to a 14-by-14-inch glass plate at the surface. Local legend immediately attached itself to the "tomb with a view," with generations of visitors whispering that Smith was entombed alongside a hammer, a chisel, or a hand-held bell so he could claw his way out or signal the living if he ever awoke.

#location
Evergreen Cemetery off Town Hill Road in New Haven, Vermont.
