The Curse of the Hayden Family

Summary

A dying woman's curse predicted that the Hayden name would perish in poverty by the third generation, a fate apparently fulfilled when the family's once-grand brick mansion in Vermont fell into total financial ruin and abandonment.

Details

In 1801, William Hayden settled in the town of Albany, Vermont, where he established a frame house and a business but reportedly failed to repay substantial debts owed to his mother-in-law, Mercie Dale. As Mercie grew seriously ill, she suspected William of poisoning her and pronounced a hex: "The Hayden name shall die in the third generation, and the last to bear the name shall die in poverty." Initially, the prediction seemed improbable as William's son amassed great wealth through railroad construction and built an impressive brick mansion in 1854 complete with massive columns, granite details, and a spring-floored ballroom. However, decades later, a combination of contested wills, debt, illness, and legal disputes unraveled the estate. The mansion was eventually abandoned, and the last surviving family member to bear the name, Armenia "Mamie" Hayden, died in poverty in 1927.
