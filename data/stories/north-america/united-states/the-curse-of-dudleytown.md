#title
The Curse of Dudleytown

#summary
Originating from a sixteenth-century English execution, a generational hex supposedly followed the Dudley family to an isolated and rocky Connecticut settlement. Over the following centuries, the village was abandoned as successive residents suffered ruin, madness, and mysterious deaths.

#details
According to lore, when Edmund Dudley was beheaded in England in 1510 for plotting against King Henry VIII, a curse was placed on his bloodline, dooming all his descendants to horror and death. Decades later, members of the Dudley family immigrated to America and established a small settlement in the heavily shadowed hills of Cornwall, Connecticut, known as Dudleytown. Although never an incorporated town, the rugged community grew around iron ore production and timber, but it was continually plagued by bizarre tragedies. Residents and descendants experienced uncharacteristic bouts of insanity, fatal lightning strikes, mysterious disappearances, and devastating fires. As hardships mounted and the local iron industry shifted elsewhere, the settlement was completely deserted by the early twentieth century, leaving behind only stone foundations, cellar holes, and enduring legends of a dark, lingering curse.

#location
Dudleytown is an abandoned historical settlement located within the Dark Entry Forest in the town of Cornwall, Litchfield County, Connecticut.
