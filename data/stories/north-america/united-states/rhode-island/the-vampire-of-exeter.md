#title
The Vampire of Exeter

#summary
Following a devastating series of family illnesses in late nineteenth-century Rhode Island, villagers exhumed a young woman's body believing she was a vampire draining the life from her surviving brother. Upon finding her corpse remarkably well-preserved with fresh blood, they burned her heart as a desperate cure.

#details
In 1892, George Brown of Exeter, Rhode Island, suffered a tragic series of deaths as his wife and two daughters succumbed to what doctors diagnosed as consumption. When his surviving son, Edwin, also fell gravely ill, superstitious villagers became convinced that one of the deceased family members was a vampire returning from the grave to prey upon the living. On a chilly March afternoon, men from the village exhumed the bodies in Chestnut Hill Cemetery. While the mother and older daughter were fully decomposed, 19-year-old Mercy Brown appeared remarkably well-preserved, with hair and nails that seemed to have grown and fresh blood within her heart. Believing they had found the source of the curse, they cut out her heart and burned it on a nearby rock, mixing the ashes into a medicinal drink for Edwin—though the young man sadly passed away shortly thereafter.

#location
Chestnut Hill Cemetery is located off Chestnut Hill Road in Exeter, Rhode Island.
