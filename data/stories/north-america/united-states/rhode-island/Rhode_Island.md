#title
The Curse of the Witch Tree

#summary
A rural Rhode Island crossroads is said to be cursed by a suspected witch buried beneath an ancient oak tree, bringing misfortune and strange occurrences to those who pass. Drivers who circle the intersection multiple times are believed to summon the restless spirit.

#details
In the town of Smithfield, an old oak tree sprouted at the intersection of Mann School Road and Colwell Road in the early 19th century. As the rural roads were paved and traffic increased over the decades, motorists frequently suffered unusual accidents and fender-benders near the tree. Local folklore emerged claiming that an unremembered outcast or witch had been buried at the isolated spot, with the oak tree planted intentionally over her grave to trap her corpse. The enduring curse of the Witch Tree inspired legends that anyone who drives around the island and tree thirteen times will awaken the dormant witch. Even after the original tree was removed due to decay and replaced by a modern island with a guardrail, the local legend persists.

#location
The intersection of Mann School Road and Colwell Road in Smithfield, Rhode Island.

#title
The Devil's Footprints of North Kingstown

#summary
Satan chased a local woman along a granite ledge in Rhode Island before leaping into Narragansett Bay, leaving his cloven hoofprints burned into the stone. The impressions remain visible on a rock face near Wickford to this day.

#details
According to colonial-era legend in North Kingstown, Rhode Island, the Devil pursued a woman who had fled through the area, seizing her by the arm as she attempted to escape near Wickford. When she cried out in resistance, her attacker revealed himself as Satan, stamping his feet fiercely onto the granite bedrock. He then grabbed her by the waist and leaped into Narragansett Bay, transforming into a serpent as he vanished into the water. In the wake of his departure, deep impressions resembling a cloven hoof and a human foot with a distinct great toe were left permanently burned into the stone ledge.

#location
Devil's Foot Road is situated off Post Road in North Kingstown, Rhode Island, where the granite ledge known as Devil's Foot Rock rests near the Quonset area.

#title
The Ghostly Echoes of Edgar Allan Poe at the Providence Athenæum

#summary
The historic Providence Athenæum library on Benefit Street preserves the lingering memory and reported ghostly presence of Edgar Allan Poe following his ill-fated romance and dramatic breakup there.

#details
In 1848, the atmospheric Providence Athenæum served as the backdrop for a poignant and tragic chapter in the life of author Edgar Allan Poe. Poe had been courting Sarah Helen Whitman, a Providence poet and intellectual with a deep interest in spiritualism. After Poe proposed marriage, Sarah learned he had broken a promise to remain sober and summoned him to the Athenæum library to call off their engagement. Poe died the following year, and over the decades, visitors and staff at the library have reported eerie atmospheric disturbances, including spectral sightings of Poe lingering near the dark alcoves where his romantic hopes ended.

#location
The Providence Athenæum on Benefit Street in Providence, Rhode Island.

#title
The Mercy Brown Vampire Incident

#summary
In 1892, the family and neighbors of a grieving father in Exeter exhumed the body of his deceased daughter Mercy, believing she was an undead entity causing tuberculosis deaths among her surviving relatives.

#details
During a devastating outbreak of tuberculosis—then widely known as "consumption"—in the rural town of Exeter, Rhode Island, several members of the Brown family fell ill and perished one by one. Convinced by contemporary folklore that multiple deaths within a single family signaled that an undead relative was draining the life from the living, villagers and family members persuaded the grieving father to exhume the bodies of his wife and daughters. Upon opening the crypt of 19-year-old Mercy Lena Brown, villagers noted that her body appeared remarkably well-preserved compared to the others. Interpreting this as proof of her undead status, they cut out her heart and liver, burned them to ashes, and mixed the remnants into a tonic given to her ailing brother in an effort to break the curse. 

#location
The cemetery of the Baptist Church in Exeter, Rhode Island.

Michael E. Bell, *Food for the Dead - On the Trail of New England's Vampires*.

#title
The Phantom Child of Tower Hill

#summary
A narrow, wooded stretch of Tower Hill Road in Cumberland, Rhode Island, is haunted by the spectral figure of a young boy walking his dog alongside restless historical spirits.

#details
Tower Hill Road in Cumberland, Rhode Island, is a secluded, tree-lined country road notorious among locals and ghost hunters for eerie paranormal encounters. Visitors who park along the road at midnight frequently report an oppressive atmosphere of dread, unnatural fog banks hugging vehicle doors, and unexplained handprints left on car windows. Alongside the heavy, negative energy attributed to historical skirmishes from King Philip's War in the 1600s, local folklore and eyewitness accounts speak of the ghostly apparition of a little boy walking his dog along the dark roadside.

#location
Tower Hill Road, running between Diamond Hill Road and Wrentham Road in Cumberland, Rhode Island.

#title
The Phantom of Aldrich Mansion
#summary
A grand estate overlooking Narragansett Bay is said to be haunted by the spirit of a young woman who perpetually wanders toward a forbidden balcony.
#details
Commissioned by U.S. Senator Nelson W. Aldrich in 1896 and completed in 1912, the sprawling Aldrich Mansion in Warwick, Rhode Island, is steeped in Gilded Age grandeur and local folklore. Employees and visitors frequently report seeing the apparition of a woman in her late twenties or thirties roaming the interior halls. Witnesses note that she always seems to be walking purposefully toward the doors opening onto the front balcony—the very spot where local legend claims one of the senator's daughters tragically committed suicide by jumping, despite a lack of historical records confirming the event.
#location
The Aldrich Mansion is located at 836 Warwick Neck Avenue in Warwick, Rhode Island.

#title
The Spectral Guest of the White Horse Tavern
#summary
A colonial-era overnight guest who died mysteriously at America's oldest operating tavern is said to still wander its dining rooms and hallways.
#details
Built in 1652 in Newport, Rhode Island, the White Horse Tavern holds a long and storied history as the oldest operating tavern in the United States. In the 1720s, an overnight guest died in his sleep of unknown causes, sparking quarantine fears. Today, staff and guests report seeing the apparition of a man dressed in shabby colonial attire, frequently spotted near the fireplaces in the main dining room or upstairs. Other unexplained phenomena include a mysterious entity that taps people on the shoulder, the sound of heavy footsteps in empty rooms, and the soft crying of a little girl near the second-floor restrooms.
#location
The White Horse Tavern is located at the corner of Farewell and Marlborough Streets in Newport, Rhode Island.

#title
The Spirits of Belcourt Castle

#summary
For over half a century, residents and visitors of the opulent Belcourt Castle on Bellevue Avenue in Newport documented numerous paranormal encounters with restless spirits and animated antique artifacts.

#details
Built in the late 19th century in Newport, Rhode Island, the 60-room Belcourt Castle amassed not only lavish European furnishings and antiquities but also a rich lore of paranormal activity. For 52 years, resident Harle Tinney and her family experienced unexplainable phenomena throughout the mansion, ranging from glowing apparitions walking through walls to moving suits of 15th-century armor and antique Gothic chairs that produced sudden flashes of light and burning odors upon contact. Tinney compiled over twenty of these documented encounters into her accounts, establishing Belcourt Castle as one of Newport's most thoroughly haunted historic estates.

#location
Belcourt Castle on Bellevue Avenue in Newport, Rhode Island.

#upstream-source
Harle Tinney, *The Ghosts of Belcourt Castle*.

#title
The Vampire of Exeter

#summary
Following a devastating series of family illnesses in late nineteenth-century Rhode Island, villagers exhumed a young woman's body believing she was a vampire draining the life from her surviving brother. Upon finding her corpse remarkably well-preserved with fresh blood, they burned her heart as a desperate cure.

#details
In 1892, George Brown of Exeter, Rhode Island, suffered a tragic series of deaths as his wife and two daughters succumbed to what doctors diagnosed as consumption. When his surviving son, Edwin, also fell gravely ill, superstitious villagers became convinced that one of the deceased family members was a vampire returning from the grave to prey upon the living. On a chilly March afternoon, men from the village exhumed the bodies in Chestnut Hill Cemetery. While the mother and older daughter were fully decomposed, 19-year-old Mercy Brown appeared remarkably well-preserved, with hair and nails that seemed to have grown and fresh blood within her heart. Believing they had found the source of the curse, they cut out her heart and burned it on a nearby rock, mixing the ashes into a medicinal drink for Edwin—though the young man sadly passed away shortly thereafter.

#location
Chestnut Hill Cemetery is located off Chestnut Hill Road in Exeter, Rhode Island.

#title
The Warning Voice in the Nightingale-Brown House
#summary
A late-night custodian working alone in a historic Providence mansion was startled by a disembodied voice warning him never to turn off the light in a portrait room.
#details
Built in 1792 by merchant Captain John Nightingale on Benefit Street in Providence, Rhode Island, the Nightingale-Brown House has long been associated with dark histories and eerie sensations. According to local folklore compiled by Rory Raven, a new custodial worker cleaning the sprawling building late at night experienced an intense feeling of being watched while working in a room dominated by a large, grim portrait. The moment he finished vacuuming and switched off the wall switch, a sepulchral voice out of the darkness commanded, "DON'T TURN THAT LIGHT OUT." When the shaken custodian reported the encounter to his veteran co-worker, the older man casually confirmed that strange occurrences haunted that specific room and advised him never to turn off the light again.
#location
The Nightingale-Brown House is located on Benefit Street in Providence, Rhode Island.
#upstream-source
Haunted Providence: Strange Tales from the Smallest State by Rory Raven.
