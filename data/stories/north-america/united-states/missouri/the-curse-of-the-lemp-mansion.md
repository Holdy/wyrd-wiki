#title
The Curse of the Lemp Mansion

#summary
Following the sudden death of a favored heir and the collapse of their brewing empire, the wealthy Lemp family of St. Louis suffered decades of tragic suicides and mysterious misfortunes within their historic home.

#details
In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, the Lemp family rose to immense wealth and prominence as pioneers of the American lager industry in St. Louis, Missouri, anchored by their sprawling brewery and the grand 33-room mansion they called home. However, the family's fortune turned to profound tragedy starting in 1901 with the sudden death of William Lemp Sr.'s favorite son, Frederick. Devastated by grief and mounting pressures, William Lemp Sr. shot himself in his office in 1904. Over the following decades, the family was besieged by a seemingly relentless string of suicides and untimely deaths, including William Lemp Jr., his sister Elsa, and Charles Lemp, who also took their own lives after enduring severe depression, isolation, and scandal. Whispers of a dark family curse grew among locals as the grand mansion fell into disrepair, becoming famously haunted by the spirits of the tragic family members and an isolated child hidden away in its attic.

#location
The Lemp Mansion is located at 3322 De Menil Place in St. Louis, Missouri, situated near Cherokee Street and just west of the Mississippi River.
