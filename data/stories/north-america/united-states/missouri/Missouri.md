#title
The Civil War Hauntings of the Anderson House

#summary
The historic Anderson House in Lexington, Missouri, which served as a makeshift hospital during the Civil War Battle of the Hemp Bales, is haunted by the lingering spirits of fallen soldiers and poltergeist activity.

#details
Built in the mid-19th century, the stately Anderson House became the focal point of the 1861 Civil War Battle of the Hemp Bales, where combatants utilized stacked hemp bales for cover. The house traded hands multiple times and operated as a blood-soaked field hospital, leaving permanent bloodstains and structural battle scars. Decades later, tour guides and visitors frequently report seeing phantom Confederate and Union soldiers roaming the pristine grounds—which have never been farmed since the battle—as well as hearing unexplained sounds, experiencing physical pushes on the stairs, and witnessing heavy wooden locking bars lifted and thrown across rooms.

#location
The Anderson House is located in Lexington, Missouri.

#title
The Curse of the Lemp Mansion

#summary
Following the sudden death of a favored heir and the collapse of their brewing empire, the wealthy Lemp family of St. Louis suffered decades of tragic suicides and mysterious misfortunes within their historic home.

#details
In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, the Lemp family rose to immense wealth and prominence as pioneers of the American lager industry in St. Louis, Missouri, anchored by their sprawling brewery and the grand 33-room mansion they called home. However, the family's fortune turned to profound tragedy starting in 1901 with the sudden death of William Lemp Sr.'s favorite son, Frederick. Devastated by grief and mounting pressures, William Lemp Sr. shot himself in his office in 1904. Over the following decades, the family was besieged by a seemingly relentless string of suicides and untimely deaths, including William Lemp Jr., his sister Elsa, and Charles Lemp, who also took their own lives after enduring severe depression, isolation, and scandal. Whispers of a dark family curse grew among locals as the grand mansion fell into disrepair, becoming famously haunted by the spirits of the tragic family members and an isolated child hidden away in its attic.

#location
The Lemp Mansion is located at 3322 De Menil Place in St. Louis, Missouri, situated near Cherokee Street and just west of the Mississippi River.

#title
The Guardian of Rockcliffe Mansion

#summary
Perched high above the Mississippi River in Hannibal, Missouri, the grand Rockcliffe Mansion is said to be haunted by its original builder, lumber baron John J. Cruikshank, whose phantom footsteps still echo up the servant's staircase.

#details
Built between 1898 and 1900 by wealthy lumber baron John J. Cruikshank, Rockcliffe Mansion is a massive 30-room Gilded Age estate that once hosted Mark Twain in 1902. After Cruikshank died in his bed in 1924, his family abruptly abandoned the home, leaving it fully furnished, vacant, and deteriorating for over four decades while local children dared each other to explore its ruins. Following its eventual restoration and conversion into a bed and breakfast, caretakers, guests, and owners reported numerous ghostly phenomena. Most famously, witnesses hear the heavy boot steps of Cruikshank climbing the servant's staircase around 2:00 a.m. just as he did in life, while his indentation has occasionally been found rumpling the bed linens in his former bedroom.

#location
Rockcliffe Mansion is located on a rocky bluff overlooking the Mississippi River in Hannibal, Missouri.

#title
The Hornet Spook Light

#summary
A mysterious, glowing orange orb has been witnessed traveling along a rural gravel road on the Missouri-Oklahoma border for well over a century, defying scientific explanation.

#details
For more than a hundred years, an anomalous glowing sphere known as the Hornet Spook Light has been reported along a four-mile stretch of gravel road—historically called Devil's Promenade—near Hornet, Missouri, and Quapaw, Oklahoma. Described variously as an orange ball of fire ranging from the size of a baseball to a basketball, the orb dances, spins, rises above treetops, and travels at high speeds before fading away. Local legends attribute the light to the spirits of a young Quapaw couple who plunged to their deaths to escape pursuing warriors, a grieving miner searching for his family with a lantern, or a decapitated Osage chief seeking his missing head. Although numerous scientific investigations, including studies by the Army Corps of Engineers, have attempted to explain the phenomenon through natural gas, will-o'-the-wisps, or shifting tectonic electrical charges, none have provided a conclusive answer.

#location
The four-mile gravel road known as Devil's Promenade, located southwest of Joplin, Missouri, near the hamlet of Hornet.

#title
The Legend of "Aunt Sally" at Conley House

#summary
Conley House on the University of Missouri campus in Columbia is bound to the enduring legend of a woman known as "Aunt Sally," whose spirit allegedly haunts the historic residence.

#details
Built during the 19th century as part of the University of Missouri's early history in Columbia, Conley House became central to campus folklore due to the legend of "Aunt Sally." According to tradition, Sally lived in the house during its early years and met an untimely end, with local lore claiming her spirit remained tethered to the property and reacted strongly whenever doors or attic spaces were left ajar. Generations of students and staff have documented an oppressive atmosphere, unexplained temperature drops, shifting air pressure, and phantom sounds within the historic home.

#location
Conley House is located on the campus of the University of Missouri in Columbia, Missouri.

#title
The Legend of Momo the Missouri Monster

#summary
During the summer of 1972, multiple residents of Louisiana, Missouri, reported terrifying encounters with a massive, foul-smelling, hairy bipedal creature roaming near Marzolf Hill. The sightings sparked a massive local panic and an armed search party before concluding with mysterious, unexplainable footprints discovered at a nearby farm.

#details
In July 1972, a wave of bizarre encounters shook the small town of Louisiana, Missouri. The phenomenon began when eight-year-old Terry Harrison and his brother saw a six-to-seven-foot-tall, black, hairy creature standing near the edge of their yard at the base of Marzolf Hill. The beast was described as having hair covering its face and carrying what appeared to be a dead dog under its arm, while giving off an overpowering, nauseating odor. Over the following days, local resident Edgar Harrison heard heavy clanging and animal-like growls near the water reservoir atop Marzolf Hill, prompting large groups of armed townspeople to investigate. Sightings continued around the area, including one by local resident Ellis Minor, who spotted the coal-black creature standing directly in the middle of Great River Road. The panic eventually drew national media attention and a multi-day search party led by local police and state conservation officers. The flap finally concluded in early August after unexplained, massive footprints appeared abruptly in the middle of a garden at the Suddarth farm northwest of town without any matching tracks leading to or from the site.

#location
Marzolf Hill on the outskirts of Louisiana, Missouri.

#title
The Lumber Baron of Rockcliffe Mansion

#summary
Rockcliffe Mansion in Hannibal, Missouri, remains haunted by its eccentric creator, lumber baron John Cruickshanks, who died staring out at the Mississippi River.

#details
Constructed at the turn of the 20th century by wealthy lumber baron John Cruickshanks overlooking the Mississippi River, Rockcliffe Mansion was an architectural marvel filled with opulent mahogany wood and expensive wallpaper. After Cruickshanks passed away in his bedroom facing the river, his grief-stricken family abandoned the mansion, leaving the original furniture and belongings untouched for decades. When the mansion was eventually restored, caretakers and visitors reported regular paranormal disturbances, including the sound of front doors opening and closing, unseen footsteps walking up staircases, and a ghostly figure resembling Cruickshanks enjoying a drink in the bar. Furthermore, modern visitors note that the mattress on the bed where he died continually sinks on one side as if someone is sleeping on it.

#location
Rockcliffe Mansion is located in Hannibal, Missouri.

#title
The Phantom Couple of Upper Blackwell Road

#summary
A phantom couple frequently appears along Upper Blackwell Road trying to flag down passing vehicles, only to vanish into thin air the moment a car slows down. Additionally, the nearby Black Tram Bridge is associated with chilling legends of phantom cars and supernatural pursuits.

#details
Upper Blackwell Road, a 3.6-mile stretch leading to the small town of Blackwell, Missouri, has long held a reputation for local hauntings and eerie occurrences. Among its most famous spectral inhabitants is a ghostly couple who stands by the roadside trying to flag down passing cars. Drivers who attempt to slow down or offer assistance find that the figures disappear completely before contact can be made. Further along the route at the Black Tram Bridge, local lore warns of similar supernatural phenomena, including reports of phantom vehicles that chase motorists who cross the bridge at night.

#location
Upper Blackwell Road is a 3.6-mile stretch of road running directly into the town of Blackwell in Missouri.

#title
The Specters of the Elms Hotel

#summary
Built in the late 19th century in Excelsior Springs, Missouri, the historic Elms Hotel is said to be haunted by a Prohibition-era gambler in the basement pool, a diligent 1920s maid on the third floor, and a distressed woman searching for her child.

#details
Opened initially in 1888 and rebuilt after devastating fires in 1898 and 1910, the Elms Hotel in Excelsior Springs became a luxurious mineral resort and a favored hideaway for gangsters such as Al Capone during Prohibition. Over the decades, staff and guests have reported persistent supernatural occurrences throughout the limestone building. In the basement lap pool—once part of a Prohibition-era speakeasy where illegal gambling and drinking took place—visitors report cold spots and the presence of a male spirit believed to be a gambler killed during a mob altercation. On the third floor, a benevolent spirit resembling a 1920s maid is frequently seen observing cleaning staff, while another spirit, a grieving woman searching for her child, startles guests by moving objects and roaming the halls.

#location
The Elms Hotel is located at 401 Regent Street in Excelsior Springs, Missouri.

#title
The Tragic Apparitions of the Lemp Mansion

#summary
The historic Lemp Mansion in St. Louis is haunted by members of the wealthy brewing family and other lingering spirits following a legacy of tragic suicides and family turmoil.

#details
Beginning in the late 19th century, the Lemp family built an immense beer empire in St. Louis and transformed a 33-room Victorian mansion into their family home. However, the family was struck by a series of devastating tragedies, including the sudden death of favorite son Frederick and four family suicides—most notably patriarch William Lemp Sr., his son William Lemp Jr., sister Elsa Lemp, and brother Charles Lemp—all occurring via gunshot within the estate. Following the end of the brewing dynasty and decades as a declining boarding house, visitors and staff reported enduring paranormal phenomena, such as phantom footsteps, slamming doors, poltergeist activity, and spectral sightings. Notable ghosts include the "Monkey Face Boy" confined to the attic, the apparition of William Lemp Jr. peering over bathroom stalls, and the unmistakable scent of lavender linked to Lillian Lemp.

#location
The Lemp Mansion is located at 3322 De Menil Place in St. Louis, Missouri.
