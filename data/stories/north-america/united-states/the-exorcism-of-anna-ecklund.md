#title
The Exorcism of Anna Ecklund

#summary
An American woman named Emma Schmidt, known under the pseudonym Anna Ecklund, endured a decades-long demonic possession that culminated in an intensive series of exorcisms at an Iowa convent in 1928. During the harrowing rituals, she reportedly levitated, spoke in languages she had never learned, and displayed violent physical reactions to holy objects.

#details
Beginning in her youth, Emma Schmidt exhibited a severe physical and psychological aversion to religious items, holy water, and churches. After years of escalating disturbances and failed medical explanations, the Catholic Church formally determined she was possessed by multiple entities—identified as Beelzebub, Judas Iscariot, Lucifer, her abusive father, and her aunt. In 1928, Father Theophilus Riesinger undertook a prolonged exorcism at the Franciscan convent in Earling, Iowa, spanning three separate sessions across August, September, and December. Throughout the ordeal, Schmidt's body swelled and distorted, she vomited foreign debris, spoke in Latin and German, and exhibited terrifying strength and levitation. On December 23, 1928, Father Riesinger commanded the entities to depart for the final time, allowing Schmidt to return to a peaceful life of faith until her later years.

#location
The Franciscan convent located in the small town of Earling, Iowa.
