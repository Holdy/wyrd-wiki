#title
The Apparitions of the Vieux Carré Stage

#summary
Behind the curtains of a historic New Orleans theater, staff frequently encounter spectral figures, including a phantom patron in a captain's hat and a shadowy colonial-era apparition. These lingering manifestations are believed to be the residual energy of artists and patrons who never truly left the spotlight.

#details
Operating for over a century in the French Quarter, Le Petit Théâtre de Vieux Carré is steeped in theatrical superstition and ghostly encounters. Late-night stage crews and technical directors have reported seeing unearthly figures vanish into thin air, such as a man wearing a captain's hat sitting quietly in the upper balcony before disappearing. Other encounters include a spectral apparition in colonial-era clothing rushing directly through staff members backstage, and a mysterious woman in white frequently seen gazing plaintively out of the courtyard windows.

#location
Le Petit Théâtre is located in the French Quarter at 616 St. Peter Street, New Orleans, Louisiana.

#title
The Curse of Julia Brown

#summary
According to local legend, a Louisiana woman named Julia Brown predicted her own death and cursed the small swamp town of Frenier, ensuring that a devastating 1915 hurricane would wipe out the entire settlement.

#details
In the small farming community of Frenier, surrounded by the Manchac Swamp in Louisiana, a local resident named Julia Brown frequently sat on her porch singing and playing guitar. Over time, local folklore transformed her into a voodoo priestess who grew embittered by ungrateful neighbors and warned them with an ominous song: "One day I'm going to die and take the whole town with me." On September 29, 1915, Julia passed away of old age, and as villagers gathered for her funeral, a violent Category 4 hurricane struck the region. The ensuing storm surge and powerful winds utterly destroyed Frenier and the neighboring settlement of Ruddock, leaving behind mass graves and cementing the legend that Julia's final curse had doomed the entire community.

#location
Frenier Cemetery is located in the wetlands of St. John the Baptist Parish near LaPlace, Louisiana, situated between Lakes Pontchartrain and Maurepas.

#title
The Curse of Julia Brown and the Vanishing of Frenier

#summary
Before passing away, a local healer named Julia Brown placed a fatal curse on her village out of resentment for feeling unappreciated. Shortly after her funeral, a devastating 1915 hurricane struck, wiping the settlement of Frenier entirely off the map.

#details
Julia Brown was a well-known healer and midwife who lived in the small timber and rail village of Frenier, situated along the western rim of Lake Pontchartrain in Louisiana. Feeling ignored and unappreciated by her neighbors, she composed a chilling song declaring that when she died, she would take the entire town down with her. In September 1915, Julia passed away, and as mourners gathered at her funeral under heavy skies, a massive hurricane roared ashore. The catastrophic storm surge and fierce winds obliterated Frenier and neighboring Ruddock, submerging the settlements into the Manchac Swamp and leaving behind only scattered pilings and the raised grounds of Frenier Cemetery.

#location
Frenier Cemetery is located on a small, elevated rise of land within the Manchac Swamp along the western rim of Lake Pontchartrain, near LaPlace, Louisiana.

#title
The General and the Meticulous Dead

#summary
A historic French Quarter mansion once inhabited by Confederate General P.G.T. Beauregard is haunted by phantom soldiers in uniform and the general himself. Witnesses have reported hearing ghostly gunfire and encountering spirits who still roam the rooms searching for lost belongings.

#details
The Beauregard-Keyes House, built in 1826 at Chartres and Ursulines Street, has a storied past marked by Civil War occupation and a violent Sicilian mob shootout. Following the war, General P.G.T. Beauregard lived in the home as a tenant, and author Frances Parkinson Keyes later reported that his ghost still paces the halls at night, purportedly searching for his boots because he was buried in his stocking feet. In addition to the general, witnesses have reported phantom Civil War soldiers in gray and butternut uniforms standing watch or dissolving into mist, accompanied by the lingering sounds and scent of musket fire.

#location
The Beauregard-Keyes House is located at 1113 Chartres Street in the French Quarter of New Orleans, Louisiana.

#title
The Legend of Chloe at the Myrtles Plantation

#summary
According to local Louisiana folklore, an enslaved woman named Chloe poisoned her master's family in a desperate bid to win back favor, resulting in her execution and a haunting that has endured for generations.

#details
In the nineteenth century at the Myrtles Plantation just outside St. Francisville, Louisiana, an enslaved woman named Chloe served as governess for Judge Clark Woodruffe and his family. Unhappy about being forced into the judge's unwanted romantic advances and terrified of being cast out to work in the grueling fields, Chloe took to eavesdropping on the household. When the judge caught her listening at a door, he punished her brutally by severing one of her ears, forcing her to wear a green turban to hide the wound. Desperate to regain her standing, Chloe decided to bake a birthday cake laced with poisonous oleander leaves, intending to make the family only slightly ill so she could heroically nurse them back to health. Tragically, the poison was too strong, killing the judge's wife and two daughters. Terrified that the judge would retaliate against all the enslaved residents, a group of her peers caught Chloe, lynched her, and threw her weighted body into the Mississippi River. Ever since, visitors and staff have reported sightings of a spectral woman in a green turban wandering the historic halls and lifting mosquito netting from beds, making the estate one of the most famous haunted landmarks in the American South.

#location
The Myrtles Plantation, located just outside St. Francisville, Louisiana.

#title
The Phantom Footsteps of the Old State Capitol

#summary
Staff and security officers at the historic Gothic-style capitol building frequently encounter phantom footsteps, slamming doors, and shadowy apparitions of former political figures.

#details
Guards patrolling the historic Old State Capitol in Baton Rouge late at night have reported running into unseen barriers, hearing heavy footsteps cross balconies above the House of Representatives, and witnessing doors slam shut in empty hallways. Popular local lore attributes these ghostly occurrences to Senator Pierre Couvillian, who allegedly suffered a fatal heart attack during a heated legislative debate, or to political prisoners once held in the building's basement.

#location
Louisiana's Old State Capitol is situated at 100 North Boulevard overlooking the Mississippi River in Baton Rouge, Louisiana.

#title
The Phantom Hitchhiker of the Bourg-Larose Highway

#summary
Motorists traveling a lonely Louisiana highway late at night frequently encounter a young woman in white thumbing for a ride who mysteriously vanishes before reaching her destination. This eerie apparition is a deeply ingrained piece of regional folklore in Lafourche Parish.

#details
For decades, drivers traversing the Bourg-Larose Highway between the communities of Bourg and Larose in Lafourche Parish, Louisiana, have reported seeing a young woman standing by the roadside at night, signaling for a hitch. Some compassionate or frightened motorists pull over to let her into their vehicles. Once inside, she either remains completely silent or gives vague directions before abruptly disappearing into thin air while the car is still moving, leaving behind a cold seat or the faint scent of flowers. Other sightings describe her standing near the bayou or vanishing into the shadows of the cypress trees, rooted in regional Cajun and Creole folklore regarding restless spirits.

#location
The haunting takes place along the Bourg-Larose Highway (comprising sections of Louisiana Highway 24 and Highway 1) stretching between the communities of Bourg and Larose in Lafourche Parish, Louisiana.

#title
The Phantom of the Electric Chair

#summary
Following her historic 1942 execution in the courthouse basement, the restless spirit of Toni Jo Henry is said to haunt the halls with inexplicable cold spots, flickering lights, and phantom noises. As Louisiana's only executed female inmate, her lingering presence remains a fixture of local lore.

#details
Toni Jo Henry stood trial three times before being sentenced to death by Louisiana's electric chair in November 1942—becoming the first and only woman executed in the state's electric chair, which was housed in the Calcasieu Parish Courthouse in Lake Charles. Ever since her execution, staff and visitors have reported eerie phenomena throughout the building, including sudden cold spots, fluttering lights, mysterious noises, and even the faint scent of burning hair. Local historians and courthouse employees take these unexplained occurrences as the undeniable mark of an infamous outlaw refusing to be silenced.

#location
The Historic Calcasieu Courthouse is located at 320 Division Street in downtown Lake Charles, Louisiana.

#title
The Vengeful Spirit of Chloe at The Myrtles

#summary
An enslaved woman seeking revenge on her master mistakenly poisoned his wife and children with oleander leaves, binding her restless spirit to the historic estate where her ghostly apparitions and handprints are still reported.

#details
According to local legend, an enslaved woman named Chloe, who served as a mistress to judge Clark Woodruff and wore a green turban to hide an ear cut off for eavesdropping, baked a poisonous cake containing oleander juice to win back favor in the household. Instead of her intended target, Woodruff's wife and daughters consumed the cake and died, leading to Chloe's execution by other slaves. Visitors and staff have since reported sightings of a woman in a green turban, unexplained cold spots, and handprints or faces appearing in an antique mirror left uncovered after a death.

#location
The Myrtles Plantation is located at 7747 US Highway 61 in St. Francisville, Louisiana.

#title
The Wandering Sailor of St. Louis Cemetery No. 1

#summary
A 19th-century sailor whose family tomb was sold while he was at sea wanders the labyrinth of New Orleans' oldest cemetery searching for his final resting place.

#details
Henry Vignes was a nomadic 19th-century sailor who entrusted his boarding house owner with his papers and tomb deeds before departing on a voyage. Upon returning to New Orleans, he discovered she had sold his family tomb; shortly thereafter, he fell ill and died, being buried in an unmarked pauper's grave. Visitors to St. Louis Cemetery No. 1 have reported encountering his tall, blue-eyed apparition as he roams the above-ground crypts asking if anyone knows where the Vignes tomb is located.

#location
St. Louis Cemetery No. 1 is located at Basin Street and St. Louis Street in New Orleans, Louisiana.
