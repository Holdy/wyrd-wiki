#title
The General and the Meticulous Dead

#summary
A historic French Quarter mansion once inhabited by Confederate General P.G.T. Beauregard is haunted by phantom soldiers in uniform and the general himself. Witnesses have reported hearing ghostly gunfire and encountering spirits who still roam the rooms searching for lost belongings.

#details
The Beauregard-Keyes House, built in 1826 at Chartres and Ursulines Street, has a storied past marked by Civil War occupation and a violent Sicilian mob shootout. Following the war, General P.G.T. Beauregard lived in the home as a tenant, and author Frances Parkinson Keyes later reported that his ghost still paces the halls at night, purportedly searching for his boots because he was buried in his stocking feet. In addition to the general, witnesses have reported phantom Civil War soldiers in gray and butternut uniforms standing watch or dissolving into mist, accompanied by the lingering sounds and scent of musket fire.

#location
The Beauregard-Keyes House is located at 1113 Chartres Street in the French Quarter of New Orleans, Louisiana.
