#title
The Banshee and the Skeletal Fiddler of Watch Dog Butte

#summary
A wailing spectral woman and her violin-playing skeletal companion haunt a remote South Dakota butte, trapped by a curse that drives unwary listeners to their doom in the rocky canyons.

#details
In the rugged and desolate expanse of the South Dakota Badlands, a ghostly banshee materializes near a landmark known as Watch Dog butte. When travelers or cowboys approach her, she stands in mournful appeal before unhinging her jaw and unleashing a blood-curdling scream that echoes across the canyons. She is frequently accompanied by a skeletal companion who tracks down music, seizing violins and playing haunting melodies through the night. Those mesmerized by the fiddle's tune find themselves compelled to follow the skeletal musician into treacherous rocky drop-offs, meeting their fate at the edge of deep cliffs while the trapped spirits remain bound by their silent curse.

#location
Watch Dog butte within the rugged landscape of the South Dakota Badlands.

#title
The Banshee of the Bad Lands

#summary
Travelers passing Watchdog Butte after dark are haunted by a weeping, phantom woman who vanishes with a chilling shriek. She is frequently accompanied by a ghostly skeleton that lures unsuspecting listeners away with unearthly fiddle music.

#details
In the rugged, labyrinthine expanse of the South Dakota Badlands, near the geographic feature known as Watchdog Butte, terrifying nocturnal apparitions have long been reported. By moonlight, a mournful female banshee is frequently seen standing on a nearby hilltop, her hair blowing and arms tossing in desperate gestures. Whenever travelers, cowboys, or hunters pass the haunted butte at night, the surrounding rocks flash with phosphorescent light and the specter sweeps toward them as if trying to speak. If anyone asks what she wants, she throws her arms aloft and vanishes with a blood-curdling shriek that echoes across the gullies. According to local lore, this weeping spirit is sometimes joined by a glowing, fleshless skeleton that wanders through the ash and clay searching for music. If provided with a violin, the skeleton plays haunting melodies through the night, but anyone foolish enough to follow the spectral musician is led into inescapable rocky pitfalls where their soul is charmed away.

#location
Watchdog Butte is located within the Badlands region of Pennington County, South Dakota.

#upstream-source
Skinner, Charles M. *Myths and Legends of Our Own Land*. Philadelphia: J.B. Lippincott Company, 1896.

#title
The Ghost of Sheriff Seth Bullock

#summary
Guests and staff at a historic Deadwood hotel frequently report eerie encounters with its original builder and first sheriff, who still oversees the property from beyond the grave.

#details
Built in 1895 by Deadwood's first sheriff, Seth Bullock, and his partner Sol Star after a devastating fire destroyed their original hardware store, the Bullock Hotel is widely considered one of South Dakota's most haunted landmarks. Although Bullock passed away in 1919, numerous accounts from employees and visitors suggest he never truly left his post. Staff members have observed that unexplained paranormal activity—such as flying dishes, flickering lights, and activating showers—notably increases whenever workers stand idle, whistle, or hum. Guests have reported feeling unseen taps on their shoulders, hearing male voices call their names in empty hallways, and spotting the tall silhouette of the old lawman wandering the restaurant, basement, and upper-floor corridors.

#location
The Bullock Hotel is located at 633 Main Street in Deadwood, South Dakota.

#title
The Haunted Woodland of Sica Hollow

#summary
Native American folklore warns against a deep, dark woodland canyon where ancient bloodshed and an evil wanderer left behind unquiet spirits, strange lights, and red waters.

#details
Sica Hollow State Park, whose name derives from the Dakota Sioux word *Sica* meaning "evil" or "bad," is steeped in dark legends dating back long before white settlement. According to tribal oral tradition, an evil stranger named Hand lured young braves away from traditional peaceful ways into committing violent acts and bloody battles within the ravine. The bloodshed stained the local stream red—giving the stream its historic name—before divine intervention from Thunderer destroyed Hand and cleansed the valley. To this day, locals and visitors report hearing strange unexplainable voices, seeing mysterious lights flashing in the creek bottoms, and encountering an eerie, unsettling atmosphere throughout the canyon.

#location
Sica Hollow State Park is located about 15 miles northeast of Sisseton in Roberts County, northeastern South Dakota.

Written by Wambdi Wicasa (Father Stanislaus Maudlin) from the American Indian Culture Research Center.

#title
The Lady in White of Room 812

#summary
The gothic Hotel Alex Johnson in Rapid City is haunted by several spectral residents, most notably a tragic bride known as the Lady in White who haunts the eighth floor.

#details
Opened in 1928 and named after its founder Alex Carlton Johnson, the Hotel Alex Johnson has accumulated decades of documented paranormal reports compiled in a front-desk journal titled *The Haunting*. Among its ghostly inhabitants—which include the spirit of Johnson himself and a phantom young girl—the most renowned is the Lady in White. Local lore and guest accounts state that a young woman committed suicide by jumping from the window of room 812 on her wedding night in the 1970s. Visitors staying in room 812 frequently report waking up to find the window mysteriously opened, dresser contents thrown about, and an overwhelming sense of dread. Additionally, night staff and guests report hearing disembodied footsteps in high heels echoing through empty hallways and feeling unseen figures sit on the edge of their beds at night.

#location
The Hotel Alex Johnson is located at 523 Sixth Street in Rapid City, South Dakota.

#title
The Lady in White of Room 812

#summary
Guests and staff at a historic downtown hotel frequently encounter the apparition of a woman who fell to her death from an eighth-floor window in 1970.

#details
Opened in 1928, the Hotel Alex Johnson in Rapid City has long held a reputation as one of the most haunted locations in South Dakota. Its most famous spirit is the "Lady in White," said to be a woman named Shirley who died on August 18, 1970, following a fall from the eighth-floor window of Room 812. Since then, guests staying in Room 812 have reported waking up to mysteriously open windows, rummaged-through dresser drawers, and an overwhelming sense of dread. Witnesses also report seeing a female figure in a white gown wandering the eighth-floor hallways after dark, alongside other paranormal phenomena including a giggling young girl knocking on doors and the lingering presence of hotel founder Alex Johnson.

The Hotel Alex Johnson is located at 523 St. Joseph Street in downtown Rapid City, South Dakota.

#title
The Restless Sheriff of Main Street

#summary
Following his death in 1919, the spirit of Deadwood's first legendary sheriff, Seth Bullock, is said to roam the halls and basement of the historic hotel he built, keeping a watchful eye on staff and guests.

#details
In 1876, Seth Bullock arrived in Deadwood, Dakota Territory, and was soon appointed the camp's first sheriff, successfully establishing law and order in the raucous mining town. After a devastating fire destroyed his hardware store in 1894, Bullock and his business partner Sol Star constructed the luxurious sandstone Bullock Hotel on the same site. Bullock passed away in 1919, but according to numerous accounts from staff and visitors, he never truly left. Witnesses have reported the tall, imposing ghostly figure of Bullock patrolling the hallways, guest rooms, and Seth's Cellar. Unexplained phenomena attributed to his presence include self-operating appliances, shaking plates in the restaurant, phantom footsteps, unplugged alarm clocks ringing on their own, and guests being tapped on the shoulder by unseen hands.

#location
The historic Bullock Hotel is located at 633 Main Street in downtown Deadwood, South Dakota.

#title
The Shadow of Spook Road

#summary
Drivers venturing down a lonely, tree-canopied road at night frequently report being followed by a dark, ghostly figure and spotting apparitions hanging from the trees. According to local lore, the haunted stretch is also tied to occult activity and shifting geography.

#details
Spook Road, officially known as 264th Street, is a narrow, three-mile stretch of dirt road winding through heavily wooded areas south of Brandon, South Dakota. For decades, nighttime drivers have treated traveling the serpentine road as a chilling rite of passage. Legend holds that a dark spirit or shadowy human figure can be seen following motorists as they drive west from the local graveyard. Other terrifying accounts describe ghostly figures hanging from the ancient cottonwood trees overhead. Additionally, drivers report eerie anomalies such as bridges and intersections appearing to change count depending on the direction of travel.

#location
264th Street (commonly known as Spook Road), located just south of Brandon in Splitrock Township, South Dakota.
