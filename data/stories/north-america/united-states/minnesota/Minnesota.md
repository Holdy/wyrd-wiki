#title
Crazy Annie's Bridge

#summary
Local legend claims that a World War I widow drowned her three children in the creek beneath a bridge in Henderson before taking her own life, leaving her grieving and vengeful spirit to haunt the roadway.

#details
According to local folklore in Henderson, Minnesota, a woman named Annie lived in a small home near a creek with her husband and three children during World War I. Following the loss of her husband, overwhelming grief reportedly drove Annie to madness; she drowned her three children in the creek beneath the local bridge and hanged herself from a nearby tree, earning the enduring moniker "Crazy Annie." Ever since, locals and visitors traveling the gravel road toward the bridge have reported chilling paranormal encounters. Witnesses have heard disembodied anguished cries, unexplainable footsteps, and radio static, while others have experienced their car batteries suddenly draining, doors mysteriously locking, or ghostly, bodiless faces and handprints emerging from the darkness.

#location
Crazy Annie's Bridge crossing the creek near Henderson, Minnesota.

#title
The Gangster Ghosts of Wabasha Street Caves

#summary
Carved out of soft sandstone cliffs along the Mississippi River, these subterranean St. Paul caves hide a bloody history of mobster executions and restless spirits.

#details
Originally mined for silica sand and later used as mushroom farms and Prohibition-era speakeasies like the Castle Royal, the Wabasha Street Caves on St. Paul’s West Side were reportedly frequented by notorious gangsters such as John Dillinger and Ma Barker. According to local legend, a card game in the caves' Fireside Room turned deadly when four mobsters were ambushed and executed with a submachine gun, with their bodies supposedly spirited away by corrupt police or rival gang members. Today, visitors and staff report bullet holes still visible in the stone fireplace, along with sightings of phantom dancing couples and a forlorn woman roaming the underground chambers.

#location
The Wabasha Street Caves are located at 215 Wabasha Street South in St. Paul, Minnesota.

#title
The Grieving Mother of Timber Lake Road

#summary
A distraught mother whose children were murdered wanders a dark Minnesota country road searching for their killer. Phantom children and eerie wails are also frequently reported at her nearby gravesite.

#details
According to local folklore, a woman once returned to her home near Timber Lake Road to find that her children had been brutally murdered. Overcome by grief and devastation, she hanged herself. Since that tragic day, her ghostly figure has haunted the roadway, endlessly searching for the person responsible for her children's deaths. Drivers passing through the area have also reported unsettling phenomena near the nearby Colfax Cemetery, including the apparition of ghostly children playing among the headstones, howling dogs, and the sound of a woman wailing in the night.

#location
Timber Lake Road is located near New London, Minnesota, passing right by the Colfax Cemetery.

#title
The Tragic Hauntings of Glensheen Mansion

#summary
This sprawling 20,000-square-foot English-style estate on Lake Superior is shadowed by a dark history of greed, murder, and ghostly encounters.

#details
Built between 1905 and 1908 by iron ore millionaire Chester Congdon, Duluth's magnificent Glensheen Mansion became the site of a notorious double murder on June 27, 1977, when elderly heiress Elisabeth Congdon and her night nurse, Velma Pietila, were brutally killed in a botched inheritance plot. Following the tragedy, staff and visitors have reported numerous chilling experiences throughout the 39-room estate, including sudden cold spots in the bedrooms where the murders occurred, flickering lights, mysterious objects moving on their own, and wispy, shadowy figures gliding through the grand hallways.

#location
Glensheen Mansion is located at 3300 London Road in Duluth, Minnesota.
