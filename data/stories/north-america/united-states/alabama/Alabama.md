#title
Abel's Light

#summary
A grieving father who tragically died while searching for his lost son continues to haunt the roads and woods of an Alabama valley as a spectral lantern light and ghostly horseback rider.

#details
In the early 1900s in Owl's Valley near Lookout Mountain, Alabama, a four-year-old boy named Henry Abel disappeared during a bitter December evening and was never found. Consumed by grief, Henry's father spent months searching the local roads on horseback with a kerosene lantern. In March, his horse returned home alone, and his body was found hanging from a blackjack oak tree just off one of the roads he had scoured. Since that time, during the coldest winter months, a dim yellow spectral light known as Abel's Light can be viewed from the road, accompanied by the sound of horse hoof beats and a distant voice calling out for Henry.

#location
Owl's Valley (Owl's Hollow), situated along the southeastern edge of Lookout Mountain near Turkeytown in Etowah County, Alabama.

#title
Death Lights in the Tower

#summary
Following her husband's fatal fall, a grieving widow met a tragic end in her estate's tower, where phantom lights are said to still flicker over Tuscaloosa.

#details
Built in the 1830s by Dr. John R. Drish and his wife Sarah, the grand Tuscaloosa mansion became shrouded in tragedy after Dr. Drish died following a fall down the stairs in 1867. Sarah held a wake for him in the home's prominent tower. A year later, overwhelmed with grief, Sarah died as well. According to legend, she had requested that candles be lit at her funeral just as they were for her husband's wake, but her dying wish was ignored. Ever since, locals have reported mysterious, unexplainable "death lights" glowing from the empty tower, alongside phantom sightings of Sarah wandering the halls and unexplained cold spots and footsteps.

#location
The Drish House is located at 2300 17th Street in Tuscaloosa, Alabama.

#title
The Curse of Gaston's Gully

#summary
An outraged preacher placed a vengeful curse on the frontier town of Gaston after being tarred and feathered by its residents, prophesying that the earth would swallow the settlement into the mouth of hell. Over time, the town was abandoned and the site became a massive, eroding ravine known as Gaston's Gully.

#details
Founded in 1830 following the removal of the Choctaw Indians, the rough-and-tumble town of Gaston in Sumter County featured saloons and bordellos that attracted frontier riff-raff. An itinerant preacher visited the community to admonish the settlers for their sinful lifestyle, but instead of repenting, the locals tarred and feathered him, parading him down the streets and out of town. As he departed, he screamed a curse upon his tormentors, prophesying that the earth would swallow the town and drag it down into the mouth of hell. Although the town's actual demise was later hastened by a railroad bypass that caused residents to relocate and rename the settlement Ward, and local soils were naturally erodible, the terrifying ravine known as Gaston's Gully remains deeply tied to the preacher's dire curse.

#location
Gaston's Gully is located in Sumter County, Alabama, at the site of the former town of Gaston near present-day Ward.

#title
The Dead Children's Playground at Maple Hill Cemetery

#summary
A playground adjoining a historic cemetery in Huntsville is rumored to come alive at night with the spirits of children who perished during the 1918 Spanish Flu pandemic.

#details
Nestled against the limestone caves and plots of Huntsville's historic Maple Hill Cemetery, a neighborhood playground has earned the grim moniker "Dead Children's Playground." Local legend maintains that after dark—especially around midnight—the spirits of the numerous children claimed by the devastating 1918 Spanish Flu pandemic emerge from the cemetery to play. Passersby and visitors have frequently reported seeing swings moving on their own volition, mysterious orbs floating in the air, and hearing the faint sounds of children laughing and playing in the shadows.

#location
The Dead Children's Playground is located at Maple Hill Park adjacent to Maple Hill Cemetery off McClung Avenue SE in Huntsville, Alabama.

#title
The Face in the Window

#summary
A mysterious facial image is permanently etched into a window pane of the Pickens County Courthouse in Carrollton, Alabama, said to have been seared into the glass by lightning as a man sought by a lynch mob peered outside.

#details
The legend dates back to the late 19th century in Carrollton, Alabama, following the destruction of the Pickens County Courthouse by fire. Henry Wells, a freedman, was accused of setting the blaze. As a hostile lynch mob pursued him, Wells took refuge in the upper levels of the newly constructed courthouse. Peering out a second-story window to watch his pursuers, a sudden flash of lightning struck the glass, etching his likeness permanently into the pane. Despite numerous attempts over the decades to clean, scrape, or replace the glass, the ghostly facial image remained clearly visible, becoming one of Alabama's most enduring and widely visited regional legends.

#location
The Pickens County Courthouse located in Carrollton, Alabama.

#title
The Ghost of Slag Wormwood at Sloss Furnaces

#summary
A sadistic former graveyard shift foreman who died in the blast furnaces in 1906 is said to still haunt the historic industrial complex, harassing and pushing workers.

#details
James "Slag" Wormwood served as a harsh graveyard shift foreman at Sloss Furnaces in Birmingham during the early 1900s, driving his workers relentlessly and overseeing dozens of fatal workplace accidents. In 1906, Wormwood lost his footing and plunged to his death into molten iron ore. Since his demise, numerous night watchmen, supervisors, and workers have reported encounters with his spirit, including being pushed from behind, beaten, or ordered sternly to "get back to work" by a badly burned apparition.

#location
Sloss Furnaces is located at 20 32nd Street North in Birmingham, Alabama.

#title
The Legend of the Dead Children's Playground

#summary
Adjoining Huntsville's historic Maple Hill Cemetery, a small park known as the Dead Children's Playground is said to be haunted by the spirits of children who died during the 1918 Spanish Flu pandemic, with visitors reporting self-moving swings and ghostly apparitions after dark.

#details
Nestled in a low pocket of Huntsville's historic Maple Hill Cemetery—the largest and oldest in Alabama—sits a modest playground surrounded by limestone rocks and trees. Local folklore holds that this park, known as the Dead Children's Playground, is haunted by the spirits of children who perished during the devastating 1918 Spanish Flu pandemic and were buried nearby. After dark, visitors and local teenagers frequently report supernatural activity, including swings moving on their own without wind, floating orbs of light, and the sounds of children laughing and playing in the dead of night. When the city attempted to raze the playground in 2007 to make room for more cemetery plots, public outcry was so intense that a new playground was built in its place, preserving the legendary haunt.

#location
The Dead Children's Playground is located at the end of Newport Drive, just off McClung Avenue SE, adjacent to Maple Hill Cemetery in Huntsville, Alabama.

#title
The Phantoms and Burning Tower of the Drish House

#summary
A historic antebellum mansion in Tuscaloosa is said to be haunted by the tragic spirits of its original owners, including a widow whose ghost supposedly haunts the tower.

#details
Built in the 1830s by Dr. John R. Drish and his wife Sarah, the Tuscaloosa mansion became the site of multiple family tragedies. In 1867, Dr. Drish fell down the stairs to his death while intoxicated, and Sarah held a dramatic wake for him in the third-story tower. A year later, grief-stricken, she died after jumping from that same tower. Over the decades, neighbors and visitors have reported terrifying paranormal phenomena, including the tower appearing engulfed in bright flames with no actual fire present, as well as Sarah wandering the halls in a white dress amid unexplained footsteps and cold spots.

#location
The Historic Drish House is located in Tuscaloosa, Alabama.

#title
The Red Lady of Huntingdon College

#summary
An isolated student who was sent away to college against her will tragically died in her dorm room, leaving behind a lingering spirit that still haunts the historic campus hall.

#details
Martha, a young woman from New York, was unwillingly sent by her parents to Huntingdon College in Montgomery, where she struggled deeply with loneliness and isolation. She became well known on campus for her distinct fondness for the color red, frequently wearing a red robe and decorating her living quarters with red accents. Eventually, the unhappy student was found dead in her fourth-floor dormitory room in Pratt Hall, dressed in her red robe and lying upon a red carpet. Ever since, students and staff have reported eerie encounters and sightings of the "Red Lady" wandering the upper floors of Pratt Hall, tethered forever to the site of her tragic youth.

#location
Pratt Hall at Huntingdon College in Montgomery, Alabama.

#title
The Unquiet Ghost of Gaineswood

#summary
A young woman who passed away far from her Virginia home is said to perpetually haunt an antebellum mansion in Demopolis, making her presence known through phantom piano music.

#details
In the mid-19th century, General Nathan Bryan Whitfield constructed the grand Greek Revival estate known as Gaineswood in Demopolis. During the estate's early years, a young woman named Evelyn Carter worked as a companion and nanny for the household. When Evelyn fell ill and died during the winter, freezing conditions made it impossible to immediately transport her body back to Virginia, resulting in a temporary burial on the grounds. Although her remains were later returned north, her spirit supposedly refused to leave Alabama. Over the decades, visitors and staff have repeatedly reported hearing a piano playing by itself in the music room—even though the physical instrument is badly out of tune and missing keys—accompanied by heavy footsteps and the rustling of period clothing upstairs.

#location
Gaineswood is located in Demopolis, Alabama.

Kathryn Tucker Windham and Margaret Gillis Figh, Thirteen Alabama Ghosts and Jeffrey.
