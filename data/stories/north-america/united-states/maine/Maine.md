#title
The Caretaker of Fort Knox

#summary
Visitors and staff at a historic coastal fortification frequently report strange noises, shadowy figures, and physical touches attributed to a dedicated nineteenth-century ordnance sergeant. Even in death, the spirit appears to maintain his eternal watch over the grounds.

#details
Built in the mid-19th century to protect the Penobscot River Valley, Fort Knox in Prospect, Maine, never actually saw combat during its military history. Despite its peaceful operational record, modern visitors, park staff, and paranormal investigators report numerous uncanny encounters within its granite chambers and tunnels. Witnesses describe hearing unexplained footsteps, heavy breathing, disembodied voices, and laughter, while others report being touched by unseen hands. One of the most prominent apparitions is a figure dressed in a duster coat, widely believed to be Ordnance Sergeant Leopold Hegyi—who served as the fort's sole resident caretaker between 1887 and 1900—still patrolling the grounds and ensuring the post remains secure.

#location
Fort Knox is located at 740 Fort Knox Road in Prospect, Maine, overlooking the Penobscot River.

#title
The Cursed Monument of Colonel Buck

#summary
Following her execution by the town's founder, a condemned woman cursed him to forever bear the mark of her death. Decades later, a boot-shaped stain mysteriously appeared and stubbornly persisted on his granite memorial.

#details
Colonel Jonathan Buck, the founder of Bucksport, Maine, ordered the execution of a woman during the town's early years. As she faced her demise, she laid a curse upon him, vowing that his legacy would forever bear her mark. Seventy-five years after Buck passed away, a tall granite obelisk monument was erected in his honor at the local cemetery. Soon after, a dark silhouette resembling a stocking or a booted foot mysteriously manifested on the stone face directly beneath his name. Despite cleaning, polishing, and even replacing the monument entirely over the years, the stain continually returned in the exact same shape, remaining visible to this day as a testament to her vengeful hex.

#location
Colonel Jonathan Buck's monument stands in the Buck Cemetery located on Main Street in Bucksport, Maine.

#title
The Cursed Monument of Colonel Jonathan Buck

#summary
A mysterious stain resembling a human leg and foot appeared on the memorial of Bucksport's founder, giving rise to a persistent local legend that he was cursed by a woman he condemned to death. 

#details
Colonel Jonathan Buck, founder of Bucksport, Maine, passed away in 1795 and was originally laid to rest with a simple headstone. In 1852, his descendants erected a large granite obelisk to honor his legacy in the family cemetery. As the monument weathered, a dark stain resembling the lower half of a human leg and foot materialized on the stone. Despite multiple attempts to clean or replace the marble, the mark stubbornly reappeared, sparking a local legend. According to the tale, Buck had once sentenced a woman to death for witchcraft, and with her dying breath, she cursed him, vowing that her footprint would permanently mar his monument to expose his cruelty for all time. Although historians note that no witchcraft executions ever occurred in Maine and explain the stain as a natural iron vein in the granite, the chilling legend endures.

#location
The Colonel Jonathan Buck monument stands in the Buck family cemetery adjacent to Route 1 in Bucksport, Maine.

#title
The Ghost of Cling Clang at the Old Stone Store

#summary
An eccentric 19th-century clock repairman known as Cling Clang is said to haunt the historic Old Stone Store in Sullivan Harbor where he once lived and died.

#details
John Cling, nicknamed "Cling Clang" for the loud ringing noise his tin pots and metal stilts made as he leaped across fences and ditches, arrived in Sullivan, Maine from England. He made a living repairing watches and clocks for people along the coast from Bar Harbor to Calais, dressing in discarded salt bags and carrying his tools in a salt bag. During the winter months, Cling Clang lived in an overturned boat situated behind the old stone store in Sullivan Harbor. One cold February, he was found frozen to death underneath his boat. Decades later, after the granite building had served various commercial purposes and eventually became an antique shop and living quarters, the owners and visitors reported recurring supernatural phenomena, including unexplainable ringing doorbells, missing portraits, phantom footsteps, and the gray apparition of a man heading down the stairs.

#location
The Old Stone Store, located in Sullivan Harbor, Maine.

#title
The Haunted Wood Island Light

#summary
Following a fatal 1896 dispute where a tenant shot his landlord before taking his own life inside his dwelling, the ghosts of both men have reportedly haunted the isolated island lighthouse. Visitors and residents have experienced eerie phenomena on the grounds ever since.

#details
Wood Island Light in Saco Bay became the site of a tragedy in 1896 when a violent confrontation erupted between tenant Howard Hobbs and landlord Frederick Milliken. The dispute ended in Milliken's death by gunfire. After speaking with the lighthouse keeper, Thomas H. Orcutt, who urged him to surrender, Hobbs retreated to his dwelling on the island and took his own life. Ever since, local lore and visitor accounts maintain that the ghosts of both the murdered landlord and the troubled tenant haunt the island station.

#location
Wood Island Light is situated on Wood Island in Saco Bay, off the coast of Biddeford, Maine.

#title
The Phantom Bride of Black's Woods Road

#summary
Motorists traveling along a winding stretch of highway at night encounter the spectral figure of a young woman named Catherine, who haunts the roadside seeking help. Those who ignore her face terrifying paranormal disruptions and fatal accidents.

#details
According to local folklore in Down East Maine, a young woman named Catherine perished in a tragic vehicle accident on Route 182—also known as Black's Woods Road—near Fox Pond and Catherine Mountain. Depending on the version of the legend, she died alongside her companion after their vehicle crashed, leaving her either severely disfigured or decapitated. Ever since, her restless ghost has haunted the heavily forested road, appearing as a woman in a white or blue dress standing near the tree line or flagging down passing vehicles for a ride. Motorists who stop often find that she vanishes upon entering the car or discover her headless form in the backseat, while those who refuse to stop or aid her frequently suffer vehicle stalls, electromagnetic anomalies, or fatal car crashes.

#location
The haunting takes place along Route 182 (Black's Woods Road) between the towns of Franklin and Cherryfield in Maine, specifically passing near Fox Pond and Catherine Mountain.

“Dark Woods, Chill Waters: Ghost Tales From Down East Maine” by Marcus LiBrizzi.

#title
The Phantom Piano of Seguin Island Light

#summary
A lighthouse keeper on a remote island murdered his piano-playing wife with an axe after being driven mad by hearing the same tune endlessly repeated. Visitors and keepers since have reported hearing phantom piano music across the water, along with spectral voices and inexplicable locked doors.

#details
Built in 1857, Seguin Light Station on Seguin Island has accumulated numerous ghost stories over the decades. According to local folklore, a mid-19th-century keeper's wife brought a piano to the isolated island to help pass the time, repeatedly playing the same tune until her distraught husband took an axe to the instrument and then to her before taking his own life. On still nights, passing sailors and residents report hearing spectral piano music echoing across the water. Additionally, modern keepers and visitors have reported hearing a young girl laughing and running up the lighthouse steps, being mysteriously locked inside the basement by a latch, and hearing disembodied female voices calling "hello?" across an empty yard.

#location
Seguin Island Light Station is located on Seguin Island, approximately 3 miles off the coast of Georgetown, Maine.

#title
The Specter of Cling Clang

#summary
An eccentric English watch repairman who lived in an overturned boat and died in the 19th century is said to haunt the old stone store in Sullivan Harbor where he once sheltered. Decades later, residents and antique shop owners reported phantom doorbells, disembodied footsteps, and sightings of a gray specter on the stairs.

#details
In the 19th century, a mysterious man known as "Cling Clang" or John Cling arrived in Sullivan, Maine, on a ship from England. Dressed in discarded salt bags and moving on stilts with long poles that made a loud "clang" sound, he traveled the coast repairing watches and clocks. During the winter, he lived in an overturned boat behind the old granite salt store in Sullivan Harbor. One harsh February, he was found frozen to death beneath his boat. In the 1980s, when the building operated as an antique shop, owners Gail and Jim Stamp reported paranormal disturbances including phantom doorbells, glowing lights, disappearing portraits, footsteps, and the gray form of a man descending the stairs.

#location
The Old Stone Store is located in Sullivan Harbor, Maine.

#upstream-source
“Cling Clang” by Chief Stanwood, October 1, 1947, included in *Sullivan and Sorrento Since 1760* by Lelia Clark Johnson, 1953.

#title
The Tragedy and Haunting of Wood Island Light

#summary
Following a violent murder-suicide in 1896, the isolated lighthouse island off the coast of Maine became a hotbed of paranormal activity. Visitors and lighthouse keepers have reported strange moans, locked doors mysteriously jamming, and ghostly apparitions wandering the rocky shores.

#details
On June 1, 1896, a tragic dispute on Wood Island culminated when a local fisherman named Howard Hobbs shot and killed his landlord, Fred Milliken, near the island's quarters before returning to his shack to take his own life. Ever since this violent event, the Wood Island Lighthouse and its surrounding grounds have been reputed as intensely haunted. Subsequent keepers and visitors have reported hearing unexplained moans and weeping, encountering locked doors that refuse to open, hearing footsteps on empty stairs, and witnessing wispy figures moving along the island's shorelines.

#location
The Wood Island Lighthouse is situated on Wood Island in Saco Bay, just over a mile off the coast of Biddeford, Maine.
