#title
The Ghosts of the Historic Occidental Hotel

#summary
Staff and guests at the Historic Occidental Hotel in Buffalo, Wyoming, frequently report supernatural encounters, most notably with the spirit of a young girl named Emily who died of cholera in the early 1900s.

#details
Built in 1880 in Buffalo, Wyoming, the Historic Occidental Hotel hosted legendary figures of the Old West such as Buffalo Bill Cody, Calamity Jane, and Teddy Roosevelt. Over the decades, it has also gained a well-documented reputation for ghostly activity. The most famous resident spirit is Emily, the young daughter of a former resident who tragically died of cholera in the early 1900s. Visitors and staff have reported hearing her laughter, hearing a ball bouncing down the hallways, and seeing her peek out from behind doors. To keep her spirit content, staff keep a stuffed orange tabby cat—matching the cat Emily once owned—near the room where she passed away, and visiting children frequently leave handwritten notes for her. 

Beyond Emily, guests and employees have experienced numerous other unexplained phenomena throughout the building. Visitors staying in the hotel's rooms have reported phantom scents of perfume, witnessed heavy antique irons moving across floors on their own, and even felt an unseen presence slide into bed between couples at night. Staff members working in the bar and restaurant have reported wine bottles and glasses inexplicably shattering or popping their corks, phantom footsteps pacing empty corridors, and disembodied voices calling out names in deserted rooms.

#location
The Historic Occidental Hotel is located at 10 North Main Street in Buffalo, Wyoming.

#title
The Green Parka Phantom of Highway 287

#summary
A woman driving late at night near Jeffrey City repeatedly encounters a mysterious man in a heavy coat who seemingly teleports alongside the highway before stepping directly into the path of her car. Local legend indicates this apparition has haunted the route since the era of stagecoaches.

#details
While driving home from Denver late at night, a woman passing through Jeffrey City noticed a dark, hunched figure walking through the sagebrush toward the road. Approximately half a mile down the highway, she spotted the same man again, now closer and clearly wearing a large green parka. After traveling another half mile, she reached over to wake her passenger to share driving duties; as her friend stirred, the identical man materialized right at the edge of the road, stepping across the white line beside their moving vehicle. Terrified, they accelerated away and later learned that encounters with this hitchhiking phantom have been reported along the route since stagecoach days.

#location
Highway 287 just outside Jeffrey City, Wyoming, along the historic route over South Pass.

#title
The Haunting of the Irma Hotel

#summary
Founded in 1902 by Buffalo Bill Cody in Cody, Wyoming, the historic Irma Hotel is haunted by a vanishing gentleman in a fedora and a restless soldier in Room 35.

#details
Named after Buffalo Bill’s youngest daughter, the Irma Hotel in Cody, Wyoming, has welcomed guests for over a century—some of whom apparently never checked out. Restaurant staff have repeatedly encountered a well-dressed gentleman in a fedora sitting patiently in booth three, only to vanish completely when approached to take an order. Meanwhile, guests staying in Room 35 (the Paul Stock Room) have reported sightings of a man in a U.S. military uniform. Local archival research uncovered a historical precedent matching this apparition: a 1912 newspaper account detailing a soldier named Frank Cunningham who experienced severe hallucinations and shot up that very room.

#location
The Irma Hotel is located at 1192 Sheridan Ave, Cody, Wyoming.

#title
The Legend of Rosie at the Plains Hotel

#summary
Following a tragic shooting in the early 1900s involving a jealous newlywed bride, her unfaithful groom, and a mysterious woman, the historic Plains Hotel has remained haunted by their restless spirits.

#details
Opened in 1911 in downtown Cheyenne, Wyoming, the Plains Hotel quickly became a premier destination for travelers on the Western frontier. Alongside its reputation for luxury and architecture, a enduring local legend took root concerning a newlywed bride named Rosie. According to local lore, Rosie and her husband were staying at the hotel on their honeymoon when she discovered him in the lounge with another woman. After following them upstairs, Rosie allegedly shot both her husband and his companion in a fit of rage before returning to her room to take her own life. Ever since, staff and guests have reported persistent paranormal phenomena throughout the building, including disembodied crying and laughter echoing from Room 444, the apparition of Rosie wandering the fourth-floor corridors in a blue dress, and sightings of the other woman and the groom on different floors of the hotel.

#location
The Plains Hotel is located at 1600 Capitol Avenue in downtown Cheyenne, Wyoming.

#title
The Legend of the Pie Lady and Hauntings at the Wyoming Frontier Prison

#summary
Opening in 1901 in Rawlins, Wyoming, the state's first penitentiary is plagued by spectral voices, wet footprints in dry showers, and the tragic urban legend of the "Pie Lady."

#details
The Wyoming Frontier Prison in Rawlins opened its doors in December 1901 and housed over 13,000 inmates throughout its 80-year history, witnessing harsh punishments, hangings, and executions in its gas chamber. Among its many ghost stories—such as disembodied crying in the abandoned showers and shadow figures on death row—is the famous urban legend of the "Pie Lady." According to local lore, a kind Rawlins woman regularly baked pies for the inmates. After being paroled, one inmate allegedly raped and murdered her on the outside, leading fellow inmates to enact swift vigilante justice by hanging him from the second-floor balcony. Visitors and paranormal investigators have reported seeing and hearing remnants of these dark events within the prison blocks.

#location
The Wyoming Frontier Prison is located at 500 Walnut St, Rawlins, Wyoming.

#title
The Spirits of Fort Laramie

#summary
Park rangers and visitors at Fort Laramie in southeastern Wyoming report uncanny encounters with spectral entities, including mysterious fragrances, moving objects, and legendary phantoms.

#details
Fort Laramie, established as a major fur trading post and military garrison on the Oregon Trail in Goshen County, Wyoming, has witnessed over a century and a half of history—and lingering supernatural energy. Staff members and visitors have reported numerous ghostly phenomena across the historic grounds, such as coins balancing themselves on open cash boxes in empty rooms, footsteps marching across second-story floors, and sudden, potent floral scents filling mid-19th-century officers' quarters. Among the most enduring local legends is the "Lady in Green," a young woman who allegedly disappeared on horseback in the 1840s and is said to reappear near the fort periodically.

#location
Fort Laramie National Historic Site is situated at the confluence of the Laramie and North Platte rivers in Goshen County, southeastern Wyoming.
