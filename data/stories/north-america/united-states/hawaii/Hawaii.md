#title
Pele's Curse

#summary
Visitors who remove volcanic rocks or sand from the Hawaiian Islands are believed to be plagued by persistent bad luck, accidents, and financial ruin until the items are returned to their origin. Countless tourists mail pilfered stones back to park staff each year in an attempt to lift the curse.

#details
According to island folklore, Pele—the fiery Hawaiian goddess of volcanoes—protects her home and fiercely guards its natural elements. Anyone who removes lava rocks, sand, or pumice as souvenirs from sacred sites such as Kilauea Crater is said to incur her wrath. Those affected frequently report a cascade of varied misfortunes, ranging from failed marriages and car crashes to sudden illnesses and financial disasters. The only known remedy to appease the goddess and break the hex is to mail the stolen stones back to Hawaii Volcanoes National Park or return them to the exact location from which they were taken.

#location
Kilauea Crater is located within Hawaii Volcanoes National Park on the Big Island of Hawaii.

#title
The 1947 Pali Pork Apparition

#summary
In April 1947, five individuals who drove to the Nuuanu Pali Lookout at midnight carrying raw pork and fish to test a local taboo reported witnessing a shifting human and animal apparition before fleeing as an overwhelming odor of decay filled the air.

#details
According to historical accounts from April 1947, a group of five men and women arrived at Honolulu police headquarters at 12:45 AM to report a terrifying supernatural encounter at the Nuuanu Pali Lookout. Intrigued by the long-standing Hawaiian legend that bringing pork—the spiritual embodiment of the pig-god Kamapuaʻa—over the Pali from the windward to the leeward side brings misfortune or supernatural wrath, the group decided to test the superstition. They drove to the lookout at midnight, parking their car near the monument while carrying raw pork and fish. Shortly after arriving, a large figure of a man appeared sitting on the side of the hill with his arms locked over his knees. Every time they flashed their vehicle's headlights, the figure moved, and when the lights were turned off, it reappeared in a different spot before seemingly shifting shape into an animal in the pitch-dark night. Overcome with fear, they tossed the pork and fish out of the car, instantly triggering an overpowering stench of death and decomposition that filled the surrounding air.

#location
The Nuuanu Pali Lookout off the Pali Highway on the island of Oahu, Hawaii.

#upstream-source
Adapted from 1947 newspaper reports and historical archives of Honolulu police records.

#title
The Apparition of Kiona‘ole Road

#summary
A ghost tour guide on Oahu's closed Kiona‘ole Road encounters the spectral figure of a young girl standing by a stunted banyan tree, later identified from historical records as a 1975 murder victim.

#details
While leading a ghost tour along the deserted stretch of Kiona‘ole Road (formerly Old Pali Road) near a stunted banyan tree, a tour guide noticed a fair-skinned girl wearing octagonal glasses and a knitted shawl standing quietly next to the tree. Assuming she was a late tour guest, the guide finished group prayers, only to discover moments later that no such person was with the group or on the bus. After a guest mentioned the tragic 1975 murder of 13-year-old Dawn "Dede" Bustamante—who was kidnapped and killed near that very road—the guide viewed an archival photograph of the victim and recognized her face as the exact apparition seen by the tree.

#location
Along Kiona‘ole Road (formerly Old Pali Road) near the stunted banyan tree and Pali Golf Course on the island of Oahu, Hawaii.

#title
The Ghostly Guest of the Moana Surfrider
#summary
Waikiki's historic Moana Surfrider hotel is haunted by the tragic spirit of Jane Stanford, who was poisoned there in 1905, alongside other spectral residents roaming its grand halls.
#details
Opened in 1901 as Waikiki's first grand beachfront resort, the Moana Surfrider gained a dark reputation following the mysterious death of Jane Stanford, co-founder of Stanford University, who died in her hotel room from strychnine poisoning in February 1905. Guests and staff have frequently reported sightings of a woman in Victorian-era clothing resembling Stanford in her former room, as well as an old-fashioned young girl wandering the grand staircase and a military officer near the veranda. Unexplained footsteps, cold spots, disembodied voices, and whispering banyan trees in the courtyard continue to be reported at the historic property.
#location
The Moana Surfrider Hotel at 2365 Kalakaua Avenue in Waikiki, Honolulu, Oahu, Hawaii.

#title
The Haunting of the Kasha House in Kaimuki
#summary
A home in the Kaimuki neighborhood of Honolulu became the center of terror in 1942 when a demonic feline spirit from Japanese folklore reportedly attacked a family and later hunted visitors for decades.
#details
In August 1942, Honolulu police responded to a chaotic disturbance call at a two-story home in the Kaimuki neighborhood after neighbors heard terrified shrieks. Inside, a young boy and his sisters were allegedly being strangled by an unseen entity, which the mother blamed on her estranged husband. Decades later in 1972, a police officer witnessed a young woman being attacked by an invisible assailant outside the same property, an assault so violent it choked her and stalled the patrol car until a sergeant used Hawaiian salt and water to repel the spirit. The house was eventually demolished in 2016, but local lore maintains the entity remains bound to the location.
#location
The former house was located near the intersection of 8th Avenue and Harding Avenue in the Kaimuki neighborhood of Honolulu, Oahu, Hawaii.

The Headless Spectre and Ancient Guardians of Nu'uanu Pali Drive

#summary
Nu'uanu Pali Drive and the Pali Highway are said to be haunted by ancient battle casualties, fierce guardian deities, and the tragic apparition of a teenage girl whose severed head terrified motorists.

#details
The winding pass of Nu'uanu Pali on Oahu was the site of the brutal 1795 Battle of Nu'uanu, where Kamehameha I's forces drove hundreds of opposing warriors over the cliff edge to their deaths. In addition to the restless spirits of fallen warriors, local folklore tells of contemporary tragedies, such as Cassie, a teenage girl who died by suicide off Nu'uanu Pali Drive; her severed head was infamously discovered grinning outside a car window. The highway is also said to be guarded by ancient deities and serpent-like mo'o, with locals warning travelers never to bring pork products into the area lest they burst into flames or anger the volcano goddess Pele.

Nu'uanu Pali Drive and the Pali Highway winding through the Ko'olau mountain range on Oahu, Hawaii.

#title
The Legend and True History of Morgan's Corner

#summary
Morgan's Corner on Nuʻuanu Pali Drive in Honolulu is famous for spine-chilling urban legends of a hanging body, but it is also the site of a brutal 1948 murder that ultimately led to the abolition of the death penalty in Hawaiʻi.

#details
Morgan's Corner is a sharp hairpin turn on Nuʻuanu Pali Drive in Honolulu, named after Dr. James Morgan who resided in the area during the 1920s and 1940s. Local folklore has long warned teenagers and night-time visitors about a classic urban legend: a couple whose car breaks down beneath an old tree, after which the boyfriend goes for help and is later discovered hanging from the branches, his fingertips scratching against the car's roof in the wind. Beyond the spooky campfire tales, however, the location was the actual site of a horrific crime. On March 11, 1948, two escaped convicts, James Majors and John Palakiko, broke into the home of 68-year-old widow Therese Adele Wilder at 3939 Pali Road and murdered her during a robbery. The ensuing sensational trial, public outrage, and subsequent legal battles led by attorney Harriet Bouslog drew immense international attention. The controversy eventually prompted Governor Samuel W. King to commute the men's death sentences to life in prison, and in 1957, the Hawaiian legislature officially abolished capital punishment entirely.

#location
Morgan's Corner is located at the prominent hairpin turn on Nuʻuanu Pali Drive in Honolulu, Oahu, Hawaii.

#summary

Security guards and visitors have repeatedly reported seeing the ghostly figure of Queen Liliʻuokalani walking the palace grounds around 5:30 in the morning or peering out from the second-floor bedroom where she was imprisoned following the 1893 overthrow. Witnesses have also reported hearing a piano mysteriously playing in the Blue Room, disembodied Hawaiian chanting in the upper chambers, and restless spirits residing among the ancient banyan trees planted on the palace grounds by Queen Kapiʻolani.

#title
The Royal Phantoms of ‘Iolani Palace

#summary
As the only royal palace in the United States, downtown Honolulu's ‘Iolani Palace is haunted by phantom intruders, guards firing at invisible soldiers, and the spectral forms of King Kamehameha III and his headless ancestor.

#details
Built in downtown Honolulu, ‘Iolani Palace is steeped in royal history and supernatural lore. In 1893, guards stationed near the palace grounds reported firing at mysterious intruders who vanished without a trace, confounding local authorities. Decades later, State Archives documented numerous accounts of ethereal visitations on the palace grounds. Witnesses told of colossal, thunderous footsteps striking the locked iron front gates, causing them to swing open to reveal the formidable figure of the warrior Iwikauikaua carrying his own head under his arm, followed by King Kamehameha III marching up the palace steps to protect and guide the realm.

The Specters and Curses of Nuʻuanu Pali
#summary
The historic Nuʻuanu Pali Lookout is plagued by the ghosts of ancient warriors, an apparition seeking unfaithful partners, and a strict taboo against carrying pork across the pass.
#details
Following the bloody 1795 Battle of Nuʻuanu where hundreds of warriors were driven off the sheer cliffs by King Kamehameha I, the Pali Lookout became notorious for supernatural encounters. Visitors and ghost tour groups frequently report ghostly apparitions, disembodied voices, and a tragic spirit of a high school girl who allegedly died by suicide and now calls out the names of unfaithful men near the edge of the cliff. Additionally, local legend warns against carrying pork products across the pass between the windward and leeward sides, as it is believed to anger the pig god Kamapuaʻa and volcano goddess Pele, frequently resulting in mysterious car stalls and inexplicable phenomena.

#title
The Spectral Guest of the Moana Surfrider

#summary
Following her tragic and unsolved poisoning in 1905, the co-founder of Stanford University, Jane Stanford, is said to haunt her former room and hallways at the Moana Surfrider Hotel in Waikiki.

#details
Opened in 1901 as Waikiki's first grand resort, the Moana Hotel (now the Moana Surfrider) became the site of a dark mystery in February 1905 when Jane Lathrop Stanford checked in to escape the cold California winter. On February 28, 1905, she fell violently ill and passed away from strychnine poisoning in her hotel room. Though the murder was never solved, her spirit reportedly never left. Guests and staff have frequently reported encounters with a woman in Victorian-era clothing wandering the halls and an inexplicable chill in the rooms. Additionally, visitors beneath the historic courtyard banyan tree—planted in 1904—have reported hearing disembodied whispers and laughter.

#location
The Moana Surfrider Hotel located on Kalakaua Avenue in Waikiki Beach, Honolulu, Oahu, Hawaii.
