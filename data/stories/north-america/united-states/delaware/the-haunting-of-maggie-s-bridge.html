The Haunting of Maggie's Bridge

Summary

In late 19th-century Delaware, a young woman named Maggie Bloxom was tragically decapitated when her horse and carriage plunged off a bridge into a branch of the Nanticoke River. Ever since, visitors who call out her name at midnight can summon her headless spirit from the surrounding woods.

Details

Sometime in the late 1800s, a young woman named Maggie Bloxom was driving her horse and carriage down Woodland Church Road, roughly a mile south of the Woodland Ferry landing. As she crossed the small bridge spanning a branch of the Nanticoke River, her horse became violently spooked, rearing up and plunging the carriage over the side into the water below. In the horrific accident, Maggie was decapitated. Local legend dictates that if someone visits the bridge at midnight and calls out her name three times, they will hear the phantom sound of horse hooves, see a shadow emerge from the woods, and ultimately watch Maggie appear without her head, desperately wanting to be reconnected to it. Witnesses have also reported various unexplained phenomena around the site, including cars stalling or shifting out of gear, and mysterious flashing lights resembling fireflies drifting from the nearby woods.
