#title
Maggie's Bridge

#summary
The headless ghost of a young woman named Maggie haunts a secluded bridge and surrounding woodlands in Seaford, Delaware. Visitors frequently report shadowy figures and unexplained orbs in this eerie area.

#details
In the late 19th century, a young woman named Maggie Bloxom suffered a tragic accident resulting in her decapitation on a small bridge in Seaford. Ever since, her restless spirit has been said to linger around the crossing, which is now widely known as Maggie's Bridge. Paranormal investigators and locals frequently report shadowy figures moving through the adjacent woods, and photographs taken at the site often capture unexplained orbs in the darkness.

#location
Maggie's Bridge crosses a small creek on Woodland Church Road (Route 78) in Seaford, Delaware.

#title
The Curse of the Deer Park Tavern

#summary
After taking an unfortunate tumble into the mud outside St. Patrick's Inn, author Edgar Allan Poe allegedly laid a curse upon the historic building in Newark.

#details
In the mid-19th century, renowned author Edgar Allan Poe arrived by carriage on a rainy night at St. Patrick's Inn in Newark. As he stepped down from the vehicle, he tripped and fell face-first into the mud outside the entrance. Furious over the mishap, Poe reportedly cursed the establishment, declaring that anyone who entered its doors would be compelled to return. Although the original log structure burned down in 1851 and was subsequently replaced by a brick hotel that eventually became the Deer Park Tavern, local legend maintains that Poe's hex endures alongside the spirits of travelers, surveyors, and guests from centuries past.

#location
Deer Park Tavern located at the corner of New London Road and West Main Street in Newark, Delaware.

#title
The Haunting of Maggie's Bridge

#summary
In late 19th-century Delaware, a young woman named Maggie Bloxom was tragically decapitated when her horse and carriage plunged off a bridge into a branch of the Nanticoke River. Ever since, visitors who call out her name at midnight can summon her headless spirit from the surrounding woods.

#details
Sometime in the late 1800s, a young woman named Maggie Bloxom was driving her horse and carriage down Woodland Church Road, roughly a mile south of the Woodland Ferry landing. As she crossed the small bridge spanning a branch of the Nanticoke River, her horse became violently spooked, rearing up and plunging the carriage over the side into the water below. In the horrific accident, Maggie was decapitated. Local legend dictates that if someone visits the bridge at midnight and calls out her name three times, they will hear the phantom sound of horse hooves, see a shadow emerge from the woods, and ultimately watch Maggie appear without her head, desperately wanting to be reconnected to it. Witnesses have also reported various unexplained phenomena around the site, including cars stalling or shifting out of gear, and mysterious flashing lights resembling fireflies drifting from the nearby woods.

#location
Maggie's Bridge spans a branch of the Nanticoke River on Woodland Church Road, approximately one mile south of the Woodland Ferry landing near the village of Woodland, west of Seaford, Delaware.

#title
The Headless Soldier of Cooch’s Bridge

#summary
Cooch’s Bridge in Newark, Delaware, the site of a fierce Revolutionary War battle, is said to be haunted by a soldier who lost his head to a cannonball.

#details
On September 3, 1777, Cooch’s Bridge was the site of the only major Revolutionary War battle fought on Delaware soil. According to local folklore, the violent clash left behind residual energy and restless spirits, most notably a headless soldier who was decapitated during the cannon fire. Passersby and locals have shared tales of encountering the phantom soldier wandering the historic bridge and surrounding area after dark.

#location
Cooch’s Bridge crossing in Newark, Delaware.

#title
The Phantoms of Fort Delaware

#summary
Built in the 19th century and used as a brutal Civil War prison camp, Fort Delaware on Pea Patch Island is widely reported to be haunted by the spirits of Confederate prisoners and a mysterious "blue man."

#details
Serving as a coastal defense fortress and later a massive prison camp housing nearly 13,000 Confederate prisoners at once—with thousands dying under harsh conditions—Fort Delaware on Pea Patch Island has accumulated decades of ghost lore. Paranormal investigators and visitors have reported disembodied footsteps, doors opening and closing on their own, chilling whispers, and the apparition of a "blue man" captured in photographs within the historic brick barracks.

#location
Fort Delaware State Park on Pea Patch Island in the Delaware River.

#title
The Powdered Wig of Woodburn Mansion

#summary
The official Delaware governor's residence in Dover, known as Woodburn, has been haunted since the early 19th century by the ghost of its original builder, Charles Hillyard III.

#details
Built in the late 18th century, Woodburn has served as the home for Delaware's governors and carries a long-standing tradition of spectral visits. The haunting dates back to around 1815, when a guest reported encountering a phantom man dressed in a powdered wig, knee britches, and a ruffled shirt, identified as the home's builder, Charles Hillyard III. Over the subsequent two centuries, various visitors and residents—including a governor's wife who reportedly left out wine for him—have continued to experience his spectral presence in the historic halls.

#location
Woodburn, the governor's mansion located on Kings Highway in Dover, Delaware.

#title
The Spirits of Pea Patch Island

#summary
Fort Delaware, a former 19th-century military fortification and Civil War prison camp on Pea Patch Island, is widely reputed to be haunted by the restless spirits of prisoners and guards.

#details
Constructed in the 19th century to guard the ports of Wilmington and Philadelphia, Fort Delaware famously served as a grim prisoner-of-war camp for Confederate soldiers during the American Civil War. Due to harsh overcrowding and poor conditions, thousands of men suffered and perished on the grounds. Over the decades following its abandonment and conversion into a state park, visitors and paranormal investigators have reported frequent supernatural phenomena. Encounters include phantom footsteps echoing through empty stone corridors, self-opening doors, shadowy figures, and sightings of a mysterious "blue man" spectral apparition captured in doorways and photographs.

#location
Fort Delaware State Park on Pea Patch Island in the Delaware River.

#title
The Tippling Ghost of Woodburn Mansion

#summary
Woodburn Mansion, the historic governor's residence in Dover, Delaware, is haunted by a playful spirit known as the "tippling ghost" who has a penchant for helping himself to guests' alcoholic drinks.

#details
Built in 1798, Woodburn Mansion has served as the official home of Delaware's governors since 1965. Over the decades, residents, staff, and visitors have reported numerous unexplainable occurrences within its historic walls. Among the most famous spirits said to inhabit the estate is the "tippling ghost," an entity believed to be a former occupant or guest with a lifelong love for spirits. According to local folklore and accounts archived by the state, this phantom is known to sample drinks left out for guests, rearranging glasses or leaving empty bottles in rooms where no one has been. Beyond the tippling ghost, footsteps, shadowy figures, and unexplainable cold spots have frequently been reported throughout the historic mansion, cementing its reputation as one of the First State's most enduring haunted landmarks.

#location
Woodburn Mansion is located at 151 Kings Highway in Dover, Delaware.

Caroline Woods, _Haunted Delaware_.
