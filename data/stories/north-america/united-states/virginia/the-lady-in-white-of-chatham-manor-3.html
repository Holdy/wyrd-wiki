The Lady in White of Chatham Manor

Summary

Sent away from England by her father to sever a forbidden romance, a young woman was met instead by George Washington when she attempted to elope, and her tragic spirit now walks the grounds of Chatham Manor.

Details

Built in 1771 by William Fitzhugh, Chatham Manor in Fredericksburg, Virginia, is said to be haunted by a spirit known as "The Lady in White." According to local legend, a young English woman was sent to the plantation by her father to break off her relationship with a man considered beneath her social class. Unknown to her father, the young man followed her to Virginia and arranged an elopement. However, a servant overheard their plans and reported them directly to General George Washington, who was visiting the estate. On the night of the escape, the young woman climbed out her bedroom window only to find George Washington waiting for her rather than her lover. She was forcibly shipped back to England, where she died of a broken heart on June 21, 1790. Vowing to return, her glowing apparition in a colonial-style gown is said to walk the river path at Chatham Manor every seventh year on the anniversary of her passing.
