#title
The Curse of the Coffin

#summary
An old poor farm coffin retrieved as a theater prop is believed to be cursed, bringing cold chills, autonomous movement, and sightings of an elderly gentleman wandering backstage. Contacting the artifact is said to afflict individuals with bodily harm.

#details
During the 18th and 19th centuries, the Wise County Poor Farm in southwest Virginia sheltered the destitute, where expensive coffins were borrowed for funeral services at Crockett Hall and subsequently stored in the cellar while bodies were buried directly in shrouds. In 1975, a theater professor at the University of Virginia's College at Wise retrieved one of these historic coffins from the cellar to use as a stage prop. Shortly after bringing the box into the theater department, students and faculty frequently reported seeing an eerie, elderly gentleman wandering the backstage hallways. The coffin, frequently referred to simply as "the Box," appeared to move on its own, fostering a enduring belief among staff that anyone who comes into physical contact with the artifact risks being afflicted with bodily harm by a lingering spirit from the old poor farm.

#location
Crockett Hall is located on the campus of the University of Virginia's College at Wise in Wise County, Virginia.

#title
The Female Stranger of Gadsby's Tavern

#summary
In 1816, a mysterious veiled woman died of an illness at Alexandria's City Hotel after arriving from the Caribbean with a secretive companion, and her ghost continues to haunt the historic building.

#details
In October 1816, a mysterious couple arrived by ship from the Caribbean in Alexandria, Virginia, renting a room at the City Hotel (now known as Gadsby’s Tavern). The woman was desperately ill and wore a black veil, while her male companion refused to reveal their identities to local doctors or nurses, making everyone swear oaths of secrecy. Three weeks later, on October 14, 1816, the woman died. Her companion arranged for her burial at St. Paul’s Cemetery under the poignant moniker "The Female Stranger" before abruptly leaving town without paying outstanding debts. Ever since, staff and visitors have reported paranormal phenomena throughout Gadsby’s Tavern, including full-bodied apparitions, phantom crying from Room 8, objects moving on their own, and lanterns mysteriously heating up as if just blown out.

#location
Gadsby's Tavern is located at 134 North Royal Street in Alexandria, Virginia.

#title
The Ghost Bride of Crawford Road

#summary
Drivers traveling along Crawford Road frequently report encountering the apparition of a bride hanging from or standing atop the Historical Tour Road overpass. According to legend, the spirit is that of a reluctant bride who fled her wedding and took her own life at the bridge.

#details
Crawford Road in Yorktown, Virginia, is notorious for a chilling legend involving a ghost bride. As the story goes, a young woman who was forced into an unwanted marriage fled to the roadway and hanged herself from the Historical Tour Road overpass. Ever since, motorists passing beneath the bridge have reported unsettling phenomena, including car batteries mysteriously dying. Drivers who look in their rearview mirrors often see the ghostly figure of the bride swinging back and forth, and some have even heard a terrifying thud on the roof of their vehicle as if she had fallen onto it.

#location
The Historical Tour Road overpass bridging across Crawford Road in Yorktown, Virginia.

#title
The Grey Lady of Historic Tuckahoe

#summary
A woman forced into an unhappy marriage after being separated from her true love haunts the boxwood path of her historic childhood home.

#details
Historic Tuckahoe, located outside Richmond, Virginia, is haunted by the ghost of Mary Randolph, known as the Grey Lady. Mary had fallen in love beneath her social standing, prompting her powerful family to drag her back from New England and force her into marriage with a clergyman, driving her mad. Visitors and staff have reported seeing her spectral figure with blurred features pacing the long boxwood pathway—dubbed the "Ghost Walk"—often accompanied by phantom children's laughter when no children are present. Other paranormal phenomena at the estate include spinning chandeliers and phantom party sounds echoing through the halls at midnight.

#location
Historic Tuckahoe is located at 12101 Tuckahoe Plantation Lane in Richmond, Virginia.

#title
The Lady in White of Chatham Manor

#summary
A young Englishwoman sent away by her father to prevent her from eloping with her true love instead encountered George Washington at a Virginia plantation, inspiring a centuries-old ghostly legend. Her apparition is said to return every seven years on the anniversary of her death.

#details
In the late 18th century, a wealthy English girl was sent by her disapproving father to stay at Chatham Manor in Virginia to break off her relationship with a gentleman he deemed beneath her station. Unbeknownst to her father, the young man followed her to America and arranged to elope with her. However, a servant overheard their plans and informed another prominent guest at the manor: General George Washington. On the night of the intended elopement, as the young woman climbed out her bedroom window, she was met not by her lover, but by Washington, who ensured she was shipped back to England to marry someone matching her social class. Heartbroken and unable to forget her true love, she died on June 21, 1790, and reportedly vowed to return. Legend holds that her glowing, spectral figure in a colonial-style gown walks the river path at Chatham Manor every seventh year on June 21 between midday and midnight.

#location
Chatham Manor is located at 120 Chatham Lane in Fredericksburg, Virginia, situated along the Rappahannock River.

#title
The Legend of Witches Pond

#summary
Local folklore holds that an 18th-century woman accused of witchcraft was executed and thrown into a Stafford County swamp, where her restless spirit and eerie phenomena continue to manifest. Visitors report hearing unearthly shrieks in the woods and seeing a shadowy figure hovering above the water.

#details
For generations, residents in Widewater, Virginia, have whispered about Witches Pond, a murky body of water hidden in the woods near a colonial-era cemetery off U.S. 1 and Telegraph Road. According to regional legend, an 18th-century woman named Edith was accused of witchcraft, put to death, and tossed into the water. Lore claims the pond turns blood red in the spring and that Edith's restless spirit haunts the surrounding trails. Over the years, thrill-seekers and visitors have reported hearing blood-curdling shrieks that grow closer in the dark, witnessing a shadowy figure hovering just above the water, and feeling an oppressive presence. While the supernatural stories are largely local myth, the woods hold a genuine grim history: in November 1998, the skeletal remains of an unidentified woman were discovered just 400 feet from the pond, cementing the area's eerie reputation in both folklore and real-world mystery.

#location
Witches Pond is located in the wooded area behind the Brent Family Cemetery off U.S. 1 and Telegraph Road in Widewater, Stafford County, Virginia.

#title
The Richmond Vampire of the Church Hill Tunnel

#summary
Following a catastrophic tunnel collapse in 1925, witnesses reported seeing a fanged, decomposed creature emerge from the rubble and take refuge inside a mausoleum at Hollywood Cemetery.

#details
The legend of the Richmond Vampire is rooted in the tragic Church Hill Tunnel collapse in Richmond, Virginia, on October 2, 1925, where a crew working to clear an unstable abandoned tunnel was buried under falling bricks. Amidst the chaos and rescue efforts, survivors and witnesses claimed that a tall, partially decomposed man with blood dripping from his mouth and sharp fangs emerged from the collapsed tunnel. Calmly ignoring the disaster, the creature fled toward the nearby Hollywood Cemetery and entered the locked tomb of W.W. Pool. Shocked workers who followed found the heavy crypt door locked from the inside, or reportedly witnessed the casket lid closing on its own. Local lore connects the mysterious crypt—which lacks a birth or death date for Pool—to this legendary vampire, who is said to still lurk within the cemetery's historic grounds.

#location
The Church Hill Tunnel is situated beneath Church Hill in Richmond, Virginia, near Hollywood Cemetery.

#title
The Spirit of Virginia Croxton at the Essex Inn

#summary
An elderly resident who lived eighty years in a 19th-century mansion continues to share a guest room with overnight visitors long after her death.

#details
The Essex Inn in Tappahannock, Virginia, built in 1850, is famously haunted by Virginia Scott Croxton, a woman who lived in the mansion for all of her eighty years before passing away in 1939 in what is now Elisabeth's Room. Guests staying in the room have repeatedly reported feeling an invisible presence slide under the bedsheets and lie beside them, mimicking the motion of a spouse. Innkeepers and housekeepers also report rocking chairs moving by themselves, tools rearranging overnight, and piano keys playing unassisted in the parlor.

#location
The Essex Inn is located at 203 Water Lane in Tappahannock, Virginia.
