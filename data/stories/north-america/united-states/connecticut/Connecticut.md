#title
The Bride of Whirlwind Road

#summary
A jilted bride who drowned herself in the 1930s haunts Whirlwind Road, appearing soaked on the roadside before vanishing into thin air.

#details
In the 1930s, a young woman was devastated after being left at the altar by her fiancé. Overcome by heartbreak, she ended her life by drowning. Her spirit now haunts Whirlwind Road in Wallingford, Connecticut, where drivers frequently spot her standing in the dark, entirely soaked and appearing in distress. Those who slow down or look away find that she disappears instantly, leaving behind an eerie presence that has startled motorists and local police alike.

#location
Whirlwind Road is located in the town of Wallingford, Connecticut.

#title
The Haunting of the Historic Griswold Inn

#summary
Guests and staff at the centuries-old Griswold Inn in Essex have reported persistent paranormal activity, including spectral apparitions, disembodied footsteps, and moving objects.

#details
Operating continuously since 1776 in the historic village of Essex, Connecticut, the Griswold Inn has weathered the Revolutionary War, British occupation, and Prohibition. Over its nearly 250-year history, the inn has accumulated numerous ghost stories told by guests and long-term staff. The most frequently sighted spirit is the "Lady in White," who is said to glide through guest rooms—particularly Room 17—and common hallways. Patrons have also reported encounters with a stern former owner in period attire, the ghost of a spectral sea captain standing near the fireplace, and the unexplained sound of children's laughter echoing through empty corridors late at night. Additionally, visitors frequently experience sudden cold spots, doors opening and closing on their own, and objects being inexplicably displaced from tabletops.

#location
The Griswold Inn is located at 36 Main Street in Essex, Connecticut.

#title
The Hex of Charles Island

#summary
Following the kidnapping of a tribal chief's daughter, an ancient hex was placed upon Charles Island dictating that any shelter built there would inevitably crumble. True to the curse, over the centuries every structure erected on the island has met with ruin alongside a succession of tragedies and untimely deaths.

#details
According to local folklore, the island was cursed by a Paugussett tribal chief after his daughter was kidnapped, proclaiming that any shelter built on the land would crumble to the ground. True to his words, centuries of attempts to settle or build upon Charles Island—including early homesteads, a 19th-century resort hotel, and a 20th-century religious retreat—have all failed and fallen into ruin. Compounding the hex, the isolated landmass has also been the site of numerous mysterious deaths, accidents, and pirate lore involving Captain William Kidd, cementing its reputation as a place of lingering peril and supernatural sorrow.

#location
Charles Island is a 14-acre island located approximately half a mile off the coast of Milford, Connecticut, accessible at low tide via a sandbar tombolo connected to Silver Sands State Park.

#title
The Phantoms of the Sterling Opera House

#summary
Built in the late 19th century, a historic Connecticut theater and municipal building is said to be haunted by unseen actors, disembodied footsteps, and inexplicable electrical anomalies.

#details
Opened in 1889 as a grand vaudeville palace and performance venue, the Sterling Opera House in Derby later served as the city hall and police station before being abandoned in the mid-20th century. Over the decades, municipal workers, caretakers, and paranormal investigators have reported frequent unexplainable occurrences within its decaying halls. Witnesses have described feelings of being watched, abrupt drops in temperature, doors slamming shut on their own, and phantom seats in the auditorium reportedly occupied by unseen spectators. Both local officials and television broadcast crews have experienced equipment failures and batteries draining instantly while exploring the dark, historic interior.

#location
Sterling Opera House is located at 156 Elizabeth Street in Derby, Connecticut.

#title
The Tormented Depths of Old New-Gate Prison

#summary
A subterranean 18th-century copper mine and state prison known to inmates as "Hell" is said to be haunted by the troubled spirits of prisoners who suffered and died within its dark, damp tunnels.

#details
Originally opened as a copper mine in 1709, the cavernous underground network in East Granby was converted into Connecticut's first state prison in 1773. Inmates were housed in total darkness deep beneath the earth, enduring brutal conditions, forced labor, and numerous violent riots and escapes. Among the tragic figures tied to the site's dark history is an inmate named Abel Starkey, who plunged to his death down a 70-foot ventilation shaft in 1827 just before his sentence ended. Today, visitors and historians exploring the historic ruins and tunnels frequently report feeling sudden, inexplicable nausea, hearing phantom voices, and witnessing shadowy apparitions in the subterranean cells.

#location
Old New-Gate Prison and Copper Mine is located on New-Gate Road in East Granby, Connecticut.

#title
The White Lady of Union Cemetery

#summary
A spectral woman in a white Victorian nightgown and veil is said to haunt a historic Connecticut graveyard and the surrounding roads, frequently startling nighttime drivers.

#details
For decades, motorists driving past Union Cemetery in Easton, Connecticut, have reported encountering a ghostly figure known as the White Lady. Described as a woman with long dark hair wearing a white Victorian gown or nightgown, she is said to wander the grounds of the 18th-century graveyard and occasionally materialize on the adjacent Route 59. One of the most famous accounts comes from a local driver who reported striking what felt like a solid brick wall in the middle of the road, only to find no person, blood, or damage when he and police officers searched the empty roadway. Paranormal investigators Ed and Lorraine Warren documented numerous sightings of the spirit, suggesting the apparition is an earthbound soul still lingering due to an unresolved, tragic death.

#location
Union Cemetery is located near the junction of Route 59 and Route 136 in Easton, Connecticut.

#title
The White Lady of Union Cemetery

#summary
Motorists and paranormal investigators in Easton have frequently encountered a spectral woman in a Victorian nightgown along the roadway and grounds near the historic Union Cemetery.

#details
Union Cemetery in Easton, Connecticut, dates back to the 1760s and has long held a reputation as one of the most haunted burial grounds in the United States. Among its most famous spectral inhabitants is the "White Lady," a spirit said to wear a diaphanous white Victorian gown or wedding dress with long dark hair. In September 1993, local firefighter Glenn Pennell was driving along the road near the cemetery when he suddenly saw a woman in a white nightgown standing in the center of the road. Unable to stop in time, his truck struck her, feeling as though he had hit a solid brick wall and sending the back end of his vehicle into the air. When Pennell and other witnesses searched the area immediately after, no body, blood, or clothing could be found. Famed demonologists Ed and Lorraine Warren also investigated the cemetery, reporting photographic evidence of the White Lady surrounded by dark shapes, as well as encounters with mysterious glowing red eyes in the adjacent woods.

#location
Union Cemetery is situated at the intersection of Routes 59 and 136 in Easton, Connecticut.

#upstream-source
*Graveyard: True Hauntings from an Old New England Cemetery* by Ed Warren (1992).

#title
The Wicked Witch of Monroe

#summary
In 19th-century Connecticut, an eccentric and sharp-tongued woman named Hannah Cranna was widely feared as a local witch whose suspected dark arts and uncanny premonitions culminated in eerie supernatural occurrences during her funeral.

#details
Living on Craig Hill in Monroe, Connecticut, Hannah Cranna earned a fearsome reputation as the "Wicked Witch of Monroe" following the mysterious death of her husband, who toppled over a cliff under suspicious circumstances. Known for intimidating neighbors into giving her free food and firewood, she allegedly placed malicious curses on those who crossed her, such as preventing a local fisherman from ever catching another trout and stripping a woman of her ability to bake. Before dying of natural causes at age 77, Cranna left strict instructions that her coffin must be carried to the graveyard by hand rather than on a vehicle, and that she must be buried before sundown. When mourners attempted to transport her casket on a sled in the heavy snow, the coffin miraculously slid off and returned all the way to her front door. After workers finally struggled to bury her at Gregory's Four Corners Burial Ground just as the sun set, her former home mysteriously burned down to the ground.

#location
Gregory’s Four Corners Burial Ground is located on Spring Hill Road near the border of Trumbull and Monroe, Connecticut.

#upstream-source
David Phillips' Legendary Connecticut.
