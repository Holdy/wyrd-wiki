The White Lady of Union Cemetery

Summary

Motorists and paranormal investigators in Easton have frequently encountered a spectral woman in a Victorian nightgown along the roadway and grounds near the historic Union Cemetery.

Details

Union Cemetery in Easton, Connecticut, dates back to the 1760s and has long held a reputation as one of the most haunted burial grounds in the United States. Among its most famous spectral inhabitants is the "White Lady," a spirit said to wear a diaphanous white Victorian gown or wedding dress with long dark hair. In September 1993, local firefighter Glenn Pennell was driving along the road near the cemetery when he suddenly saw a woman in a white nightgown standing in the center of the road. Unable to stop in time, his truck struck her, feeling as though he had hit a solid brick wall and sending the back end of his vehicle into the air. When Pennell and other witnesses searched the area immediately after, no body, blood, or clothing could be found. Famed demonologists Ed and Lorraine Warren also investigated the cemetery, reporting photographic evidence of the White Lady surrounded by dark shapes, as well as encounters with mysterious glowing red eyes in the adjacent woods.
