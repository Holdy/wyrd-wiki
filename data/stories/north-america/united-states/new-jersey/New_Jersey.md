#title
Ghosts of Ringwood Manor

#summary
Ringwood Manor, a historic 18th-century estate in New Jersey, is haunted by multiple spirits, including a murdered housemaid and Revolutionary War figures.

#details
Built in the 1700s and expanded in 1807, Ringwood Manor was home to General Robert Erskine, George Washington's Surveyor-General during the Revolutionary War. Local folklore holds that the estate and its grounds are haunted by several ghosts after dark. In the Manor House, visitors and staff have reported hearing footsteps, heavy objects dropping, and soft crying emanating from a second-floor bedroom where a housemaid was allegedly beaten to death. Behind the manor pond, the ghost of General Erskine can be seen at dusk sitting on his grave and gazing across the water. Furthermore, an unmarked grave of French soldiers who fought with Rochambeau lies nearby, and their spirits are said to walk the shores of the pond at night while speaking in soft French voices.

#location
Ringwood Manor is situated within Ringwood State Park in the borough of Ringwood, Passaic County, New Jersey.

#title
The Devil's Tree

#summary
A solitary oak tree in Bernards Township is said to carry a dark curse that brings swift misfortune upon anyone who damages, disrespects, or defaces it. Visitors also report supernatural anomalies, including abnormal warmth in winter and a phantom pickup truck that guards the grounds.

#details
Standing alone in an undeveloped field on Mountain Road in Bernards Township, New Jersey, the Devil's Tree has accumulated a rich tapestry of grim folklore over generations. According to local legend, the tree is heavily cursed: those who commit the transgression of urinating on its trunk, carving into its bark, or speaking disparagingly about it are said to face immediate retribution, frequently suffering devastating car crashes or mechanical breakdowns as they attempt to drive away. 

The origins of the tree's dark reputation are entangled with multiple haunting narratives. Some accounts claim the oak was used for lynchings during colonial times, while another legend tells of a local farmer who murdered his family before hanging himself from its branches. Folklore also warns that anyone attempting to cut down the tree will meet an untimely end. Additionally, locals describe bizarre phenomena surrounding the site: the ground beneath the tree reportedly remains free of snow even in the harshest winters, the bark is strangely warm to the touch year-round, and an aggressive black Ford pickup truck is rumored to chase away trespassers before vanishing into thin air.

#location
The Devil's Tree is located in an undeveloped field on Mountain Road in the Martinsville section of Bernards Township, Somerset County, New Jersey.

#title
The Ghost Boy of Clinton Road

#summary
Motorists who stop at the bridge on Clinton Road and throw coins into the creek are said to have their coins tossed back by the spirit of a young boy who died there.

#details
Winding through the isolated wilderness of West Milford, New Jersey, Clinton Road is heavily laden with local folklore, of which the legend of the ghost boy is the most famous. According to accounts passed down among locals and late-night visitors, a young boy tragically died either by drowning or being struck by a car near the bridge at Dead Man's Curve. It is believed that his spirit lingers beneath the bridge in the dark, tea-colored waters of the stream. Drivers who stop their vehicles on the bridge and toss coins into the creek report hearing a splash or seeing ripples, followed by the coins being mysteriously thrown back or hurled against their car windows. 

#location
The bridge at Dead Man’s Curve on Clinton Road in West Milford, Passaic County, New Jersey.

#title
The Ghost of the Death Cell at Burlington County Prison

#summary
Visitors and volunteers at the historic Burlington County Prison in Mount Holly report disembodied voices, unexplained technical malfunctions, and the lingering spirit of an executed criminal chained to a floor ring.

#details
Built in 1811 by architect Robert Mills and operating until 1966, the Burlington County Prison in Mount Holly holds a dark history of executions and violent deaths. Among its most infamous spirits is Joel Clough, a 29-year-old man convicted of the stabbing death of a woman in Bordentown after being jilted. Following an escape attempt, Clough was captured and confined to the maximum-security Death Cell in the center of the top floor—a windowless dungeon featuring an iron ring in the center of the floor where condemned prisoners were chained naked. Clough was executed in the 1830s, and his body was reportedly buried in a prison yard corner where a large tree now stands. Modern visitors, staff, and paranormal investigators report hearing his moaning and languishing from the cell, while researchers testing EVP equipment at his reported burial site experience sudden, inexplicable tape recorder shutdowns and anomalies on anniversaries of his execution.

#location
The Historic Burlington County Prison Museum is located on High Street in Mount Holly, New Jersey.

#title
The Ghosts of Burlington County Prison
#summary
The historic 1811 prison in Mount Holly, New Jersey, is said to be haunted by the restless spirits of executed inmates, including the murderer Joel Clough.
#details
Built in 1811 by architect Robert Mills, the Burlington County Prison in Mount Holly served as a correctional facility until 1966, making it the oldest continually operating prison in the United States at the time of its closure. Throughout its history, it was the site of numerous hangings and violent incidents, including the imprisonment and execution of Joel Clough, a man convicted of murdering his former fiancée in the 1830s. Clough spent his final days chained in the maximum-security "death cell" on the top floor before being executed, and his body was reportedly buried in the prison yard near a large tree. Today, staff, visitors, and paranormal investigators report chilling phenomena, including disembodied moans in the death cell, electromagnetic meter spikes, and inexplicable mechanical failures on recording equipment left near Clough's suspected burial site on the anniversary of his execution.
#location
Burlington County Prison Museum, located on High Street in Mount Holly, New Jersey.

#title
The Haunting of the Proprietary House
#summary
Perth Amboy's historic colonial mansion, once home to New Jersey's last royal governor, is plagued by phantom footsteps, swinging chandeliers, and ghostly apparitions.
#details
Constructed in 1762, the Proprietary House in Perth Amboy served as the official residence for New Jersey’s royal governors under British rule, housing colonial governor William Franklin—the illegitimate son of Benjamin Franklin—until his arrest by American revolutionaries in 1776. Over the centuries, the grand Georgian mansion experienced fires, served as a hotel, and underwent various renovations, accumulating a heavy atmosphere of historical trauma. Today, visitors and paranormal investigators report frequent supernatural occurrences, such as a mysterious "woman in white" wandering second-floor chambers, the phantom presence and measured footsteps of Governor Franklin in his former quarters, doors unbolting themselves, and shadows darting across the colonial-era cellar.
#location
The Proprietary House, located in Perth Amboy, New Jersey.

#title
The Haunting of the Spy House

#summary
The historic Seabrook-Wilson Homestead along the Sandy Hook Bay is plagued by reports of phantom children, old sailors, and rocking chairs moving on their own in locked rooms.

#details
The Seabrook-Wilson Homestead, widely known as the Spy House, is a weather-beaten wooden building dating back to around 1650 located on Port Monmouth Road in the Port Monmouth section of Middletown, New Jersey. Local legend claims that during the Revolutionary War, patriot innkeeper Thomas Seabrook used the tavern to get British soldiers drunk and extract military secrets. While historians debate the veracity of wartime espionage, generations of visitors and park rangers have reported eerie encounters with spectral inhabitants. Witnesses have described seeing a young boy in colonial attire peering from an upstairs window before backing away into an empty, locked room. Other visitors looking into the darkened building have witnessed an old man reading in a rocking chair that rocked back and forth entirely on its own inside the boarded-up homestead.

#location
The Seabrook-Wilson Homestead (Spy House) is located at 119 Port Monmouth Road in the Port Monmouth section of Middletown, New Jersey.

#title
The Phantoms of the Proprietary House

#summary
Visitors on haunted history tours of the colonial Proprietary House in Perth Amboy encounter the restless specters of Revolutionary War figures and former residents.

#details
Standing at 149 Kearny Avenue in Perth Amboy, the Proprietary House is a historic colonial governor's mansion dating back to the pre-Revolutionary era. Over the centuries, the grand brick building has gained a reputation for intense paranormal activity, with tour guides and visitors reporting encounters with the scary specters of Governor and Mrs. Franklin, local celebrity William Dunlap, a frightful ghost of a Revolutionary War soldier, and old Matt Bruen. Specific focal points of the hauntings include the colonial brick wine cellar—rumored to have been used as a dungeon during the Revolution—the haunted staircase where a bloody murder allegedly took place, and dining room corners where eccentric former occupants still linger.

#location
The Proprietary House is located at 149 Kearny Avenue in Perth Amboy, New Jersey.

#title
The Spirits of the Emlen Physick Estate
#summary
Cape May's iconic 1879 Victorian mansion is haunted by the lingering presence of its original occupants, including unexplained footsteps, cold spots, and sightings of a woman in white.
#details
The Emlen Physick Estate is an 18-room Victorian mansion built in 1879 by architect Frank Furness for Dr. Emlen Physick, who lived there with his mother Frances and aunt Emilie until his death in 1916. After falling into disrepair and nearly facing demolition in the 1970s, the estate was preserved and turned into a museum, but visitors, staff, and paranormal investigators quickly noted that the house felt remarkably inhabited. The most persistent legend involves a "Woman in White"—believed to be Dr. Physick's mother, Frances—who is frequently spotted looking out upper-floor windows or drifting down hallways. Other reported phenomena include disembodied footsteps pacing empty staircases, the murmur of indistinct voices, sudden temperature drops, and an intense feeling of being watched in vacant rooms.
#location
The Emlen Physick Estate, located at 1048 Washington Street in Cape May, New Jersey.
