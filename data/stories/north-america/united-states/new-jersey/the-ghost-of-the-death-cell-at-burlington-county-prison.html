The Ghost of the Death Cell at Burlington County Prison

Summary

Visitors and volunteers at the historic Burlington County Prison in Mount Holly report disembodied voices, unexplained technical malfunctions, and the lingering spirit of an executed criminal chained to a floor ring.

Details

Built in 1811 by architect Robert Mills and operating until 1966, the Burlington County Prison in Mount Holly holds a dark history of executions and violent deaths. Among its most infamous spirits is Joel Clough, a 29-year-old man convicted of the stabbing death of a woman in Bordentown after being jilted. Following an escape attempt, Clough was captured and confined to the maximum-security Death Cell in the center of the top floor—a windowless dungeon featuring an iron ring in the center of the floor where condemned prisoners were chained naked. Clough was executed in the 1830s, and his body was reportedly buried in a prison yard corner where a large tree now stands. Modern visitors, staff, and paranormal investigators report hearing his moaning and languishing from the cell, while researchers testing EVP equipment at his reported burial site experience sudden, inexplicable tape recorder shutdowns and anomalies on anniversaries of his execution.
