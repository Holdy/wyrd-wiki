The Devil's Tree

Summary

A solitary oak tree in Bernards Township is said to carry a dark curse that brings swift misfortune upon anyone who damages, disrespects, or defaces it. Visitors also report supernatural anomalies, including abnormal warmth in winter and a phantom pickup truck that guards the grounds.

Details

Standing alone in an undeveloped field on Mountain Road in Bernards Township, New Jersey, the Devil's Tree has accumulated a rich tapestry of grim folklore over generations. According to local legend, the tree is heavily cursed: those who commit the transgression of urinating on its trunk, carving into its bark, or speaking disparagingly about it are said to face immediate retribution, frequently suffering devastating car crashes or mechanical breakdowns as they attempt to drive away.

The origins of the tree's dark reputation are entangled with multiple haunting narratives. Some accounts claim the oak was used for lynchings during colonial times, while another legend tells of a local farmer who murdered his family before hanging himself from its branches. Folklore also warns that anyone attempting to cut down the tree will meet an untimely end. Additionally, locals describe bizarre phenomena surrounding the site: the ground beneath the tree reportedly remains free of snow even in the harshest winters, the bark is strangely warm to the touch year-round, and an aggressive black Ford pickup truck is rumored to chase away trespassers before vanishing into thin air.
