#title
The Apparitions of Bruce Mansion

#summary
Built in 1876 in Brown City, the Victorian Bruce Mansion is said to be haunted by former residents and tragic figures from its past.

#details
Constructed by Scottish immigrant John G. Bruce, the mansion famously survived an 1881 fire that leveled the surrounding town. In the decades following, the house hosted local funerals and witnessed personal hardships, most notably John Walker, who died under mysterious circumstances in the home in the 1920s after his wife left him. Visitors and paranormal investigators over the years have reported hearing disembodied growls in the dirt-floored cellar, encountering a prankster spirit opening doors, and seeing spectral apparitions wandering the dark mahogany hallways and cupola tower.

#location
The Bruce Mansion is located in Brown City, Michigan.

#title
The Curse of Seven Gables Road

#summary
An aggrieved witch cursed a rural Michigan road and its property, paving the way for a horrific family massacre and deadly supernatural encounters for anyone who trespasses past its rusted gate.

#details
According to local folklore, a malicious witch once lived in a seven-gabled home situated at the dead end of Seven Gables Road in Dansville, Michigan, where she placed a dark hex upon the property and its rustic boundary fence. Legend dictates that anyone who dared to jump over the gate and heard a chilling scream would be struck dead by the curse. Decades later, a new family moved into the home, whereupon the father tragically went insane, murdered his family, hung their bodies from the gables, burned the house to the ground, and took his own life. To this day, thrill-seekers and visitors wandering the overgrown state game area trails report hearing phantom screams echoing through the trees, unexplained heavy breathing, and oppressive supernatural dread near the rusted gate ruins.

#location
The site of the former house and rusted gate at the dead end of Seven Gables Road, located off E. Dexter Trail in Dansville, Ingham County, Michigan.

#title
The Paulding Light

#summary
A mysterious glowing orb appears nearly every night in a remote forest valley in Michigan's Upper Peninsula, inspiring various local legends and ghost stories.

#details
Since the 1960s, visitors and locals have gathered along a rural stretch of road outside of Paulding, Michigan, to witness an unexplained luminous phenomenon. The light typically appears in the valley after dark, bouncing, hovering, and shifting colors between white, red, and green above the tree line and power lines. Popular local folklore offers several explanations for the mysterious light, the most famous being the ghost of a railroad brakeman who was killed while trying to stop a train crash on tracks that once ran through the valley. Other legends attribute the light to the spirit of a slain mail courier or a Native American dancer. Although subsequent scientific studies and student investigations have proposed that the lights are merely distant automobile headlights refracting off US Highway 45, the supernatural legends and nightly viewings endure.

#location
The phenomenon is observed from a designated viewing area on Robbins Pond Road, located off US Highway 45 between Watersmeet and Paulding in Michigan's Upper Peninsula.

#title
The Phantom of the Railway Depot

#summary
Housed in an 1886 railway depot in Ann Arbor, Michigan, the Gandy Dancer restaurant is reportedly haunted by lingering spirits from its past as a transit hub and temporary holding morgue for fallen soldiers, with staff experiencing flying glassware and shadowy figures.

#details
Originally constructed in 1886 as the Michigan Central Railroad Depot, the grand building was a bustling center of travel. According to historical accounts shared by long-time employees, the depot was also used during World War I to temporarily hold the bodies of fallen soldiers being shipped home to their families for burial. Decades later, converted into a restaurant named after railroad section hands, diners and staff have encountered bizarre, unexplainable phenomena. Lights have reportedly turned upside down, glasses have flown off shelves without physical cause, and witnesses have seen a well-dressed gentlemanly apparition wandering the historic dining rooms.

#location
The Gandy Dancer restaurant is located at 401 Depot St, Ann Arbor, MI 48104, near the edge of downtown Ann Arbor.

#title
The Red Rug of Agnes Felt

#summary
Agnes Felt, who passed away just six weeks after moving into her luxurious summer estate in Holland, Michigan, is said to still haunt the halls, leaving behind eerie phenomena such as a mysteriously relocated rug in her former bedroom.

#details
Built between 1925 and 1928 by self-made millionaire and inventor Dorr E. Felt as a summer retreat for his wife Agnes, the magnificent estate on the dunes of Lake Michigan was meant to be a place of leisure. However, Agnes fell ill and died just six weeks after the family moved in. Over the decades, as the mansion changed hands—serving as a prep school, a convent, and later a state prison—it fell into abandonment before restoration efforts began. During early restoration work, staff laid a red rug in Agnes's former bedroom, only to return the next day to find it rolled up and pushed neatly into a corner. When straightened out, the rug was found rolled up in the corner again later that same day. Since then, staff and visitors have reported inexplicable sounds, cold spots, and fleeting apparitions.

#location
The Felt Mansion is located at 6597 138th Ave, Holland, MI 49423, situated in the sand dunes near the shores of Lake Michigan.

#title
The Smoking Keeper of Seul Choix Point

#summary
The 19th-century Seul Choix Point Lighthouse on the Upper Peninsula shore is haunted by the lingering spirit of its former keeper and other unfortunate souls.

#details
Commissioned in 1886 and completed in 1895, the Seul Choix Point Lighthouse in Gulliver, Michigan, is famously associated with Captain Joseph Willie Townsend, who served as keeper until his death in 1910. Because winter weather prevented an immediate burial, his body was temporarily kept in the cold cellar, leading to enduring legends of his cigar-smoking ghost haunting the quarters. Investigators later recorded disembodied audio responses belonging to Mary Pebble, a woman whose body was similarly sheltered in the lighthouse bathroom during a severe winter storm in 1919.

#location
The Seul Choix Point Lighthouse is located on the Upper Peninsula shore of Lake Michigan near Gulliver, Michigan.

#upstream-source
Haunted Travels of Michigan by Kat Tedsen.

#title
The Sorrowful Specters of Cobblestone Farm

#summary
Built in the 1840s in Ann Arbor, Michigan, this historic pioneer farmhouse is said to be haunted by the spirits of families who endured immense tragedy, including a woman spotted looking out an upper-story window and a young boy near the old well.

#details
The land has been farmed for two centuries, and its iconic Classical Revival cobblestone house was constructed in the 1840s by Dr. Benajah Ticknor, whose family suffered the heartbreaking loss of eleven of their twelve children to illnesses. Later owned by the Campbell family, the property survived a devastating 1924 fire that destroyed the outbuildings when family members threw wet blankets onto the roof. Today, operating as a historic farm museum, visitors and paranormal investigators have reported numerous ghostly encounters: a woman peering from an upper window, a man sitting quietly in the dining room, and a young boy running across the grounds. During paranormal investigations, researchers recorded intelligent knocking responses, sudden temperature spikes, and disembodied whispers uttering names from the farm's past.

#location
Historic Cobblestone Farm is located at 2781 Packard St, Ann Arbor, MI 48108.

#upstream-source
Haunted Travels of Michigan by Beverlee J. Rydel and Kathleen R. Tedsen.

#title
The Spectre of Milan-Oakville Road

#summary
A ghostly bride in a wedding gown wanders a desolate stretch of road, crying and pleading for help where she and her new husband tragically died in a car accident over a century ago.

#details
Over a hundred years ago, a newlywed couple in their twenties suffered a horrific, fatal car crash while traveling along what was then a dirt road east of Milan, Michigan. Ever since, motorists driving along Milan-Oakville Road have reported hearing the phantom sounds of a vehicle collision when no other cars are present. Drivers who pull over and turn off their engines often hear the spectral crash replaying upon restarting their vehicles, immediately followed by the appearance of the bride in white, who runs up to cars crying and pleading for help before vanishing into thin air.

#location
Milan-Oakville Road, located east of Milan, Michigan.

#title
The Spirits of Battle Alley

#summary
The historic Holly Hotel in Michigan is reputed to be haunted by several permanent spectral residents, including its original innkeeper and a playful young girl.

#details
Built on Battle Alley in the early 1890s, the Holly Hotel survived two major fires and has long been regarded as one of Michigan's most active haunted locations. Paranormal investigators and guests have frequently reported encountering Mr. Hirst, the original hotelier, who manifests through the scent of cigar smoke, baritone laughter, and occasional top-hatted apparitions. Other noted spirits include Nora Kane, a gracious hostess who enjoys playing the piano and leaves behind the scent of flowery perfume, as well as an anonymous young girl who tinkers with kitchen utensils and meat cleavers.

#location
The Holly Hotel is located at 110 Battle Alley in Holly, Michigan.
