#title
Black Aggie

#summary
In Druid Ridge Cemetery, a life-sized shrouded bronze statue known as Black Aggie was said to come to life at night and crush anyone who dared sit in her lap.

#details
In the Agnus family plot at Druid Ridge Cemetery in Pikesville, Maryland, a melancholic, life-sized shrouded bronze statue of a grieving figure stood as a memorial for many years. According to local folklore, the monument—nicknamed Black Aggie—harbored a sinister curse. At the stroke of midnight, her eyes would glow red, and anyone who met her gaze would be struck blind. The most famous legend warned that if anyone dared to sit on her lap at night, the stone statue would come to life and crush them to death in a dark embrace. Local fraternity pledges frequently dared initiates to spend the night crouching beneath the monument. During one such initiation, an initiate sat beneath the figure as shadows clustered around him; the statue's head turned, its eyes glowed red, and its arms reached out. The terrified student collapsed and died of fright at the base of the monument, prompting the Agnus family to eventually remove and donate the statue.

#location
Druid Ridge Cemetery located in Pikesville, Maryland.

#title
The Blood and Spirits of Rackliffe House

#summary
Built in the 1700s, the historic Rackliffe House is said to be haunted by the violent spirit of its murdered owner, John Rackliffe, and a distraught mother who took her own life. Visitors and tenants have reported inexplicable sounds of dancing in the attic, slamming doors, and crashing noises.

#details
Rackliffe House was built in the 1700s on Sinepuxent Neck in Worcester County. According to local folklore and historical records, third-generation owner John Rackliffe was a notoriously cruel slave owner who was ambushed and murdered by his slaves in 1790. Over the years, the plantation home became the site of multiple tragedies, including a widow hanging herself in the attic out of grief after her son was recruited to fight in the War of 1812, and a lady falling to her death down the stairs. Today, tenants and visitors frequently report supernatural occurrences, such as the sounds of dancing in the attic, slamming doors, objects moving on their own, and unexplained crashing noises.

#location
Rackliffe House is located at 11700 Tom Patton Lane in Berlin, Maryland, situated on Sinepuxent Neck behind the Assateague Island visitors center.

#title
The Curse of Moll Dyer

#summary
Accused of witchcraft and driven into a harsh winter storm by fearful townspeople, a colonial woman cursed Leonardtown before freezing to death against a boulder. Her dying malediction left a lasting legacy of misfortune, blighted crops, and supernatural dread upon the area.

#details
In the late seventeenth century, Leonardtown, Maryland, was gripped by severe famine, crop failures, and a deadly epidemic during an exceptionally bitter winter. Deeply superstitious and desperate, the townspeople blamed an outcast woman named Moll Dyer, accusing her of practicing witchcraft to plague the community. A mob formed and burned down her hut, forcing her to flee into the freezing woods with barely any clothing. Knowing she would succumb to hypothermia, Dyer pressed her hand against a limestone boulder, raised her other hand to the moon, and pronounced a fierce curse upon the people of Leonardtown. Days later, her frozen body was discovered still clinging to the rock. To this day, the cursed stone and the surrounding lands are said to bring illness, misfortune, and unexplained paranormal phenomena to those who encounter them.

#location
Moll Dyer Rock is preserved on the grounds of the Tudor Hall Mansion located in Leonardtown, Maryland.

#title
The Ghost of Snow Hill Inn

#summary
The historic Snow Hill Inn is reportedly haunted by the restless spirit of a young medical student named JJ who died under violent and mysterious circumstances in 1904. Staff and guests note that lights and radios in his former room frequently turn on and off by themselves.

#details
In the 1870s, the Snow Hill Inn was owned by Dr. John S. Aydelotte. His son, JJ, pursued a medical career and attended the University of Maryland’s School of Pharmacology in Baltimore. At age 22, JJ was found dead on a lodging house floor with severe knife wounds to his throat. While officially ruled a suicide following a note left to his father, local folklore and contemporary newspaper accounts also whispered of a jealous dispute involving an affair. Today, JJ's spirit is believed to haunt the Snow Hill Inn, amusing or alarming guests by manipulating lights and radios in his old room.

#location
The Snow Hill Inn is situated near the intersection of Market Street and Washington Street in Snow Hill, Maryland.

#title
The Haunting of Hager's Fancy

#summary
The 18th-century stone homestead built by town founder Jonathan Hager is widely regarded as one of the most haunted sites in Maryland, steeped in legends of lingering spirits from past generations.

#details
Constructed in 1739 over a natural spring by German immigrant Jonathan Hager, the historic limestone house—often referred to as Hager's Fancy—served as both a frontier fortress and a trading post. Over the centuries, successive families and visitors have reported paranormal encounters within its twenty-two-inch thick stone walls. Local folklore and museum ghost tours recount sightings of a mysterious lady in green looking out the windows, along with unexplained noises and spectral activity attributed to former residents such as the Hammond and Downin families who lived there in the 19th century.

#location
The Jonathan Hager House Museum is located at 110 Key Street within Hagerstown City Park in Hagerstown, Maryland.

#title
The Headless Peddler of MD-440

#summary
In 1763, a traveling peddler was murdered and decapitated along a Maryland road, after which his headless specter was seen haunting the nearby creek and roadway.

#details
In 1763, a poor peddler traversing the Dublin-Stafford Road (now designated as MD-440) near Dublin, Maryland, was waylaid, murdered, and found decapitated near Rocky Run. Local residents buried his remains near the creek where he was discovered. Shortly after his burial, witnesses reported seeing his headless specter wandering along the creek and roadway. Decades later, in 1843, a farmer unearthed a human skull in the area; presuming it belonged to portal-slain traveler, it was interred alongside the peddler's original remains, following which the ghostly sightings reportedly stopped, though the stream forever retained the name Peddler’s Run.

#location
Along MD-440 (formerly the Dublin-Stafford Road) near Rocky Run in Dublin, Maryland.

#title
The Legend of Moll Dyer

#summary
During the harsh winter of 1697–1698, an isolated resident of Leonardtown, Maryland, was accused of witchcraft and driven from her home by a superstitious mob, ultimately freezing to death on a limestone boulder. Her ghost and a reputed curse continue to haunt the surrounding community to this day.

#details
In the late 17th century, a severe influenza epidemic and brutal winter weather struck the small community of Leonardtown, Maryland. Seeking a scapegoat for their suffering, puritanical townspeople blamed Moll Dyer, an older woman living in a small hut on the southern edge of town, accusing her of witchcraft and consorting with the Devil. A mob gathered and set fire to her dwelling. Moll narrowly escaped into the freezing woods, fleeing for miles until exhaustion and exposure overcame her. Before dying on a large boulder, she placed her hand upon the stone and called down a curse upon Leonardtown. Days later, her frozen body was discovered with her hand fused to the rock, leaving a permanent indentation. The boulder, known as Moll Dyer Rock, was eventually moved to the grounds of Tudor Hall Mansion, where visitors still report feelings of illness or misfortune when touching it, and local folklore associates the nearby Moll Dyer Road with ghostly phenomena and mysterious vehicle accidents.

#location
The Moll Dyer Rock is located on the grounds of the Tudor Hall Mansion in Leonardtown, Maryland, near Moll Dyer Road.

#title
The Phantoms of Point Lookout Lighthouse

#summary
Inhabitants and paranormal investigators at the historic Point Lookout Lighthouse have reported decades of inexplicable phenomena, including phantom footsteps, disembodied voices, and shadowy apparitions.

#details
Built in 1830 at the confluence of the Potomac River and the Chesapeake Bay, the Point Lookout Lighthouse served as a beacon for mariners for over 135 years. Following its decommissioning and subsequent occupancy by various civilian tenants and researchers in the late 1970s and 1980s, numerous eerie events were documented. Residents reported hearing furniture being moved in empty rooms, footsteps pacing the halls and stairs during storms, and doors locking themselves from the inside. Psychics and investigators visiting the lighthouse recorded unexplained voices, felt cold spots, and witnessed figures in the basement and near the cupola, echoing tragedies from its long history.

#location
Point Lookout Lighthouse is situated at the southern tip of St. Mary's County at Point Lookout State Park in Scotland, Maryland.

#title
The Tragic Spectre of Poplar Hill Mansion

#summary
Salisbury's oldest building, the 1805 Poplar Hill Mansion, is haunted by the tragic spirit of a young enslaved woman named Sara who perished in a fireplace fire. Visitors and staff have reported scorch marks on the nursery floor and unexplained apparitions peering from the windows.

#details
Built in 1805 by Dr. John Huston, Poplar Hill Mansion served as both a home and a medical practice. Local legend centers on a 19-year-old enslaved woman named Sara who worked as a caretaker for the children. According to accounts, Sara suffered fatal burns after her dress caught fire near the fireplace in the second-floor rear bedroom nursery—either by accident or during a physical altercation. Her spirit is said to still inhabit the mansion, where occupants report sightings of a slave woman looking out windows, along with the lingering presence of Dr. Huston and other phantom residents.

#location
Poplar Hill Mansion is located at 117 Elizabeth Street in Salisbury, Maryland.
