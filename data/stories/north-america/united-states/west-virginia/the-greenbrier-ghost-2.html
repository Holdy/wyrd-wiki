The Greenbrier Ghost

Summary

In 1897 West Virginia, a young woman's spirit allegedly returned from the grave to tell her mother how her husband had murdered her, prompting an exhumation and a historic murder conviction.

Details

In October 1896, Elva Zona Heaster married a blacksmith named Edward Shue in Greenbrier County, West Virginia. Just a few months later, on January 23, 1897, Zona was found dead in her home. A local doctor performed a hasty and incomplete examination, and her death was officially ruled as natural causes due to heart disease. She was promptly buried at the local cemetery. However, Zona's grieving mother, Mary Jane Heaster, insisted that her daughter's ghost visited her bedside over multiple nights, rotating her head 360 degrees and explaining that Edward had flown into a violent rage and broken her neck.

Driven by the apparition's claims, Mary Jane successfully convinced the local prosecutor to investigate further. Zona's body was exhumed and subjected to a proper autopsy in a local schoolhouse, which revealed that her neck had indeed been broken and her windpipe crushed. Edward Shue was arrested, tried at the Greenbrier County Courthouse, and convicted of first-degree murder in July 1897. Although the ghost's testimony was not formally entered as legal evidence in court, the sensational tale remains famous as the only recorded murder case where a ghostly visitation directly inspired an investigation and subsequent conviction.
