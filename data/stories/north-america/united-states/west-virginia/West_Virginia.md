#title
Little Lily of Ward R

#summary
Visitors and investigators touring the former asylum in Weston frequently encounter the spirit of a young girl named Lily who passed away within the building in the 19th century.

#details
The Trans-Allegheny Lunatic Asylum is said to be haunted by a young girl named Lily, who allegedly lived and died within the hospital's walls in the 1800s. According to local lore, she was either a young patient or born to a woman who arrived at the asylum during the Civil War. Her spirit is believed to haunt the fourth floor in Ward R, where visitors often leave toys like rubber balls that mysteriously move across the floor. Audio recordings from her former room have captured ghostly EVPs resembling a child's voice whispering. Beyond Lily, security guards and staff have reported sightings of a gray female figure peering from the open kitchen windows and watching those walking the corridors at night.

#location
The Trans-Allegheny Lunatic Asylum is located at 71 Asylum Drive in Weston, West Virginia.

#title
The Condemned Spirits of Moundsville Penitentiary

#summary
The Gothic stone architecture of the West Virginia Penitentiary houses decades of violent history and restless spirits, including executed inmates and shadowy entities pacing the cellblocks.

#details
Operating for over a century before closing in 1995, the West Virginia State Penitentiary witnessed numerous executions, riots, and homicides, earning a reputation as one of the most haunted prisons in America. Legend attributes some of the unrest to its proximity to the ancient Grave Creek Mound. Among the most reported hauntings is the ghost of Orville Adkins, an inmate whose 1938 hanging at the North Wagon Gate was notoriously botched when the trap door opened prematurely, causing him to fall twenty feet before being hanged a second time. Visitors and staff have reported his heavy footsteps echoing along the stone walkway, alongside sightings of the "Shadow Man," phantom voices pleading for help from solitary confinement, and paranormal activity in the infamous basement recreation room known as the Sugar Shack.

#location
The West Virginia State Penitentiary is located at 818 Jefferson Avenue in Moundsville, West Virginia.

#title
The Cornstalk Curse

#summary
Following his treacherous murder by American soldiers at a frontier fort, the Shawnee leader Chief Cornstalk placed a two-century curse upon the surrounding land, dooming it to repeated tragedy and disaster. Over the following generations, the blighted region suffered numerous catastrophic events, including historic mining disasters, structural collapses, and severe storms.

#details
In 1777, the respected Shawnee Chief Cornstalk visited Fort Randolph at Point Pleasant to negotiate peace, but was detained as a hostage by American soldiers. When a soldier was killed outside by hostile warriors, an enraged mob broke into the quarters and brutally murdered Cornstalk, his son, and their companions. As he lay dying, Cornstalk pronounced a curse upon the land, wishing that the Great Spirit would blight the region and paralyze the strength of its people. For the next two hundred years, the area was plagued by a succession of historic tragedies, industrial accidents, and freak disasters—culminating in the collapse of the Silver Bridge in 1967 before finally waning around the bicentennial of his death.

#location
Tu-Endie-Wei Park is situated at the confluence of the Ohio and Kanawha rivers in Point Pleasant, West Virginia, marking the historic site of Fort Randolph where Chief Cornstalk was killed and buried.

#title
The Ghost of Mamie Thurman

#summary
Following her brutal unsolved murder in 1932, the apparition of Mamie Thurman has been reported walking along 22 Mine Road and hitchhiking on coal trucks. Additionally, motorists traveling the mountain pass experience an eerie phenomenon where vehicles left in neutral mysteriously roll uphill, pushed by her unseen spirit.

#details
In June 1932, 31-year-old Mamie Thurman was found murdered with her throat slashed and head shot on a remote mountainside in Logan County, West Virginia. Although her handyman was eventually convicted in a sensational trial, rumors of corruption and powerful figures escaping justice persisted, leaving her murder an enduring local mystery. Ever since, locals, coal truck drivers, and visitors traversing 22 Mine Road—also known as 22 Mountain or Trace Mountain near Holden—have reported encountering her restless spirit wandering the roadside or vanishing from truck cabs, alongside the unexplained gravitational anomaly where cars roll backward up the hill.

#location
22 Mine Road is located near Holden along the Logan-Mingo county line in southern West Virginia.

#title
The Ghostly Queen of Berkeley Springs Castle

#summary
Built as a lavish Gilded Age token of love, Berkeley Springs Castle is said to be haunted by the spirited ghost of its former mistress, Rosa Suit, along with whispers of poisoning and tragic deaths.

#details
In 1885, a wealthy 51-year-old Maryland businessman named Colonel Samuel Taylor Suit fell in love with 22-year-old Rosa Pelham while visiting the spa town of Berkeley Springs. Rosa reportedly agreed to marry him on the condition that he build her a magnificent castle. Colonel Suit spent a fortune constructing the hand-cut stone edifice modeled after English castles. However, just months after moving in, Colonel Suit died under mysterious circumstances, with local rumors whispering that Rosa had poisoned him. 

Left a wealthy young widow, Rosa spent the next three decades hosting legendary, extravagant parties for political and social elites. Over time, financial ruin struck due to economic recessions and the strict conditions of her late husband's will, forcing her to lose the castle and live out her final years in poverty. Today, the historic estate is widely reported by visitors and paranormal investigators to be haunted by restless spirits, and some claim the phantom presence of "Queen Rosa" and her former guests still linger within its stone walls and winding passages.

#location
Berkeley Springs Castle (Samuel Taylor Suit Cottage) overlooks the town of Berkeley Springs in Morgan County, West Virginia.

#title
The Greenbrier Ghost

#summary
In 1897 Greenbrier County, West Virginia, the spirit of a newlywed woman allegedly visited her mother over multiple nights to reveal that she had been murdered by her husband, prompting an exhumation that led to his conviction.

#details
In October 1896, Elva Zona Heaster married a local blacksmith named Edward Shue in Greenbrier County, West Virginia. Just three months later, on January 23, 1897, Zona was found dead in her home. A local doctor examined her briefly and a coroner's jury ruled the cause of death as heart disease. However, her mother, Mary Jane Heaster, was unconvinced and later claimed that Zona's ghost visited her at night, turning her head completely around and stating that Edward had violently broken her neck during an argument. Driven by her mother's persistent claims and growing community suspicions, the local prosecutor ordered the body to be exhumed and autopsied. The subsequent examination revealed that Zona's neck had indeed been broken and her windpipe crushed. Edward Shue was arrested, tried, and found guilty of first-degree murder in July 1897, cementing the tale as a legendary instance where a spectral apparition purportedly helped bring a killer to justice.

#location
Soule Chapel Methodist Cemetery is located near Smoot in Greenbrier County, West Virginia, where Zona Heaster Shue is interred.

#title
The Greenbrier Ghost

#summary
In 1897 West Virginia, a young woman's spirit allegedly returned from the grave to tell her mother how her husband had murdered her, prompting an exhumation and a historic murder conviction.

#details
In October 1896, Elva Zona Heaster married a blacksmith named Edward Shue in Greenbrier County, West Virginia. Just a few months later, on January 23, 1897, Zona was found dead in her home. A local doctor performed a hasty and incomplete examination, and her death was officially ruled as natural causes due to heart disease. She was promptly buried at the local cemetery. However, Zona's grieving mother, Mary Jane Heaster, insisted that her daughter's ghost visited her bedside over multiple nights, rotating her head 360 degrees and explaining that Edward had flown into a violent rage and broken her neck. 

Driven by the apparition's claims, Mary Jane successfully convinced the local prosecutor to investigate further. Zona's body was exhumed and subjected to a proper autopsy in a local schoolhouse, which revealed that her neck had indeed been broken and her windpipe crushed. Edward Shue was arrested, tried at the Greenbrier County Courthouse, and convicted of first-degree murder in July 1897. Although the ghost's testimony was not formally entered as legal evidence in court, the sensational tale remains famous as the only recorded murder case where a ghostly visitation directly inspired an investigation and subsequent conviction.

#location
Soule Chapel Methodist Cemetery in Smoot, Greenbrier County, West Virginia, where Zona Heaster Shue is buried.

#title
The Lady in White of Room 208

#summary
Guests staying at a historic inn in Lewisburg frequently report eerie disruptions, phantom noises, and ghostly apparitions, most notably a spectral woman known as the Lady in White.

#details
The Historic General Lewis Inn is widely reputed to be haunted by multiple restless spirits. Room 208 is particularly infamous for the apparition of a woman in a white dress, whose portrait hangs in the room and whose presence leaves visitors uneasy. Other rooms, such as 206 and 202, have experienced unexplained activity, including unprompted sounds, phantom laughter, and toy balls mysteriously lighting up without human interference. Additionally, the spirit of a former enslaved person named Reuben is said to haunt the dining room table.

#location
The Historic General Lewis Inn is located at 301 E Washington St in Lewisburg, West Virginia.

#title
The Phantom Builder of the Blennerhassett Hotel

#summary
Built in the late 19th century in downtown Parkersburg, the historic Blennerhassett Hotel is purportedly haunted by its founder, William Chancellor, who continues to roam the hallways and play practical jokes.

#details
The Blennerhassett Hotel was built in 1889 by William N. Chancellor, a wealthy two-time mayor of Parkersburg who made his fortune in oil and real estate. Over a century later, the grand hotel has earned a reputation as one of the most haunted locations in West Virginia, most famously inhabited by the apparition of Chancellor himself. Dressed in a fine three-piece gray suit, Chancellor's ghost is often identified by a distinct scent of cigar smoke—and sometimes actual wisps of smoke appearing around his portrait in the lobby.

Guests and staff have reported numerous encounters with Chancellor throughout the corridors, elevators, and guest rooms. In one instance, a guest turned off their bedside light and felt a heavy weight at the end of the bed, only to see Chancellor sitting there who declared, "I was here first!" before disappearing. Other phantom phenomena reported at the hotel include elevators moving on their own, mirrors reflecting unexpected figures such as a sea captain and a man in a white tuxedo, and the sounds of an invisible woman shrieking or laughing hysterically in the ballroom.

#location
The Blennerhassett Hotel is located at 4th and Market Streets in downtown Parkersburg, West Virginia.
