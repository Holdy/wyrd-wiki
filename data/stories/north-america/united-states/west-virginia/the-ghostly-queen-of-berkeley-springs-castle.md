#title
The Ghostly Queen of Berkeley Springs Castle

#summary
Built as a lavish Gilded Age token of love, Berkeley Springs Castle is said to be haunted by the spirited ghost of its former mistress, Rosa Suit, along with whispers of poisoning and tragic deaths.

#details
In 1885, a wealthy 51-year-old Maryland businessman named Colonel Samuel Taylor Suit fell in love with 22-year-old Rosa Pelham while visiting the spa town of Berkeley Springs. Rosa reportedly agreed to marry him on the condition that he build her a magnificent castle. Colonel Suit spent a fortune constructing the hand-cut stone edifice modeled after English castles. However, just months after moving in, Colonel Suit died under mysterious circumstances, with local rumors whispering that Rosa had poisoned him. 

Left a wealthy young widow, Rosa spent the next three decades hosting legendary, extravagant parties for political and social elites. Over time, financial ruin struck due to economic recessions and the strict conditions of her late husband's will, forcing her to lose the castle and live out her final years in poverty. Today, the historic estate is widely reported by visitors and paranormal investigators to be haunted by restless spirits, and some claim the phantom presence of "Queen Rosa" and her former guests still linger within its stone walls and winding passages.

#location
Berkeley Springs Castle (Samuel Taylor Suit Cottage) overlooks the town of Berkeley Springs in Morgan County, West Virginia.
