#title
The Blue Light Lady of Fort Hays

#summary
Following her death during the 1867 cholera epidemic, a devoted nurse's spirit—known as the Blue Light Lady—became a recurring apparition sighted by generations of Kansans near a historic hill in Hays.

#details
During the cholera epidemic of 1867 at Fort Hays, Elizabeth Polly served as a tireless caregiver and "angel of mercy" to suffering soldiers until she contracted the illness and passed away. On her deathbed, she requested to be buried on a prominent hill near the fort so she could continue overlooking the prairie. Since the 1890s, numerous witnesses have reported encountering her ghost wandering the landscape dressed in a flowing blue gown. Sightings over the years have spanned multiple generations, including college students, a local sheriff in the 1950s who believed he nearly struck her with his car on a roadway near the hill, and a harvester working the fields in the 1970s.

#location
A historic hill near Fort Hays in Hays, Kansas.

#title
The Gates of Hell at Stull Cemetery

#summary
An abandoned church and cemetery atop a Kansas hill gained international notoriety for rumors that it serves as a portal where the Devil appears twice a year.

#details
Since the 1970s, local legends surrounding the small town of Stull claimed that Stull Cemetery and its adjacent crumbling, roofless stone church constituted one of the world's gateways to hell. Tales asserted that Satan himself appeared at extraordinary times like Halloween and the Spring Equinox to commune with witches and gather the spirits of those who died violent deaths. The folklore spawned massive crowds, trespassing, and vandalism, prompting local authorities and property owners to secure the site with security fencing and patrols.

#location
Stull Cemetery on Emmanuel Hill in Stull, Kansas.

#title
The Ghostly Guardian of the Eldridge Hotel

#summary
Room 506 of the historic Eldridge Hotel in Lawrence is said to be haunted by Colonel Shalor Eldridge, whose spirit manifests through unexplained cold spots, self-opening doors, and breath marks on mirrors.

#details
The Eldridge Hotel in Lawrence, Kansas, originally built as the Free State Hotel in 1855, has a turbulent history marked by destruction during the pro-slavery raid of 1856 and Quantrill's Raid during the Civil War in 1863. Rebuilt multiple times by Colonel Shalor Eldridge, the hotel became a landmark before falling into disrepair and being restored into luxury suites. Today, guests and staff report paranormal phenomena concentrated on the fifth floor, particularly in Room 506, where doors open and close on their own, lights turn on and off spontaneously, and the watchful specter of Colonel Eldridge is said to reside.

#location
The Eldridge Hotel is located at the corner of Massachusetts Street and 7th Street in downtown Lawrence, Kansas.

#summary
The oldest standing residence on Fort Leavenworth is said to be haunted by multiple spectral entities, most notably a violent, long-haired woman who claws at visitors.

#details
Built in 1832 on Summer Place, the duplex known as "The Rookery" has been continuously occupied since its construction and is reputed to be one of the most haunted houses in Kansas. #title
The Heartland Specter of the Sallie House

#summary
A small house in Atchison is haunted by the spirit of a young girl named Sallie, who allegedly died during a botched appendectomy in the 19th century.

#details
Situated on North Second Street in Atchison, Kansas, a modest house once belonging to a local doctor became famous as the "Sallie House" due to intense paranormal activity. According to local folklore, a young girl named Sallie died on the premises during an emergency appendectomy when anesthesia failed to take full effect. Later residents in the 1990s reported heavy poltergeist activity, including toys moving, appliances turning on by themselves, and physical attacks and scratches suffered by visitors and renters. Though later occupants reported calmer conditions, the legend of Sallie endures as one of Kansas's most chilling hauntings.

#location
The Sallie House is located at North Second Street in Atchison, Kansas.

#title
The Legend of Stull Cemetery and the Gateway to Hell

#summary
Stull Cemetery is renowned in Kansas folklore as a cursed site and a portal to the underworld where the devil makes twice-yearly appearances. The grounds also house a cursed grave marker that punishes those who disturb it, alongside numerous paranormal occurrences.

#details
Established in the mid-19th century in rural Kansas, Stull Cemetery became the center of persistent urban legends regarding occult activity and demonic forces. Folklore claims that the ruins of the 1867 church within the cemetery serve as one of the seven gateways to hell, visited by Satan on Halloween and the vernal equinox. Furthermore, a local legend speaks of an 19th-century woman or witch whose weathered grave marker is cursed, dooming anyone who dares to tamper with it. Visitors and paranormal investigators have long reported anomalous phenomena at the site, including disembodied voices, sudden temperature drops, equipment failures, and the spectral figure of a grieving mother known as the "White Lady."

#location
Stull Cemetery is situated off Route 1023 in the rural unincorporated community of Stull, Kansas, west-southwest of Lawrence.

#title
The Phantoms of the Devil's Washboard

#summary
Motorists traveling down a notoriously steep stretch of Kansas road report hearing the phantom screams of teenagers who tragically died there in a 1950s car crash. The spectral sounds echo across the hills as a chilling reminder of the fatal accident.

#details
In the 1950s, a group of teenagers engaging in high-speed racing across a series of three increasingly steep hills on South Donmyer Road south of Gypsum, Kansas—locally dubbed the "Devil's Washboard"—lost control of their vehicle, resulting in a fatal crash that claimed the lives of all seven teens. As a grim warning against street racing, local authorities initially left the mangled wreckage perched by the roadside. Over the decades, visitors and drivers traversing this lonely stretch of road have reported hearing disembodied screams and ghostly echoes of the crash in the dead of night.

#location
South Donmyer Road, specifically across the three steep hills known as the "Devil's Washboard" located just south of Gypsum, Kansas.

#title
The Restless Spirits of the Rookery at Fort Leavenworth

#summary
The Rookery, the oldest surviving residence on Fort Leavenworth, is plagued by multiple apparitions, including a hostile long-haired woman and a phantom child.

#details
Built in 1832 at Fort Leavenworth, Kansas, the duplex known as The Rookery has been continuously occupied by military personnel and is reputed to be the most haunted building in the state. Generations of residents have reported encounters with a variety of phantoms, most notably a violent long-haired woman who claws at unsuspecting visitors, an elderly woman chattering in the corner, a tantrum-throwing child, and an older man in a nightshirt roaming the halls. These restless spirits are believed to be tied to the deep, violent frontier history of the historic military post.

#location
The Rookery is located at 12 and 14 Summer Place on the historic grounds of Fort Leavenworth, Kansas.

#title
Theorosa's Bridge

#summary
A remote bridge spanning Jester Creek in Sedgwick County is haunted by the spirit of a distraught mother searching for her lost child. Visitors to the site have reported eerie glowing lights, vehicle malfunctions, and the mournful cries of a baby.

#details
According to local folklore, the bridge—crossing Jester Creek near 109th Street North and Meridian Street—has been plagued by ghostly activity for generations. Several conflicting legends attempt to explain the haunting, most involving a woman named Theorosa. One account tells of pioneer settlers attacked by Native Americans who kidnapped a baby named Theorosa, prompting the grief-stricken mother to search endlessly for her child. Another version suggests a Native American mother whose baby was dropped into the creek during a skirmish, while a third attributes the haunting to an unwed mother who drowned her infant in shame before taking her own life. Over the decades, the original wooden and iron structures mysteriously burned down in 1974 and 1976. Today, a modern concrete bridge stands in their place, where motorists still report sudden cold breezes, shaking cars, and disembodied voices echoing over the water.

#location
Theorosa's Bridge spans Jester Creek just west of 109th Street North and Meridian Street, located between Valley Center and Sedgwick in Sedgwick County, Kansas.

Haunted Kansas: Ghost Stories and Other Eerie Tales by Lisa Hefner Heitz (University Press of Kansas, 1997)
