#title
The Pittee Girls of the St. Augustine Lighthouse

#summary
Following a tragic 1873 construction accident where three young girls drowned in a supply cart, the historic Florida lighthouse and keepers' house have become renowned for paranormal activity attributed to the children's restless spirits.

#details
In 1871, construction began on a new tower for the St. Augustine Lighthouse to replace the aging Spanish watchtower. Hezekiah Pittee, the superintendent of construction, moved his family from Maine to live on-site, including his daughters Mary, Eliza, and Carrie. The children frequently turned the railway supply cart—used to haul materials from Salt Run—into a makeshift Victorian rollercoaster, riding it down to the water. On July 10, 1873, the safety stop board was left unplaced, and the cart flipped into the water, trapping four girls underneath and resulting in the drowning deaths of three of them. In the decades following the tragedy and subsequent restoration of the burned keepers' house, keepers, staff, and visitors have repeatedly reported unexplained phenomena. Witnesses have heard ghostly giggling at the top of the tower, felt their shoelaces inexplicably tied, encountered playful energy during games of hide-and-seek, and sighted full apparitions of young girls in Victorian attire.

#location
The St. Augustine Lighthouse is located at 81 Lighthouse Avenue in St. Augustine, Florida.
