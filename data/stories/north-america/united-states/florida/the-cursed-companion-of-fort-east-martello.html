The Cursed Companion of Fort East Martello

Summary

A handmade, sailor-suited doll given to a young Key West boy at the turn of the 20th century is said to be possessed by malevolent spirits, causing electronic malfunctions and moving on its own.

Details

Around 1904, a young boy named Robert Eugene "Gene" Otto was given a 40-inch tall, excelsior-stuffed doll by a household servant. Gene and the doll became inseparable, and whenever misbehavior occurred, young Gene would famously blame the doll, saying, "I didn't do it. Robert did it." After Gene grew up, became an artist, and passed away in 1974, subsequent owners and tenants of his former home reported hearing footsteps in the attic, giggling, and seeing the doll move across rooms unassisted. In 1994, the doll was donated to the Fort East Martello Museum. Since going on display, visitors and staff have reported that cameras and electronics malfunction in its presence, and daily letters arrive from people apologizing to the doll for disrespect or asking for its forgiveness.
