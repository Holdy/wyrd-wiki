#title
Robert the Doll

#summary
A handmade doll given to a young boy in Key West at the turn of the 20th century became the center of a chilling local legend involving unexplained movements, giggling, and lifelong paranormal activity.

#details
In 1904, four-year-old Robert Eugene "Gene" Otto received a life-sized, sailor-suited doll from a household servant. The boy named the doll after himself, and the two became inseparable companions. Throughout his childhood, Gene frequently blamed the doll for mischief and household disruptions, claiming, "I didn't do it. Robert did it." As Gene grew into an artist and eventually passed away in 1974, subsequent residents of the Otto home reported hearing phantom footsteps in the attic, mysterious giggling, and watching the doll shift position and change facial expressions on its own. In 1994, the doll was donated to the Fort East Martello Museum in Key West, Florida, where staff and visitors continue to report electronic malfunctions, unexplained occurrences, and misfortune befalling those who disrespect the doll without asking permission.

#location
The Fort East Martello Museum located at 3501 South Roosevelt Boulevard in Key West, Florida, where Robert the Doll is permanently housed.

#title
The Cursed Companion of Fort East Martello

#summary
A handmade, sailor-suited doll given to a young Key West boy at the turn of the 20th century is said to be possessed by malevolent spirits, causing electronic malfunctions and moving on its own.

#details
Around 1904, a young boy named Robert Eugene "Gene" Otto was given a 40-inch tall, excelsior-stuffed doll by a household servant. Gene and the doll became inseparable, and whenever misbehavior occurred, young Gene would famously blame the doll, saying, "I didn't do it. Robert did it." After Gene grew up, became an artist, and passed away in 1974, subsequent owners and tenants of his former home reported hearing footsteps in the attic, giggling, and seeing the doll move across rooms unassisted. In 1994, the doll was donated to the Fort East Martello Museum. Since going on display, visitors and staff have reported that cameras and electronics malfunction in its presence, and daily letters arrive from people apologizing to the doll for disrespect or asking for its forgiveness.

#title
The Ghosts of the I-4 Dead Zone

#summary
Unexplained apparitions, ghostly hitchhikers, and disembodied voices haunt a stretch of Interstate 4 where a 19th-century yellow fever cemetery was paved over. Motorists traversing this highway frequently experience terrifying paranormal phenomena and tragic accidents.

#details
In the late 19th century, a group of German immigrants established St. Joseph’s Catholic Colony along the southern shore of Lake Monroe. In 1887, a devastating yellow fever outbreak struck the settlement, claiming multiple lives and leaving the deceased buried in the surrounding woods without proper last rites. When Interstate 4 was constructed years later, this burial ground was bulldozed and paved over without relocating the graves. Ever since, drivers crossing the I-4 bridge experience bizarre electronic interference, floating orbs of light, and apparitions of men and women standing by the roadside or hitchhiking, only to vanish into thin air.

#location
The quarter-mile stretch of Interstate 4 where the highway crosses the St. John's River in Seminole County, Florida.

#title
The Haunting of Captain Tony's Saloon

#summary
Key West’s oldest bar, built around a historic gallows tree and former morgue, is famously haunted by the spirits of individuals buried beneath its floorboards, including an executed young woman named Elvira Drew.

#details
Operating in a building that has served as a morgue, bordello, and speakeasy, Captain Tony's Saloon in Key West features an indoor tree that once served as a gallows where dozens of criminals and pirates were hanged. During 1980s renovations, workers uncovered the skeletal remains of 15 to 18 people beneath the floorboards, alongside gravestones embedded in the floor. One prominent gravestone belongs to Elvira Drew, a 19-year-old woman who was hanged in 1822 for killing her abusive, alcoholic husband. Patrons and staff recount eerie encounters with Elvira and other restless spirits who linger among the bar's historic artifacts and gravestones.

#location
Captain Tony's Saloon is located at 428 Greene Street in Key West, Florida.

#title
The Phantom of the Tampa Theatre

#summary
A historic 1920s movie palace in downtown Tampa is said to be haunted by the ghost of a long-time projectionist who died after working in the booth and a mysterious apparition that occupies Seat 308.

#details
Opened in October 1926 as one of America's most elaborate movie palaces, the Tampa Theatre developed a reputation for ghostly encounters over the decades. The most famous resident spirit is Foster "Fink" Finley, who worked as a projectionist at the theater from 1930 until 1965 and reportedly suffered a fatal heart attack shortly after leaving his post. Projectionists and staff working in the booth have since reported unexplained activity and sightings attributed to Fink. Additionally, theater directors and staff have reported encounters with an unidentified man in a fedora sitting in Seat 308 when the building is empty and closed to the general public.

#location
Tampa Theatre is located at 711 N Franklin Street in downtown Tampa, Florida.

#title
The Pittee Girls of the St. Augustine Lighthouse

#summary
Following a tragic 1873 construction accident where three young girls drowned in a supply cart, the historic Florida lighthouse and keepers' house have become renowned for paranormal activity attributed to the children's restless spirits.

#details
In 1871, construction began on a new tower for the St. Augustine Lighthouse to replace the aging Spanish watchtower. Hezekiah Pittee, the superintendent of construction, moved his family from Maine to live on-site, including his daughters Mary, Eliza, and Carrie. The children frequently turned the railway supply cart—used to haul materials from Salt Run—into a makeshift Victorian rollercoaster, riding it down to the water. On July 10, 1873, the safety stop board was left unplaced, and the cart flipped into the water, trapping four girls underneath and resulting in the drowning deaths of three of them. In the decades following the tragedy and subsequent restoration of the burned keepers' house, keepers, staff, and visitors have repeatedly reported unexplained phenomena. Witnesses have heard ghostly giggling at the top of the tower, felt their shoelaces inexplicably tied, encountered playful energy during games of hide-and-seek, and sighted full apparitions of young girls in Victorian attire.

#location
The St. Augustine Lighthouse is located at 81 Lighthouse Avenue in St. Augustine, Florida.

#title
The Pittee Sisters of the St. Augustine Lighthouse

#summary
Three young construction foreman's daughters tragically drowned in 1873 when their railway cart tipped into the water, and their playful spirits are said to still haunt the lighthouse and keepers' house.

#details
During the construction of the new St. Augustine Lighthouse tower in July 1873, the young daughters of superintendent Hezekiah Pittee—along with another local girl—frequently rode a supply railway cart down to the water as a makeshift rollercoaster. On July 10, 1873, with the stopping block removed, the cart flipped into the water, trapping and drowning three of the girls. In the decades since, both visitors and staff have reported numerous ghostly phenomena attributed to the children, including mysterious giggling in the empty tower, shoelaces tied together on the stairs, and electrical spikes on EMF meters in the charred basement of the keepers' house.

#location
The St. Augustine Lighthouse and Maritime Museum is located at 81 Lighthouse Avenue in St. Augustine, Florida.

#title
The Sentinels of the Pensacola Lighthouse

#summary
Standing tall since 1859 on the Pensacola Naval Air Station, this historic lighthouse survived Civil War bombardment and severe storms, and is widely rumored to be haunted by a runaway slave and a former murder victim.

#details
Authorized by Congress and completed in 1858, the Pensacola Lighthouse weathered the trials of the Civil War—including being targeted by Union artillery cannon fire from Fort Pickens in 1861—as well as hurricanes, lightning strikes, and earthquakes. Beyond its rich military and maritime history, local folklore maintains that the lighthouse is haunted by restless phantoms, most notably a runaway slave who sought refuge in the tower and the spirit of an earlier murder victim whose tragic demise left an enduring mark on the structure.

#location
The Pensacola Lighthouse is located on the Pensacola Naval Air Station in Pensacola, Florida.

#title
The Spirits of the St. Augustine Lighthouse

#summary
Built in the 1870s on Anastasia Island, this historic brick lighthouse and its keeper's house are famously haunted by the children who tragically drowned in a construction accident and a former keeper who fell to his death.

#details
Construction of the current 165-foot tower began in 1871 and was completed in 1874. During the building process on July 10, 1873, a rail cart carrying materials rolled into the water, resulting in the drowning deaths of two of the site supervisor's daughters, Mary and Eliza Pittee, along with an unnamed companion. Ever since, visitors and staff have reported hearing children's laughter, small footsteps, and doors moving on their own in the keeper's house. Furthermore, stories link shadows and staircase phenomena to Joseph Andreu, a head keeper who fell to his death from an earlier tower while painting it in 1859.

#location
The St. Augustine Lighthouse is located at 81 Lighthouse Avenue on Anastasia Island in St. Augustine, Florida.
