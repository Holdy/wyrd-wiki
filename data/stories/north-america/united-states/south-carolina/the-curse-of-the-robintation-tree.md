#title
The Curse of the Robintation Tree

#summary
A protective curse guarded a historic South Carolina plantation house as long as a landmark live oak remained untouched. Meanwhile, a shape-shifting specter haunted the roadway adjacent to the tree.

#details
Situated along the Cooper River at Comingtee Plantation, local folklore centered on a prominent live oak known as the "Robintation Tree." According to tradition, the tree grew near an indigenous burial site, and a protective curse dictated that as long as the tree stood untouched, no serious harm could ever befall those living on the property. Over the years, root doctors, bee hunters, and visitors chipped away at the tree and its roots, leading to fences being erected to protect it. Additionally, the tree and surrounding road were said to be haunted by a peculiar shape-shifting spirit—a robintation—which would appear as a small animal like a cat or dog before swelling to the size of a hog and then a calf as travelers watched.

#location
The ruins of Comingtee Plantation and the site of the Robintation Tree are located along the Cooper River within the Bonneau Ferry Wildlife Management Area in Berkeley County, South Carolina.
