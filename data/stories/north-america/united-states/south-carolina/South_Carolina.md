#title
Alice of The Hermitage

#summary
Heartbroken and untimely deceased in the 1840s, Alice Flagg's spirit is said to wander her former home and nearby cemeteries searching for her lost engagement ring.

#details
Dating back to the 1840s, the legend of Alice Flagg tells of a young woman from Georgetown County who fell deeply in love with a man her family deemed socially unacceptable. When her brother discovered a secret engagement ring worn around her neck on a ribbon, he violently tore it away and threw it into the nearby marsh. Shortly afterward, Alice fell ill with a fever and died with a broken heart. Ever since, her ghostly apparition has been reported wandering near the historic Hermitage plantation, along the marshbanks of Murrells Inlet, and among the grounds of All Saints Cemetery in Pawleys Island, still searching for her lost ring.

#location
All Saints Cemetery and the grounds of The Hermitage in Pawleys Island, South Carolina.

#title
The Curse of the Robintation Tree

#summary
A protective curse guarded a historic South Carolina plantation house as long as a landmark live oak remained untouched. Meanwhile, a shape-shifting specter haunted the roadway adjacent to the tree.

#details
Situated along the Cooper River at Comingtee Plantation, local folklore centered on a prominent live oak known as the "Robintation Tree." According to tradition, the tree grew near an indigenous burial site, and a protective curse dictated that as long as the tree stood untouched, no serious harm could ever befall those living on the property. Over the years, root doctors, bee hunters, and visitors chipped away at the tree and its roots, leading to fences being erected to protect it. Additionally, the tree and surrounding road were said to be haunted by a peculiar shape-shifting spirit—a robintation—which would appear as a small animal like a cat or dog before swelling to the size of a hog and then a calf as travelers watched.

#location
The ruins of Comingtee Plantation and the site of the Robintation Tree are located along the Cooper River within the Bonneau Ferry Wildlife Management Area in Berkeley County, South Carolina.

#title
The Ghost of Alice Flagg

#summary
A tragic 19th-century plantation heiress forbidden from marrying a common laborer dies of illness after her brother rips away her secret engagement ring, causing her restless spirit to forever search for the lost token along the South Carolina coast.

#details
In 1849, Alice Belin Flagg lived at her family's coastal estate, The Hermitage, near Murrells Inlet and Pawleys Island, South Carolina. She fell deeply in love with a local lumber man named John Braddock, defying the rigid expectations of her aristocratic family. When her brother and patriarch, Dr. Allard Flagg, fiercely opposed the romance and sent Alice away to boarding school in Charleston, John gave her a plain gold engagement ring inscribed with "Love never fails, John," which she wore on a ribbon around her neck. Falling gravely ill with fever, Alice was brought home to her family estate, where Allard discovered the hidden ring and angrily threw it into a nearby creek. Alice died of her illness shortly thereafter while frantically searching for her missing token, and her ghost is said to still haunt both The Hermitage and All Saints Cemetery, eternally searching for her lost ring.

#location
All Saints' Waccamaw Episcopal Church is located along Ocean Highway (U.S. Route 17) in Pawleys Island, Georgetown County, South Carolina.

#title
The Ghost of Zoe

#summary
Grieving the loss of her sister, spinster Zoe St. Amand haunts her former Queen Street home, now operating as a historic Charleston restaurant.

#details
Zoe St. Amand and her sister Elizabeth lived together as spinsters for over sixty years in a Victorian-era house on Queen Street in Charleston. After Elizabeth passed away following a brief illness, Zoe struggled deeply with the loss and was eventually moved to a nursing home, where she died in 1954. Decades later, when the building was renovated and opened as Poogan's Porch restaurant, Zoe's spirit reportedly made her displeasure known. Staff and patrons have frequently encountered her apparition—peering around corners, walking through dining rooms, stepping through the front door like a customer, and occasionally startling or pushing guests.

#location
Poogan's Porch restaurant at 72 Queen Street in Charleston, South Carolina.

#title
The Gray Man of Pawleys Island

#summary
A spectral figure in a gray coat wanders the beaches and sand dunes of coastal South Carolina to warn residents of impending hurricanes. Those who heed his sudden appearance and evacuate are rewarded with minimal property damage.

#details
According to local coastal lore dating back to the 19th century, the Gray Man is believed to be the spirit of a young man named Beauregard who tragically sank into quicksand and died while rushing on horseback to visit his fiancée. Over the decades, visitors walking the shoreline during hurricane season have reported encountering a silent figure of medium build dressed entirely in gray, who abruptly vanishes into the sea mist and leaves behind no footprints other than the observer's. Sightings of the phantom have frequently preceded devastating storms, most notably Hurricane Hazel, with homeowners who heeded the warning returning to find their properties miraculously spared amid widespread devastation.

#location
The sandy beaches and dunes of Pawleys Island, located along the Atlantic coast of Georgetown County, South Carolina.

#title
The Legend of Julia Legare

#summary
Entombed alive after falling into a death-like coma in the mid-19th century, young Julia Legare's spirit is said to repeatedly unlatch and unhinge her family mausoleum door.

#details
In the mid-1800s, Julia Legare fell severely ill while visiting family on Edisto Island and slipped into a coma. Believing her to be dead, the local physician declared her deceased, and she was hurriedly buried in the family mausoleum at the Edisto Island Presbyterian Church. Fifteen years later, when the tomb was opened for another family member, Julia's remains were found crumpled at the base of the door, revealing the horrifying truth that she had woken up from her coma inside the tomb. Ever since, the heavy marble door of the mausoleum has continually swung open, resisting chains, locks, and even heavy machinery, leading locals to believe Julia's ghost guards the tomb to ensure no door is ever placed on it again.

#location
The J.B. Legare mausoleum at the Edisto Island Presbyterian Church graveyard on Edisto Island, South Carolina.

#title
The Phantom Hitchhiker of Wateree River Bridge

#summary
Since the 1930s, a young woman has been seen hitchhiking on a bridge over the Wateree River, engaging drivers in conversation before vanishing just before reaching the end of her journey.

#details
Dating back to the 1930s when the bridge was newly built, motorists traveling westbound across the Wateree River Swamp Bridge have frequently encountered a young female hitchhiker. Looking to catch a ride to Columbia to check on her ill mother, the woman appears completely solid and engages in normal, pleasant conversation with her driver. However, right before the vehicle reaches the end of the bridge, she inexplicably disappears from the passenger seat, doomed to repeat her journey over and over again through the decades.

#location
The Wateree River Swamp Bridge carrying US-378 between Sumter and Columbia, near Eastover, South Carolina.

#title
The Shuttered Echoes of the Dr. John Lining House

#summary
A historic Charleston home built during the colonial era is believed to be haunted by a woman traumatized by the Yamasee Indian uprising, whose spirit turns off lamps on the third floor.

#details
The Dr. John Lining House is one of Charleston’s oldest surviving structures, constructed between 1690 and 1715 on high ground near King Street. According to family history and deed records, the early 18th-century female owner survived the terrifying Yamasee War of 1715, while her family outside the city walls perished in brutal fashion. Her mind left shattered by the trauma, her presence allegedly lingers in the building's upper rooms. Decades later, when a descendant lived in the third-floor children's quarters, his bedside lamp would repeatedly and inexplicably switch itself off precisely at 10:00 p.m., even when plugged in and fully functional.

#location
The Dr. John Lining House is located at 106 Broad Street in downtown Charleston, South Carolina.

#title
The Tragic Search of Alice Flagg

#summary
A heartbroken young woman denied marriage to her true love died of a fever after her engagement ring was thrown away, and her ghost now wanders her former estate and churchyard searching for the token.

#details
In 1849, Alice Flagg lived with her family at The Hermitage in Murrells Inlet, South Carolina. She fell deeply in love with John Braddock, a common lumberman, much to the fury of her tyrannical brother and mother who demanded she marry within the aristocracy. When Alice was sent away to boarding school in Charleston, she secretly kept an engagement ring on a ribbon around her neck. Upon falling gravely ill and returning home, her brother discovered the ring, tore it from her neck in a rage, and threw it into a creek. Alice died shortly thereafter in profound grief, and ever since, her spirit has been seen roaming the grounds and All Saints' churchyard in a white dress, endlessly searching for her lost ring.

#location
All Saints’ Waccamaw Episcopal Church graveyard is located off Kings River Road near Pawleys Island in Georgetown County, South Carolina.

#title
The Vault of the Chapel of Ease and the Lands End Light

#summary
Following an 1861 wartime desecration of an elaborate family mausoleum on St. Helena Island, unexplainable phenomena and a mysterious phantom lantern light have haunted the local roads and churchyard.

#details
Built around 1740, the Chapel of Ease on St. Helena Island was abandoned when Union troops invaded in November 1861. A decade earlier, Edgar and Eliza Fripp had been interred in an elaborate stone mausoleum in the church graveyard. During the occupation, soldiers broke into the vault in search of treasure, ruining the door, which workers subsequently sealed with bricks. Bizarrely, the next morning the bricks were found neatly stacked outside the vault, a cycle that repeated despite multiple attempts to seal it, convincing workers supernatural forces were at work. Nearby along Lands End Road, drivers have long reported encountering the "Lands End Light," a solitary floating lantern light that approaches vehicles before abruptly vanishing into thin air.

#location
The Chapel of Ease ruins and graveyard are located off Lands End Road on St. Helena Island in Beaufort County, South Carolina.
