#title
The Hauntings of the St. James Hotel

#summary
Established in 1872 during the lawless days of the American frontier, the St. James Hotel in Cimarron, New Mexico, witnessed dozens of violent deaths and now hosts numerous restless spirits, most notably the malevolent ghost of murdered poker player Thomas James Wright in Room 18.

#details
Built by Henri Lambert—a former chef to President Abraham Lincoln—the Lambert Inn (later renamed the St. James Hotel) quickly became a notorious hotspot for outlaws, gunfighters, and travelers along the Santa Fe Trail. During its wild early decades, the saloon and hotel were the backdrop for at least 26 murders, leaving 22 bullet holes still embedded in the dining room ceiling. Legendary figures such as Wyatt Earp, Jesse James, Doc Holliday, and Buffalo Bill Cody all stayed within its walls. 

Over the years, numerous ghost stories and paranormal encounters became woven into the local legend of the hotel. The second floor is said to be the most active zone, haunted by the lingering scent of cigar smoke and phantom entities. Room 17 is frequently visited by the spirit of Mary Elizabeth Lambert, Henry's second wife, who is said to protect the hotel and whose room often fills with the scent of rose perfume or an insistent window tapping. Most famously, Room 18 is permanently locked to guests because it is haunted by Thomas James Wright, an ill-tempered gambler who was shot in the back and killed in the doorway immediately after winning the hotel in a poker game. Witnesses have reported violent cold spots, angry orange light anomalies, and physical pushing inside the room. Additionally, visitors have reported phantom card games in the hallways and unexplained apparitions of 19th-century travelers.

#location
The St. James Hotel is located at 617 S Collison Ave in Cimarron, New Mexico.
