#title
The Ghost of Room 18 at the St. James Hotel

#summary
Built in 1872 by former presidential chef Henri Lambert, the St. James Hotel in Cimarron witnessed dozens of murders during the Wild West era, and its locked Room 18 is said to be haunted by a card player who was murdered after winning the hotel in a poker game.

#details
The St. James Hotel was established by Henri Lambert, who previously served as a chef to Abraham Lincoln. During Cimarron's lawless boomtown days, the hotel and its saloon were the backdrop for at least 26 murders, leaving bullet holes still visible in the pressed-tin ceiling of the dining room. Among its most notorious legends is Room 18, which is kept locked to protect guests from the malevolent spirit of Thomas James Wright. Wright was shot in the back and killed just outside the room after winning the rights to the hotel in a poker game. Visitors and staff have reported harrowing encounters in the room, including being pushed, seeing floating balls of colored light, and experiencing phantom poker games and cold spots throughout the hallways.

#location
The St. James Hotel is located at 617 S. Collison Avenue in Cimarron, New Mexico.

#title
The Ghost of the KiMo Theatre

#summary
A historic theater in Albuquerque is haunted by the spirit of a young boy killed in a 1951 explosion, requiring regular offerings of doughnuts to keep performances running smoothly.

#details
Opened in 1927 and designed in the distinctive Pueblo Deco architectural style, the KiMo Theatre in Albuquerque became a celebrated cultural landmark. In 1951, tragedy struck when a boiler exploded in the lobby, killing a six-year-old boy named Bobby Darnall who had fled the balcony in fright. According to local folklore, Bobby's ghost continues to linger in the historic building, playing pranks on staff and tripping performers. To appease his mischievous spirit and prevent technical mishaps during stage productions, cast members traditionally hang doughnuts on a backstage water pipe as an offering.

#location
The KiMo Theatre is located at 423 Central Ave. N.W., Albuquerque, New Mexico.

The Ghostly Gambler of La Fonda on the Plaza

#summary
Standing on a historic Santa Fe site that has hosted inns since 1607, the La Fonda Hotel is haunted by the spirit of a desperate salesman who leaped down a well after losing his company's money in a card game.

#details
La Fonda sits at the historic terminus of the Santa Fe Trail, a location that has housed lodging and gambling dens since the early 1600s, witnessing numerous brawls, court sessions, and hangings. Decades ago, a traveling salesman who lost his entire company payroll in a game of chance became so distraught that he threw himself down a deep well located right outside the gambling hall. Today, the hotel's La Plazuela dining room sits directly over that former well. Both guests and employees have reported seeing a ghostly apparition walk to the center of the dining room before vanishing into the floor, while others have encountered mysterious figures walking third-floor hallways near the old stairwells.

#title
The Haunting of La Fonda Hotel

#summary
The historic La Fonda Hotel in Santa Fe is haunted by several restless spirits, most notably a distraught salesman who leapt into a well after losing his company's funds.

#details
Standing at the termination of the historic Santa Fe Trail, the site of the La Fonda Hotel has hosted travelers and inns since the early 1600s. Over the centuries, the location witnessed numerous violent episodes, including lynchings and the 1867 fatal shooting of Territorial Supreme Court Chief Justice John P. Slough in the lobby. Over a century ago, a traveling salesman who lost all of his company's money in a card game became so distraught that he jumped to his death down a deep well located just outside the gambling hall. Today, the hotel's La Plazuela dining room sits directly over that old well. Guests and staff have frequently reported seeing a ghostly figure walk to the center of the room, re-enact the fatal jump, and vanish into the floorboards. Other reported apparitions include a tall man in a long black coat who wanders the upper hallways and disappears into stairwells.

#location
La Fonda Hotel is located at 100 E. San Francisco Street in the historic downtown district of Santa Fe, New Mexico.

#title
The Hauntings of the St. James Hotel

#summary
Established in 1872 during the lawless days of the American frontier, the St. James Hotel in Cimarron, New Mexico, witnessed dozens of violent deaths and now hosts numerous restless spirits, most notably the malevolent ghost of murdered poker player Thomas James Wright in Room 18.

#details
Built by Henri Lambert—a former chef to President Abraham Lincoln—the Lambert Inn (later renamed the St. James Hotel) quickly became a notorious hotspot for outlaws, gunfighters, and travelers along the Santa Fe Trail. During its wild early decades, the saloon and hotel were the backdrop for at least 26 murders, leaving 22 bullet holes still embedded in the dining room ceiling. Legendary figures such as Wyatt Earp, Jesse James, Doc Holliday, and Buffalo Bill Cody all stayed within its walls. 

Over the years, numerous ghost stories and paranormal encounters became woven into the local legend of the hotel. The second floor is said to be the most active zone, haunted by the lingering scent of cigar smoke and phantom entities. Room 17 is frequently visited by the spirit of Mary Elizabeth Lambert, Henry's second wife, who is said to protect the hotel and whose room often fills with the scent of rose perfume or an insistent window tapping. Most famously, Room 18 is permanently locked to guests because it is haunted by Thomas James Wright, an ill-tempered gambler who was shot in the back and killed in the doorway immediately after winning the hotel in a poker game. Witnesses have reported violent cold spots, angry orange light anomalies, and physical pushing inside the room. Additionally, visitors have reported phantom card games in the hallways and unexplained apparitions of 19th-century travelers.

#location
The St. James Hotel is located at 617 S Collison Ave in Cimarron, New Mexico.

#title
The Mad Trucker of the Devil's Highway

#summary
Drivers traveling late at night along a remote desert highway in New Mexico have reported terrifying encounters with a relentless, speeding semi-truck piloted by a deranged phantom driver.

#details
Spanning the barren stretches of what was once designated as U.S. Route 666 (now U.S. Route 491) in New Mexico, motorists often find themselves driving alone in the dark when a massive semi-truck suddenly appears. Moving at breakneck speeds well over 100 miles per hour, the truck straddles the center of the asphalt, barreling directly toward oncoming vehicles. According to persistent local legends, the phantom vehicle is driven by a homicidally insane trucker—sometimes described as wreathed in flames or driven by malevolent intent—who forces terrified drivers off the road before vanishing into the night.

#location
The New Mexico section of U.S. Route 491 (formerly U.S. Route 666), running north from Gallup toward the Colorado and Utah borders.

#title
The Saloon Girl of the Laguna Vista Lodge

#summary
Built in 1898 in Eagle Nest using stolen railroad ties, the Laguna Vista Saloon is haunted by the spirit of a stranded honeymooner-turned-saloon-girl who endlessly searches for her lost husband.

#details
Originally constructed as the El Monte Hotel, the Laguna Vista building in Eagle Nest served as a bustling hub for travelers and gamblers in the early 20th century. According to local folklore, a young woman staying at the hotel during her honeymoon was left destitute when her husband went hunting in the mountains and never returned, forcing her to work as a saloon girl to survive. Her restless spirit allegedly remains in the building, manifesting as a phantom piano that plays by itself, dining chairs that repeatedly slide back to the instrument, and kitchen anomalies where equipment operates without power and kitchen staff experience flying kitchenware and disembodied warnings.

#location
The Laguna Vista Lodge is located in Eagle Nest, New Mexico.

#title
The Spectral Mistress of the Staab House

#summary
The historic Staab Mansion at La Posada Hotel in Santa Fe is said to be haunted by Julia Staab, who fell into a profound depression following the death of her newborn child and lived as a recluse until her death in 1896.

#details
Abraham and Julia Staab built a magnificent three-story French Second Empire mansion in 1882 following Abraham's accumulation of wealth supplying the U.S. Army during the Civil War. Their lives took a tragic turn when their seventh child died shortly after birth, plunging Julia into a lifelong grief that resulted in her remaining isolated on the upper floor until she passed away at age 52. Ever since, staff and guests have reported sightings of Julia's translucent figure near fireplaces and grand rooms, along with poltergeist activity such as flying drinking glasses, self-activating fireplaces, and waitstaff being pushed from underneath their serving trays.

#location
La Posada Hotel is located at 330 East Palace Avenue in Santa Fe, New Mexico.

#title
The Spirit of Bobby Darnall at the KiMo Theatre

#summary
The historic KiMo Theatre in Albuquerque is said to be haunted by a young boy named Bobby Darnall, who tragically perished in a 1951 boiler explosion.

#details
Opened in September 1927 and designed in the flamboyant Pueblo Deco architectural style, the KiMo Theatre became a beloved cultural landmark in downtown Albuquerque. On August 2, 1951, tragedy struck when a water heater exploded beneath the lobby concession stand. Six-year-old Bobby Darnall, who had been sitting in the balcony and ran down to the lobby after being frightened by a film, was killed in the blast. Since the disaster, Bobby's mischievous spirit has haunted the theatre. He is known to play pranks on performers and technical crew members, tripping people or causing unexplained commotions. To maintain peace during productions, cast members routinely leave offerings of candy, toys, and doughnuts on the water pipe behind the stage. According to local lore, whenever these offerings are neglected or removed, productions are plagued by immediate lighting, sound, and technical disasters until the treats are restored.

#location
KiMo Theatre is located at 423 Central Ave. N.W. in downtown Albuquerque, New Mexico.
