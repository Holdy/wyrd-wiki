#title
The Ghost of the KiMo Theatre

#summary
A historic theater in Albuquerque is haunted by the spirit of a young boy killed in a 1951 explosion, requiring regular offerings of doughnuts to keep performances running smoothly.

#details
Opened in 1927 and designed in the distinctive Pueblo Deco architectural style, the KiMo Theatre in Albuquerque became a celebrated cultural landmark. In 1951, tragedy struck when a boiler exploded in the lobby, killing a six-year-old boy named Bobby Darnall who had fled the balcony in fright. According to local folklore, Bobby's ghost continues to linger in the historic building, playing pranks on staff and tripping performers. To appease his mischievous spirit and prevent technical mishaps during stage productions, cast members traditionally hang doughnuts on a backstage water pipe as an offering.

#location
The KiMo Theatre is located at 423 Central Ave. N.W., Albuquerque, New Mexico.
