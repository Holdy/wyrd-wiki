#title
The Bell Witch Haunting

#summary
Between 1817 and 1821, a farming family in Middle Tennessee was subjected to relentless physical and verbal harassment by an invisible, shapeshifting entity.

#details
Starting in 1817 on the Bell family farm near the Red River in Adams, Tennessee, patriarch John Bell Sr. encountered a strange animal that vanished into thin air. This sighting was followed by eerie noises inside the family home, including knocking, gnawing sounds, and dragging chains. The unseen entity—later dubbed "Kate" or the Bell Witch—became increasingly aggressive, pulling hair, slapping, and pinching family members, particularly John Bell and his daughter Betsy. The spirit eventually learned to speak fluently, holding conversations, singing hymns, and famously reciting two different sermons word-for-word from miles away at the exact same time. The torment culminated in the mysterious poisoning and death of John Bell in December 1820, after which the entity gradually quieted and vanished.

#location
The historic Bell family farm and cave property located along the Red River in Adams, Robertson County, Tennessee.

#upstream-source
An Authenticated History of the Famous Bell Witch by Martin V. Ingram (1894).

#title
The Bell Witch of Robertson County

#summary
Between 1817 and 1821, John Bell and his family were relentlessly terrorized by a mysterious, shape-shifting invisible spirit that eventually led to Bell's untimely death.

#details
From 1817 to 1821, the Bell family farm in Adams, Tennessee, became the center of a terrifying haunting by a poltergeist known as the Bell Witch. The entity began with strange knocking and gnawing noises, before escalating to physical assaults on John Bell and his daughter Betsy, as well as conversing openly with the family. Even farm workers and visiting friends experienced encounters with dog-like apparitions and invisible entities. After three years of constant torment, John Bell's health declined rapidly, and he died in December 1820, allegedly poisoned by the spirit. The hauntings ceased as mysteriously as they began once Bell passed away.

#location
The historic Bell Witch property is located along the Red River in the town of Adams, Robertson County, Tennessee.

#title
The Bell Witch of Tennessee

#summary
Between 1817 and 1821, John Bell and his family were terrorized on their remote farm by a malicious, invisible entity that engaged in physical abuse, uncanny vocal imitations, and ultimately contributed to Bell's death. The harrowing accounts attracted widespread attention throughout the region, including a rumored supernatural encounter involving future President Andrew Jackson.

#details
In 1804, John Bell and his family settled on a 1,000-acre farm along the Red River in Robertson County, Tennessee. Starting around 1817, the household began experiencing bizarre nocturnal disturbances, including scratching sounds on bedposts, chains dragging across floors, and the sound of wings flapping against ceilings. The unseen entity quickly escalated its torment, physically striking, pinching, and pulling the hair of family members—most frequently John Bell Sr. and his daughter Betsy. 

The spirit demonstrated uncanny abilities, such as perfectly reciting two different church sermons delivered twelve miles apart at the exact same hour. Despite attempts by local conjurers, neighbors, and brave volunteers to trap or exorcise the entity, all failed. The spirit harbored a fierce animosity toward John Bell Sr., subjecting him to relentless curses and physical suffering until he took to his bed. On December 19, 1820, John Jr. found a strange, smoky liquid in a medicine vial left by the spirit, which gloated that it had given a dose to "Old Jack." John Bell died the following day, and the spirit reportedly disrupted his funeral by singing bawdy drinking songs. Although activity diminished after his death, the legend persisted and became one of America's most famous ghost stories.

#location
The historic site of the Bell family farm and the Bell Witch Cave are located near the town of Adams along the Red River in Robertson County, Tennessee.

#upstream-source
*An Authenticated History of the Famous Bell Witch: The Wonder of the 19th Century, and Unexplained Phenomenon of the Christian Era* by M. V. Ingram (1894) and *A Mysterious Spirit: The Bell Witch of Tennessee* by Charles Bailey Bell.

#title
The Bloodstained Halls of Carnton Plantation

#summary
Serving as a Confederate field hospital during the Civil War's bloodiest hours, Carnton Plantation is famously haunted by phantom soldiers, weeping children, and the ghost of Carrie McGavock.

#details
The Carnton Plantation in Franklin, Tennessee, home of John and Carrie McGavock, became a field hospital during the devastating Battle of Franklin on November 30, 1864, where nearly 10,000 soldiers were killed or wounded. Carrie McGavock dedicated herself to caring for the wounded while her home absorbed the bloodshed—stains that remain visible on the wooden floors to this day. Today, visitors and staff report persistent paranormal phenomena on the grounds, including Confederate generals pacing the porches, children peering from upper-windows, the phantom sounds of charging horses and cannon fire, and the apparition of Carrie McGavock sitting on the porch in a pink gown gazing toward the adjoining soldiers' cemetery.

#location
Carnton Plantation is located at 1333 Carnton Lane in Franklin, Williamson County, Tennessee.

#title
The Haunting of the Bell Family

#summary
Between 1817 and 1821, John Bell and his family in Robertson County were relentlessly tormented by a mysterious and malevolent unseen entity known as the Bell Witch. The spirit's attacks and prophecies culminated in John Bell's mysterious death, making it one of America's most famous documented hauntings.

#details
In 1804, John Bell moved his family from North Carolina to a 320-acre farm along the Red River in Adams, Tennessee. Beginning in the summer of 1817, the family experienced bizarre disturbances, hearing eerie scratching noises and eventually a disembodied voice that spoke aloud in every room of the house. The entity specifically targeted John's youngest daughter, Betsy, subjecting her to violent beatings that left her unconscious. Word of the haunting spread rapidly, drawing hundreds of curious onlookers and investigators to the property. The spirit vowed to cause John Bell's death, which occurred on December 20, 1820, when a strange vial of liquid was discovered near his bed. 

#location
The Bell Witch Cave and Farm is located at 430 Keysburg Road in Adams, Robertson County, Tennessee.

#title
The Restless Spirits and Hound of Rotherwood Mansion

#summary
Built in 1818 in Kingsport, Rotherwood Mansion is haunted by the tragic spirit of a heartbroken woman and the malevolent ghost of a cruel former owner accompanied by a hellhound.

#details
Rotherwood Mansion was built in Kingsport, Tennessee, in 1818 by Frederick Ross. According to local lore, his daughter Rowena fell into a deep depression and drowned herself in the Holston River wearing her wedding dress after seeing the apparition of her first love, who had tragically drowned before their wedding. Following her death, the plantation was sold to Frederick's harsh bookkeeper, Phipps, whose cruelty left a dark mark on the estate. Generations later, renovation workers and visitors reported sightings of Rowena as a Lady in White, as well as terrifying encounters with Phipps materializing from walls alongside a massive black dog with glowing red eyes.

#location
Rotherwood Mansion sits near the Holston River in Kingsport, Sullivan County, Tennessee.

#title
The Specter of Room 311

#summary
Room 311 of the historic Read House Hotel in Chattanooga is said to be haunted by the spirit of Annalisa Netherly, a guest allegedly murdered there in 1927. Guests frequently report intense paranormal phenomena, including flickering lights, running water, and shadowy figures.

#details
According to local lore, in 1927, a woman named Annalisa Netherly checked into Room 311 at the Read House Hotel and was brutally killed in the bathroom by a jealous lover. Ever since, the room has maintained a reputation as one of the most haunted hotel spaces in the South. Visitors and overnight guests have documented persistent unexplained noises, phantom running water echoing from the vintage clawfoot tub, eerie cold spots, and sightings of Annalisa's apparition. The hotel has preserved several 1920s elements in the room, maintaining its historic atmosphere.

#location
The Read House Hotel is located at 107 W. Martin Luther King Jr. Boulevard in Chattanooga, Tennessee.

#title
The Torment and Curses of the Bell Family

#summary
A malicious spirit plagued John Bell and his family with relentless physical torments and curses over several years, ultimately resulting in Bell's mysterious death. The entity's attacks terrorized the homestead along the Red River until the family was broken and scattered.

#details
Beginning in 1817 on a farm along the Red River in Robertson County, Tennessee, a prosperous farmer named John Bell and his family were subjected to increasingly violent and bizarre disturbances by an invisible entity. The entity manifested as strange animal apparitions, emitted terrifying scratching and dragging noises, and physically beat, pinched, and pulled the hair of family members, particularly John Bell and his daughter Betsy. Despite visits from curious neighbors, future President Andrew Jackson, and local conjurers who were all mocked or driven off, the spirit continued its relentless harassment, culminating in vicious curses and the fatal poisoning of John Bell in December 1820 before eventually fading away.

#location
The historic Bell Witch Cave and former homestead property are located along the Red River in Adams, Tennessee.

#title
The Warning of Netherland Inn Road

#summary
Following a fatal accident in thick mountain fog, a tragic motorist's spirit continually haunts a Tennessee highway to caution passing drivers. His spectral figure can be seen along the roadside frantically waving his arms to warn travelers of perilous road conditions.

#details
Hugh Hamblen tragically lost his life in a car accident while crossing Netherland Inn Road to reach his waiting vehicle during a heavy mountain fog. Since that fateful night, motorists traveling along the misty highway have frequently reported encountering his restless ghost lingering near the shoulder. Appearing out of the dense vapor, Hamblen's apparition frantically waves his arms in an apparent attempt to warn drivers about the hazardous, low-visibility conditions that claimed his life.

#location
Netherland Inn Road in Kingsport, Tennessee, runs near the historic Netherland Inn and Rotherwood Mansion.
