#title
Stuckey's Bridge and Old Man Stuckey

#summary
According to local legend, a murderous innkeeper named Stuckey lured travelers to his riverside home in the late 19th century before being caught and hanged from a bridge over the Chunky River, where his ghost reportedly still haunts visitors.

#details
In rural Lauderdale County just outside Meridian, Mississippi, an old truss bridge spans the Chunky River. Local folklore holds that in the late 1800s, an outlaw named Stuckey—believed by some to be a former member of the notorious Dalton Gang—established an inn along the dirt stage road near the riverbanks. At dusk, Stuckey would walk to the bridge holding a lantern to lure exhausted travelers and merchants navigating the waterway. Once his guests fell asleep, Stuckey would murder them in their beds, steal their belongings, and bury their bodies along the edge of the river. After more than twenty victims disappeared, the local sheriff and a posse tracked down the killer, arrested him, and executed him by hanging him directly from the bridge. His lifeless body was left to swing over the river for five days before dropping into the water below. Although the original bridge was replaced in 1901 by the current metal truss bridge, visitors and ghost hunters frequently report paranormal activity, including the sight of an old man carrying a lantern along the water's edge, ghostly splashes of a body falling into the river, or the apparition of Stuckey's corpse swaying from the bridge's framework.

#location
Stuckey's Bridge spans the Chunky River on a rural road outside Meridian in Lauderdale County, Mississippi.

#title
The Ghosts of McRaven House

#summary
Built in 1797 in Vicksburg, Mississippi, McRaven House is widely regarded as one of the state's most haunted mansions, bearing the lingering spirits of a murderous highwayman, a teenage mother, and Civil War soldiers.

#details
Originally constructed in 1797 by highwayman Andrew Glass—who used the home to hide his stolen loot before being murdered by his jealous wife—McRaven House saw successive owners who met tragic ends. In 1836, Sheriff Stephen Howard purchased the estate for his 15-year-old pregnant wife, Mary Elizabeth, who tragically died in childbirth in the home's middle bedroom that same year. During the Civil War, owner John H. Bobb transformed the property into a Confederate field hospital, enduring cannon fire before being fatally shot nearby by Union troops in 1864. Today, visitors and staff frequently report sightings of Mary Elizabeth, Andrew Glass, and other former residents roaming the historic rooms and grounds.

#location
McRaven House is located at 1445 Harrison Street in Vicksburg, Mississippi.

#title
The Haunting of McRaven House

#summary
Built in 1797 by a notorious highwayman who met a violent end, McRaven House in Vicksburg became home to decades of tragedy, including a young bride's death in childbirth and Civil War bloodshed. Today, the historic mansion is widely regarded as one of the most haunted houses in Mississippi, inhabited by the restless spirits of its former occupants.

#details
The history of McRaven House began in 1797 when the first portion of the structure was built by Andrew Glass, a highwayman who used the remote home as a hideout after robbing travelers along the Natchez Trace. Glass met a violent end on the property, setting the stage for decades of supernatural lore. In 1836, Sheriff Stephen Howard purchased the home for himself and his 15-year-old pregnant wife, Mary Elizabeth, who tragically died in the middle bedroom while giving birth later that year. Her personal belongings, including her wedding shawl, remain in the house, and her apparition is frequently sighted. During the Civil War, owner John H. Bobb turned the house into a Confederate field hospital, and the home endured cannon fire before Bobb was fatally shot nearby by Union troops in 1864. Subsequent residents, the Murrays, also experienced multiple family deaths inside the building, leaving behind the spirits of William Murray and his daughters to roam the halls.

#location
McRaven House is located at 1445 Harrison Street in Vicksburg, Mississippi.

#title
The Phantom of Rowan Oak

#summary
Judith Sheegog, a resident of the historic Oxford home before William Faulkner purchased it, supposedly fell to her death from the balcony after a tragic romance with a Union soldier and continues to haunt the property.

#details
Before the famous author William Faulkner purchased the property known as Rowan Oak in 1930, the estate belonged to the Sheegog family. According to local folklore, a young woman named Judith Sheegog fell deeply in love with a wounded Union soldier who was being cared for at the home during the Civil War. Planning to elope with him, Judith allegedly either fell or threw herself from the front balcony on the night of their planned escape, dying from the fall. Her father buried her under a magnolia tree at the end of the walkway. Witnesses over the decades have reported seeing a figure in a long white gown standing in upstairs bedrooms before walking out onto the balcony and dissolving into the surrounding cedar trees.

#location
Rowan Oak is located at 916 Old Taylor Road in Oxford, Mississippi.

#title
The Phantoms of Chamberlain Road

#summary
Motorists traveling along Chamberlain Road in Gautier encounter supernatural pedestrians, including a phantom hitchhiker who claims to have died at the cemetery and a terrifying woman who leaps in front of passing vehicles.

#details
Franklin Cemetery, also known as the Garden of Hope Cemetery, is nestled among ancient live oaks just off Chamberlain Road in Gautier, Mississippi. Local lore warns drivers passing the hallowed grounds after dark to be on the lookout for eerie apparitions. Two prominent specters haunt the roadside: a hitchhiker known as the gravedigger, who flags down passing cars and vanishes after telling his driver that he died at the site; and Bloody Sarah, a malevolent spirit in a blood-stained dress who startles drivers by leaping directly into the path of their vehicles before vanishing with a chilling laugh.

#location
Franklin Cemetery is situated just off Chamberlain Road in Gautier, Jackson County, Mississippi.

#title
The Specter of Monmouth Historic Inn

#summary
Constructed in 1818 in Natchez, Mississippi, Monmouth Historic Inn is said to be haunted by its most famous resident, nineteenth-century Mississippi Governor John A. Quitman.

#details
Built in 1818 on twenty-six acres of forest, the Greek Revival mansion of Monmouth was home to General John A. Quitman, who served as Governor of Mississippi and a military hero. Following a long and painful illness, Quitman's life at the estate came to an end, and his heirs eventually sold the property after the devastation of the Civil War. After decades of disrepair, the estate was restored into a historic inn in 1978. Today, guests and staff members wandering the antebellum gardens and rooms report sensing the lingering spirit of General Quitman, who appears to continue inspecting and watching over his beloved home.

#location
Monmouth Historic Inn is located at 36 Melrose Avenue in Natchez, Mississippi.

#title
The Tragedy of Madeline at King's Tavern

#summary
A young woman named Madeline, murdered during a jealous rage by her employer's wife, reportedly haunts King's Tavern after her skeletal remains were discovered walled up behind a fireplace.

#details
Built in the 1760s using wood from flatboats along the Mississippi River, King's Tavern in Natchez is considered one of the oldest buildings in the state. Local legend states that a young woman named Madeline was hired by the original owner, Mr. King, and soon entered into an affair with him. Consumed by jealousy, Mrs. King had Madeline murdered and her body walled up inside the tavern alongside the murder weapon, a Spanish jeweled dagger. During renovations in the 1930s, three human skeletons were discovered behind the fireplace, adding chilling physical evidence to the local folklore. Today, staff and visitors report sightings of Madeline in old-fashioned clothing, unexplained sounds of a baby crying in the attic, and windows and mirrors reflecting ghostly apparitions.

#location
King's Tavern is located in Natchez, Mississippi.

#title
The Witch of Yazoo

#summary
A notorious witch who tortured fishermen swore a dying curse that she would return in twenty years to burn Yazoo City to the ground. Exactly twenty years after her burial in Glenwood Cemetery, a massive fire devastated the town and left her grave's iron chain broken.

#details
According to local folklore, an evil woman living near the banks of the Yazoo River lured fishermen into her hut, torturing and killing them before the local sheriff discovered their bodies. Fleeing into the swamp, she sank into quicksand and swore a vengeful curse that she would return in twenty years to burn Yazoo City to ashes. After she died, the townspeople buried her in Glenwood Cemetery, placing a heavy iron chain around her gravestone to keep her spirit trapped. Exactly twenty years later, on May 25, 1904, a catastrophic fire swept through Yazoo City and destroyed hundreds of homes and businesses, while witnesses found the iron chain around the witch's grave snapped in two.

#location
The witch's grave is located within Glenwood Cemetery in Yazoo City, Mississippi.

Willie Morris, *Good Old Boy and the Witch of Yazoo*
