#title
The Alkali Lake Monster

#summary
In 1921, newspaper reports emerged from Hay Springs, Nebraska, describing a gigantic, alligator-like creature surfacing in Walgren Lake, giving rise to decades of regional lake-monster folklore.

#details
First documented by the *Hay Springs News* in 1921, stories circulated among residents of a massive, unknown animal surfacing in what was then known as Alkali Lake in western Nebraska. By 1923, a local resident named J. A. Johnson reported a dramatic sighting of a forty-foot gray-brown creature resembling a massive alligator equipped with a horn on its head. The legend quickly gained wider notoriety when Nebraska newspaperman John G. Maher promoted the story to out-of-state newspapers, dubbing the beast *Giganticus Brutervious*. The tale became a permanent fixture of regional folklore, later being recorded by author Mari Sandoz in her biographical work *Old Jules* and featured by the Federal Writers' Project in 1938.

#location
Walgren Lake (formerly Alkali Lake), located just south of Hay Springs, Nebraska.

#upstream-source
Mari Sandoz (*Old Jules*); Federal Writers' Project (1938).

#title
The Haunted Seven Sisters Road

#summary
An old gravel road in Otoe County, Nebraska, is said to be haunted by the restless spirits of seven sisters who were murdered by their brother a century ago.

#details
According to local legend, a young man living on a farm southeast of Nebraska City became enraged following an argument with his family. Hiding in the woods, he subsequently forced or enticed each of his seven sisters to the top of seven surrounding hills, where he hanged them from trees. Although no official historical records substantiate the gruesome event, the local folklore persists. Years later, the hills were altered and Seven Sisters Road (now L Street) was constructed across the landscape. Today, motorists traveling down the dark, winding stretch of road frequently report unexplained phenomena, including cars mysteriously stalling, headlights dimming, speedometers freezing, and windows rolling up and down on their own. Visitors also claim to hear the terrified screams of women, ghostly bell ringing, and muffled whispers, while seeing shadowy figures and glowing red eyes in the darkness.

#location
Seven Sisters Road (L Street), located approximately five miles southeast of Nebraska City in Otoe County, Nebraska.

#title
The Haunting of the Captain Bailey House

#summary
Visitors to this historic seven-gabled home in Brownville report that doors refuse to stay shut due to the lingering presence of a Civil War captain and his wife.

#details
The Captain Bailey House was originally built near the Missouri River by Civil War officer Captain Benson M. Bailey. When the river began shifting its course and endangering the structure in 1877, the entire brick building was dismantled and rebuilt brick-by-brick at its current downtown location. According to local lore, Captain Bailey and his wife Rebecca were poisoned by a jealous neighbor. Today, locals and visitors report enduring supernatural phenomena in the house, most notably interior doors that mysteriously refuse to remain closed.

#location
The Captain Bailey House Museum is located at 404 Main Street in Brownville, Nebraska.

None.

#title
The Legend of Blackbird Hill

#summary
The ghost of a young woman murdered by her husband in 1849 is said to haunt a hilltop overlooking the Missouri River, where her chilling screams echo every October 17.

#details
In 1849, a young woman living atop Blackbird Hill unexpectedly reunited with her former fiancé whom she thought had died abroad years earlier. When her husband returned home and discovered she wished to leave him, he attacked her with a hunting knife and leaped with her in his arms off the cliff into the Missouri River. Today, the path where the murder occurred remains barren, and witnesses report hearing the woman's terrifying screams of agony on the anniversary of her death every October 17.

#location
Blackbird Hill is located eight miles north of Decatur, Nebraska, overlooking the Missouri River on the Omaha Indian Reservation.

#title
The Phantoms of Fort Omaha

#summary
Established in 1878, the historic military fort in Omaha carries a long history of restless spirits, ranging from a 19th-century inmate to a murdered nurse.

#details
Fort Omaha has accumulated numerous ghost stories since its founding in 1878. One of its earliest recurring specters was Peter Gronwold, an inmate from the Douglas County Poor Farm who served a local officer and suffered a fatal psychotic episode in 1890; his ghost reportedly haunted the fort for two decades. Over the years, other tragedies left their mark on the campus, including an 1918 gas explosion that killed soldiers and a 1945 murder in the fort's hospital where a nurse was killed. Later, during 1970s renovations, workers reported unexplained phantom knocking and tools mysteriously moving between rooms.

#location
Fort Omaha is located at 5730 N 30th Street in Omaha, Nebraska.

#title
The Salt Witch of the Nebraska Plains

#summary
A pillar of snowy salt on the Nebraska plains marks the site where an Indian chief encountered his deceased wife's spirit attacked by a wicked hag before both vanished into the earth.

#details
An Indian chief, grief-stricken over the death of his beloved wife, traveled far across the western prairie. While sleeping near the junction of the Saline and Platte rivers, he awoke to a wailing sound and witnessed an old hag attempting to murder a younger woman whose face he recognized as his dead wife. He struck down the hag with his tomahawk, but before he could embrace his wife, the earth opened up and swallowed both women, leaving behind a pristine pillar of salt. For years, local tribesmen gathered salt here while beating the ground with clubs to keep the custodial Salt Witch from causing evil.

#location
A pillar of salt located on the Nebraska plain, approximately 40 miles above the confluence of the Saline River and the Platte River.

#upstream-source
Charles M. Skinner, Myths and Legends of Our Own Land (1896).

#reference
https://www.legendsofamerica.com/ne-saltwitch/
