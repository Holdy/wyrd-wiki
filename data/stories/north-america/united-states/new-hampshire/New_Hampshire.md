#title
The Curse of Mount Chocorua

#summary
After his young son tragically died due to a settler's poison, Native American chief Chocorua cast a devastating dying curse from a mountain summit upon the white settlers and their land. Ever since, the mountain's upper slopes have remained barren and cursed, bringing ruin to crops, livestock, and homes in the region.

#details
Chief Chocorua lived in the White Mountains of New Hampshire and befriended a settler named Cornelius Campbell. One day, while Chocorua was away on tribal business, he asked Campbell to watch his young son. During his absence, the boy found and drank a bottle of poison that Campbell had set out for foxes, resulting in his death. Overcome with grief and anger, Chocorua retaliated by slaying Campbell's family, prompting local settlers to chase him up to the peak of a steep mountain. Cornered on a high boulder, Chocorua raised his hands to the sky and pronounced a fierce curse condemning the white men, their crops, dwellings, and cattle to destruction, before leaping to his death. Following his fall, the mountain's summit became barren and treeless, its waters toxic to livestock, and the surrounding area plagued by his enduring malediction.

#location
Mount Chocorua is a prominent peak located in the White Mountains of New Hampshire, spanning the towns of Tamworth and Albany.

#title
The Ghost of Crawford Notch

#summary
Betrayed and abandoned by her fiancé during the harsh winter of 1778, a young servant named Nancy Barton froze to death in the White Mountains while trying to track him down. Her restless spirit is said to still wander the rugged landscape near Crawford Notch.

#details
In the late 1770s, a young woman named Nancy Barton was employed as a servant in the household of Colonel Joseph Whipple in Jefferson, New Hampshire. Betrayed by her fiancé—a hired man who deserted her and reportedly stole her savings—Nancy set out on foot to pursue him southward through the unforgiving wilderness of the White Mountains. As she pressed into Crawford Notch, she was caught in a brutal winter storm. Searchers eventually discovered her frozen body near a stream, which was subsequently named Nancy’s Brook, not far from where the Notchland Inn now stands in Hart's Location. She was buried nearby in what is known as Nancy's Grave. According to local legend, Nancy never completed her pursuit in spirit; travelers, hikers, and drivers passing through Crawford Notch frequently report hearing disembodied weeping, feeling footsteps following them in the woods, or catching a glimpse of a young woman in eighteenth-century attire wandering the roadside.

#location
Nancy's Grave and Nancy's Brook are situated in Crawford Notch near the Notchland Inn in Hart's Location, New Hampshire.

#title
The Haunting of the Chase House

#summary
A historic 18th-century home and former orphanage in Portsmouth, New Hampshire, is said to be haunted by the restless spirit of a young child.

#details
Built in 1762 by merchant Stephen Chase, the stately yellow home later operated as the Chase Home for Children orphanage in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. In August 1893, a severe diphtheria outbreak struck the children living there, resulting in the tragic death of a young orphan girl. Over the years, local ghost lore expanded to claim that a young girl took her life in an upstairs room, leading to decades of paranormal reports, strange glowing orbs, and sightings of a ghostly child peering out from the windows.

#location
The historic Chase House is located at 1 Middle Street (at the corner of Court and Washington Streets) in Portsmouth, New Hampshire.

#title
The Legend of Ocean-Born Mary

#summary
In Henniker, New Hampshire, a historic homestead became the center of a enduring legend claiming the ghost of a woman born during a pirate attack haunts the property.

#details
Mary Wallace was born aboard a ship carrying Scotch-Irish immigrants to America in July 1720, just as pirates attacked the vessel. According to the legend, upon hearing the cry of the newborn infant, the pirate captain halted the attack, spared the passengers, and gifted the mother a length of green silk. Years later, local folklore intertwined this tale with a homestead in Henniker, New Hampshire, where generations of ghost hunters and visitors claimed to see the apparition of a red-haired woman and hear her rocking in a chair—though historians eventually debunked the claim, proving Mary Wallace never actually lived in that specific house.

#location
The Ocean-Born Mary House is located on Bear Road just outside of Henniker, New Hampshire.

#title
The Legend of Ocean-Born Mary

#summary
A historic mansion in Henniker is said to be haunted by the spirit of Mary Wallace, a woman named after a pirate captain who spared her ship in 1720.

#details
In 1720, a baby girl named Mary Wallace was born on a ship captured by the notorious pirate captain Don Pedro. Spared by the captain on the condition that the newborn be named after his mother, Mary grew up to be a striking red-haired woman. According to local folklore, Captain Pedro later tracked Mary down and married her; after he was allegedly murdered on the grounds over treasure, Mary buried him beneath the kitchen hearthstone. Following her death in 1814 at the age of 94, subsequent residents and visitors have reported sightings of her tall, red-haired apparition with glowing green eyes wandering the hallways, staircase, and windows.

#location
The Ocean-Born Mary House stands on a hillside overlooking the town of Henniker, New Hampshire.

#title
The Phantom Faces at Lakes of the Clouds Hut

#summary
An Appalachian Mountain Club crew member sent to open the snow-bound Lakes of the Clouds Hut on Mount Washington was discovered cowering under a sink and clutching an axe after encountering grotesque apparitions peering through securely boarded-up windows.

#details
In the spring, an AMC crew member named George was sent up to the Lakes of the Clouds Hut on Mount Washington with a two-way radio to assess winter damage and prepare the cabin for the upcoming season. Because of the brutal winter winds, the hut's windows were tightly boarded up from the outside. When George failed to respond to evening radio check-ins, a crew hiked up the mountain the following morning to check on him. Finding the cabin doors unlocked and his gear left on the dining room floor, they searched the dark interior until they heard a whimper coming from beneath the kitchen sink. There they found George huddled in absolute terror, clutching an axe in his white knuckles and repeatedly begging them to get him off the mountain. After recovering in a hospital weeks later, George explained that shortly after arriving, he felt an unseen presence approach him from behind. When he spun around, he saw a distorted, grotesque face pressed against the dining room windowpane—despite the heavy wooden boards covering it from the outside. As he watched in horror, the exact same face appeared across every window in the room, seeming to melt right through the barriers into the cabin before he blacked out from fear.

#location
Lakes of the Clouds Hut, located near the summit of Mount Washington in New Hampshire.

#upstream-source
Marianne O'Connor, *Haunted Hikes in New Hampshire*.

#reference
https://www.nhmagazine.com/the-scariest-nh-ghost-story-ever/

#title
The River Road Jogger

#summary
On Halloween night, a phantom jogger wearing a red sweatshirt and shorts is frequently seen running along River Road in Manchester before phasing directly through onlookers. First appearing in the 1970s, this harmless apparition ignores spectators who gather to watch him pass.

#details
Beginning in the early 1970s, locals and ghost enthusiasts in Manchester, New Hampshire, have gathered along River Road on Halloween night to witness a bizarre recurring legend. Between 1:45 and 2:20 in the morning, a spectral man dressed in a red sweatshirt and red running shorts appears, jogging steadily along the roadside. The jogger does not interact with anyone, ignores the crowds of people waiting to see him, and runs as though he is entirely human—until he phases directly through anyone standing in his path. Despite extensive local lore, no historical record exists of any jogger being killed or meeting a tragic end along this stretch of road.

#location
River Road runs alongside the Merrimack River in Manchester, New Hampshire.

#upstream-source
*Manchester Ghosts, New Hampshire’s Haunted City* by Renee Mallett.

#title
The Spectre of Lakes of the Clouds Hut

#summary
A caretaker opening a high-altitude mountain shelter on Mount Washington was left terrorized after witnessing a melting, distorted human face press through the window glass.

#details
In the early spring before the Lakes of the Clouds hut opened for the season, an Appalachian Mountain Club crew member named George hiked up Mount Washington to inspect the building and report via radio. When radio communication suddenly stopped, a rescue team ascended the mountain and discovered George hiding in a state of shock underneath the kitchen sink cabinet, with his radio fully charged nearby. Weeks later recovering in a hospital, George recounted that a figure had approached him from behind in the dining hall; when he turned, he saw a distorted human face pressed against the window panes that seemed to melt through the glass directly toward him. Believed to be the restless spirit of a lost hiker, the encounter left George so traumatized that he never returned to the mountain.

#location
The Lakes of the Clouds Hut is located in the alpine saddle between Mount Monroe and Mount Washington in the White Mountains of New Hampshire.

#upstream-source
Haunted Hikes of New Hampshire by Marianne O’Connor.
