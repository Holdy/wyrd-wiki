#title
The Curse of Cleopatra's Needle in Central Park

#summary
An ancient Egyptian obelisk transported to New York City carries a baleful reputation, with rumors of dark occult energies, mysterious alignments, and misfortune tied to its removal from its native soil.

#details
Erected in Central Park in 1881, the 3,500-year-old red granite obelisk known as Cleopatra's Needle was brought from Alexandria, Egypt, through arduous engineering feats financed by industrialist William Vanderbilt and led by Mason Henry Honychurch Gorringe. Ever since its installation, the monumental stone has been shrouded in folklore concerning curses, Masonic alignments with other Manhattan obelisks, and strange dark energies. Some lore connects the monument's esoteric symbolism and time capsule contents to malevolent influences, while others note the ironic tragedy that befell Gorringe himself, who died following a train accident shortly after publishing his book on the obelisks.

#location
Cleopatra's Needle stands on Greywacke Knoll in Central Park, immediately behind the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York City.

#title
The Headless Cavalier of Old Fort Niagara

#summary
A spectral French officer, decapitated during a duel over a local woman's heart in 1839, is said to haunt the grounds and emerge from the stone well of Old Fort Niagara during full moons.

#details
Built by the French in 1726 at the mouth of the Niagara River, Old Fort Niagara has witnessed centuries of conflict, warfare, and bloodshed. According to local folklore dating back nearly two centuries, the historic stronghold is haunted by the ghost of a French military officer. In 1839, two French soldiers who fell in love with a maiden from the local Seneca nation resolved their rivalry by engaging in a duel. One officer was killed and decapitated; his head was thrown into Lake Ontario while his torso was tossed down the fort's stone well. Over the years, visitors and staff have reported hearing disembodied whispers, slamming doors, and shadowy apparitions, with the headless specter frequently sighted climbing out of the historic well during a full moon.

#location
Old Fort Niagara, located at 102 Youngstown-Lockport Road in Youngstown, New York.

#title
The Headless French Officer of Old Fort Niagara

#summary
According to long-standing regional folklore, the historic French Castle at Old Fort Niagara is haunted by the headless ghost of an officer killed in a duel.

#details
Dating back to the colonial era, Old Fort Niagara is steeped in military tragedy, disease, and conflict. The most famous local legend tells of two French officers who engaged in a fatal sword duel. The victor allegedly decapitated his fallen opponent, throwing his head into Lake Ontario and stuffing his headless body down the fort's well. On nights of the full moon, the headless specter is said to rise from the depths of the well, wandering the stone corridors in search of its missing cranium.

#location
Old Fort Niagara is situated at 4 Scott Avenue in Youngstown, New York.

#title
The Headless French Soldier of Old Fort Niagara

#summary
During the 18th-century French occupation of Old Fort Niagara, a deadly duel between rival officers ended in murder and decapitation, and the victim's headless ghost is said to still haunt the fort's French Castle. According to local legend, the specter rises from the interior stone well at midnight to search for his missing head.

#details
During the French control of Old Fort Niagara, a gathering was held inside the stone fortress known as the French Castle, attended by French officers and local maidens. Two rival officers, Henri Le Clerc and Jean-Claude De Rochefort, competed fiercely for the affections of an Iroquois maiden named Onita. Fueled by wine and mutual animosity, their rivalry escalated into a sword duel. Henri tripped and fell, and Jean-Claude ruthlessly killed him. Panicked and desperate to conceal his crime, Jean-Claude decapitated Henri, threw the head into the nearby Niagara River where the current swept it toward Lake Ontario, and dumped the headless body down the dry stone well located inside the castle's front hall. Although the crime went undiscovered at the time, local lore states that on moonlit nights—especially at the stroke of midnight—the headless apparition of Henri Le Clerc rises from the stone well and awkwardly stalks the halls of the castle in search of his lost head.

#location
The French Castle at Old Fort Niagara, situated in Youngstown, New York, where the Niagara River flows into Lake Ontario.

#title
The House of Death on West 10th Street

#summary
Known as the House of Death, 14 West 10th Street in Greenwich Village is a notoriously haunted 1830s townhouse where multiple restless spirits, including that of Mark Twain, are said to linger.

#details
Built in the 1830s, the townhouse at 14 West 10th Street in Greenwich Village has earned a grim reputation as one of the most haunted buildings in New York City. Among its numerous reported phantoms is author Mark Twain, who lived in the home from 1900 to 1901. Despite being a vocal skeptic, Twain once witnessed an unexplained moving piece of kindling near the fireplace and fired upon it, leaving behind blood drops though no intruder was ever found; today, his spirit is frequently reported sitting near windows or on lower-level stairs. In the decades following, scores of residents and visitors—most famously psychic actress Jan Bryant Bartell—experienced extreme psychological dread, visions, and chilling encounters within its walls. The house's dark history was further cemented in 1989 when attorney Joel Steinberg tragically killed his adopted daughter Lisa inside the home, deepening its eerie lore.

#location
14 West 10th Street, Greenwich Village, New York City, NY.

#upstream-source

#title
The Lantern-Bearer of Beardslee Castle

#summary
A mysterious phantom carrying a glowing lantern terrorizes motorists along the gravel road near Beardslee Castle, blinding drivers and forcing vehicles into ditches.

#details
Situated along Route 5 between Little Falls and St. Johnsville, New York, Beardslee Castle is a nineteenth-century stone mansion modeled after an Irish castle, surrounded by dark woods and the waters of the East Canada Creek. Local legend holds that the castle and its surrounding gravel paths are haunted by various spectral figures, including a phantom man carrying a lantern. According to accounts shared by locals, drivers traveling the dark road parallel to the creek have encountered the glowing light veering directly toward their windshields, blinding them and causing cars to veer off into ditches. Diners inside the stone castle have also reported unexplained paranormal phenomena, such as napkins pulled from their laps and candles flickering in response to unseen presences.

#location
Beardslee Castle, located on Route 5 in Little Falls, New York.

#title
The Mystery Man of the Glens Falls Road

#summary
During the Great Depression, motorists along Route 9 in Warren County frequently gave rides to a phantom hitchhiker who prophesied economic recovery before vanishing into thin air.

#details
In January 1933, local newspapers reported on a mysterious man who frequented the Glens Falls-Lake George Road, securing free rides from passing drivers. After spending time in the vehicle, the hitchhiker would suddenly disappear without explanation, leaving behind a prediction that the global economic depression would soon end. Similar sightings were also reported along the Hudson Falls to Whitehall highway, becoming a prominent piece of local folklore during an era when hitchhiking was widespread.

#location
Route 9 (the Glens Falls-Lake George Road) runs north-south through Warren County in upstate New York.

#title
The Translucent Woman of Hyde Hall

#summary
Visitors and staff at a historic 19th-century mansion in Cooperstown have reported seeing the semi-transparent apparition of a young woman walking the staircases.

#details
Built in the early 19th century overlooking Glimmerglass Lake, Hyde Hall has long possessed a reputation for gentle hauntings. Staff members and directors have witnessed unexplained phenomena, including tools moving on their own and auditory events like parties with clinking glasses. Most notably, the executive director witnessed a translucent young woman in a long dress with a braid descending the tiger maple staircase, appearing so clear yet ethereal that wall cracks were visible straight through her form—a sighting later corroborated by a decorator looking for the same phantom.

#location
Hyde Hall is located at 267 Glimmerglass State Park Road in Cooperstown, New York.

#title
The Whispering Wards of Rolling Hills Asylum

#summary
Originally established as a 19th-century poor house and infirmary, this sprawling complex in Western New York is renowned for intense paranormal activity stemming from thousands of recorded deaths.

#details
Founded in 1827 as the Genesee County Poor House, the facility provided shelter for society's most vulnerable populations, including orphans, widows, and the mentally ill, before expanding into an infirmary and nursing home. Across its decades of operation, more than 1,700 deaths were documented on the property in unmarked graves and wards. Today, visitors and paranormal investigators frequently report intense supernatural phenomena throughout the 60,000-square-foot complex, including shadow people, Class A electronic voice phenomena (EVPs), poltergeist activity, and disembodied voices echoing through the dark hallways.

#location
Rolling Hills Asylum is located at 11001 Bethany Center Road in East Bethany, New York.
