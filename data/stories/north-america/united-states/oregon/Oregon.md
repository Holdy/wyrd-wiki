#title
The Bandage Man of Cannon Beach

#summary
A terrifying, blood-soaked apparition known as the Bandage Man haunts lonely stretches of coastal highway near Cannon Beach. He terrorizes motorists by climbing into truck beds and violently pounding on vehicle roofs.

#details
The legend of the Bandage Man centers around a phantom wrapped from head to toe in dirty, bloody gauze, accompanied by the overwhelming stench of rotting and burnt flesh. According to local folklore, he frequently targets drivers and teenagers traveling along Highway 101 near Cannon Beach. Witnesses have reported hearing a heavy thump on their tailgates or truck beds, followed by the entity climbing aboard, shaking the vehicle, and violently pounding against the roof or windows. When the motorists speed away in a panic and inspect their vehicles upon reaching town, they find no trace of the attacker except for a lingering foul odor and scraps of dirty gauze.

#location
Highway 101 between the Highway 26 junction and the northern entrance to Cannon Beach, Oregon.

#title
The Friendly Phantoms of the Geiser Grand Hotel

#summary
Baker City's historic Geiser Grand Hotel is said to be haunted by a headless chef, a mysterious Lady in Blue, and the spirit of former owner Maybelle Geiser who still inspects guests' jewelry in Room 302. Built during the gold rush era, the luxury hotel has welcomed countless spirits back to life alongside its living guests following a massive 1990s restoration.

#details
Opened in 1889 as the Hotel Warshauer and later renamed by Swiss immigrant Albert Geiser in 1900, the Geiser Grand Hotel became the crown jewel of Baker City during the regional gold rush. After falling into severe disrepair and closing in 1968, the building was rescued and restored in the 1990s—a process that seemingly awakened its dormant ghosts. Among the reported apparitions is a headless chef who was crushed by a dumbwaiter and reacts angrily by throwing pots and slamming doors, as well as the elegant "Lady in Blue" who haunts the staircases. In Room 302, guests report encounters with Maybelle Geiser, the former owner who lived in the room and is known to playfully inspect visitors' jewelry and knock on walls.

#location
The Geiser Grand Hotel is situated at 1996 Main Street in downtown Baker City, Oregon.

#title
The Gentle Specter of the McCully House

#summary
Built in 1861, the historic McCully House in Jacksonville is said to be haunted by the comforting spirit of its original owner, Dr. John McCully. Although McCully fled the town in financial disgrace shortly after the house was completed, guests and residents have reported encountering his calm apparition sitting peacefully in a chair, bringing reassurance rather than fear.

#details
Following the construction of the McCully House in 1861, Dr. John McCully left Jacksonville under a cloud of financial disgrace and never returned. His wife, Jane, stayed behind and managed the home as a boarding house while establishing the town's first school for girls. Decades later, overnight guests and residents reported seeing a well-dressed gentleman sitting quietly in a room. Instead of causing terror, the ghostly presence conveyed a sense of warmth and comfort, leading locals to believe it is the spirit of Dr. McCully returning to the home he so abruptly abandoned.

#location
The McCully House is located at 240 E. California Street in the historic gold-rush town of Jacksonville, Oregon.

#title
The Haunting of the Historic Wolf Creek Inn

#summary
Guests and staff at Oregon's oldest continuously operating hotel frequently encounter restless spirits, disembodied voices, and objects moving on their own. The most notable paranormal activity is concentrated in Room 8 of the 140-year-old stagecoach stop.

#details
Completed in 1883 along the Applegate Trail, the Wolf Creek Inn once served as a refuge for early stagecoach pioneers and famous guests like President Rutherford B. Hayes and John Wayne. Over the decades, visitors and inn managers have reported an array of eerie phenomena, including doors slamming shut on their own, pianos playing in empty parlors, and a baking pan in the kitchen flying across the floor on multiple occasions. Paranormal investigators and mediums who visited the inn concluded that a heavy spiritual presence lingers throughout the building, with Room 8 being particularly active, though the overall consensus is that the ghosts are friendly and harmless.

#location
The Historic Wolf Creek Inn is located at 100 Front Street in the town of Wolf Creek, Oregon.

#title
The Mischievous Spirits of Hotel Oregon

#summary
McMinnville's historic Hotel Oregon is home to mischievous ghosts, including a regular spirit named John who roams the rooftop bar and a lingering presence in Room 203 associated with former resident Ollie. Employees and guests have experienced sudden cold drops, unexplained sounds, and spinning steel office cylinders in the cellar.

#details
Operating for over a century in downtown McMinnville, Hotel Oregon is steeped in local history and paranormal lore. Employees closing up the building have experienced unnerving events, such as a heavy steel cylinder in the cellar office spinning wildly on its own late at night accompanied by a sudden drop in temperature. Guests staying in Room 203—dedicated to former resident Ollie Ruffler White—frequently report feeling an oppressive sense of being watched or pushed out of the room. Additionally, an entity known as John has been sighted wandering the hallways and the rooftop bar, contributing to the hotel's long-standing reputation as one of Oregon's most actively haunted spots.

Hotel Oregon is located at 310 NE Evans Street in McMinnville, Oregon.

#title
The Phantoms of the Wolf Creek Tavern

#summary
Built in 1883 along the historic stagecoach route in southern Oregon, the Wolf Creek Tavern is renowned for persistent paranormal activity, including phantom piano music, moving furniture, and spectral visitors.

#details
Constructed in 1883 by pioneer Henry Smith as a first-class stagecoach stop along the Applegate Trail, the Wolf Creek Tavern served as a remote refuge for travelers, cowboys, and legendary figures such as Jack London and Clark Gable. Over the decades, guests and staff have reported numerous unexplainable phenomena throughout the building. Reports include ghostly fingers playing the parlor piano, furniture rearranging itself, doors slamming shut in empty hallways, and baking pans mysteriously flying across the kitchen floor. Room 8 and the low-ceilinged attic—where cash-strapped nineteenth-century cowboys once wedged their spurs into the rafters to keep from rolling off their sleeping shelves—have become focal points for sightings of shadowy apparitions and lingering spirits from the inn's storied past.

#location
The Wolf Creek Tavern is located at 100 Front Street in the town of Wolf Creek, Josephine County, Oregon.

#title
The Restless Spirits of the White Eagle Saloon

#summary
Portland's historic White Eagle Saloon is haunted by the tragic spirit of a murdered prostitute named Rose, a vigilant bouncer named Sam, and a malevolent presence lurking in the basement. Established in 1905, the century-old building bears a dark legacy of vice, violence, and unexplainable paranormal phenomena.

#details
Opened in 1905 by Polish immigrants, the White Eagle operated on multiple levels, featuring a working-class bar on the ground floor, a boarding house and brothel upstairs, and a basement rumored to connect to Portland's infamous Shanghai tunnels. The building's dark history includes violent brawls and the unsolved murder of a young woman working upstairs, whose throat was slashed. Today, witnesses frequently report encounters with her ghost—known as Rose—who leaves behind the heavy scent of floral perfume, weeping sounds, and cold spots in Room 2. Downstairs, a large apparition of a former bouncer named Sam patrols the bar to keep order, while visitors venturing into the basement report an oppressive, malevolent atmosphere and sudden physical shoves from unseen forces.

#location
The White Eagle Saloon is located at 836 N Russell Street in Portland's Mississippi District.

#title
The Weeping Spirits of the Helms House

#summary
The historic Helms House in Jacksonville is reportedly haunted by the grief-stricken spirits of a mother and her young daughter who died during a 19th-century epidemic.

#details
Built in 1878 next to an earlier log cabin, the Italianate-style house belonged to German immigrants Herman and Augusta von Helms, a family that suffered immense tragedy, including the loss of three daughters to a typhoid epidemic and another to murder. Their youngest daughter, Minnie, died at less than two years old and was buried on the property. According to local lore, the grieving spirit of Augusta or the young child can be heard crying at night, while phantom apparitions wander the staircases and hallways before abruptly vanishing.

#location
The Helms House is located on South Orange Street in Jacksonville, Oregon.

Historic Jacksonville Inc. and articles by Terrie Claflin in The Mail Tribune and Dennis McCarthy in The Sunday Oregonian.
