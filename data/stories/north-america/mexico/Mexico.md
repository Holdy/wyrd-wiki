#title
The Children of the Third Curve

#summary
Two ghost children appear holding hands at midnight on the third curve of the Tuxtla Gutiérrez-Ocozocoautla Highway, asking passing motorists for help before vanishing without a trace.

#details
On the Tuxtla Gutiérrez-Ocozocoautla Highway in Chiapas, travelers navigating the hill section at midnight frequently report encountering a ten-year-old girl and her five-year-old brother. The young siblings stand hand-in-hand by the roadside, pleading for assistance from passing drivers. Although concerned motorists often return to the spot after reporting the stranded children in nearby towns, they consistently find the roadside completely empty.

#location
The third curve along the hill section of the Tuxtla Gutiérrez-Ocozocoautla Highway in the state of Chiapas, Mexico.

#title
The Legend of the Accursed Bell

#summary
An infamous bell, forged from a cursed Moorish armor and the spirit of a wicked law student, was banished by the Spanish Inquisition to Mexico where it served for decades as the clock-bell of the National Palace. Despite having its tongue removed, the bell possessed lingering demonic influences that defied final destruction.

#details
A devil-possessed Moorish suit of armor fell into the hands of a corrupt Spanish law student, Don Gil de Marcadante, who engaged in dark sacrileges before mysteriously vanishing from his Inquisition prison cell. When the Holy Office melted the cursed armor into a sacred cross, the imprisoned demons were later unleashed when the cross was recast into a convent bell in Spain that rang furiously by itself at midnight. Following a formal investigation and trial by the Inquisition's Consejo, the bell was sentenced to have its tongue torn out and be permanently banished overseas to Mexico, where it was eventually mounted as a tongueless clock-bell on the National Palace. Even decades later during the French Intervention, attempts to melt down the bell failed as the metal stubbornly turned sour in the furnace and refused to be recast.

#location
The bell was housed in the bell-gable above the central entrance of the National Palace (Palacio Nacional), located facing the Zócalo in the historic center of Mexico City.

#title
The Queretaro Exorcism Chapel

#summary
Faced with a rising surge of demonic possessions across Mexico, the Roman Catholic Church established a specialized facility dedicated entirely to performing exorcisms.

#details
As reports of demonic possession steadily increased—with Mexico City alone recording roughly ten cases every month—the Roman Catholic Church recognized the need for a dedicated space to conduct formal exorcisms. In response, a specialized chapel was opened in the central city of Querétaro. Named La Capilla de las Benditas Animas del Purgatorio (The Chapel of the Blessed Souls in Purgatory), this facility was established to carry out sacred rites involving holy water, prayers, and direct commands to expel evil entities from afflicted individuals exhibiting signs such as speaking unknown languages or displaying anomalous knowledge. To address skepticism from critics and medical professionals who argue that symptoms of perceived possession stem from conditions like schizophrenia, epilepsy, or mental illness, the chapel's priests, including Father Rogelio Cano, instituted strict protocols requiring all subjects to undergo thorough medical and psychiatric evaluations before any exorcism can be authorized.

#location
La Capilla de las Benditas Animas del Purgatorio located in the central city of Querétaro, Mexico.
