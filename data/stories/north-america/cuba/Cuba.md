#title
The Curse of El Pelú

#summary
After being abused and expelled from Cuba's first city by local residents, a destitute Spanish beggar cursed the settlement so that all future plans and ambitious ideas would inevitably crumble. Passed down through generations, this enduring curse is still blamed by locals for any misfortune or unfulfilled endeavor in the region.

#details
In the late 19th century, a destitute Spanish immigrant named Vicente Rodríguez—known widely as "El Pelú" due to his unkempt hair, beard, and ragged clothing—arrived in Baracoa after losing his fortune. He wandered the streets as a beggar, occasionally sustained by charitable locals. However, many residents grew fearful of him and began treating him with cruelty, ultimately stoning him, humiliating him, and forcing local authorities to banish him from the city. As he was being forced onto the dock to board a boat back to his homeland, he turned back and pronounced a bitter curse upon the settlement: that while many great plans and ideas would continually be generated in Baracoa, they would all fall apart and nothing would ever be successfully fulfilled. Generations of inhabitants have since blamed this delayed curse for both everyday setbacks and major hardships plaguing the town.

#location
The historic city of Baracoa, located in the easternmost province of Guantánamo, Cuba.

#title
The Haunting at Kilometer 78

#summary
An abandoned house situated directly beside a major Cuban highway is plagued by spectral phenomena and the ghost of a former resident. Multiple families over the years have been forced to flee the property due to unexplained voices, sounds, and apparitions at night.

#details
Along the Havana-Pinar del Río highway at kilometer 78 in the municipality of San Cristóbal, Artemisa province, stands a solid concrete house that has remained desolate for over two decades. Despite the region's housing needs, every family that has attempted to occupy the building has quickly abandoned it after experiencing paranormal disturbances. Local residents report hearing phantom voices, the sounds of shifting objects, and strange lights after dark. According to popular local legend, the property is haunted by the spirit of Juan "el Colorao," a man who lived and died in the building long ago, as well as the ghosts of two brothers who perished there during a violent quarrel.

#location
The haunted house sits right next to the Havana-Pinar del Río highway at kilometer 78 in the municipality of San Cristóbal, Artemisa province, Cuba.

#title
The Possession of Remedios

#summary
In 1682, the parish priest of Remedios documented that a local woman named Leonarda was possessed by Lucifer, who spoke through her during formal ecclesiastical proceedings. The resulting panic prompted unsuccessful attempts by church authorities to relocate the entire town.

#details
In 1682, Father José González de la Cruz, the rector priest and inquisitorial commissioner of San Juan de los Remedios del Cayo, asserted that the town was besieged by demonic forces. Most notably, a Cuban-born slave woman named Leonarda was claimed to be physically possessed by Lucifer, the Prince of Darkness, alongside several other local individuals. On September 4, 1682, formal minutes were recorded before a notary public inside the parish church, documenting that the entity spoke directly through Leonarda and confessed that demonic possession had occurred due to human sins. Convinced that the town sat upon a gateway to Hell, Father José attempted to force the populace to abandon their settlement and relocate to Hato del Copey, though many skeptical residents resisted and remained behind.

#location
The historic parish church located in the central square of San Juan de los Remedios, Villa Clara Province, Cuba.
