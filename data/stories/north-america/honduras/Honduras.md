#title
The Curse of Gracias

#summary
Following the gruesome execution of an indulgence seller who sought refuge within its sacred walls, the furious friars of La Merced placed a lingering generational curse upon the city of Gracias. Plagues and misery devastated the community for generations until a compassionate priest finally lifted the curse over a century later.

#details
In the 18th century, a cunning indulgence seller (bulero) fled from an angry mob after a violent dispute during a card game in the town of Gracias, Honduras. He sought sanctuary behind the shrine of the Virgin of La Merced inside the imposing Church of La Merced. The enraged townspeople stormed the church, desecrating the temple—striking the venerated statue of the Virgin in the process—before capturing the indulgence seller, dragging him outside, decapitating him, and submerging his head in boiling oil. Horrified by the sacrilege, the Mercedarian friars draped the temple in mourning, walked the streets casting curses on the populace while scattering salt as a symbol of the doom awaiting future generations, and abandoned the city. A relentless plague soon fell upon the survivors, and the town was cast into decades of misery, pestilence, and architectural decay. Although a mission of priests attempted purification rites at "Las Mesas" and the damaged image of the Virgin was sent away for repair (where the wound inexplicably reappeared), the town remained under the cloud of the curse until 1852, when Father Manuel de Jesús Subirana successfully lifted it.

#location
Church of La Merced (Iglesia de La Merced) located in the historic center of Gracias, Department of Lempira, Honduras.
