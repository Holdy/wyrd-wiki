The Possession Outbreak at Moruga Composite School

Summary

In 2010, multiple students at a secondary school in southern Trinidad experienced sudden, synchronized seizures and trance-like episodes that locals attributed to demonic possession. The dramatic flare-up resulted in school closures and drew visiting exorcists before being analyzed as a case of mass sociogenic illness.

Details

In 2010, an unprecedented series of events unfolded at the Moruga Composite School in rural southern Trinidad, where several young female students abruptly began manifesting violent seizures, nausea, fainting spells, and altered, trance-like behavior. As the condition rapidly spread among the student body, causing widespread panic and forcing the school to shut down, religious observers and local residents insisted the teenagers were victims of demonic possession and spiritual attack. Pastors and self-proclaimed exorcists rushed to the school compound armed with holy water and rosaries to perform deliverances. Medical and sociological analyses later categorized the incident as mass hysteria or psychosomatic possession rooted in cultural frameworks of spiritual warfare, capturing national attention.
