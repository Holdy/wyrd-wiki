#title
The Haunting of Buck Hill

#summary
During the Great Depression, a heartbroken father lost his young daughter in the woods near a lonely Ottawa Valley hill and now haunts the roadway. Generations of visitors test their nerve by performing a nighttime ritual to summon his spirit.

#details
According to local folklore stemming from the Great Depression, a man and his young daughter lived at the bottom of Buck Hill near Round Lake. When the family dog slipped out one day, the little girl ran after it into the dense surrounding woods and became permanently lost despite her father's desperate searches. Following his passing, locals believe the grieving father's spirit remained bound to the area, endlessly searching for his child. Today, visitors drive to the isolated road at night, turn their headlights on and off three times, and shout "Daddy, Daddy, Daddy!" into the darkness to evoke phantom lights, revving engine sounds, and other paranormal phenomena.

#location
Buck Hill Road is located near Round Lake within the township of Killaloe-Hagarty-Richards in the Ottawa Valley, Ontario.

#title
The Possession of Barbe Hallay

#summary
In 1660 New France, a teenage girl named Barbe Hallay was allegedly possessed by demons and tormented by hellish spectres after a rejected suitor sought revenge through sorcery. Plagued by violent outbursts and foreign voices, she was later subjected to years of intense exorcisms by nuns at a Quebec hospital.

#details
In 1660, a ship carrying French colonists arrived in the port of Quebec, carrying among its passengers a miller named Daniel Vuil and a 15-year-old girl named Barbe Hallay. During the voyage, Vuil asked Hallay's parents for permission to marry their daughter, but was refused due to his bad moral character. Upon arrival, Hallay found employment as a maid at the Giffart manor house in the nearby village of Beauport. 

Soon after, strange phenomena began to plague the estate, with phantom music playing, stones flying through the air, and terrifying visions centering around Hallay. She reported being tormented by animals, hellish spectres, and a spirit bearing the likeness of Daniel Vuil, often speaking in voices that were not her own. Convinced that Vuil had used diabolical arts to induce a demonic possession out of spite, colonial authorities arrested, tried, and executed him. 

Hallay's afflictions only worsened after the execution. She was transferred to the Hotel-Dieu de Quebec hospital in Quebec City and placed in the care of Mother Catherine de Saint-Augustine, who diagnosed her with severe demonic possession. For two years, the nun endured physical assaults while conducting nightly exorcisms and prayers. Ultimately, through religious intercession and sacred relics, Hallay was cured of her demonic affliction.

#location
The Giffart manor house in Beauport (now a borough of Quebec City), Quebec, where the demonic manifestations and disturbances first began.

#title
The Tuberculosis Curse of Castle Kilbride

#summary
A tragic generational curse of tuberculosis claimed the lives of a mother and multiple daughters in a grand Victorian estate, where the mourning matriarch's spirit is still said to roam.

#details
Built in 1877 by flax baron and politician James Livingston, Castle Kilbride in Baden, Ontario, became the backdrop for a heartbreaking family tragedy. The curse began with Louise Livingston's mother, who succumbed to tuberculosis in 1863, and later passed down a devastating lineage of loss onto Louise's own daughters. Following a trip to the 1893 Chicago World's Fair, daughter Rebecca contracted the disease and died in 1895, quickly followed by her sisters Agnes and Barbara. When another ailing daughter, Caroline, returned home to be cared for, Louise rushed up the mansion's grand staircase upon hearing her cry out, only to collapse at the top step and pass away herself within the hour. Today, visitors and guides inside the historic house frequently report sightings of a mysterious Woman in Black—believed to be grieving mother Louise—still haunting the halls.

#location
Castle Kilbride is located at 60 Snyder's Road West in Baden, Ontario, Canada.
