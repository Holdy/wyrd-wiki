    Mandy the Haunted Doll of the Quesnel Museum

    Summary

    Donated in 1991, an antique doll known as Mandy has been blamed for restless poltergeist activity, disarranged museum labs, and eerie electronic malfunctions at the local history museum.

    Details

    Acquired by the Quesnel and District Museum and Archives in British Columbia in 1991, Mandy is a children's doll dating back to the 1920s that was donated by an owner who claimed she could hear a baby crying in her basement near where the doll was kept. Since taking up permanent residence inside a locked glass display case at the museum, Mandy has been linked to numerous unexplainable paranormal occurrences. Staff arriving the morning after her acquisition found the museum laboratory in total disarray, as though a child had thrown a violent temper tantrum. Visitors and staff have repeatedly reported equipment malfunctions, drained camera batteries, missing items, and the uncanny sensation that Mandy's eyes follow people across the room.
