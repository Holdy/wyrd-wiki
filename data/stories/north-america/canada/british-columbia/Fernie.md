#title
The Apparition of the Ingram Block

#summary
The historic Ingram Block in downtown Fernie is haunted by the restless spirit of William Ingram, a prominent local businessman who was murdered there in 1939.

#details
William Ingram was a well-known businessman who was attacked and struck on the back of the head with an iron pipe on the night of November 21, 1939, dying of his injuries five days later. Decades later, when the top floor of the Ingram building was used for artist studios and offices, tenants frequently experienced unexplained phenomena. Artists reported hearing a key turn in the downstairs lock followed by heavy, slow footsteps climbing the stairs, even when convex mirrors installed to check the stairwell showed no one was there. Others reported the strong, lingering scent of cigar smoke and shadowy figures moving through the building.

#location
The historic Ingram Block building located in the 500 block between 1st and 2nd Avenue in downtown Fernie, British Columbia.

#title
The Ghost of Benson on the Coal Creek Railway

#summary
Following a tragic murder-suicide sparked by a workplace dispute in the early days of Fernie, a miner named Benson was said to haunt the railway tracks where he ended his life.

#details
In the early frontier days of Fernie, a miner named Benson grew enraged when his fireboss moved him out of spite. In a fit of sullen anger, Benson shot at his fireboss at the mine offices and then fled to the nearby railway tracks in Coal Creek, where he fatally shot himself. Tragically, the superintendent had already ordered Benson's reinstatement and the fireboss had only survived with minor injuries. For decades afterward, residents and passersby reported supernatural encounters along that specific section of the tracks, including cold hands pulling people backward and dark, shadowy figures.

#location
The historic railway tracks at Coal Creek, just outside Fernie, British Columbia.

#title
The Spectral Residents of the Royal Hotel

#summary
The Royal Hotel in downtown Fernie is said to be haunted by several distinct spirits, ranging from a mischievous young girl to an elderly resident who loves music.

#details
Situated on the corner of 1st Avenue in Fernie, the Royal Hotel has accumulated a reputation for heavy supernatural activity. Staff and visitors have reported a variety of ghostly occurrences, including phantom parties in the dining room on Christmas morning when the building was otherwise empty. A mischievous fair-haired girl frequently startles staff by tugging hair and offering cold handshakes, while an elderly man named Julius Beach—who passed away in an upstairs room in 1984—is known to turn on music in unoccupied rooms and rock in chairs. Additionally, a reclusive spirit is said to inhabit the basement, disliking loud noises and whispering directly to anyone quiet enough to listen.

#location
The Royal Hotel at the corner of 1st Avenue in Fernie, British Columbia.
