#title
The Apparition at Sir Edward Walter's Lodge

#summary
Built in the 1860s on the isolated mudflats of Lulu Island, a wealthy Englishman's hunting lodge became famously haunted by a man in a derby hat, unexplained footsteps, and a spectral walking cane.

#details
In the 1860s, wealthy Englishman Sir Edward Walter constructed an isolated hunting lodge on the sodden mudflats of Lulu Island in what is now Richmond, British Columbia (near the intersection of No. 3 Road and Westminster Highway). After his wife quickly grew disenchanted with the mud and loneliness and returned to England, Walter also abandoned the property. 

Decades later, in 1916, waterworks manager Charles Jones purchased the estate and moved his family into the historic building. Throughout their time living there, the Jones family—and later residents such as the Berry family—experienced persistent paranormal activity. Footsteps on the stairs and landings when no one else was home were so common that they were eventually ignored, while violent clanging noises inside the walls occasionally kept everyone awake for days. 

Most notably, multiple residents reported seeing an apparition of a formally dressed man wearing a jacket and a derby hat. Young Arnold Jones and members of the Berry family both reported waking up to find the phantom watching them from the bedroom before it faded into the darkness. On another eerie night, while Arnold sat reading under an oil lamp, a brass-tipped walking cane slowly protruded an inch-wide crack beneath a closed door, moved side to side, and then withdrew into nothingness. Charles Jones also witnessed the silhouette of a woman looking out a master bedroom window when his wife was elsewhere tending to the children. The historic lodge remained standing as a local landmark until it was demolished in January 1962 to make way for a parking lot.

#location
The former site of Sir Edward Walter's house near the intersection of No. 3 Road and Westminster Highway on Lulu Island in Richmond, British Columbia.
