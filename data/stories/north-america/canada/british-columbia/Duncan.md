#title
The Apparition of Chief Tzouhalem

#summary
The spirit of the formidable 19th-century Quamichan warrior and mystic, Chief Tzouhalem, is said to haunt the mountain and cliff edges that bear his name.

#details
Chief Tzouhalem was a prominent and feared leader of the Cowichan people who spent his final years living in caves on the side of Mount Tzouhalem after being banished by his community. According to local legend and oral history, his spirit never left the mountain where he once sought spiritual power and cast adversaries from the cliffs. Hikers and visitors near the summit and historic sites have frequently reported feeling intensely watched and followed by an unseen presence associated with the powerful chief.

#location
Mount Tzouhalem rises above the Cowichan Valley near Duncan, British Columbia.

#title
The Headless Rider of the Stone Butter Church

#summary
While playing hide-and-seek as a boy in the graveyard of an abandoned stone church, a local gravedigger encountered the apparition of a dark-clad headless horseman.

#details
Built in 1870 on Comiaken Hill near Duncan, the Old Stone Butter Church fell into disrepair after operating for only a decade, eventually acquiring a widespread local reputation for being haunted. Decades later, a Quamichan traditional gravedigger named Harold Joe recalled that when he was young, he hid in the long grass of the church's old graveyard during a nighttime game. Looking up, he saw the silhouette of a headless man seated upon a horse, an entity later confirmed by elders to be one of the dark spirits haunting the resting place of the dead.

#location
The Old Stone Butter Church graveyard is located on Comiaken Hill off Tzouhalem Road in Duncan, British Columbia.
