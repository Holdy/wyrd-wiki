#title
The Stonemason's Castle

#summary
Built by an eccentric European stonemason who originally planned to be buried inside its massive concrete and stone walls, the historic Stone House in Creston is said to be haunted by his lingering presence.

#details
Master stonemason Rudolph Schultz began construction on his unusual home in Alice Siding, near Creston, British Columbia, in 1952 and continued building it for 15 years until his death in 1967. Having completed his training in Germany and Russia, Schultz designed the building with foot-thick stone walls and a flat roof of solid reinforced concrete built over repurposed railroad tracks, intending it to serve as a European-style castle. He even constructed a brick crypt inside the home for his final resting place, though community objections led to him ultimately being buried in Forest Lawn Cemetery. Today, visitors and staff at the building—which now operates as the Creston Museum—report encountering the eccentric stonemason's spirit haunting the halls and rooms he spent his life crafting.

#location
The historic Stone House (housing the Creston Museum) is located at Alice Siding just outside Creston, British Columbia.
