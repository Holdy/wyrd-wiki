The Ghost of Caretaker Arthur Sharpe at the Washington Avenue Grill

Summary

Built as a 1913 lumber mill, the historic Washington Avenue Grill is said to be haunted by a missing 1930s caretaker and the spirits of two tragic lovers.

Details

Originally constructed in 1913 by the Campbell River Lumber Company as a mill, the building that now houses the Washington Avenue Grill has served various roles over the decades, including as a church, school, and railway worker quarters. In 1934, a man named Arthur Sharpe moved in as the building's caretaker. After living there for ten years, Sharpe mysteriously vanished without a trace, and local lore holds that his spirit never left the premises. In addition to Sharpe, staff and diners have reported encounters with two other apparitions: a young woman who was struck and killed by a car directly outside the restaurant, and her grieving lover who took his own life shortly after. The woman's ghost reportedly wanders from a neighboring cemetery through the restaurant and up the stairs, while her partner's spirit is known to interact physically with visitors and employees.
