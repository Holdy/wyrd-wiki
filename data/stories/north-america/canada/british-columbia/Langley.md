#title
The Haunting of the Little White House

#summary
A 1930s teenager named Hazel, who tragically died in the building after ingesting poison, is said to haunt a historic boutique in Fort Langley, frequently locking doors and moving objects.

#details
In the 1930s, a 15-year-old girl named Hazel lived in the house that is now known as the Little White House in Fort Langley. After becoming pregnant, she tragically ingested rat poison and was found dead on the living room sofa. Decades later, after the building became a boutique and tea room, owners and staff began experiencing unexplained paranormal activity. Security footage has captured doors mysteriously swinging open with no draft or wind present, staff have been locked inside rooms by solid, lockless doors requiring a locksmith, and invisible presences have left indentations on furniture. The spirit, affectionately called "Hazel" or the "lady in white," is believed to be quite peaceful, though she tends to play cheeky pranks whenever renovations or new staff arrive.

#location
Little White House & Co is located at 9090 Glover Road in Fort Langley, British Columbia.

#title
The Phantom Child of the Big House

#summary
Staff and visitors at the historic Fort Langley trading post have repeatedly reported encounters with a mysterious young boy in period clothing who vanishes without a trace.

#details
Over the years, staff and visitors at the Fort Langley National Historic Site have reported frequent encounters with a young boy dressed in period clothing inside the Big House. While some visitors initially praised the fort for employing a young costumed interpreter to greet guests, and others angrily emailed management about letting an unsupervised child wander, staff were left baffled because the fort has never hired anyone that young. Interpreters—including self-described skeptics—have reported walking in to unlock the Big House in the morning only to find a terrified little boy cowering in the corner who completely vanishes the moment they look away.

#location
The Big House is located within the Fort Langley National Historic Site at 23433 Mavis Avenue in Langley, British Columbia.

#title
The Phantom Gunshot and Scream of the Fraser River

#summary
Local folklore and police reports claim that the waterfront of Fort Langley echoes with a tragic 19th-century gunshot and a heartbroken woman's scream.

#details
During the 1800s, a First Nations man named Louis Satakarata dit Rabaska worked at the Hudson's Bay Company post in Fort Langley. After marrying a local woman against his father's wishes, a severe family confrontation erupted. Distraught, Louis crossed the water to McMillan Island and took his own life with a musket shot. When a retrieval party brought his body back, his wife let out a harrowing, grief-stricken scream. According to local oral tradition and modern accounts, the tragic events continue to reverberate across the river: local residents and RCMP officers occasionally receive middle-of-the-night calls reporting a mysterious gunshot echoing across the water from McMillan Island, immediately followed by the sound of a woman screaming from within the historic fort grounds.

#location
The Fort Langley waterfront and McMillan Island sit along the Fraser River in Langley, British Columbia.
