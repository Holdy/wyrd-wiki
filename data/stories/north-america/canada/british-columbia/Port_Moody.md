#title
The Fading Spirits of Ioco Ghost Town

#summary
The historic company town of Ioco in Port Moody is remembered through local ghost walks and folklore recounting its boomtown past and the atmospheric remnants of abandoned worker housing.

#details
Established in the early 20th century as a company town for workers at the local Imperial Oil refinery, Ioco grew into a tight-knit community complete with dozens of houses, churches, grocery stores, and recreational facilities. As the industry modernized and the residential area faded away, it earned a reputation as a local ghost town. Today, historical events and guided ghost walks explore the eerie, atmospheric history and preserved remnants of the old townsite across the Burrard Inlet.

#location
The historic Ioco townsite is situated in the northern section of Port Moody, British Columbia, along the Burrard Inlet.

#title
The Phantoms of the Port Moody Station Museum

#summary
Visitors and paranormal investigators at the 1908 Port Moody Station Museum have reported hearing mysterious footsteps, recording the voice of an unseen spirit named Peter, and encountering a ghostly woman aboard the retired *Venosta* sleeping car.

#details
Built in 1908, the historic Port Moody Station building and its adjacent retired CPR sleeping car, the *Venosta*, have long been associated with local accounts of supernatural activity. Paranormal investigators and museum staff have documented unexplained equipment malfunctions, audio recorders capturing disembodied voices—including a spirit claiming to be named Peter—and heavy, draining energy throughout the building. Additionally, a spectral woman is frequently reported haunting the interior of the historic *Venosta* train car, where she is believed to be responsible for turning flashlights and cameras on and off during investigations.

#location
The Port Moody Station Museum is located at 2734 Murray Street in Port Moody, British Columbia.
