#title
The Benevolent Fire Chief of the Old Fire Hall

#summary
The historic castle-like fire station on Nicol Street is said to be haunted by Nanaimo's first fire chief, who died of pneumonia after battling a harsh winter blaze in 1937.

#details
Serving as Nanaimo's old Firehall No. 2, the distinctive stone and brick building on Nicol Street witnessed the tragic decline of Fire Chief John Parkin, who became severely chilled after being drenched by freezing hose water while leading crews during a freezing winter fire in 1937. He passed away in the building a month later. Subsequent tenants and shop owners operating out of the historic structure have reported benign supernatural encounters, including a worker who felt an unseen force gently guide a heavy falling hammer away from her head.

#location
The Old Fire Hall is located at 108 Nicol Street in Nanaimo, British Columbia.

#title
The Phantom Child and Apparitions of Beban House

#summary
Built in the 1930s by a prominent timber baron family, Beban House is frequently reported to be haunted by a young Chinese boy and the apparition of a woman.

#details
Constructed in 1930 by logging magnate Frank Beban along Bowen Road, the rustic log-sided mansion has accumulated numerous ghost stories over the decades as it transitioned through daycare, office, and municipal uses. Over the years, multiple children attending daycare in the building independently drew pictures of a young Chinese boy wearing a white gown with a long braid who played with a red ball. Additionally, visitors, staff, and police officers using community office space have reported inexplicable sounds such as faucets turning on, locked doors opening, and the shadowy apparition of a woman appearing in upper windows.

#location
Beban House is located at 2290 Bowen Road in Nanaimo, British Columbia.

#title
The Rolling Cannonballs of the Nanaimo Bastion

#summary
Nanaimo's iconic coastal landmark is reportedly haunted by former caretakers and phantom noises, most notably heavy cannonballs rolling across the wooden floorboards.

#details
Built in 1853 by the Hudson’s Bay Company as a fur-trade outpost and defense fortification, the octagonal wooden Bastion on Nanaimo's waterfront carries a long legacy of ghostly lore. Visitors and staff have repeatedly reported hearing heavy, mysterious thuds and the distinct sound of cannonballs rolling across the upper wooden floorboards when nobody else is upstairs. Paranormal investigators and historians attribute the activity to former 20th-century caretaker Joseph Muir and other lingering spirits watching over the historic landmark.

#location
The Nanaimo Bastion is located at 98 Front Street along the waterfront in downtown Nanaimo, British Columbia.
