#title
The Blue Lady of the Penticton Museum

#summary
In the late 1980s, janitorial staff at the Penticton Museum and Archives frequently encountered a specter known as "The Blue Lady" until uncatalogued human remains in the basement were removed.

#details
During the late 1980s, the Penticton Museum and Archives struggled to retain nighttime janitorial staff due to frequent encounters with a ghost dubbed "The Blue Lady." While conducting inventory in the museum basement, curator and local historian Randy Manuel discovered a box labeled "Pacific Milk Tins" that contained uncatalogued human skeletal remains belonging to three individuals. Once the bones were sent away to a forensic laboratory at Simon Fraser University and permanently removed from the building, the nocturnal hauntings mysteriously ceased, allowing the museum to finally keep its night staff.

#location
The Penticton Museum and Archives located in Penticton, British Columbia.

#title
The Haunting of the 1921 Engineer's House

#summary
A historic 1921 house near Penticton's Fairview Cemetery is said to be haunted by Jack Beaton, a troubled railway engineer who died mysteriously inside the home.

#details
Jack Beaton worked as an engineer for the Kettle Valley Railway and lived in a house built in 1921 near the Fairview Cemetery. Known for a turbulent domestic life and heavy drinking, Beaton was found dead in the home's back room on the morning of January 1, 1942, following a mix of alcohol and pills. Decades later, local historian Randy Manuel noted that Beaton's spirit appeared to linger in the structure. During a visit by a television production crew investigating paranormal claims, a specialized electronic device picked up sudden static and perceived words when Beaton's presence was allegedly felt in the room, leaving the crew thoroughly shaken.

#location
A historic house built in 1921 located near the Fairview Cemetery in Penticton, British Columbia.

#title
The Spirits of the S.S. Sicamous

#summary
The historic paddlewheeler S.S. Sicamous is rumored to be haunted by former crew members and local journalists whose personal artifacts were brought aboard.

#details
The S.S. Sicamous, a historic paddlewheeler moored on the shores of Okanagan Lake, has long been a focal point for ghostly legends in Penticton. According to local historians and archivists, strange phenomena began occurring after the personal desk, photographs, and belongings of former Penticton Herald writer and photographer Ed Aldredge were transferred to the ship. Staff members subsequently reported hearing unexplained footsteps, slamming doors, and faint voices echoing through the vessel. Historians often attribute these noises to Aldredge's spirit interacting with the ghost of Captain Joseph B. Weeks, the ship's former captain. Additionally, the vessel is believed to host the spirit of RCMP Constable Geoffrey Aston, who was tragically shot on a sister vessel in 1912.

#location
The S.S. Sicamous, moored at the S.S. Sicamous Marine Heritage Park on Okanagan Lake in Penticton, British Columbia.
