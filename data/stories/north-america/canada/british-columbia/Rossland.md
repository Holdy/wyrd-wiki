#title
The Ghost Cat of Plewman Basin

#summary
Following a tragic 1947 plane crash in a remote mountain basin near Rossland, local legend holds that a massive, ghostly white cat haunts the ridge and trails. This spectral feline is believed by locals to embody the wandering souls of the nine victims who perished in the wreckage.

#details
On October 18, 1947, a Royal Canadian Air Force B-25 Mitchell bomber carrying nine military personnel and civilians crashed into Plewman Basin during a severe snowstorm. Due to blinding fog and howling winds, no one heard the plane go down, and the wreckage remained undiscovered for five years until a hunter stumbled upon the site. Over time, local folklore and regional folk songs emerged telling of a supernatural phenomenon in the basin: whenever the winter moon is bright and the snow is deep, a great white cat—far too large to be a normal lynx or cougar—appears like a phantom among the trees. Popular legend links this supernatural predator to the spirits of the plane crash victims, watching quietly over the lonely backcountry area.

#location
Plewman Basin is a horseshoe-shaped alpine basin located at the foot of Mount Plewman in the Rossland Range, just west of Rossland, British Columbia.
