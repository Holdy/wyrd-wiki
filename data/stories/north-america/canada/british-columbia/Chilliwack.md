#title
The Poltergeist of Brooks Avenue

#summary
In late 1951, a Chilliwack chicken farmer and her teenage niece were driven to exhaustion by weeks of violent exterior bangings and mysterious disturbances that attracted massive crowds of onlookers.

#details
Beginning in November 1951, 38-year-old chicken farmer Anna Duryba and her 14-year-old niece, Kathryn, found themselves tormented by weeks of strange noises and broken windows at their home on Brooks Avenue. Heavy, rapid bangings echoed against the outer walls of the house on a regular basis, sometimes occurring dozens of times in a single week. The disturbances were severe enough to prompt Kathryn to temporarily relocate to Vancouver while her aunt tried to cope with the chaos. 

Local figures weighed in on the mystery, with Reverend William Clarke publicly diagnosing that a poltergeist was at large. Despite repeatedly telling reporters that she did not believe in ghosts, Anna found herself exhausted not only by the unexplained noises and broken glass, but also by the hordes of curious townspeople who flocked to her property to gawk at the house, creating severe parking and traffic problems for the neighborhood.

#location
A residential property on Brooks Avenue in Chilliwack, British Columbia.

#title
The Specter of Williams Street

#summary
In the 1960s, a stately three-storey manor in Chilliwack became a national sensation when its residents and hundreds of curious onlookers reported phantom footsteps, moving furniture, and unexplained apparitions.

#details
Built in 1909 by travelling salesman W. E. Bradwin at the corner of Williams Street North and Portage Avenue, the large, multi-story manor became the center of intense local folklore when Hetty and Douglas Fredrickson unknowingly purchased it in 1965. Shortly after moving in, the family experienced bizarre phenomena: drawers opened and slammed shut on their own, a heavy iron bedstead shifted across the floor, and footsteps echoed upstairs while everyone was downstairs. Hetty eventually discovered a hidden doorway leading to a small, forgotten turret room. She also reported seeing an illuminated mist smelling of perfume and experienced vivid dreams of a terrified woman in a red dress with yellow flowers, which inspired her to paint a likeness of the spirit. 

When the local newspaper covered the haunting in 1966, media outlets across the country and as far away as Japan picked up the story. The publicity backfired when up to 700 sightseers descended on the property on a single Sunday, breaking the front steps and forcing the couple to post "no sightseers" signs. Overwhelmed by the relentless crowds, the Fredricksons moved away in 1968. Subsequent tenants reported that ghosts still roamed the halls, including a mother ghost who watched television with the family, before the historic building burned down in the 1970s.

#location
Near the intersection of Williams Street North and Portage Avenue in Chilliwack, British Columbia.
