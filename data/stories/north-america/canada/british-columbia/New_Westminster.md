#title
The Ghost of the Met Hotel Basement

#summary
The historic Met Hotel on Columbia Street, one of the few survivors of New Westminster's 1898 Great Fire, is haunted by a former caretaker named George and female entities recorded via electronic voice phenomena.

#details
Housed in the historic Burr Block, The Met Hotel survived the Great Fire of New Westminster in 1898. Paranormal investigators and staff have noted intense activity, particularly in the basement where a former caretaker named George lived and died. Staff have reported George floating through hallways, walking through walls, and playing pranks like invisible hands clapping loudly in front of visitors' faces. Audio recordings (EVPs) captured during investigations have also recorded a female entity identifying herself as Mary.

#location
The Met Hotel is located at 411 Columbia Street in New Westminster, British Columbia.

#title
The Phantom Engineer of the Samson V

#summary
The historic sternwheeler Samson V moored in New Westminster is haunted by Frank Creeden, a chief engineer who suffered a fatal heart attack in the engine room in 1942.

#details
The Samson V is a historic snag boat that served on the Fraser River removing logs and obstacles. In October 2022, paranormal investigator Greg Mansfield witnessed a grey, transparent shadow figure moving swiftly through the vessel's galley, appearing clearly as a man wearing a thick sweater and cropped hair. This apparition is widely believed to be Frank Creeden, the ship’s chief engineer who died of a heart attack in the engine room in 1942. Staff and visitors over the decades have repeatedly reported the full-bodied apparition of the sailor brushing past them before vanishing into thin air.

#location
The Samson V Museum is moored at Westminster Pier Park along the Fraser River waterfront in New Westminster, British Columbia.

#title
The Spectres of Irving House

#summary
Built in 1865, Irving House in New Westminster is famously haunted by riverboat captain William Irving, who died in the home in 1872 and whose presence is marked by mysterious footsteps, pipe tobacco scents, and phantom indentations on his deathbed.

#details
Captain William Irving, known as "The King of the River," had Irving House built in 1865 in the Gothic Revival style using redwood shipped from California. After his death in the home in 1872, staff, visitors, and family members reported continuous paranormal occurrences. Betty Miller, mother of a former curator, felt the ghost of William Irving brush past her on the stairs whispering "I must make haste" on his birthday. Over the decades, caretakers and visitors have reported white feathers drifting down the stairs, cold spots, the distinct scent of pipe tobacco, and an unexplained body-sized indentation appearing on the antique mattress in John's room where the captain passed away.

#location
Irving House is located at 302 Royal Avenue in New Westminster, British Columbia.
