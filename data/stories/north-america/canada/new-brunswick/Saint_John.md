#title
The Crypt of James Putnam in the Loyalist Burial Ground

#summary
The Loyalist Burial Ground in Saint John contains the historic crypt of James Putnam, encircled by iron bars to deter grave robbers, where visitors claim to feel his lingering spirit.

#details
Established after the arrival of United Empire Loyalists in 1783, the Loyalist Burial Ground holds generations of early settlers. Deep within the grounds lies the crypt of Chief Justice James Putnam, a descendant of the family linked to the Salem Witch Trials, who died in 1789. His crypt is surrounded by a cast-iron fence originally intended to keep grave robbers at bay and form a physical barrier between the living and the dead. Today, many visitors report feeling an uneasy presence and the lingering spirit of James Putnam within the cemetery.

#title
The Phantoms of Wolastoq Park

#summary
Wolastoq Park in Saint John, built on the former site of the Provincial Lunatic Asylum, is reportedly haunted by the spirits of former patients, staff members, and mysterious shadowy figures.

#details
For 150 years, the scenic green space known today as Wolastoq Park was home to the Provincial Lunatic Asylum, which housed hundreds of patients. After the healthcare facility was demolished, reports emerged of apparition-like former patients and staff wandering the park grounds. Witnesses have described mysterious shadowy figures, phantom crying babies, malfunctioning electronics, and a general feeling of unspeakable dread lingering in the area.

#title
Walter Golding's Haunting at the Imperial Theatre

#summary
The historic Imperial Theatre in Saint John is said to be haunted by its former passionate manager, Walter Golding, who still watches over the lobby and lounge named after him.

#details
Built in 1913 on the site of an old dramatic lyceum, the Imperial Theatre was envisioned by theatre manager Walter Golding to be the finest theatre on the East Coast, hosting acts like Harry Houdini and Ethel Barrymore. Golding lived with his family in an apartment just above the upper lobby, and his life was deeply intertwined with the historic venue. Following his passing, visitors and staff have reported sightings of Golding in the dim light of the lobby and a lingering sense of being watched while walking through Golding's Lounge after dark.
