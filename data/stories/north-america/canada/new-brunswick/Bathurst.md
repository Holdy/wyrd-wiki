#title
The Apparition of the Doucet Hennessy House

#summary
A historic 200-year-old homestead in Bathurst is reportedly haunted by the ghost of a Scottish immigrant whose body was controversially kept in a snowbank after his unexpected winter death.

#details
The Doucet Hennessy House, sitting on a hill along St. Peter Avenue overlooking the Bay of Chaleur, has accumulated numerous ghostly reports over the decades, including children playing on the stair landing and full-bodied apparitions at the top of the stairs. Historians and residents associate the primary apparition with Rufus Millikan, a Scottish Protestant who boarded with the Acadian Doucet family and died unexpectedly during the harsh winter of 1818. Because there was no embalming and the house was heated by wood, the family placed his body out in a snowbank rather than notifying the local Protestant congregation for proper rites, sparking outrage and an official complaint to Fredericton. Years later, visitors and tenants reported terrifying encounters, including a young boy who was so startled by the apparition of a man standing at the top of the stairs that he fell backward all the way down the staircase.

#location
The Doucet Hennessy House, located at the top of a hill on St. Peter Avenue in Bathurst, New Brunswick, overlooking the Bay of Chaleur.

#title
The Fireship of Baie des Chaleurs

#summary
A spectral, flaming three-masted ship occasionally emerges in the waters of Chaleur Bay near Bathurst, believed by locals to be cursed after historical acts of piracy and violence.

#details
For centuries, residents and sailors along the coast of Bathurst, New Brunswick, and Chaleur Bay have reported sightings of an eerie, glowing ghost light shaped like a burning three-masted ship. According to local folklore, one prominent version of the legend traces the haunting back to 1501, when a Portuguese captain arrived on Heron Island in Chaleur Bay and engaged in the slave trade against the local Mi'kmaq people, resulting in his death. When his brother returned a year later seeking vengeance, the ship was attacked and burned, with the crew leaping into the icy waters and swearing to haunt the bay for a thousand years. On calm or stormy nights, witnesses have claimed to see the fiery arc of the phantom ship patrolling the bay, and some old tales even suggest that plunging one's feet into the waters of Chaleur Bay allows listeners to hear the distant screams of the burning passengers.

#location
Chaleur Bay (Baie des Chaleurs), off the coast of Bathurst, New Brunswick, and Heron Island.
