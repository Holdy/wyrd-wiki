#title
The John Craig Light

#summary
Fishermen and residents off Lamèque Island and Miscou Island have long reported seeing a fiery phantom ship engulfed in flames as an omen of tragedy or disaster.

#details
For over two centuries, residents and fishermen around Lamèque Island and Miscou Island in the Baie des Chaleurs have reported sightings of an eerie, fully rigged phantom ship engulfed in red-orange flames. Local tradition ties this apparition, often called the "John Craig Light," to a tragic shipwreck that occurred over 200 years ago involving a vessel named the *John Craig*. Regarded by local Acadian fishermen as both a spectral warning light and a harbinger of approaching storms or death, the blazing ship is said to silently bob upon the dark waters before vanishing into the night.

#location
The coastal waters surrounding Lamèque Island and Miscou Island in the Baie des Chaleurs, New Brunswick.

#upstream-source

#title
The Phantom Death Cart of Lamèque

#summary
Residents of Lamèque Island have historically recounted nocturnal hearings of a squeaking phantom cart that foretells an impending death in the community.

#details
In the traditional oral folklore of Lamèque Island, locals have long whispered of a mysterious, squeaking cart that travels along the island's roads in the dead of night. Reminiscent of the Breton legend of the Ankou, this invisible or ghostly carriage produces the distinct, haunting sound of wooden wheels grinding against axles on the eve of a community member's passing. Passed down through generations and preserved in regional ethnographic archives, the phantom cart serves as an ominous auditory harbinger of mortality.

#location
The rural roads of Lamèque Island (Île-de-Lamèque), New Brunswick.
