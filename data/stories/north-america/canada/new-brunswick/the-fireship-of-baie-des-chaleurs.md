#title
The Fireship of Baie des Chaleurs

#summary
A spectral, flaming three-masted ship occasionally emerges in the waters of Chaleur Bay near Bathurst, believed by locals to be cursed after historical acts of piracy and violence.

#details
For centuries, residents and sailors along the coast of Bathurst, New Brunswick, and Chaleur Bay have reported sightings of an eerie, glowing ghost light shaped like a burning three-masted ship. According to local folklore, one prominent version of the legend traces the haunting back to 1501, when a Portuguese captain arrived on Heron Island in Chaleur Bay and engaged in the slave trade against the local Mi'kmaq people, resulting in his death. When his brother returned a year later seeking vengeance, the ship was attacked and burned, with the crew leaping into the icy waters and swearing to haunt the bay for a thousand years. On calm or stormy nights, witnesses have claimed to see the fiery arc of the phantom ship patrolling the bay, and some old tales even suggest that plunging one's feet into the waters of Chaleur Bay allows listeners to hear the distant screams of the burning passengers.

#location
Chaleur Bay (Baie des Chaleurs), off the coast of Bathurst, New Brunswick, and Heron Island.
