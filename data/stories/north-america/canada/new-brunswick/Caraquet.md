#title
The Fire Ship of Baie des Chaleurs

#summary
A glowing, ghostly sailing ship engulfed in eternal flames haunts the dark waters of Baie des Chaleurs near Caraquet, frequently appearing before storms.

#details
For generations, residents, fishermen, and sea captains along the coast of Caraquet, Grand Anse, and Miscou Island have witnessed a mysterious glowing vessel drifting across the Baie des Chaleurs. Known locally as the "fire-ship" or *feu-du-mauvais-temps*, the apparition typically appears as a half-moon shape or glowing hull with vibrating rigging that dances over the water. In 1905, Harvard naturalist and historian William F. Ganong investigated the phenomenon after numerous credible local residents—including a Caraquet homeowner who mistook the glowing apparition for her husband's schooner—reported seeing the eternal flame ship prior to severe maritime storms.

#location
The coastal waters of Baie des Chaleurs adjacent to the town of Caraquet, New Brunswick.

#title
The Ghost Ship of Caraquet Island

#summary
A burning French refugee ship attacked by pirates during the 1757 deportation era repeatedly reappears off Caraquet Island as a spectral apparition accompanied by cries and moans.

#details
According to local tradition recounted in Caraquet, during the autumn raids of 1757 along the Baie des Chaleurs, pirates seized a vessel carrying Acadian refugees and burned it in the open sea off the coast of Caraquet Island. Survivors who made it to shore were terrified by the ghostly sight of the burning ship accompanied by echoing screams and groans. Locals note that the phantom vessel returns periodically, with documented sightings including an early morning appearance recorded near the Oyster Eco-Museum in Caraquet.

#location
The open waters and coastline surrounding Caraquet Island and the Oyster Eco-Museum in Caraquet, New Brunswick.
