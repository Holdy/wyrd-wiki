#title
The Crosses of Sugarloaf Mountain

#summary
Two sisters tragically fell to their deaths while hiking Sugarloaf Mountain in 1924, prompting enduring local legends and memorial crosses painted on the cliffs.

#details
On November 9, 1924, two sisters, Dorval McLean and Lottie Ramsay, hiked up Sugarloaf Mountain in Campbellton before a planned family dinner. When they failed to return, an overnight search party discovered both women had fallen from the high cliffs to their deaths. The tragic event stunned the community and became a lasting part of local folklore, inspiring various speculations about the cause of the fall and leading local veterans to paint two large red crosses on the rock face to commemorate the sisters.

#location
The sheer rock cliffs of Sugarloaf Mountain in Campbellton, New Brunswick.

#title
The Ghost Cow of Campbellton

#summary
In the summer of 1886, a Campbellton resident spent two nights terrified that he was being haunted by a ghost cow, only to discover a live animal trapped in a haystack.

#details
During the summer of 1886 in the booming town of Campbellton, New Brunswick, free-range cows wore cowbells that sparked fierce public debate among residents. Amidst this local controversy, resident James Taylor began hearing strange, muffled mooing sounds echoing near his home at night. Convinced for two days and nights that he was being haunted by a phantom ghost cow, Taylor finally investigated his property and found a missing cow named Bossy stuck inside a haystack.

#location
