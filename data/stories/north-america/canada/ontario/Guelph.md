#title
The Ghost of the Albion Hotel

#summary
The historic Albion Hotel in downtown Guelph is said to be haunted by the spirit of a woman linked to American gangster Al Capone who died tragically on the premises.

#details
Built in 1856, the Albion Hotel holds the second liquor licence in Ontario and has long been a focal point for local folklore. According to local ghost walks and historical accounts, infamous American gangster Al Capone frequented the hotel during the Prohibition era, utilizing underground tunnels to visit his mistress. Legend holds that a woman associated with the Capone era met her end within the building, and her restless spirit has since been witnessed peering from upstairs windows or lingering in the historic rooms.

#location
The Albion Hotel is located at 49 Quebec Street in downtown Guelph, Ontario.

#title
The Haunting of Guelph's First Jail and Gallows

#summary
The site of Guelph's original county jail, which hosted at least nine executions between 1847 and 1919, is plagued by reports of shadowy figures and unexplained paranormal activity.

#details
Constructed in 1839 on Woolwich Street, the old county gaol witnessed numerous public and private executions, including those of convicted murderers whose final moments went tragically awry. Tour guides and courthouse staff working near the preserved limestone walls have frequently reported an eerie sense of being watched, shadows moving down empty corridors, and unexplained spikes on electromagnetic field detectors. Spirits such as inmates who died within the prison walls—including those who maintained their innocence—are said to still walk the exercise yard and surrounding administrative grounds.

#location
The historic old Guelph jail site is situated behind the County of Wellington Courthouse and Administrative Centre at 74 Woolwich Street in Guelph, Ontario.

#title
The Spirits of the Baker Street Burial Ground

#summary
Downtown Guelph's Baker Street parking lot, which served as a public burial ground in the 19th century, is frequently associated with sightings of a spectral woman in white.

#details
Before it was paved over and utilized as a municipal parking lot, the triangular block bounded by Baker Street was Guelph's first non-denominational public cemetery from 1827 to 1853. Although many bodies were eventually relocated, archaeological digs and construction over the decades have repeatedly uncovered human skeletal remains. Local legend and paranormal researchers maintain that the displaced souls never truly left the grounds, with numerous witnesses reporting encounters with a phantom woman in a white dress wandering the asphalt after dark.

#location
The Baker Street parking lot is located at the Baker District site in downtown Guelph, Ontario.
