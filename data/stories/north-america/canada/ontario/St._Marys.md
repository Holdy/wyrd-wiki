#title
Spirits and Tragedies Along the Thames River

#summary
Riverside folklore in St. Marys preserves dark historical accounts of fatal quarry accidents, a mysterious mill fire, and a disgraced nineteenth-century doctor.

#details
Local history and ghost lore tied to the early years of St. Marys recount tragic events that allegedly left lingering spirits along the water. Guided walking tours and local legends highlight several chilling occurrences, including fatal accidents in the town's deep limestone quarries, a devastating and mysterious fire at an old local mill, and the unsettling tale of a disgraced nineteenth-century medical doctor whose spirit is said to haunt the river path.

#location
The tour route begins at the flagpole along the river path at The Falls in St. Marys, Ontario.

#title
The Spirits of the Castle in the Bush

#summary
Built in 1854, the historic St. Marys Museum is said to be haunted by former residents, most notably the stern businesswoman Fanny Weir.

#details
Originally constructed in 1854 as the largest home in the village—historically known as the "Castle in the Bush"—this grand limestone house passed through several prominent families, including founders George Tracy, Daniel McDougall, and later John and Fanny Weir. Local accounts and paranormal investigations indicate that Fanny Weir continues to inhabit the home with an authoritative presence, reportedly displeased that her husband John remarried after her passing. Visitors and investigators have also reported disembodied voices, unexplainable cold spots, and activity attributed to other members of the Weir family roaming the halls.

#location
St. Marys Museum is located at 177 Church Street South in St. Marys, Ontario.
