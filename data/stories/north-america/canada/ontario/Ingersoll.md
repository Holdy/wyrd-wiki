#title
The Haunting of Elm Hurst Inn

#summary
Built in 1867 by dairy pioneer James Harris, the historic Elm Hurst Inn in Ingersoll is said to be haunted by former residents, with staff and guests frequently reporting spectral sightings and unexplained phenomena.

#details
The Elm Hurst Inn & Spa, situated in Ingersoll, Ontario, centers around a grand Gothic Revival mansion built in 1867 by prominent 19th-century dairy farmer James Harris—famous for creating the legendary 7,000-pound "Mammoth Cheese" that traveled to world exhibitions in the United States and England. Beyond its agricultural fame, the estate has long been associated with local ghost lore. Over the years, employees and overnight guests have reported eerie paranormal occurrences inside the historic building, including inexplicable cold spots, phantom footsteps echoing through the upper-floor hallways, and doors operating of their own accord. Local tradition attributes these manifestations to former inhabitants of the house, such as Mary Ranney (James Harris’s second wife) and Mary Jane, a nineteenth-century resident who succumbed to illness on the property. For generations, visitors and passersby have also recounted sightings of a sorrowful "Lady in Grey" peering out from the upper balcony, preserving the inn's reputation as Ingersoll's premier haunted landmark.

#location
Elm Hurst Inn & Spa is located at 415 Harris Street in Ingersoll, Ontario.
