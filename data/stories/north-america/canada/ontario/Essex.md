#title
The Curse of Peche Island

#summary
A 100-acre island in the Detroit River remains undeveloped after a grieving widow cast a vengeful curse following her eviction in the 19th century.

#details
In 1833, the family of Hiram Walker acquired most of Peche Island except for four acres belonging to Leon Laforet. Following Leon's death, his widow Rosalie was allegedly forced out of her home by thugs hired by the Walkers who threw money on the table and demanded the deed. As she departed, Rosalie placed a chilling curse on the land, declaring that no one would ever successfully do anything with the island. True to her words, a string of tragic misfortunes followed: Willis Walker died suddenly at 28, Hiram Walker died of a stroke in 1899, the president of the Windsor Detroit Ferry Co. mysteriously died the same year they purchased it, and the island's grand summer mansion burned to the ground in 1929. Subsequent housing and ski resort development plans in the 1950s and 1960s also collapsed, leaving the island completely untouched to this day.

#location
Peche Island is situated in the Detroit River, off the coast of Tecumseh and Windsor in Essex County, Ontario.

#title
The Haunting of Texas Road

#summary
An 11-kilometre stretch of rural roadway in Amherstburg is said to be haunted by a headless horseman, ghostly lanterns, and tragic specters.

#details
Texas Road has long been a source of dark local folklore and urban legends in Essex County. According to local lore, the road is haunted by the spirit of a man whose body was found abandoned in a ditch in 1881 near the local cemetery, as well as a decapitated woman in white roaming the graves on foggy nights. Other legends tell of a headless horseman riding through the wooded areas, accompanied by the sounds of galloping horses and the crack of a whip. Motorists have also reported being chased along the bridge by a phantom Jeep with a single headlight—a chilling manifestation tied to a historical tragedy where a young man took his own life after his girlfriend was thrown into the creek during a crash.

#location
Texas Road between Concession 8 and Walker Road in Amherstburg, Ontario.

#title
The Phantoms of Amherstburg's Historic Houses

#summary
Staff and visitors at two historic Amherstburg buildings frequently encounter ghostly apparitions, unexplained piano music, and disembodied voices from the 19th century.

#details
Park House Museum and Gordon House in Amherstburg are steeped in paranormal activity spanning over two centuries. At the Park House Museum, visitors and curators have reported encounters with the spirit of Mrs. Park on the second floor, where doors open and close by themselves, cradles rock unassisted, and a phantom woman in a black dress has been mistaken for a living staff member while giving tours. Nearby at Gordon House—which served as a home in the early 1800s and later a maternity house—witnesses have heard phantom piano music in parlors without pianos, crying babies in the former birthing room, fully dressed soldiers from the War of 1812 walking the halls, and even an office desk hovering in mid-air before gently settling back down.

#location
Park House Museum and Gordon House are located on Dalhousie Street in Amherstburg, Ontario.
