Dorothea's Eternal Vigil at the Hockey Hall of Fame

Summary

Visitors and staff at a downtown Toronto landmark originally built as a bank have reported inexplicable footsteps, flickering lights, and eerie presences. These phenomena are attributed to the tragic spirit of a 19-year-old bank employee who took her own life inside the building in 1953.

Details

On March 11, 1953, 19-year-old bank teller Dorothea Mae Elliott arrived early to work at the Bank of Montreal branch located at the corner of Yonge and Front Streets. Distressed over personal troubles and a rumored affair with a branch manager, Dorothea went upstairs to the second-floor women's washroom and tragically shot herself with a revolver kept for security. She passed away the following day at St. Michael's Hospital. Ever since the building was transformed into the Hockey Hall of Fame, cleaning staff and visitors have reported doors and windows opening on their own, lights flickering, and faint footsteps echoing through the empty Great Hall—manifestations widely believed to be the restless spirit of Dorothea.
