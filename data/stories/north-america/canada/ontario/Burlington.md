#title
The 1830s Time Capsule of Ireland House

#summary
Burlington's oldest surviving home, built in the 1830s by English immigrant Joseph Ireland, serves as a hotbed of paranormal activity and ghostly encounters tied to nearly 150 years of family history.

#details
Completed in 1837 by farmer Joseph Ireland along the Guelph Line, Ireland House remained occupied by generations of the same family until 1985, leaving behind an untouched time capsule of Victorian furniture, diaries, and personal artifacts—including a traditional human hair wreath. Operating today as a historic museum, the house is frequently investigated by paranormal groups. Visitors and staff report sudden shifts in atmosphere, unexplained cold spots, and distinct presences echoing the daily lives of the Ireland family who lived and loved there for over a century.

#title
The Ghostly Patriot of St. Luke's Cemetery

#summary
Every year on the anniversary of his death, the spirit of Mohawk leader and Burlington founder Joseph Brant is said to manifest as a fully-formed apparition visiting his daughter's final resting place.

#details
Joseph Brant, a prominent leader of the Mohawk people and namesake for much of Burlington, died in 1807. Although his remains were later relocated to the Mohawk Chapel in Brantford, local legend and eyewitness accounts maintain that on November 24th right around the witching hour, Brant appears at St. Luke's Cemetery. Standing six feet tall in full traditional Indigenous dress while holding a rifle and a sword, he reportedly lets out a loud battle cry before fading away among the gravestones. Visitors to the 192-year-old cemetery have also captured mysterious light orbs and witnessed dark shadows moving among the headstones.

#location
St. Luke's Anglican Church Cemetery on Plains Road East, Burlington, Ontario.

#title
The Spectral Hostess of Emma's Back Porch

#summary
The historic 19th-century building in Burlington that once housed the famous Estaminet and Emma's Back Porch restaurants is said to be haunted by its former owner and mysterious phantom children who manifest through chilling basement noises and apparitions.

#details
Originally built in the 1800s and later run by Emma and George Byrens starting in 1919, the establishment hosted numerous dignitaries, politicians, and celebrities. Following its decades as a beloved restaurant, staff and managers reported intense supernatural activity, particularly in the basement—affectionately dubbed "The Dungeon"—where dishwashers encountered a silent little boy sitting on the floor whose form could be passed right through. Employees also frequently heard disembodied sounds of clinking glasses, heavy pounding on the walls, and ghostly figures along the historic staircases.
