#title
The Church Street Poltergeist

#summary
In February 1970, an apartment at 237 Church Street in St. Catharines became the site of a heavily documented poltergeist outbreak centered around an eleven-year-old boy, which was witnessed by multiple police officers and clergymen.

#details
In early February 1970, Niagara Regional Police officers responded to a domestic dispute call at Apartment 1 of 237 Church Street in St. Catharines. Upon arriving, officers found the apartment in total disarray with furniture and household objects displaced by unseen forces. Over the course of several weeks, responding officers—including Bob Crawford and Bill Weir—alongside local clergy, witnessed extreme paranormal phenomena. Bowling trophies flew off shelves one by one, a kitchen wall clock unplugged itself and fell without making a sound, a heavy chest of drawers slid across the room, and beds and chesterfields levitated while people were sitting on them. The epicenter of the disturbances was the family's eleven-year-old son, Peter, who was occasionally pinned to the floor by heavy furniture or targeted by invisible entities before the family was advised to temporarily relocate.

#location
Apartment 1 at 237 Church Street, St. Catharines, Ontario.

#title
The Haunting of Oak Hill

#summary
Built in 1860 by Welland Canal pioneer William Hamilton Merritt, the historic Oak Hill estate in St. Catharines has long held a reputation for hauntings, marked by unexplained noises, phantom odours, and eerie phenomena in its historic rooms and underground tunnels.

#details
Following the destruction of his previous home by an arsonist fire, William Hamilton Merritt constructed Oak Hill around 1860 at the corner of St. Paul Street West and Yates Street in St. Catharines. Merritt was a prominent merchant and the driving force behind the construction of the first Welland Canal, as well as an abolitionist associated with the Underground Railroad, featuring basement tunnels leading down toward Twelve Mile Creek. After Merritt's death, the estate passed through various private owners and served as a military convalescent home and an inn before being purchased in 1938 to house the broadcasting studios of CKTB radio. Over the decades, staff and visitors have reported a steady stream of unexplained noises, strange drafts, phantom odours, and anomalous presences throughout the grand old home.

#location
Oak Hill (Merritt House) at the corner of St. Paul Street West and Yates Street, St. Catharines, Ontario.
