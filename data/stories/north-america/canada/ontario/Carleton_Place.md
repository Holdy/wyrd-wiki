#title
The Ghost Lovers of Springside Hall

#summary
An elderly couple who spent fifty years living in Springside Hall continue to haunt the dining room bay window after their deaths, where residents have witnessed them dancing together in the shadows.

#details
Springside Hall, originally known as the Morphy House, was home to an elderly couple for half a century. During their lives together, the husband would sit in the dining room bay window and occasionally take his wife's hand to dance while she played tango music. After they passed away and new occupants moved in, strange electrical anomalies began occurring in the dining room: electrical plugs popped out of sockets, old porcelain doll eyes inexplicably cracked, and radios tuned to white noise abruptly switched to deafening tango music. Late one night, the resident witnessed two glowing ghostly figures holding each other tight and tango dancing in the shadows of the dining room bay window, finally reunited in tranquility.

#location
Springside Hall (the Morphy House) is located at 77 Lake Ave East in Carleton Place, Ontario.

#title
The Ghost of Ida Moore at the Moore House

#summary
Following her tragic death from tuberculosis at age twenty, the spirit of young musician Ida Moore became known for lingering in her family's historic log home, playing pranks, turning radios on and off, and pacing the second floor.

#details
In 1850, J.P. Moore built a log home on land granted to his father, William Moore, one of the earliest settlers of Carleton Place. The home was later occupied by his musically gifted daughter, Ida, who tragically died from tuberculosis at just twenty years of age in 1900. After her passing, the building served various purposes before being donated to the town and relocated. Long before and after the move, occupants and visitors reported unexplained phenomena, including objects moving on their own, loud footsteps pacing the second floor, cold spots, and radios turning on and off by themselves. Volunteers and historians frequently report feeling watched or hearing ghostly echoes of the young woman who still seems to haunt her former home.

#location
The historic Moore House is located at 170 Bridge Street in Carleton Place, Ontario.

#title
The Hauntings of the Leslie Block

#summary
Built in 1895 as a furniture showroom and undertaking parlor, the Leslie Block is reportedly haunted by the spirit of a former Civil War nurse who died on the second floor.

#details
Jacob Leslie constructed the Leslie Block on Bridge Street in 1895 to house a furniture store and an undertaking business where bodies were prepared and embalmed. According to local lore, Emma Myers—a former Civil War nurse who worked as an assistant embalmer—met a tragic end on the second floor after falling into a coma. Following her death, successive shop owners and tenants reported lingering cold spots, slamming doors, phantom shadows, and people mysteriously getting locked inside the second-floor bathroom. Dogs brought into the building flatly refused to climb the stairs, and decades later, lingering embalming fluid famously corroded the back wall of the building until it collapsed in 1953.

#location
The Leslie Block is located on Bridge Street in Carleton Place, Ontario.
