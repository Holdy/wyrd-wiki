#title
The Ghost of Phoebe Watson at Homer Watson House

#summary
Staff and visitors at a historic Kitchener art gallery frequently encounter the spirit of Phoebe Watson, the artist's sister who passed away in 1947.

#details
Homer Watson, a renowned Canadian landscape artist, famously hosted spiritualist seances in his home after his wife's death. Following Homer's passing, his sister Phoebe continued living in the house until her death in 1947, after which her apparition began appearing throughout the property. Visitors and employees have reported seeing an elderly woman in full Victorian dress with her hair in a bun assisting guests or walking down the hallways. Staff members have also experienced being tapped on the shoulder, having research papers mysteriously left out on the floor, and being locked outside when the front door locked itself. Additionally, employees have heard the sounds of children playing hide-and-seek in the basement after hours.

#location
Homer Watson House and Gallery is located at 175 Mill Street in Kitchener, Ontario.

#title
The Phantom Seamstress of Joseph Schneider House

#summary
Visitors and staff at Kitchener's oldest standing building report a persistent feeling of being watched and sightings of a phantom seamstress working in the upper rooms.

#details
Built in 1840, the Joseph Schneider House is Kitchener's oldest surviving structure, now preserved as a living history museum near downtown. People touring the restored 19th-century rooms have consistently reported an eerie, lingering sense of being watched, particularly from the direction of the small attic windows. According to local folklore and staff observations, a phantom seamstress occasionally manifests in the upstairs bedroom and attic space, diligently working away at her sewing as if preserving the domestic routines of the past.

#location
Joseph Schneider House is located at 466 Queen Street South in Kitchener, Ontario.

#title
The Traynor Avenue Poltergeist

#summary
Residents of a Kitchener townhouse complex experienced intense paranormal disturbances in the 1980s, including self-activating plumbing, flying objects, and unseen entities.

#details
During the 1980s, tenants living in units 18 and 25 of a townhouse complex at 300 Traynor Avenue reported a series of baffling and frightening occurrences. Witnesses described bath taps suddenly turning on full blast, tea towels flying off racks in closed rooms, footsteps echoing on stairways, and the phantom sound of cats lapping milk. More disturbingly, residents reported sudden waves of fear and physical sensations of being held by invisible arms. While some tenants fled the complex due to the activity, others grew accustomed to the unseen presence and affectionately considered it a "good neighbor."

#location
The townhouse complex is located at 300 Traynor Avenue in the Vanier neighbourhood of Kitchener, Ontario.
