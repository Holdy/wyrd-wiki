#title
The Legend of the Devil's Elbow

#summary
South of Port Elgin along Highway 21, a notoriously sharp road bend and dark local swamp earned the grim moniker "the Devil's Elbow" from early settlers and Indigenous oral traditions due to tales of local spirits and misery.

#details
In the 19th century, early Scottish settlers traveling from Concession 13 toward Port Elgin navigated a Goderich Road route that swung south along the Bruce/Saugeen Townline. Settler accounts, such as those recorded by Donald MacGillivray, noted that travelers first passed a dark, snake-filled swamp before reaching a severe, elbow-shaped turn in the road. This bend became known as the Devil's Elbow—a name cemented by the presence of a notorious local tavern that was widely blamed for inciting local misfortune and misery. Long before the highway was straightened and paved in the 1930s, both Odawa traditions and pioneer folklore regarded the surrounding waters and winding terrain as haunted by dark forces and lingering spirits.

#location
The location is the historic bend on Highway 21 in North Bruce, just south of Port Elgin within Saugeen Shores, Ontario, near the crossing of Black Creek along the Bruce-Saugeen townline.
