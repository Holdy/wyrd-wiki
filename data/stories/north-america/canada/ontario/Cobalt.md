#title
Hermie of the Crystal Vault

#summary
A historic 1906 bank building turned boutique at 7 Prospect Avenue in Cobalt is haunted by a mischievous miner spirit named Hermie who rearranges items and drops books.

#details
Originally established as a bank in 1906 and later serving as an inn and mining office, 7 Prospect Avenue became active with paranormal phenomena after modern renovations stripped away old ceiling tiles. Shop owners and visitors frequently encounter "Hermie," a spirit believed to be a fallen miner. Encounters include crystals and books being inexplicably thrown to the floor—including a local ghost history book left open overnight—and hockey team photographs capturing dozens of glowing orbs hovering near the old bank vault stairs.

#location
The Crystal Vault is located at 7 Prospect Avenue in Cobalt, Ontario.

#title
The Phantom of the Bunker Military Museum

#summary
Housed in a landmark 1910 silver exchange and luxury hotel, the Bunker Military Museum in Cobalt is stalked by a silent, dark phantom that mimics the mannequin exhibits.

#details
Marg Harrison, the museum board president who was skeptical of the paranormal for nearly seven decades, experienced multiple sightings of a menacing, dark figure standing motionless among 65 uniformed mannequin soldiers on display in the "parade ground" room. Since the building was converted into a military museum in 2012, staff and visitors have reported numerous chilling encounters and unexplained presences within the historic boomtown structure.

#location
The Bunker Military Museum is located in the historic Royal Exchange building in Cobalt, Ontario.

#title
The Specter of the Right of Way Headframe

#summary
The historic headframe of the Right of Way Mine in Cobalt is said to be haunted by violent pounding noises and phantom mists, recalling the tragic deaths of miners who perished deep underground.

#details
During tours of the towering Right of Way Mine headframe—which dives 164 meters into the bedrock beneath Cobalt Lake—tour guide Dan Larocque experienced terrifying unexplained pounding on the lower floor and windows while completely alone on the third level. Photographic evidence from the site frequently reveals thick unexplainable mists and floating orbs. The mine claimed the lives of up to 27 men during its operation, many of whose bodies were never recovered from the stygian darkness.

#location
The Right of Way Headframe stands at the north end of Cobalt Lake in Cobalt, Ontario.
