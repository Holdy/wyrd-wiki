#title
The Almonte Ghost of 1886

#summary
In late 1886, residents of Almonte were terrorized for nearly a month by a horned apparition walking the streets to the sound of clanking chains.

#details
During the Victorian era in December 1886, the town of Almonte was gripped by fear and curiosity over a mysterious entity that nightly walked along local streets, specifically near Farm Street. Described by terrified locals in local newspaper accounts as a hideous creature with horns accompanied by the chilling sound of clanking chains, the apparition caused public uproar. A vigilance committee was even proposed to tar and feather whoever was behind the ghoulish prank, though the true identity of the phantom remained a baffling local mystery.

#location
Farm Street in Almonte, Mississippi Mills, Ontario.

#title
The Ghost in the Glen

#summary
The historic estate known as "The Glen" in Almonte is haunted by former textile mill owner Archibald Rosamond, who leaves behind the aroma of pipe tobacco and moves furniture.

#details
Built in 1917–1918 by wealthy textile mill owner Archibald Rosamond, the large estate on Malcolm Street known as "The Glen" became home to the Cameron family in the 1950s. Soon after moving in, Peggy Cameron and her children experienced heavy footsteps on the stairs, doors unlocking and slamming on their own, heavy wooden chests shifting across the attic floor, and the distinct, aromatic scent of pipe tobacco wafting through the halls. Recognizing Rosamond's habits from historical accounts, the family came to accept their mischievous resident phantom, affectionately attributing missing items and unexplained noises to "Archie."

#location
"The Glen" on Malcolm Street in Almonte, Mississippi Mills, Ontario.

45.2239917, -76.2013682

#title
The Ghosts of the Mill of Kintail

#summary
The historic Mill of Kintail is said to be haunted by sculptor Robert Tait McKenzie and his wife Ethel, whose spirits react to building renovations and guard their former summer home.

#details
In 1932, prominent Canadian doctor and artist Dr. Robert Tait McKenzie converted a long-abandoned 1830s grist mill along the Indian River into a summer home for himself and his wife, Ethel. Following Dr. McKenzie's death in 1938, visitors and local artists working in the mill have frequently reported paranormal phenomena. The spirit of Ethel McKenzie is said to become agitated when her former bedroom and washroom are disturbed by renovations, while Dr. McKenzie's ghost reportedly roams the halls at night to guard his beloved domain.

#location
The Mill of Kintail is located along the Indian River in Ramsay Township, Mississippi Mills, Ontario.

#title
The Insane Spinster Ghost of Appleton

#summary
A historic 1830s home in Appleton is haunted by a spinster driven mad by a brood of children, whose cold presence and footsteps still linger.

#details
Legend tells of a house built in the 1830s in the village of Appleton for an affluent couple who invited the husband's spinster sister to live with them. After her brother died, leaving twelve children behind, the couple insisted the children call the spinster "Mummy dear." The overwhelming chaos slowly drove her insane, leading to an early death. Decades later, a family who purchased the home—complete with its seven-grave family cemetery—reported a frigid spiritual presence, the swish of skirts through hollyhocks, and angry whispering as the phantom spinster haunts the halls.

#location
A historic house in the village of Appleton, Mississippi Mills, Ontario.
