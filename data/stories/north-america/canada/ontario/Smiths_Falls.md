#title
The 1927 Cemetery Specters

#summary
A wave of ghostly sightings in the Anglican church cemetery in 1927 drew international press coverage and nightly civilian vigils before investigators uncovered a hidden pit and a painted hangman's noose.

#details
During the summer and autumn of 1927, residents of Smiths Falls reported numerous sightings of ghostly figures roaming through the town's Anglican church cemetery. The eerie phenomenon escalated to the point where it attracted international press attention, prompting citizens to gather at the graveyard night after night on ghost watches. When the excitement finally subsided, an examination of the grounds revealed a deep, plank-covered pit containing a trap door and a hangman's noose stained with red paint, though the true origin of the spectacle remained an unsolved local legend.

#location
The Anglican church cemetery in Smiths Falls, Ontario.

#title
The Phantom Operator of the Bascule Bridge

#summary
Locals near the historic Smiths Falls railway bridge reported witnessing the translucent ghost of a grumpy former operator managing the bridge machinery amidst plumes of red steam.

#details
Built in 1912–1913 along the Toronto-Ottawa line of the Canadian Northern Railway, the Smiths Falls Bascule Bridge was originally operated by Sid Tennant, a notoriously foul-tempered man. After his horse bolted and he died instantly in an accident near the bridge, the structure remained permanently poised at a 45-degree angle. Years later, a local resident walking home from the tavern after dark saw a plume of red steam rise from the bridge tender's tower and watched the translucent figure of Old Man Tennant reappear to operate the massive concrete counterweight before vanishing with a sharp pop.

#location
The Bascule Bridge spanning the Rideau Canal in Smiths Falls, Ontario.

#title
The Soldier in the Walls

#summary
Residents of a historic 1905 house in Smiths Falls repeatedly heard phantom boot steps and experienced eerie apparitions linked to a World War II soldier who grew up there.

#details
For years, a family living in a century-old house built in 1905 in Smiths Falls heard distinct, heavy boot pacing across the hardwood floors from room to room late at night. One night, a family member witnessed a reddish crawling hand upon the bedroom wall. Years later, genealogical and military research revealed that the ghostly presence belonged to Private Norman D. Tysick (known as James), a 26-year-old soldier who died during the Italian campaign in World War II and whose parents were the home's original occupants.

#location
An early 20th-century historic house built in 1905 in Smiths Falls, Ontario.
