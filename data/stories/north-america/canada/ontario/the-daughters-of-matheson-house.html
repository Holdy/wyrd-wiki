The Daughters of Matheson House

Summary

Built in the early 1800s, the historic Perth Museum—formerly the Matheson family home—is said to be haunted by the lingering spirits of the Senator's daughters who famously tended its lush gardens.

Details

Originally constructed as an opulent limestone residence for distinguished Canadian Senator Roderick Matheson, the grand manor on Gore Street East was occupied by the family for nearly a century. Of the Senator's seven daughters, six never married and spent much of their lives passionately tending the property's lush rear gardens filled with apple trees and rosebushes. After the last family member passed away in 1929, the building transitioned through various uses, including the Birkacre Tea Room and a restaurant named Vanity Fair that famously hosted spiritualists, clairvoyants, and tarot card readers like Madame Cornel and Madame Kildare during the late 1930s and 1940s. Over the decades, staff, visitors, and volunteers have reported paranormal occurrences, including unexplained shadows, shifting temperatures, disappearing objects, and ghostly figures resembling the Matheson daughters strolling through the moonlit gardens at dusk.
