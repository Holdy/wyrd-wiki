#title
The Specter of Middleton Avenue

#summary
In 1928, a house on Middleton Avenue became a local sensation when residents and onlookers reported phantom intruders, flying stones, and unassisted music.

#details
In February and March 1928, the home of Mr. and Mrs. Jos. Bellefeuille at 66 Middleton Avenue in Timmins became the center of public attention following reports of bizarre paranormal phenomena. Mr. Bellefeuille returned home one evening to find an apparition descending the stairs holding a revolver, prompting his wife to faint. After locking the house and fleeing to a neighbor's, police investigated but found no one inside. Over subsequent days, heavy stones were thrown around, mysterious bells rang, and a broken gramophone allegedly played records on its own without spring power. Hundreds of curious townspeople gathered outside the property as local rumors of a haunting spread throughout the community.

#location
The house is located at 66 Middleton Avenue in Timmins, Ontario.

#title
The Wilson Avenue Window Banging Mystery

#summary
In 1940, local police were repeatedly called to a Wilson Avenue home to investigate mysterious, relentless window banging that continued despite police surveillance.

#details
Over a three-week period in December 1940, the residence at 73 Wilson Avenue in Timmins was plagued by persistent, unexplained noises that brought police to the scene multiple times a day. Nightly between 8:30 p.m. and 2:30 a.m., a sharp, three-stroke banging sound—resembling a fist striking glass—echoed against a front window. Even when police officers staked out the interior and exterior of the house in pitch darkness, the loud raps continued while no one was near the window. Though the mysterious occurrences sparked rumors of a haunting, investigators eventually discovered that a 15-year-old boy in the home was flicking the loose frame with his toe from bed.

#location
The house is located at 73 Wilson Avenue in Timmins, Ontario.
