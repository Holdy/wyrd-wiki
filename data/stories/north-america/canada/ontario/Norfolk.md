#title
Ethel Steele of Lighthouse Theatre

#summary
The historic theatre in Port Dover is reportedly haunted by Ethel Steele, a devoted local homemaker and director whose spirit manifests as unexplained activity and whom actors tap for good luck.

#details
Housed in Port Dover’s historic town hall building, the Lighthouse Theatre is widely believed to be haunted by Ethel Steele (1881–1979), a pillar of the local community and theatre scene who directed numerous productions during her lifetime. Today, a portrait of Mrs. Steele hangs in the second-floor lobby. Actors and performers maintain a tradition of tapping her portrait for good luck before going on stage. Staff members and paranormal investigators have reported mysterious occurrences inside the building, including computers turning on by themselves, pens flying off desks, footsteps heard in empty halls, and the gentle clinking of glasses.

#location
Lighthouse Theatre, located at 247 Main Street in Port Dover, Norfolk County, Ontario.

#title
The Phantom Rider of Spooky Hollow

#summary
A dramatic wooded ravine in Norfolk County is said to be haunted by the ghost of a traveling salesman murdered at a long-gone hotel who searches on horseback for his lost head.

#details
Spooky Hollow is a dramatic 1.2-kilometre forested gorge and stretch of road leading toward Normandale from Highway 24 in Norfolk County. Local legend traces its ominous reputation back to a hotel fire over a century ago. According to folklore, a traveling salesman was murdered and dismembered at the hotel, and his headless ghost can still be seen riding a spectral horse through the hollow at night. During the Great Depression, the Cornell family raised ten children in the hollow's sole house and recalled that while superstitious visitors would lock their cars in terror as they passed through, the family lived there peacefully, though the home also held memories of family members passing away in the front room.

#location
Spooky Hollow, a 1.2-kilometre forested stretch and ravine along the road connecting Highway 24 to Normandale in Norfolk County, Ontario.
