#title
The Hauntings of the Bytown Museum

#summary
Housed in Ottawa's oldest surviving stone building, the Bytown Museum is reported to be haunted by canal-construction workers and historical figures from the city's earliest days.

#details
Situated at the foot of Parliament Hill alongside the Rideau Canal locks, the Bytown Museum occupies a building dating back to the construction of the canal in the 1820s. Volunteers, staff, and visitors have documented intense paranormal activity within its stone walls, including heavy, phantom footsteps rushing down staircases when no one else is present, doors violently vibrating on their hinges, and porcelain dolls that witnesses claim to hear crying at night. In one particularly eerie account, a staff member's computer spontaneously crashed and rebooted with a blank screen that typed out the name "Lt.-Col. John By" over and over again.

#location
The Bytown Museum is located at 1 Canal Lane at the foot of Parliament Hill in Ottawa, Ontario.

#title
The Phantom of the Château Laurier

#summary
Ottawa’s iconic luxury hotel is said to be haunted by its original visionary, Charles Melville Hays, who perished on the RMS Titanic just weeks before the hotel opened.

#details
The opulent Fairmont Château Laurier opened its doors in 1912 as a grand symbol of the capital's prosperity. However, its primary financial force and Grand Trunk Railway president, Charles Melville Hays, never lived to witness its opening; he tragically died during the sinking of the RMS Titanic on April 15, 1912, as he returned to Canada. Ever since, staff and guests have reported strange phenomena throughout the historic building. Apparitions resembling Hays have been spotted roaming the grand hallways, accompanied by sudden drops in temperature, doors swinging open and shut on their own, and phantom sounds of a child playing in the upper-floor suites.

#location
The Fairmont Château Laurier is located at 1 Rideau Street in downtown Ottawa, Ontario.

#title
The Spirits of the Nicholas Street Gaol

#summary
The historic 19th-century Carleton County Gaol, now operating as a hostel, is said to be haunted by the restless spirits of former inmates, most notably Patrick J. Whelan, who was publicly hanged there in 1869.

#details
Built in 1862, the Carleton County Gaol on Nicholas Street was designed as a model British prison, but its reality was notoriously harsh, dark, and overcrowded. Inmates—including children—endured brutal conditions until the prison was closed in 1972 and later converted into a youth hostel. It earned lasting notoriety as the site of Canada's last public execution by hanging, where 28-year-old Patrick J. Whelan was put to death for the assassination of Father of Confederation Thomas D'Arcy McGee, maintaining his innocence until his final breath. Over the decades since its conversion, guests and staff have frequently reported chilling paranormal encounters, including slamming iron doors, disembodied whispers echoing through the vaulted stone corridors, and unexplained apparitions lingering on Death Row and near the historic gallows.

#location
The Saintlo Ottawa Jail Hostel is located at 75 Nicholas Street in downtown Ottawa, Ontario.
