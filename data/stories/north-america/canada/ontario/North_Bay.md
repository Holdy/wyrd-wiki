#title
The Everlasting Stain of the CPR Station

#summary
A returning World War II soldier tragically took his life in the washroom of North Bay's CPR station, leaving a persistent stain that maintenance workers could never erase and which continues to manifest today.

#details
Built in 1903 as a Canadian Pacific Railway Station (now housing the Discovery North Bay Museum), the building witnessed countless emotional reunions and departures during World War II. In 1945, a soldier returning from active duty overseas entered the station's men's washroom and shot himself. The tragedy left a bloodstain on the floor that maintenance crews repeatedly failed to scrub away. Decades later, during museum renovations in 2004, a volunteer painting near the former washroom had a paint can mysteriously tip over, forming the exact shape of the original stain through fresh coats of paint. Other reported phenomena at the station include a woman in white waiting at the top of the stairs and the spectral apparition of a man named Mr. Bloom rushing through the double doors toward the tracks.

#location
Discovery North Bay Museum at 100 Ferguson Street, North Bay, Ontario.

#title
The Ghostly Projectionist of the Capitol Centre

#summary
The historic Capitol Centre theatre in downtown North Bay is said to be haunted by Harold, a lovelorn former projectionist whose heavy footsteps and chilling presence still linger in the projection room.

#details
Opened on June 1, 1929, as the grand Capitol Theatre, the building served as North Bay's premier entertainment venue before being transformed into the Capitol Centre arts hub in 1985. Local legend tells of a projectionist named Harold who fell deeply in love with a box office attendant named Abigail. After his advances were rejected, Harold retreated into solitude, spending most of his time in the projection room. Following his death, his spirit reportedly remained bound to the theatre. Staff and visitors frequently report moving shadows, an oppressive atmosphere, thumping noises, and heavy disembodied footsteps echoing through the building, with many refusing to enter the projection room alone.

#location
The Capitol Centre at 150 Main Street East, North Bay, Ontario.

#title
The Moaning Bride of Chapman House

#summary
A luxurious 1923 residence on Main Street West is said to be haunted by the tragic spirit of a young bride who was permanently injured by a falling bathtub just before moving into her dream home.

#details
Built in 1923 by lumber merchant Cyril Chapman for his young bride Mary Brock, the three-story brick home featured imported hardwoods, New York wainscoting, and a lion-footed bathtub. Days before occupancy, workers hoisting the heavy bathtub through an upper-window pulley system dropped it; the tub struck Mary, paralyzing her for the rest of her short life. For decades, local residents reported hearing eerie moaning sounds, the buzzing of deer flies, flickering candles, and sightings of a figure in a wedding gown near the upper windows on moonlit nights. Although later building investigations attributed the moaning and buzzing to plumbing vent stacks and roof flashing responding to wind, modern owners still report security alarms inexplicably tripping on moonlit nights.

#location
Chapman House on Main Street West, North Bay, Ontario.
