#title
The Forgotten Graves of A.R. Goudie

#summary
The lawn outside the A.R. Goudie facility serves as an unmarked 19th-century graveyard where over 260 unclaimed residents were buried, leaving an unsettling atmosphere for nighttime visitors.

#details
Local historical records and accounts indicate that the field in front of what is now the A.R. Goudie Eventide Home contains an unmarked cemetery where more than 260 individuals were buried between 1869 and 1907. These people had lived in a 19th-century house of refuge providing shelter for the homeless and destitute, and those who passed away unclaimed by family were interred directly on the front lawn. Visitors walking past the site after dark report an eerie, oppressive sensation of being watched and an overwhelming curiosity about the forgotten souls resting beneath the grass.

#location
The lawn directly in front of the A.R. Goudie Eventide Home in the Waterloo-Kitchener area, Ontario.

#upstream-source
The Cord / historical archives referenced by the Kitchener Public Library.

https://thecord.ca/exploring-k-ws-most-haunted-locations/

#title
The Phantom Seamstress of Joseph Schneider House

#summary
The oldest standing building in Kitchener, the Joseph Schneider House, is reportedly haunted by a phantom seamstress who continues working in the upper rooms and attic.

#details
Constructed in 1840, the Joseph Schneider House is a historic landmark and museum that preserves the early pioneer heritage of the area. Beyond its historical artifacts, the building is famous for accounts of a phantom seamstress. Visitors and staff have reported seeing an apparition working away in the upstairs bedroom and attic windows. Even when no figure is directly visible, those stepping onto the property describe a sharp drop in atmosphere and a constant, heavy feeling of being watched from the small attic panes.

#location
Joseph Schneider House at 466 Queen St S, Kitchener, Ontario.

#upstream-source
The Cord / Kitchener Public Library local folklore archives.

https://codygroup.ca/spooky-properties-haunted-houses-legends-in-waterloo-region/

#title
The Vanishing Hitchhiker of Waterloo Region

#summary
Motorists driving along roads in the Waterloo Region have reported picking up an elderly, bearded hitchhiker who inexplicably vanishes from the back seat while the vehicle is in motion.

#details
Dating back to documented accounts published in the 1930s, local motorists have shared encounters with a mysterious elderly man sporting a patriarchal beard. Drivers picking him up along regional roads note that he briefly converses—mentioning economic times and forecasting when the Great Depression would end—before abruptly disappearing from the locked rear seat while the car travels at highway speeds. The tale has become a persistent piece of roadside folklore across the Waterloo area.

#location
Roadways throughout the Waterloo Region, Ontario.

#upstream-source
Niagara Falls Evening Review (1933) / Kitchener Public Library local history collection.
