    The Spirits of the Historic SDG Jail

    Summary

    Operating from 1833 until 2002, the Historic SDG Jail in Cornwall is said to be haunted by the restless spirits of executed prisoners, an infamous "Lady in Black," and a young boy named Matthew. Visitors and staff frequently report disembodied voices, phantom footsteps, slamming cell doors, and unexplained operational sounds.

    Details

    Built in 1833, the Stormont, Dundas and Glengarry (SDG) Jail in Cornwall, Ontario served as a penal facility for 168 years until its closure in 2002. Throughout its history, the jail witnessed numerous executions, suicides, and deaths from illness or violence. Because many bodies went unclaimed by families, an estimated hundred or more individuals were buried directly on the prison grounds, including executed murderers like Henry Seguin and Peter Balcombe whose remains were unearthed during northern construction in 1985.

    In the decades since its decommissioning, the jail has gained widespread notoriety as one of Ontario's most active paranormal hotspots. Visitors and staff have reported numerous unearthly phenomena, including full-body apparitions of a "Lady in Black" and the spirit of a young boy named Matthew who reportedly plays in the courtroom. Witnesses frequently hear phantom footsteps echoing from empty rooms, the clinging of keys and chains, whistling, and a mysterious harmonica playing in the dark. Investigators and staff have also encountered shadow people, slamming cell doors, electrical anomalies, and phones dialing 911 while completely unattended.
