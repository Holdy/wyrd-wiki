#title
The Ghostly Apparition of Lily Herchemer at the Hochelaga Inn

#summary
Guests and staff at the historic Hochelaga Inn report frequent encounters with the phantom of Lily Herchemer, a young woman who perished in a tragic fire in the 19th century.

#details
Built in 1879 by John McIntyre and his wife Harriet (a relative of Sir John A. Macdonald), the Victorian-era building now operating as the Hochelaga Inn holds a long-standing local legend regarding a woman named Lily Herchemer. According to local folklore, Lily fell deeply in love with a sailor against her parents' strict wishes. To signal him when it was safe to visit, she would hang a lit lantern in her bedroom window. One night, she fell asleep with the lantern burning, and a sudden gust of wind knocked it over, igniting the room and engulfing her in flames. Over the years, guests and innkeepers have reported eerie phenomena throughout the inn, including a ghostly woman in a black dress sitting by the front windows or rustling past hallways, and visitors waking abruptly in the night to sensations of dread or chilling laughter at the foot of their beds.

#location
The Hochelaga Inn is located at 24 Sydenham Street in Kingston, Ontario.

#title
The Restless Spirit of Teresa Beam in the Downtown Alleyway

#summary
A downtown Kingston alleyway is haunted by Teresa Beam, a pregnant woman murdered in 1868 whose restless spirit pleads with passersby to help locate her unburied remains.

#details
In 1868, Teresa Beam was murdered in Kingston by her husband, John Napier, while she was pregnant. Because her body was concealed nearby, her remains never received a proper Christian burial. Decades later, businesses operating in the buildings alongside the pathway between Princess Street and King Street East began experiencing inexplicable pounding on doors and scraping sounds along the walls. Occupants who utilized Ouija boards in attempts to understand the phenomena received messages from a spirit identifying as Teresa, asking for her bones to be found and laid to rest. Numerous witnesses and pedestrians walking through the alley have reported seeing a short woman dressed in black with a veiled face, or experiencing sudden physical paralysis and choking sensations while passing through the corridor.

#location
The historic alleyway is situated between Princess Street and King Street East in downtown Kingston, Ontario.

#title
The Wandering Guard and Rebels of Fort Henry

#summary
Fort Henry is haunted by the executed rebel leader Nils Von Schoultz and a grey-bearded soldier who continue to patrol the historic limestone fortress and underground tunnels.

#details
Fort Henry was originally constructed in the 19th century to protect the Kingston Royal Dockyard. Beyond its harsh military history—where punished soldiers marching in heavy wool coats occasionally dropped dead under the summer heat—the fortress served as the execution site for Nils Von Schoultz in December 1838. Schoultz, a Swedish military officer and leader in the Hunter's Lodge, led an unsuccessful uprising to free Canada from British rule and was subsequently hanged at the fort. Tour guides and security personnel have frequently reported encountering a "Wandering Ghost"—a bearded soldier wearing a blue bombardier’s uniform—patrolling the lower fort after hours, alongside shadowy figures, unexplained footsteps, and sudden drops in temperature within the limestone underground tunnels.

#location
Fort Henry National Historic Site is located on Point Henry overlooking the St. Lawrence River in Kingston, Ontario.
