#title
Dorothea's Eternal Vigil at the Hockey Hall of Fame
#summary
Visitors and staff at a downtown Toronto landmark originally built as a bank have reported inexplicable footsteps, flickering lights, and eerie presences. These phenomena are attributed to the tragic spirit of a 19-year-old bank employee who took her own life inside the building in 1953.
#details
On March 11, 1953, 19-year-old bank teller Dorothea Mae Elliott arrived early to work at the Bank of Montreal branch located at the corner of Yonge and Front Streets. Distressed over personal troubles and a rumored affair with a branch manager, Dorothea went upstairs to the second-floor women's washroom and tragically shot herself with a revolver kept for security. She passed away the following day at St. Michael's Hospital. Ever since the building was transformed into the Hockey Hall of Fame, cleaning staff and visitors have reported doors and windows opening on their own, lights flickering, and faint footsteps echoing through the empty Great Hall—manifestations widely believed to be the restless spirit of Dorothea.
#location
The Hockey Hall of Fame is located at 30 Yonge Street at the corner of Front Street in downtown Toronto, Ontario.

#title
The Long-Faced Woman of Mackenzie House
#summary
Caretakers and visitors at a historic downtown Toronto home have reported terrifying encounters with a malicious apparition known as the "Long-Faced Woman." The spirit famously manifested directly above a bedroom headboard, staring down at a sleeping resident with a twisted smile before vanishing into thin air.
#details
In the 1950s, after a caretaker couple named Mr. and Mrs. Edmund moved into Mackenzie House at 82 Bond Street, they quickly established the building's reputation for intense paranormal activity. Late one night, Mrs. Edmunds awoke to a creaking sound behind her bed and looked up to see a terrifying apparition—a woman with a remarkably long face peering down at her from the impossibly narrow space behind the headboard, wearing a horrifying smile. When Mrs. Edmunds screamed, her husband woke up, but the entity had already vanished. The same entity was subsequently witnessed by other occupants and visiting grandchildren during sleepovers.
#location
Mackenzie House is located at 82 Bond Street in downtown Toronto, Ontario.

#title
The Tragic Specter of the Keg Mansion
#summary
Staff and visitors at a prominent Victorian steakhouse in Toronto have reported seeing the ghostly apparition of a heartbroken maid swinging from a noose near the grand staircase. According to local folklore, the maid took her own life out of overwhelming grief following the 1915 death of the house's matriarch, Lillian Massey.
#details
Originally built as Euclid Hall for the McMaster family in 1867 and later acquired by the wealthy Massey family, the mansion at 515 Jarvis Street became the site of a tragic legend. Following the death of family matriarch Lillian Massey in November 1915, a devoted maid allegedly distraught over the loss fashioned a noose from a length of rope and hanged herself from the railing overlooking the main foyer near the second-floor washrooms. Decades later, after the building was converted into a Keg Steakhouse restaurant, closing staff frequently reported a chilling "glimpse" of a woman's lifeless body swinging from the grand staircase before vanishing into thin air.
#location
The Keg Mansion is located at 515 Jarvis Street in Toronto, Ontario.
