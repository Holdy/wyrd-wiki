The Curse of Peche Island

Summary

A 100-acre island in the Detroit River remains undeveloped after a grieving widow cast a vengeful curse following her eviction in the 19th century.

Details

In 1833, the family of Hiram Walker acquired most of Peche Island except for four acres belonging to Leon Laforet. Following Leon's death, his widow Rosalie was allegedly forced out of her home by thugs hired by the Walkers who threw money on the table and demanded the deed. As she departed, Rosalie placed a chilling curse on the land, declaring that no one would ever successfully do anything with the island. True to her words, a string of tragic misfortunes followed: Willis Walker died suddenly at 28, Hiram Walker died of a stroke in 1899, the president of the Windsor Detroit Ferry Co. mysteriously died the same year they purchased it, and the island's grand summer mansion burned to the ground in 1929. Subsequent housing and ski resort development plans in the 1950s and 1960s also collapsed, leaving the island completely untouched to this day.
