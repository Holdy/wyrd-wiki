#title
The Legend of Emily at the Old Galt Post Office

#summary
Local folklore in Cambridge, Ontario, tells the tragic story of a secret affair between a 19th-century postmaster and a young employee named Emily, whose alleged suicide in the clock tower sparked decades of paranormal activity and unexplained phenomena in the building.

#details
Constructed in the late 19th century by renowned architect Thomas Fuller—who also designed Canada’s Parliament Buildings—the historic Galt Post Office along the Grand River in Cambridge, Ontario, is central to a dark local legend. According to folklore, long-serving Postmaster William S. Turnbull engaged in a secret romantic affair with a young postal worker named Emily. Because Turnbull was married, the relationship was kept strictly hidden to protect his social standing and career. As the emotional toll mounted and rumors suggested Emily may have threatened to expose the affair, Emily was allegedly found dead, hanging from a rope in the building's clock tower in 1919. Shortly thereafter, in April 1919, Turnbull died following a stroke, which local legend attributes to a broken heart and a ghostly pull across the veil. Although official historical records lack documentation of Emily or her death, decades of paranormal experiences have sustained the legend. Subsequent occupants, pub staff, and tour guides over the years have reported inexplicable occurrences, including heavy windows opening by themselves accompanied by unnatural cold drafts, musical equipment mysteriously failing and guitar strings snapping when performers mocked the tale, and phantom calls and knocking sounds during paranormal investigations.

#location
The Old Galt Post Office (now part of the Idea Exchange), located at 44 Grand Avenue South along the banks of the Grand River in the Galt core of Cambridge, Ontario.
