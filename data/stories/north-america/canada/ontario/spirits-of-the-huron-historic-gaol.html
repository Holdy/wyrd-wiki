Spirits of the Huron Historic Gaol

Summary

Staff and visitors at a historic nineteenth-century jail in Goderich have reported unsettling paranormal phenomena including heavy doors slamming on their own, phantom key jangles, and footsteps echoing through empty cell blocks.

Details

The Huron Historic Gaol, which opened in the mid-1800s and witnessed numerous inmate deaths, medical cases, and high-profile executions (including Canada’s final public hanging), has accumulated a rich reputation for haunting activity. Staff members working late hours have reported hearing sudden, loud noises followed by heavy iron cell doors slamming shut with no one else around. Additionally, visitors and employees frequently report hearing the distinct sound of jangling keys or unseen footsteps patrolling the upper tiers. An oppressive sense of being watched is also commonly reported in the second-floor cell blocks and doctor's rooms, keeping alive the folklore surrounding those who lived and died within its stone walls.
