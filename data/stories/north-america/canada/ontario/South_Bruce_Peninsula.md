#title
The Legend of Ghost Lake

#summary
An ancient native legend tells of a winter village established on the ice of Gillies Lake that catastrophically plunged into the deep waters below, leaving behind a chalky white lakebed haunted by restless spirits.

#details
Located near the northeast shore of the Bruce Peninsula overlooking Georgian Bay, Gillies Lake was historically known to early Indigenous populations as "Ghost Lake." According to centuries-old folklore, a large group had set up winter camp and built huts directly upon the frozen surface of the lake to harvest fish. Without warning, a deafening crash echoed across the ice, causing the entire village and its inhabitants to collapse into the icy depths. Survivors on the shoreline watched in horror as their homes and families vanished. Local legend holds that the striking, eerie whiteness of the lake's marl bed is composed of the bones of those who perished, and the waters remain imbued with the sorrow and presence of the lost spirits.

#location
Gillies Lake (formerly Ghost Lake) is situated near the northeast shore of the Bruce Peninsula, overlooking Georgian Bay just east of Wiarton in the South Bruce Peninsula, Ontario.

#upstream-source
Mentioned in Sherwood Fox's book *The Bruce Beckons* and originally published in Paul White's local history column in the *Owen Sound Sun Times*.

#title
The Phantom of Spirit Rock and the Ruins of The Corran

#summary
Spirit Rock and the nearby ruins of the 17-room McNeill estate are haunted by the tragic phantoms of star-crossed lovers who plunged to their deaths from the towering cliffs overlooking Colpoy’s Bay.

#details
Perched high above Colpoy’s Bay just north of Wiarton, the dramatic limestone cliffs of Spirit Rock are steeped in tragic local lore. According to legend, the phantom of a captured western warrior's daughter and an eastern chief—who fell into a forbidden love—wander the escarpment. After rival warriors threw the chief from the cliff ledge, the heartbroken woman threw herself into the abyss to join him in death. Visitors and sailors have long reported sighting ghostly figures and hearing unexplained noises near the cliffs and around the stone shell of "The Corran," a grand 17-room Victorian mansion built in 1882 by Irish aristocrat and politician Alexander McNeill, which later burned down amidst years of abandonment and vandalism.

#location
Spirit Rock Conservation Area and The Corran ruins are located along the escarpment overlooking Colpoy's Bay just north of Wiarton in the South Bruce Peninsula, Ontario.

#upstream-source
Local oral legend documented by regional historians and the Bruce County Museum & Cultural Centre.
