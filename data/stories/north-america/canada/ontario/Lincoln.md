#title
Echoes of Haines Cemetery and the One-Room Schoolhouse

#summary
Haines Cemetery and the 19th-century One-Room Schoolhouse in Jordan Village are steeped in local folklore of spectral sounds, eerie shadows, and phantom children's laughter.

#details
Nestled along the quiet, historic corridors of Jordan Village, Haines Cemetery and the adjacent One-Room Schoolhouse serve as focal points for local pioneer ghost lore. Dating back to the earliest days of European settlement in the Niagara region, the grounds hold the resting places of early orchardists, physicians, and school children. Local oral tradition and heritage walking tours recount chilling experiences after dark, including sudden drops in temperature near the old headstones, unexplained shadows flitting across the schoolhouse windows, and the faint, phantom echoes of children's voices and hurried footsteps ringing out from the empty schoolyard.

#location
Haines Cemetery and the One-Room Schoolhouse are located off Main Street in Jordan Village, Town of Lincoln, Ontario.

#title
The Phantom Cigar Smoke of the Enterprise Bulletin Building

#summary
The former Enterprise Bulletin newspaper building in Jordan Village is haunted by phantom footsteps and the distinct, unidentifiable scent of cigar smoke lingering in empty rooms.

#details
Long before modern commerce settled into the historic commercial blocks of Jordan Village, local print culture played a vital role in community life. The former Enterprise Bulletin newspaper building has long been the subject of local ghost lore, with multiple witnesses reporting eerie after-hours phenomena. Staff and visitors have frequently noted the unmistakable, heavy aroma of cigar smoke drifting through completely vacant rooms, alongside rhythmic footsteps pacing across the creaking wooden floorboards upstairs—phenomena locals attribute to the ghost of a former editor or publisher who spent decades working late nights in the press office.

#location
The former Enterprise Bulletin newspaper building is situated on Main Street in Jordan Village, Town of Lincoln, Ontario.

#title
The Spirits of the 1815 Fry House

#summary
Built by early pioneers along Twenty Mile Creek, the historic 1815 Fry House in Jordan Village is said to retain the lingering presences and unexplained spectral echoes of its original inhabitants.

#details
Constructed in 1815, the Fry House stands as one of the oldest surviving structures in Jordan Village, reflecting the rugged early settlement era of the Niagara Benchlands. Local folklore and heritage accounts passed down through generations suggest that the deep historical roots of the house have left an imprint on the property. Visitors and local historians participating in heritage tours have reported heavy, unexplained footsteps echoing through the timber-framed rooms and sudden atmospheric chill inside the historic home, widely attributed to the lingering spirits of the pioneer Fry family who once farmed and lived along the creek.

#location
The 1815 Fry House is located on Main Street within the historic community of Jordan Village in the Town of Lincoln, Ontario.
