#title
The Phantom Manager of the Sanderson Centre

#summary
A historic theatre in downtown Brantford is haunted by anomalous noises, flying furniture, and the lingering apparition of its former manager.

#details
Opened in 1919 in downtown Brantford, the Sanderson Centre for the Performing Arts became the site of startling paranormal activity. A stagehand working alone in the lighting booth heard a table crash in an adjacent room and watched a vacant chair get thrown across the room, leaving him severely shaken. During subsequent investigations by local paranormal researchers, a team member spotted an elderly man sitting quietly in a chair. When approached, the figure vanished into thin air. Upon showing a description and photograph to the theatre manager, the investigators learned the apparition matched the former manager, who historically sat in that exact spot with his wife to watch performances.

#location
The Sanderson Centre for the Performing Arts is located at 88 Dalhousie Street in downtown Brantford, Ontario.

#title
The Singing Estate of Glenhyrst

#summary
The former Cockshutt estate in Brantford, now an art gallery, is frequently visited by ghostly singing, self-playing pianos, and spectral activity.

#details
Glenhyrst Art Gallery, originally the estate of prominent local resident Edmund Cockshutt, has long been regarded by staff and regional ghost hunters as an active site of paranormal phenomena. Staff and visitors have frequently reported hearing the estate's piano playing by itself, doors opening and closing autonomously, and translucent figures crossing the surrounding gardens and grounds. Paranormal investigators documented an eerie encounter when they heard a woman singing inside the historic building even when no audio equipment or live sources were present.

#location
Glenhyrst Art Gallery is located at 20 Ava Road in Brantford, Ontario.
