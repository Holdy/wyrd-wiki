#title
The Haunting of Beild House

#summary
The historic Beild House in Collingwood is reportedly haunted by its original builder, Dr. Joseph Robbins Arthur, and a spectral woman with a head wound.

#details
Built in 1912 at the corner of Pine and Third Streets, the grand Victorian structure served as both the home and medical office of Dr. J.R. Arthur. Local lore and paranormal investigations suggest that the doctor—rumored to have cared for mental patients on site—along with his wife, still linger in the building. Guests and visitors over the decades have reported seeing shadowy figures moving through the hallways and catching terrifying glimpses of a woman descending the staircase while bleeding from a severe head wound, an apparition linked to a tragic incident suffered by the doctor's wife.

#location
The historic Beild House is located at the intersection of Pine Street and Third Street in Collingwood, Ontario.

#title
The Spirit of Simcoe Street

#summary
Former employees of a historic Collingwood newspaper building frequently reported phantom cigar smoke and footsteps attributed to the ghost of former editor David Williams.

#details
Located at the corner of Second and Ste. Marie Streets, the building once housed *The Enterprise-Bulletin* newspaper, where local historian and editor David Williams worked for decades until his death. Long after his passing, staff members working late hours in the office reported inexplicable phenomena, most notably the strong, sudden aroma of cigar smoke and the echoing sound of footsteps walking down empty hallways. Today, Williams is affectionately remembered in local folklore and on Collingwood's haunted walking tours as the "Spirit of Simcoe Street."

#location
The historic newspaper building is located at the corner of Second Street and Ste. Marie Street in Collingwood, Ontario.

#title
The Star-Crossed Lovers of Ekarenniondi

#summary
The rugged limestone cliffs of the Scenic Caves Nature Preserve near Collingwood are said to be haunted by the ghosts of an Erie chief and his forbidden Petun lover who died tragically centuries ago.

#details
According to local Petun legend and folklore, a young Erie chief fell in love with Leuantido, the daughter of a Petun chief, during peace negotiations near Georgian Bay. When her father rejected his marriage proposal, the couple continued meeting in secret among the dark fissures and limestone formations of the Blue Mountains, specifically around the sacred rock known as *Ekarenniondi*. When Leuantido's brothers discovered the affair, they ambushed and murdered the Erie chief, throwing his body off the cliffs. Overcome with grief, Leuantido leapt to her death the following night. Centuries later, visitors and hikers in the Scenic Caves Nature Preserve have reported encountering phantom apparitions, hearing mournful cries near the base of the cliffs, and experiencing unexplainable sensations of hair brushing against their ears accompanied by phantom chills.

#location
The Scenic Caves Nature Preserve is located in the Blue Mountains just west of Collingwood, Ontario, centered around the limestone formation known as Ekarenniondi.
