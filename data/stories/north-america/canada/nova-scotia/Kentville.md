#title
The Oppressive Quiet of Oak Grove Cemetery

#summary
Established in 1817, Kentville's historic Oak Grove Cemetery possesses an unnatural, heavy atmosphere and is tied to folklore surrounding its unmarked graves. Visitors have long noted an eerie quiet and oppressive feeling when walking its hilled grounds.

#details
Tucked away on a rolling, hilled landscape along the Evangeline Trail in Kentville, Oak Grove Cemetery has served as a burial ground since the late 18th century. Surrounded by moss-covered trees and gothic scenery, visitors have long reported an unnatural, oppressive quiet that seems to muffle all outside traffic and sound. The cemetery contains numerous unmarked graves of impoverished townspeople and historical figures, such as long-serving Kentville police chief Rupert Davis, fueling local ghost stories and folklore about the lingering spirits of the forgotten dead.

#location
Oak Grove Cemetery is located on a hilled landscape along the Evangeline Trail in Kentville, Nova Scotia.

#title
The Phantoms of Moccasin Hollow

#summary
Believed to be the site of a historic 1747 ambush, Moccasin Hollow is said to be haunted by the spirits of fallen soldiers. Residents historically reported strange lights and phantom sounds of combat echoing through the woods after dark.

#details
Moccasin Hollow, located west of Kentville, was the site of a bloody skirmish in 1747 when French and Indigenous forces ambushed British troops. Over time, local folklore and regional historians recorded that the hollow became notorious for paranormal activity. On certain nights, residents reported seeing mysterious lights flickering among the trees and hearing the distinct sounds of fighting and cries of battle, prompting neighborhood children and locals to strictly avoid the area after nightfall.

#location
Moccasin Hollow is situated west of Kentville, directly opposite the modern industrial park.

#title
The Specter of Gallows Hill

#summary
Gallows Hill in Kentville is said to be haunted by the restless spirit of a man who was publicly executed there in the early 19th century. Locals long avoided the wooded hillside where the criminal was hanged.

#details
Local legend holds that a convicted murderer, remembered as Bill or Powell, was executed by hanging on Gallows Hill in Kentville in 1826. In the decades following his execution, stories circulated that his vengeful spirit returned to haunt the surrounding woods, an area that eventually became a regular roosting site for crows.

#location
Gallows Hill is a historic hill located within the town limits of Kentville, Nova Scotia.
