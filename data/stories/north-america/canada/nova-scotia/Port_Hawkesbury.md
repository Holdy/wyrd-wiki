#title
The Phantom Train of Port Hawkesbury

#summary
Two women walking along the highway in Port Hawkesbury witnessed a terrifying black mechanical apparition tear through an old fish house years before railroads existed in the area.

#details
Long before any railways were built across Cape Breton Island, two elderly women were walking along the coastal highway at Port Hawkesbury, which skirts the cliffs above the Strait of Canso. Suddenly, they heard a tremendous rushing clatter and saw an enormous, black, one-eyed machine rush past them, crashing straight through an old fish house standing nearby. Terrified, they fled to the nearest home. Years later, when steam trains were finally introduced to Eastern Canada, one of the women heard a locomotive pass on the Nova Scotian mainland and immediately recognized the sound as the exact noise she had heard that day. When the railway was eventually constructed through Port Hawkesbury, its tracks were surveyed to run right through the exact spot where the old fish house once stood.

#location
Along the coastal highway in Port Hawkesbury, Nova Scotia, overlooking the Strait of Canso.

#title
The Point Tupper Railway Forerunner

#summary
A Cape Breton farmer successfully predicted where railway tracks would be laid after witnessing phantom trains running behind his home.

#details
When railway engineers were conducting initial surveys for the Point Tupper to Sydney branch of the Canadian National Railways, they stopped in front of a farmhouse in the Port Hawkesbury and Point Tupper area to survey the front land. The old farmer living there came out and informed them that they were wasting their time, telling them the trains would actually pass at the rear of his house because he had already seen phantom trains running there. True to his prediction, when a new survey was conducted the following year, the railway line was laid directly behind his farmhouse.

#location
At a farmhouse along the railway survey route in Point Tupper, adjacent to Port Hawkesbury, Nova Scotia.
