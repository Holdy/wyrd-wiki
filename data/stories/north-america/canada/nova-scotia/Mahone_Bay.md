#title
The Redcoat Apparitions of Smith's Cove

#summary
In the winter of 1940, a young girl at Smith's Cove on Oak Island witnessed spectral eighteenth-century British soldiers sitting silently on the rotting timbers of the old wharf.

#details
In 1940, five-year-old Peggy Adams—whose father served as the island's caretaker—ran home in tears from Smith's Cove on Oak Island after encountering three unusual men. One man had dark skin, while the other two wore 18th-century British red military jackets and trousers with broad yellow stripes, sitting upon the old wharf timbers. When her father hurried to the cove to investigate, he found no men and no footprints in the fresh snow. Decades later, when Peggy visited the Citadel Museum in Halifax, she immediately recognized the historical military uniforms on display as the exact attire of the spectral figures she had encountered in her childhood.

#location
Smith's Cove on Oak Island in Mahone Bay, Nova Scotia.

#title
The Teazer Light

#summary
On the anniversary of its fiery destruction during the War of 1812, the American privateer schooner *Young Teazer* is said to reappear as a blazing phantom ship roaming the waters of Mahone Bay.

#details
On June 27, 1813, the swift American privateer schooner *Young Teazer* was cornered and trapped in Mahone Bay by British warships, including the HMS *Hogue* and HMS *Orpheus*. Facing imminent capture and potential execution for parole violations, a crew member reportedly rushed below deck and detonated the ship's powder magazine. The massive explosion tore the vessel apart, killing thirty crew members and leaving charred wreckage across the water. Ever since, residents and mariners have reported seeing a fiery apparition—hauntingly referred to as the "Teazer Light"—re-enacting its explosive demise on the bay each year around the anniversary of the tragedy.

#location
The waters of Mahone Bay between Mason Island and Rafuse Island, Nova Scotia.
