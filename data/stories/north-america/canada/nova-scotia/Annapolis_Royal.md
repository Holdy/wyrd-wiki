#title
The Hidden Hauntings of O'Dell House

#summary
Built around 1869 as a stagecoach inn and tavern along the Annapolis Royal waterfront, the O'Dell House Museum is known for recurring paranormal activity and after-hours ghost investigations.

#details
The O'Dell House Museum stands on the Annapolis Royal waterfront as a testament to the town's 19th-century commercial and travel history, originally operating as a busy stagecoach inn and tavern. In local folklore and among heritage staff, the historic building is frequently associated with unexplained phenomena, leading to regular after-hours paranormal investigations conducted by groups such as Paranormal Phenomena Research & Investigation (PPRI). Visitors and investigators inside the circa-1869 structure have reported strange atmospheric shifts, unexplained noises, and shadowy apparitions moving through the historic rooms.

#location
O'Dell House Museum, located on the waterfront along St. George Street in Annapolis Royal, Nova Scotia.

#title
The Spirits of the Sinclair Inn

#summary
Operating since 1710 as a tavern, inn, and gathering place, the Sinclair Inn Museum in Annapolis Royal is rumored to be haunted by up to ten different spectral entities.

#details
The Sinclair Inn Museum, located on St. George Street, is one of Canada's oldest surviving wooden structures and has witnessed over three centuries of colonial history. Having served as a trading post, tavern, and stagecoach stop, the building's long and eventful past has given rise to numerous local legends. Over the years, staff, visitors, and paranormal investigators have reported hearing unexplained footsteps echoing in empty hallways, muffled disembodied voices, and catching glimpses of shadowy figures. Museum documentation notes that local lore attributes these eerie occurrences to as many as ten distinct ghosts who continue to inhabit the ancient rooms.

#location
Sinclair Inn Museum, located at 230 St. George Street in Annapolis Royal, Nova Scotia.
