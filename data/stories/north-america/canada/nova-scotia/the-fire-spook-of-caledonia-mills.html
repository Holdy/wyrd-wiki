The Fire Spook of Caledonia Mills

Summary

In the winter of 1922, an isolated farmhouse in Caledonia Mills was plagued by dozens of inexplicable spontaneous fires, culminating in thirty-eight blazes in a single night that forced the family to flee.

Details

Beginning in January 1922 at the remote rural farm of Alexander and Janet MacDonald in Caledonia Mills near Antigonish, strange phenomena began occurring around their teenage adopted daughter, Mary Ellen MacDonald. Household items, walls, and ceilings spontaneously burst into pale blue flames, even when soaked in water or covered in snowmelt. On one stormy night, the family and visiting neighbors battled and extinguished thirty-eight separate fires in rapid succession. The bizarre outbreak attracted massive North American media attention, drawing investigations from veteran Maritime detective Peachie Carroll and Boston psychic researcher Walter Prince, who ultimately concluded that Mary Ellen was acting as a poltergeist medium. Fearing for their lives, the MacDonalds abandoned the homestead permanently, and the mysterious fires never returned.
