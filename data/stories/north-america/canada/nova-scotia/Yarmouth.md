#title
The Death Knocks of Vengeance House

#summary
A historic Yarmouth tavern plagued by mysterious tapping sounds supposedly predicted the exact day a doubting neighbor would die.

#details
Located on the southern end of Main Street in Yarmouth, Richans Tavern—locally known as "Vengeance House" after Captain John Richan’s ship—was the site of a bizarre haunting in the late 19th century. Whenever the captain's daughter Lydia had a friend stay over, persistent tapping and knocking sounds echoed from within the walls. A pious local neighbor, convinced it was mere trickery, mocked the presence and demanded to know how many days he had left to live. Three loud knocks echoed in response. Exactly three days later, the skeptic dropped dead. The mysterious knocking continued for a time until a snake slithered into a room where one of the girls was staying, after which the ghostly rappings ceased entirely.

#location
Richans Tavern (Vengeance House), located on Main Street in Yarmouth, Nova Scotia.

#title
The Haunting of Churchill Mansion

#summary
Guests and investigators at the historic Churchill Mansion in Yarmouth report feeling the chilling presence of original owner Aaron Churchill and his niece Lottie, alongside unexplained physical phenomena.

#details
Built between 1889 and 1890 as a summer residence for prominent seafarer and entrepreneur Aaron Flint Churchill, the grand Italianate mansion later operated as an inn with a deep-rooted reputation for being haunted. According to local lore and paranormal investigations, the spirits of Aaron and his niece Lottie linger within the rooms and hallways. Visitors and overnight guests have reported sudden, intense chills, profound restlessness, and inexplicable depressions forming on beds as if an unseen person were lying beside them. During television investigations, crew members also documented heavy doors violently opening and closing on their own accord despite the complete absence of drafts.

#location
Churchill Mansion, located in Yarmouth, Nova Scotia.

#title
The Phantom Brig Yarmouth

#summary
For six decades following its disappearance at sea, a spectral 200-tonne brig named the *Yarmouth* was regularly witnessed returning to its home port.

#details
Originally built in Yarmouth in 1811 under the command of Captain Randall McDonald, the brig sailed to Antigua with a cargo of salt, fish, and lumber. After a single letter confirmed its safe arrival in the Caribbean, the ship and its entire crew vanished without a trace. Exactly one year later, the ship was reportedly spotted entering Yarmouth harbour. According to local accounts, men who rowed out toward the vessel claimed to hear Captain McDonald shouting hoarsely for them to keep off before the phantom ship vanished entirely into thin air. The ghostly vessel faithfully returned to its home harbour year after year for sixty consecutive years, gradually appearing fainter until it was last seen in 1872.

#location
Yarmouth Harbour, Yarmouth, Nova Scotia.
