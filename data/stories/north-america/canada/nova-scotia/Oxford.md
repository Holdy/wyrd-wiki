#title
The Forerunner of Westchester Mountain

#summary
A terrifying series of three heavy knocks at a remote mountain cabin served as a supernatural forerunner warning of a fatal stroke five months later.

#details
While staying at a family cabin on Westchester Mountain during hunting season, a woman was alone at midday during a torrential downpour while her husband and brother-in-law had temporarily left to drop off laundry and buy beer. Shortly after they departed, three violent, wall-jarring bangs struck the kitchen porch door with such force that her small dog panicked and trembled in fear. Finding no one outside, she realized the knocks mirrored traditional folklore of a "tommyknocker"—a supernatural forerunner predicting an imminent family death. Months later, her brother-in-law Don, a lumberjack, suffered a sudden fatal stroke while his saw was still running, confirming the eerie omen.

#location
A family cabin on Westchester Mountain, Cumberland County, Nova Scotia.

#title
The Ghost of Westchester Mountain

#summary
After being murdered for his new boots, a missing man's ghost appeared repeatedly along a mountain road until local residents finally asked what he wanted and buried his remains.

#details
Around 1880, a man named Eagles disappeared on Westchester Mountain in Cumberland County, Nova Scotia, without a trace. Sometime later, travelers along the Halifax road began seeing his apparition walking beside them. One evening, local resident Hugh Scott was riding his horse when he noticed Eagles walking close to his stirrup, prompting him to flee in terror. Months later, Hugh's brother William encountered the ghost at nearly the same spot. When William asked what he wanted, the spirit replied, "I want to be buried," directing William to his remains under a pile of brush by a nearby brook. William recovered the bones and gave them a proper burial, after which the ghost was never seen again. Years later, an old man named Shacksteed confessed on his deathbed in Springhill that he had murdered Eagles years prior to steal his new boots.

#location
The Halifax road on Westchester Mountain, Cumberland County, Nova Scotia.
