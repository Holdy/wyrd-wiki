#title
The Dream Visitation at Betsey Ramsay's Grave

#summary
A settler named Trusti Vigfússon was compelled to restore a remote gravesite along Lake Winnipeg after being visited in a dream by the ghost of grieving husband John Ramsay.

#details
In the winter of 1876, a smallpox epidemic devastated both the Icelandic settlers and Indigenous communities along the western shore of Lake Winnipeg in the New Iceland region. John Ramsay, a Saulteaux resident of Sandy Bar, lost his wife Betsey and three of their children to the disease. Years later, local settler Trusti Vigfússon experienced an unusually vivid dream in which the spirit of John Ramsay appeared to him, urging him to rebuild the protective white picket fence and care for Betsey's neglected gravesite. Taking the nocturnal visitation as a powerful preternatural directive, Vigfússon carried out the work, preserving a historic site that remains protected today.

#location
Betsey Ramsay's Grave located in an open field near the western shore of Lake Winnipeg, north of Kaltenthaler Beach in the rural Arborg and Riverton area of Manitoba.

#title
The Haunting of the Trausti Vigfússon House

#summary
The historic Trausti Vigfússon house at the Arborg & District Multicultural Heritage Village is said to be haunted by a small elderly woman and a partial phantom climbing the stairs.

#details
Originally built in 1898 and later relocated to the Arborg & District Multicultural Heritage Village along the Icelandic River, the historic log house carries a reputation for unexplained paranormal activity. Local residents and preservationists have reported encountering spectral figures within the building, most notably a small old woman who inexplicably appears and vanishes at the top of the stairs, as well as an unusual apparition described as "half a man" climbing the steps.

#location
The Trausti Vigfússon house at the Arborg & District Multicultural Heritage Village on the southeast end of Arborg, Manitoba.
