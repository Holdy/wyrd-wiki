#title
The Antique Store Ghost

#summary
An old building on Second Street in Lac du Bonnet became known for sightings of a slumped elderly man who was the building's original builder and grocery store owner.

#details
Years ago, local designer and shop owner Sheldon Sveinson purchased an old building along Second Street in Lac du Bonnet to operate a picture framing shop and curiosity store. Soon, visiting tourists and locals began asking about a mysterious old man they kept seeing wandering around the store with slumped shoulders and a distinct walk. Because no such living man was working there, Sveinson investigated and discovered the apparition matched the description of the building's original owner—who had constructed it as a grocery store in the 1960s and passed away decades prior. A co-worker independently identified the ghost when she came across an old photograph of the original owner. Adding to the eerie occurrences, after the man's wife passed away, family members reported that her favorite antique ornaments brought back to their home would inexplicably rearrange themselves.

#location
An old building located along Second Street in Lac du Bonnet, Manitoba.

#title
The Ghost Bride of Milner Ridge

#summary
A driver on PR 214 picks up a silent young woman in a wedding gown, only to watch her mysteriously vanish from the back seat while driving through Milner Ridge.

#details
Decades ago on an October night, a motorist driving along Milner Ridge Road (PR 214) between Lac du Bonnet and Seddon's Corner encountered a lone woman walking in the pitch-black cold. Dressed entirely in a white wedding gown, she appeared stranded and needed help. The driver pulled over and offered her a ride; the woman silently got into the back seat without speaking a word, her hair hiding her face. As the car traveled along the dark highway, the driver repeatedly asked her questions and received no response, noticing only a blank stare in the rearview mirror. Deciding to take her to the RCMP office in Beausejour for safety, the driver looked back again to announce the plan—only to find the back seat completely empty. Local legend attributes the ghostly apparition to a tragic bride who perished on her wedding day in pioneer times and wanders the highway seeking her lost fiancé.

#location
PR 214 (Milner Ridge Road) between Lac du Bonnet and Seddon's Corner, Manitoba.

#title
The Phantom Train

#summary
In the wooded rail corridors between Lac du Bonnet and Whitemouth, locals whisper of desperate 19th-century stowaways who died on boxcars and rise from roadside graves to catch phantom trains.

#details
During the late 19th century and the expansion of western rail lines, many desperate, penniless job-seekers hopped boxcars, rode on couplings, or hid beneath trains to travel through the Lac du Bonnet and Whitemouth areas. Tragically, many froze to death or were killed by railway police during the journey. To avoid scandal, train crews and rail police allegedly scheduled clandestine stops just outside of town to bury these deceased stowaways in secret rail-side graves. According to local folklore, on nights when the new moon is right, these tragic figures rise from the earth with their packs to wait by the tracks for a phantom train to carry them into town.

#location
The historic railway lines and rail-side burial locations between Lac du Bonnet and Whitemouth, Manitoba.
