#title
The Incessant Banging at the Downtown Rehearsal Space

#summary
Artists using a mentorship rehearsal space in downtown Yellowknife reported a recurring phantom visitor that relentlessly pounded on the emergency exit door every evening.

#details
During late-night practice and composing sessions at the Northern Arts and Cultural Centre's mentorship rehearsal space located inside the downtown mall, musicians and composers like Carmen Braden and Greyson Gritt frequently heard aggressive, minutes-long banging on the fire exit door starting around 8:15 p.m. Despite the alarming and relentless pounding that sounded like someone sitting directly outside the door, opening it was strictly discouraged by previous occupants due to rumors that the space was haunted. The eerie acoustic ambience of the decaying, cavernous dead mall heightened the sense that unseen presences were lingering just out of sight.

#location
The Northern Arts and Cultural Centre mentorship rehearsal space located inside the downtown mall in Yellowknife, Northwest Territories.

#title
The Phantom Child of Kam Lake Dairy

#summary
Musicians rehearsing at an old dairy in Yellowknife's Kam Lake area reported encounters with a phantom Inuk boy who vanished into locked rooms and unexplained shadows creeping down hallways.

#details
While practicing alone around midnight at the old Yellowknife dairy jam space in Kam Lake, drummer Kevin Dunbar was startled when a young Inuk boy suddenly jumped in front of him, acted tough, and bolted toward the doorway. Dunbar chased after the child, but upon reaching the exit, he found all doors securely closed and locked with no one in sight. On other occasions, band members witnessed an iPod flip 180 degrees on its own without any cables or pulling forces, while others felt an intense, ominous sense of dread and saw black shadows creeping down the hallways. The activity became so unsettling that an energy worker was eventually brought in to help clear the space.

#location
The old Yellowknife dairy building located in the Kam Lake area of Yellowknife, Northwest Territories.
