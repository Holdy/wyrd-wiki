    The Acton Vale Poltergeist

    Summary

    In January 1969, a modest residence in Acton Vale became the epicenter of violent, unexplained paranormal phenomena witnessed by three Catholic priests over three consecutive nights.

    Details

    On January 6, 1969, Sylvio Saint-Onge contacted parish vicar Wilfrid Bérard in distress, pleading for urgent help as invisible, aggressive forces invaded his home. The Saint-Onges were temporarily caring for a seven-year-old girl named Guylaine Breault. The disturbances began when a religious picture spontaneously moved and was nailed to a door, followed by household objects flying through the air and smashing against walls. A bust of the Virgin Mary shattered directly at the feet of three visiting clergymen—Vicars Claude Léveillée, Normand Bernier, and Wilfrid Bérard. When the priests attempted to bless the room with holy water, the unseen force intensified rather than subsided. Only young Guylaine could perceive the entity, reporting a large hairy hand and feeling physically pushed by an invisible presence. The harrowing manifestations came to an abrupt, complete halt the moment Guylaine was returned to her parents' home on Ricard Street. Though church authorities declined to launch an official ecclesiastical investigation, the three priests signed a joint statement detailing their unexplainable encounter, which made headlines across regional newspapers.
