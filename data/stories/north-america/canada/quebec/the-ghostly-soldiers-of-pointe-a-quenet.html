    The Ghostly Soldiers of Pointe à Quenet

    Summary

    Apparitions of a colonial officer and his subordinates have been witnessed marching in formation out of the cellar vault and across the lawn of an ancient riverside home in Beaconsfield.

    Details

    Set in Beaurepaire Village at one of the oldest homes on the peninsula—Maison de Beaurepaire (also known as Dornal Hall)—this local legend dates back to an oppressive summer afternoon when a cook resting in the kitchen witnessed a chilling event. A door connecting the kitchen to the remains of an old colonial fort cellar swung open, and an officer wearing a tricorne hat and powdered wig entered, followed by soldiers carrying rifles. Without a sound, the officer silently drilled his men before they marched out the back door toward Lake Saint-Louis and vanished. Recorded by prominent folklorist Édouard-Zotique Massicotte in 1927 from accounts passed down through generations of residents, the spectral soldiers are believed to be lingering echoes of the turbulent colonial era when Fort de la Pointe à Quenet stood on the site.
