    The Legend of the Devil's Rapids

    Summary

    A treasure hunter attempting a midnight ritual on Friday the 13th to claim Benedict Arnold's lost gold at the rapids near Beauceville encountered Satan himself, only to be saved from damnation when an involuntary sign of the cross coincided with a flash of lightning. Strange fireballs, ghostly apparitions, and mysterious drownings have haunted the turbulent waters ever since.

    Details

    Along a stretch of the Chaudière River southeast of Beauceville, turbulent waters known as the Rapides du Diable (Devil's Rapids) have long been said to conceal a chest of gold and silver lost by Benedict Arnold's retreating American army in 1775. According to local folklore, the treasure is guarded by Satan himself. Desperate to find the fortune, an aging military veteran acquired a grimoire of dark magic and ventured out on a Friday the 13th during a full moon. Standing between two massive sturgeon-shaped rocks dividing the riverbed, he performed a midnight ritual, sacrificed a black hen, and chanted incantations to summon the devil.

    At the stroke of midnight, the sky erupted with thunder and lightning, a fiery orb descended from the heavens, and the earth split open to reveal Satan and the gleaming treasure chest. Terrified and reacting out of Catholic habit, the old soldier made the sign of the cross as a bright flash lit up the sky. Instantly, Satan and the treasure vanished back into the earth, leaving the man scorched, unconscious, and pinned to the burning ground, where his brother found him delirious the next morning. To this day, locals maintain that the rapids are haunted by ghostly lights, eerie rolling fireballs, and the restless spirits of those claimed by the treacherous waters.
