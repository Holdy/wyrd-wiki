#title
The Angry Ashes of Riverside Meadows

#summary
A home on 60th Avenue in Riverside Meadows unsettled its residents with inexplicable blue flames dancing across the basement ceiling and appliances turning on by themselves.

#details
When a family built a house on 60th Avenue in Red Deer's Riverside Meadows neighbourhood in the 1950s, builders digging the basement were perplexed to find a thick layer of ashes deep within otherwise undisturbed ground. Once the home was finished, bizarre phenomena began to manifest: two separate clothes dryers in the basement would turn on and off with no one operating them, and record players would emit strange sounds as though someone had touched the needle. Multiple family members, including the homeowner's brother and young son, witnessed ethereal blue flames creeping across the basement ceiling without causing any heat or damage. Years later, during utility renovations, a sudden explosion sparked a fire that damaged part of the home. The eerie occurrences at the property were later documented under the title "Angry Ashes."

#location
A house on 60th Avenue in the Riverside Meadows neighbourhood of Red Deer, Alberta.

#title
The Ghost of Park Place on Ross Street

#summary
The century-old Park Place building in downtown Red Deer is reportedly haunted by a silent female apparition in vintage dress who vanishes into the basement hallway.

#details
Originally constructed over a century ago as the Alexandra Hotel along Ross Street in downtown Red Deer—and later converted for commercial business use—the historic building has long been associated with a lingering apparition. Over the years, various business owners and occupants have reported experiencing a sudden, localized drop in temperature followed by the sight of a woman dressed in Victorian or Edwardian attire. She is seen walking silently across the hallway toward the basement before completely vanishing from sight. The figure never speaks and only appears fleetingly to those working in the building.

#location
Park Place (formerly the Alexandra Hotel) on Ross Street in downtown Red Deer, Alberta.

#title
The Phantoms of Cronquist House

#summary
The historic Edwardian-style Cronquist House overlooking Bower Ponds is reported to be haunted by a woman seen at an upper window, phantom footsteps, and moving household objects.

#details
Originally built in 1911 by Emmanuel Cronquist in West Park, the historic eight-bedroom Victorian farmhouse was moved across the frozen Red Deer River in the 1970s to its current location overlooking Bower Ponds to save it from demolition. Many locals believe that moving the building disturbed the spirits within. Over the years, staff and visitors have reported numerous unexplained occurrences, including tea sets and saucers sliding across tables, doors slamming shut when no one is near, and the sound of heavy footsteps echoing on upper floors when the house is entirely empty. In one notable incident, former executive director Elizabeth Plumtree witnessed the double doors leading into the kitchen swinging rapidly back and forth as if someone had just run through them, though security alarms had not triggered and no one was found inside.

#location
Cronquist House at Bower Ponds in Red Deer, Alberta.
