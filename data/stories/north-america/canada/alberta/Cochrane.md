#title
The Ghost House of Pope Avenue

#summary
Built around 1905 on Pope Avenue and William Street in Cochrane, this historic home became known as a haunted dwelling where nightly offerings of wine vanished and invisible spirits were chased away with a cowbell.

#details
Located at the corner of Pope Avenue and William Street in Cochrane, the historic Bell-Irving house was constructed around 1905 by prominent early ranchers. In later years, the daughter of the Kerfoot family residing there became convinced that the property was haunted. Every night, she left a glass of wine on the stone pillars outside as an offering to the ghosts, and each morning she would retrieve an empty glass, believing the spirits had gladly accepted her gift. In reality, their eccentric neighbor—a man nicknamed "Rattlesnake Pete" who caught garter snakes and wore their fangs—was sneaking across the street to drink the wine. The daughter was also known to borrow a neighbor's milk cowbell and ring it throughout the house to banish lingering entities.

#location
The historic Bell-Irving house is located at the corner of Pope Avenue and William Street in Cochrane, Alberta.

#title
The Screaming Pull-Off of Scott Lake Hill

#summary
Truckers resting at an abandoned service station pull-off along the Trans-Canada Highway between Cochrane and Canmore abandoned the spot after repeatedly hearing the terrifying, disembodied screams of a woman in the night.

#details
Situated near the midpoint between Cochrane and Canmore at the eastern boundary of the Stoney Nakoda First Nation, Scott Lake Hill was once home to a small service station and residence. After the business closed, the paved service road and pull-off remained a popular, quiet spot for long-distance truck drivers to park and catch a few hours of sleep. However, truckers soon stopped using the area altogether after reporting unsettling encounters, specifically hearing the terrifying, disembodied screams of a woman echoing through the darkness. According to local lore and accounts compiled by author Barbara Smith, the hauntings were tied to rumors of an unpunished assault that had allegedly taken place on the property.

#location
Scott Lake Hill is located along the Trans-Canada Highway near the eastern boundary of the Stoney Nakoda First Nation between Cochrane and Canmore, Alberta.

#upstream-source

#title
The Whispering Halls of the Rockyview Hotel

#summary
Staff members at a historic downtown Cochrane hotel have reported hearing disembodied voices call their names in empty corridors, backed by paranormal investigations recording unexplained electromagnetic fields and phantom voices.

#details
Situated in the heart of downtown Cochrane, the historic Rockyview Hotel has long been associated with local legends of lingering spirits, including a "Ghost Bride" who supposedly fell down the grand staircase in the 1920s and a bellman named Sam. In 2018, a kitchen worker named Caesar Escobar heard a woman's voice clearly calling his name while working in the hotel kitchen. Assuming a coworker was trying to get his attention, he checked the hallway only to find himself completely alone. Subsequent visits by professional medium Bonnie Milner and Ghost Hunt Alberta recorded anomalous electromagnetic fields (EMF) and captured phantom voices on a spirit box, confirming the presence of unseen entities within the building.

#location
The Rockyview Hotel is located in downtown Cochrane, Alberta.
