#title
The Ghosts of Medalta Potteries

#summary
Employees at the historic Medalta Potteries in Medicine Hat frequently experience unexplained phenomena, including moving objects, phantom footsteps, and unseen figures.

#details
Nestled in Medicine Hat's Historic Clay District, the century-old Medalta Potteries is rumored to be haunted by the spirits of former employees who spent their lives crafting pottery there. Current staff members report hearing disembodied voices, squeaky footsteps while alone in the building, and laughter when no one is present. Workers have also described tools mysteriously vanishing and reappearing moments later, feeling taps on their shoulders in empty rooms, and spotting dark figures moving across roped-off museum exhibit areas. Rather than feeling frightened, employees believe these lingering souls have simply returned to a place that was an essential part of their lives.

#location
Medalta Potteries, located at 713 Medalta Ave SE in the Historic Clay District of Medicine Hat, Alberta.

#title
The Phantom Train of Medicine Hat

#summary
In 1908, railway workers twice encountered a glowing phantom train on the tracks near Ross Creek coulee, a haunting premonition that preceded a fatal collision.

#details
In June 1908, CPR engineer Bob Twohey and fireman Gus Day were heading east from the Medicine Hat station toward Dunmore. As their train rounded a cutbank in the Ross Creek coulee, an oncoming train with a brilliant headlight appeared, gliding laterally off the tracks before vanishing into thin air while the spectral crew waved a greeting. Shaken by the eerie encounter, Twohey sought a fortune teller who predicted his death within a month, prompting him to take time off. Days later, fireman Gus Day experienced the exact same apparition along a new engineer, J. Nicholson. On July 8, 1908, Nicholson was operating the same locomotive alongside fireman H. Thompson, while Bob Twohey—having returned to work—was at the throttle of an inbound train from Lethbridge. The two real trains collided head-on near the exact spot of the phantom sightings, killing Twohey, Nicholson, and several crew members and passengers.

#location
The Canadian Pacific Railway tracks winding around the cutbanks of the Ross Creek coulee, approximately two miles outside of Medicine Hat, Alberta.
