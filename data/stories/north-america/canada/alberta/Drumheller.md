#title
The Haunting of the Johnston House

#summary
Built in 1911 by Dr. Robert James Johnston Sr.—who tragically died in the home in 1936—this historic house on Third Avenue East is frequently visited by the spirits of Dr. Bob and his young daughter Marjorie.

#details
Built by prominent local dentist and surgeon Dr. Robert James Johnston Sr. in 1911, the heritage house at 375 3 Ave. E in Drumheller has become one of Alberta's most well-documented haunted locations. In 1936, Dr. Johnston met an untimely end in the basement when a hunting rifle discharged while he was packing for a hunting trip. Decades later, current owner Justin Bolin and various paranormal investigation teams, including the Calgary Association of Paranormal Investigations, recorded heavy footsteps ascending stairs to nowhere, cold spots, electronic voice phenomena (EVP), and apparitions of both Dr. Johnston and his young daughter Marjorie, who passed away at age 14. Despite the tragic history, residents and investigators report that the spirits are benevolent and welcoming.

#location
375 3 Ave. E, Drumheller, Alberta.

#title
The Spirits of Miner's Memorial Hall

#summary
Serving as a makeshift morgue during Drumheller's dangerous coal-mining era, Miner's Memorial Hall in downtown Drumheller remains a focal point for ghostly encounters and unexplained sounds.

#details
During the peak of Drumheller's coal-mining boom in the early 20th century, Miner's Memorial Hall played a sombre role as a makeshift morgue for miners who lost their lives in the treacherous local underground mines. Over the decades, locals and visitors traversing downtown Drumheller have reported unexplained phantom noises, cold drafts, and shadowy figures lingering within and around the hall. As one of the anchor points on Drumheller's historic downtown ghost walks, the building carries the sorrow and memories of the miners who never returned home.

#location
Miner's Memorial Hall in downtown Drumheller, Alberta.
