#title
The Ghostly Matriarch of Hawreliak House

#summary
The restored Hawreliak House at the Ukrainian Cultural Heritage Village is said to be haunted by its former homemaker, whose presence is marked by moving furniture, phantom phone rings, and slamming doors.

#details
Vaselina Hawreliak raised nine children and managed her pioneer household with strict moral values before passing away in 1967. After her home was restored and relocated to the Ukrainian Cultural Heritage Village in Lamont County, night security guards and staff began reporting eerie occurrences. Witnesses have spotted unexplained candlelights glowing in the windows at night, heard phantom telephone rings, and experienced sudden drops in temperature. Most notably, visitors and investigators walking through the upstairs rooms have had doors violently slam shut in front of them—an occurrence popularly attributed to the matriarch's disapproval of rowdiness and swearing in her home.

#location
Hawreliak House at the Ukrainian Cultural Heritage Village in Lamont County, Alberta.

#title
The Spectral Wagon Master of the Ukrainian Cultural Heritage Village

#summary
Visitors and staff at the historic pioneer museum in Lamont County have reported seeing the ghostly apparition of a wagon master driving a silent team of horses across the grounds.

#details
The Ukrainian Cultural Heritage Village, situated along Highway 16 in Lamont County, is an open-air museum recreating early Ukrainian pioneer life in Alberta. Among its most enduring ghost lore is the tale of a spectral wagon master who is said to ride through the property with a silent team of horses, reportedly searching for workers to accompany him in his phantom wagon. Other paranormal phenomena reported across the historic site include unexplained light anomalies, disembodied footsteps, phantom cries from empty cradles, and shadowy figures lingering near the windows of relocated pioneer homes.

#location
The Ukrainian Cultural Heritage Village, located along Highway 16 in Lamont County, Alberta.
