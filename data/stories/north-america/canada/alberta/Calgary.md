#title
The Haunting of Prince House

#summary
Situated within Heritage Park, the historic Prince House is said to be haunted by the spirits of lumber baron Peter A. Prince's multiple wives who passed away during his lifetime.

#details
Built by wealthy Eau Claire sawmill manager Peter A. Prince, the grand home witnessed profound personal tragedy as several of his wives died from various illnesses over the years. Visitors and park staff have frequently reported seeing female apparitions peering from the windows and witnessing glowing lights shining from the third floor, despite the upper level lacking electricity entirely. Security guards patrolling the grounds have also noted strange behavior, including guard dogs physically pulling away in terror whenever they pass the perimeter of the house.

#location
The Prince House is located within Heritage Park at 1900 Heritage Drive S.W. in Calgary, Alberta.

#title
The Phantom Firehouse and Cappy Smart's Monkey

#summary
Housed in Calgary's historic Fire Hall No. 3, this popular pub is reportedly haunted by the mischievous ghost of a pet monkey and the phantom hooves of a horse lost in the line of duty.

#details
Operating originally as Fire Station No. 3 under the city's first fire chief, Cappy Smart, the building is associated with two famous animal spirits. Cappy Smart once owned a pet monkey that was put down after attacking a child, but staff report that its unseen presence still roams the building, throwing cans off shelves, triggering the dishwasher, and playing with billiard balls. Additionally, firefighters and staff have heard the ghostly sound of clattering hooves belonging to Lightning, a department horse that perished in a fire, echoing against the pavement outside.

#location
The Hose and Hound is located at 1030 9th Avenue S.E. in the Inglewood area of Calgary, Alberta.

#title
The Specters of Deane House

#summary
Built in 1906 for a Fort Calgary superintendent, the historic Deane House is haunted by restless spirits, phantom cigar smoke, and an attic bloodstain that mysteriously shifts in size.

#details
Originally constructed for Captain Richard Deane, the house later operated as a rooming house during the Great Depression and became the site of a tragic murder-suicide in 1971. Over the years, staff and visitors have reported numerous paranormal phenomena, including the phantom scent of cigar smoke, a disconnected telephone that randomly rings, and an apparition of an Indigenous man as well as a pipe-smoking man. Most chillingly, an attic bloodstain near a stubborn closet door is said to change in size and color, while objects placed inside are inexplicably thrown about.

#location
The Deane House is located at 806 9th Avenue S.E. in the Inglewood neighborhood of Calgary, Alberta.

#title
The Spirits of the A.E. Cross House

#summary
The historic A.E. Cross House, now operating as Rouge restaurant, is plagued by slamming doors, moving furniture, and the sorrowful apparition of a woman in white.

#details
Built by politician, rancher, and Calgary Stampede co-founder A.E. Cross, the home was struck by tragedy when two of his children succumbed to diphtheria while waiting for medicine. Employees and diners at the establishment have reported numerous unexplained occurrences, including falling pots, moving chairs, slamming doors, and the sound of children's laughter echoing from the basement. Additionally, witnesses have reported seeing the apparition of a woman in white floating gracefully down the staircase.

#location
The A.E. Cross House is located at 1240 8th Avenue S.E. in Calgary, Alberta.
