    The Daysland Poltergeist

    Summary

    In February 1935, a farmhouse south of Daysland, Alberta was reportedly besieged by a hostile "evil spirit" that terrorized the residents with poltergeist activity and threatening letters until the disturbances abruptly stopped.

    Details

    Beginning in the first week of February 1935, the three-room farmhouse of Mr. and Mrs. Louis Lansing, situated south of Daysland, became the center of national media attention when the family and their hired hand, Frank Kennedy, reported being tormented by an unseen entity. The disturbances—characterized in contemporary newspaper accounts as an "evil spirit"—involved inexplicable noises, flying objects, and menacing typewritten messages left inside the home. Local clergy, including Father M.J. Schnitzler from the nearby St. Peter's Catholic Church, as well as members of the Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP), were drawn into investigating the baffling case. After weeks of intense public speculation, press reports, and theories ranging from poltergeist phenomena to household pranks, the terrifying occurrences ceased just as mysteriously as they had begun.
