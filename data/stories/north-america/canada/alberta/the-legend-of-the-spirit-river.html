    The Legend of the Spirit River

    Summary

    Early traveler accounts and Indigenous folklore attribute the name of Spirit River to a deceased warrior who returned to walk the riverbanks after his death. A companion legend describes a hunter who heard disembodied voices echoing from the river ravines, causing his entire hunting party to abandon their camp.

    Details

    According to historical accounts recorded by early traveler Hugh McIntyre, the waterway known as Chepi Sepe (Ghost or Spirit River) earned its moniker after a Beaver hunter passed away and promised to return. True to his word, his spirit was seen walking along the deep ravines and banks of the river just days after his funeral. In a related local legend, a Cree hunter tracking deer at the base of the river's hundred-foot-deep banks heard answering shouts when calling out for his companions as darkness fell. When no one appeared and the voices proved to be disembodied echoes or spirits, the terrified hunter fled the valley, prompting his entire party to pack up and leave the area to the unseen entities.
