The Restless Spirits of the Fort Saskatchewan Police Post

Summary

Staff and visitors at the historic North West Mounted Police post and museum grounds report disembodied footsteps, strange lights, and the ghostly apparition of Alberta's last executed female prisoner.

Details

Established in 1875 as the second fort constructed by the North West Mounted Police, the post later became a provincial jail where numerous inmates faced execution before capital punishment was abolished in Canada. Today, the preserved grounds and heritage buildings—including the Carpenter's Shop and Doctor Henry House—are widely reputed to be haunted. Visitors and curators have reported experiencing unexplained phenomena such as lights turning on and off by themselves, objects moving mysteriously, and chilling disembodied voices and whispers. Among the most prominent legends is the sighting of a spectral female figure identified as Florence Lassandro, the infamous "Mob Princess" who was hanged at the jail in 1923, as well as the phantom apparition of a small girl wandering the site.
