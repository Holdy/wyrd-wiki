The Ghost House of Pope Avenue

Summary

Built around 1905 on Pope Avenue and William Street in Cochrane, this historic home became known as a haunted dwelling where nightly offerings of wine vanished and invisible spirits were chased away with a cowbell.

Details

Located at the corner of Pope Avenue and William Street in Cochrane, the historic Bell-Irving house was constructed around 1905 by prominent early ranchers. In later years, the daughter of the Kerfoot family residing there became convinced that the property was haunted. Every night, she left a glass of wine on the stone pillars outside as an offering to the ghosts, and each morning she would retrieve an empty glass, believing the spirits had gladly accepted her gift. In reality, their eccentric neighbor—a man nicknamed "Rattlesnake Pete" who caught garter snakes and wore their fangs—was sneaking across the street to drink the wine. The daughter was also known to borrow a neighbor's milk cowbell and ring it throughout the house to banish lingering entities.
