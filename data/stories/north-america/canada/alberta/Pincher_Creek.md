#title
The Haunting of the Cox House

#summary
Visitors and staff at the historic Cox House frequently report disembodied voices, moving artifacts, and phantom figures in period costume.

#details
Commissioned in 1887, the A.E. & Mary Cox farmhouse was relocated to the Kootenai Brown Historical Park in 1987. Ever since, visitors have reported hearing footsteps, independent movement of objects, and eerie feelings of acceptance or rejection from an unseen force. During a 2006 paranormal investigation led by Vicki Burles, a photograph of an unknown female Cox family member mysteriously flew 10 feet off the top of the piano. Later, during a 2023 Halloween event, a visitor spotted a woman in a Victorian mourning gown looking out an upstairs window, even though no staff or volunteers were inside the house wearing that costume. In 2026, a summer student heard humming in the house and found a normally locked interior kitchen door wide open.

#location
The Cox House at the Kootenai Brown Historical Park at 1037 Bev McLachlin Drive in Pincher Creek, Alberta.

#title
The Lurker at the North West Mounted Police Outpost

#summary
Visitors passing the historic gravestone of Sergeant William Brock Wilde outside the NWMP Outpost report biting cold spots and a persistent unseen presence.

#details
Positioned outside the North West Mounted Police Outpost exhibit is the original gravestone of Sergeant William Brock Wilde, who was killed in the line of duty during a winter pursuit. Visitors frequently experience abrupt columns of freezing air even during the heat of summer. Those who pass through the cold spot often report feeling an invisible presence—dubbed "The Lurker"—that continues to follow them around the museum grounds.

#location
The North West Mounted Police Outpost exhibit at the Kootenai Brown Historical Park at 1037 Bev McLachlin Drive in Pincher Creek, Alberta.

#title
The Phantom Student of Fishburn School House

#summary
Apparitions of a child and unexplained childlike voices have been encountered inside the historic Fishburn School House.

#details
Built in 1897 as the first rural one-room schoolhouse in the area, the Fishburn School served as a community and educational hub before closing in the 1960s. In 2022, a summer student closing up the museum buildings opened the door to the schoolhouse to find a young boy in strange clothing sitting very still at a desk staring at the blackboard; when the door opened wider, the boy jumped up, ran to the window, and vanished while giggling. Later, during a 2025 ghost-hunting event, an archivist and her friends felt an overwhelming presence inside the schoolhouse, while spirit box recordings captured childish voices angrily telling them to get out.

#location
The Fishburn School House at the Kootenai Brown Historical Park at 1037 Bev McLachlin Drive in Pincher Creek, Alberta.
