    The Permanent Guest of Room Three

    Summary

    A local ranch hand who passed away in the hotel during the 1940s is said to haunt the premises, moving furniture at night and drinking whiskey left on the back stairs. His presence is further marked by a mysterious inscription on a third-floor dresser that persistently remains free of dust.

    Details

    Constructed in 1902 and expanded in 1912 to serve as a vital layover point between Lethbridge and Calgary, the historic Auditorium Hotel in Nanton became the backdrop for one of the region's most enduring local legends. According to local lore, a hard-drinking ranch hand named Rex Irwin lived at the hotel and died in Room 3 sometime in the 1940s. Following his passing, staff and visitors began reporting strange paranormal occurrences. Late at night, long after the building was locked, furniture in the dining room and tavern would be found shifted. Staff also shared the legend that leaving a shot of whiskey on the back stairs for Irwin would result in the glass being empty by morning. On the hotel's third floor—which suffered heavy damage during a 1983 boiler fire—an old wooden dresser sits covered in dust and fading visitor messages. Among the scrawls, a distinct message reading "Rex Irwin lives here" stands out because, unlike the surrounding dust, it never seems to accumulate grime or age.
