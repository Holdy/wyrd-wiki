#title
The Cannibal of the Sturgeon River Wilderness

#summary
In the winter of 1878–1879, a Cree hunter named Swift Runner camped in the wilderness near present-day Gibbons, Alberta, where he fell under the spell of the legendary Wendigo and committed horrific acts of cannibalism against his own family.

#details
In late 1878, a 6-foot-3 Cree hunter named Swift Runner led his family—including his wife, mother, and six children—into the boreal forest north of Edmonton to trap for the winter. Historical accounts and contemporary sources place their winter camp in the wilderness stretching between Athabasca Landing and present-day Gibbons, Alberta, near the Sturgeon River. By the spring of 1879, Swift Runner emerged alone at a Cree encampment near St. Albert, claiming his family had starved to death or died of exposure. However, his robust physical condition and strange demeanor raised immediate suspicions among local missionaries and North-West Mounted Police officers. 

A police detail led by Sub-Inspector Severe Gagnon escorted Swift Runner back to his winter campsite. Upon arrival, officers discovered a scene of absolute horror: a folded moose-hide teepee, cold campfire ashes containing boiled and smashed human bones with distinct teeth marks, hair, clothing, and eight boiled human skulls—including that of an infant stuffed with needlework. Under interrogation and after consuming a tobacco-laced "medicine tea," Swift Runner confessed that driven by nightmares and the malevolent influence of the Wendigo—the monstrous cannibal spirit of Cree folklore—he had murdered and consumed his wife and children one by one during the winter months. Swift Runner was tried, convicted, and subsequently hanged at Fort Saskatchewan on December 20, 1879.

#location
The wilderness encampment along the Sturgeon River near present-day Gibbons, Alberta.
