#title
The Ghost Village on the Oldman River

#summary
During a severe thunderstorm in 1875, a Northwest Mounted Police officer discovered a glowing, spectral Cree Indian camp that vanished completely after a nearby lightning strike.

#details
Before the North-West Mounted Police brought order to the Canadian prairies, the region saw frequent tribal conflicts and skirmishes. In the summer of 1875, Officer Cecil Denny was boating on the Oldman River about 15 miles upstream from Fort Macleod when a torrential rainstorm struck. Seeking shelter in a copse of trees, Denny heard distant chanting and drum beats. Breaking out into an open clearing, he witnessed an active Indian camp complete with glowing lodge fires, men, women, and children moving among the tipis. Before he could approach, a massive bolt of lightning struck a tree mere meters away, knocking him unconscious. When he awoke, the entire camp had completely vanished into thin air. The following day, Denny returned to the site with a Blackfoot guide and interpreter, who noted that a Cree camp had been massacred at that exact location years prior. There, they discovered two bleached skulls lying side-by-side in the grass.

#location
The Oldman River, approximately 15 miles upstream from Fort Macleod, Alberta.

#title
The Hanged Smugglers of the Golf Course

#summary
The grounds of the Fort Macleod Golf Club are allegedly haunted by two convicted smugglers who were hanged on the property in the early 1900s.

#details
Constructed near the historical site of an old North-West Mounted Police fort, the Fort Macleod Golf Club is said to be haunted by the restless spirits of two criminals. According to local lore, the two smugglers were tried, convicted, and hanged right at the site of what is now the course's second tee box, with their bodies subsequently buried underneath the first fairway. On dark, moonless nights and overcast days, golfers and visitors have reported seeing spectral figures hanging around the trees and floating across the fairway.

#location
The Fort Macleod Golf Club grounds in Fort Macleod, Alberta.

#title
The Phantoms of the Empress Theatre

#summary
Canada's oldest continuously operating theatre in Fort Macleod is notoriously haunted by multiple active spirits, including a former janitor who interacts with performers and staff.

#details
Built in 1912 as an opera house, the Empress Theatre has accumulated decades of paranormal lore, starting from mysterious security alarm triggers to full-bodied apparitions. Staff and visitors have repeatedly encountered "Ed," believed to be a burly 1930s janitor wearing a cowboy hat who leaves behind the distinct scents of cigar smoke, alcohol, and manure. Witnesses have reported disembodied belly laughs in dark storage rooms, doors slamming shut on their own, floating coffee cups, phantom footsteps racing up and down basement stairs, and eerie phone calls from a raspy voice asking, "How do I get out of here?" Performers and stagehands have also seen spectral figures watching from balcony seats, experienced unexplained lighting and curtain blackouts during shows, and heard the taps of invisible dancers on stage.

#location
The Empress Theatre at 2359 2nd Ave in Fort Macleod, Alberta.

#upstream-source
"Spirits of the West: Eerie Encounters from the Prairies to the Pacific" by Robert C. Belyk (TouchWood Editions, 2014).
