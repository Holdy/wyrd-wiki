#title
The Ghost of Dunvegan Bridge

#summary
Two men driving across a newly built river bridge in 1960 encountered a mysterious barefoot woman in a hooded cloak who vanished without leaving footprints in the fresh snow. Subsequent sightings reported a phantom nun or spectral figure haunting the valley and bridge.

#details
In November 1960, shortly after the completion of the Dunvegan Bridge, two men were driving through the snowy valley at night. Near the middle of the bridge, they spotted a woman standing in the swirling snow with her head bent in a hooded cloak, notably wearing bare feet. When they pulled over to check on her, one man walked back across the bridge calling out, only to find no one there and no footprints in the snow other than their own fresh tire tracks. Later investigations by locals and returning witnesses spotted the figure on nearby hillsides picking berries in the knee-deep snow, solidifying local belief that she was a spirit. Additional legends mention a Spirit River man whose terrifying encounter with the ghost left him too shaken to ever speak of it again, alongside sightings of a phantom priest at the historic Dunvegan rectory.

#location
The Dunvegan Suspension Bridge spanning the Peace River in Dunvegan Provincial Park, Alberta.

#title
The Legend of the Spirit River

#summary
Early traveler accounts and Indigenous folklore attribute the name of Spirit River to a deceased warrior who returned to walk the riverbanks after his death. A companion legend describes a hunter who heard disembodied voices echoing from the river ravines, causing his entire hunting party to abandon their camp.

#details
According to historical accounts recorded by early traveler Hugh McIntyre, the waterway known as Chepi Sepe (Ghost or Spirit River) earned its moniker after a Beaver hunter passed away and promised to return. True to his word, his spirit was seen walking along the deep ravines and banks of the river just days after his funeral. In a related local legend, a Cree hunter tracking deer at the base of the river's hundred-foot-deep banks heard answering shouts when calling out for his companions as darkness fell. When no one appeared and the voices proved to be disembodied echoes or spirits, the terrified hunter fled the valley, prompting his entire party to pack up and leave the area to the unseen entities.

#location
The steep ravines and banks of the Spirit River running behind the historic town site in Spirit River, Alberta.
