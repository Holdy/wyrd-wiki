#title
The Restless Cook of North Railway Street

#summary
Following his untimely death at the old Alberta Hotel, a Chinese cook known as Jim was buried in Okotoks without his remains being exhumed and returned to his home village as required by traditional custom. Locals recount that his spirit continues to wander the old downtown area searching for its spiritual home.

#details
In the early days of Okotoks, the rough-and-tumble Alberta Hotel stood directly across from the railway station on North Railway Street, serving as a popular watering hole for single men. Among its residents and workers was a Chinese cook universally known simply as "Jim." When Jim passed away, he was interred locally, but his bones were never disinterred and repatriated within seven years, which was mandated by traditional Chinese burial customs of the era. According to local historians leading Okotoks' annual ghost tours, Jim's spirit remains trapped and unsettled, wandering the streets and alleys of Olde Towne Okotoks in eternal search of his true home.

#location
The site of the former Alberta Hotel on North Railway Street in downtown Okotoks, Alberta.

#title
The Waiting Woman in White

#summary
Visitors and staff at the historic Okotoks railway station have reported eerie encounters with the spirit of a woman in a white dress waiting for a train. Local history notes that in 1922, the body of a woman who died aboard a train was brought into this exact station before being transferred to her family.

#details
The historic building housing the Okotoks Art Gallery—formerly the Canadian Pacific Railway Train Station in downtown Okotoks—has long been associated with paranormal activity. Staff and volunteers working alone in the building have reported unexplained sounds, objects being moved, and the lingering presence of a spirit. Historians conducting local ghost tours recount that in 1922, a woman passed away while riding a train, and her body was temporarily placed in a casket inside the station house before shipment to Blairmore. Many believe this event, coupled with frequent sightings of a figure in a long white dress waiting on the platform, cemented the station's reputation as one of the town's most haunted locations.

#location
The Okotoks Art Gallery, located at 49 North Railway Street in downtown Okotoks, Alberta.

#title
The Whistling Janitor of Okotoks Junior High

#summary
Generations of students and staff at the century-old Okotoks Junior High School have reported hearing unexplained noises and feeling an unseen presence in the hallways. Legend attributes these occurrences to the spirit of a former custodian who continues his duties long after death.

#details
Okotoks Junior High School is a historic building steeped in decades of local education and memories. Over the years, teachers, students, and staff have shared unsettling accounts of paranormal activity within its aged corridors. The most enduring local legend is that of a phantom janitor who supposedly haunts the building, famously known for the phenomenon of whistling echoing through empty hallways—giving rise to the local saying that he still "whistles while he works." Despite the passing decades and modern renovations to the school, reported encounters with this unseen caretaker remain a staple of Okotoks' folklore.

#location
Okotoks Junior High School, situated on historical school grounds in Okotoks, Alberta.
