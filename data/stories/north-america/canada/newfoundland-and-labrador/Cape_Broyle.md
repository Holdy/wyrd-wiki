#title
The Devil's Stairway of Cape Broyle Head

#summary
A precipitous cliff formation on the Southern Shore is tied to local legends of a quick-tempered sea captain and supernatural encounters along the treacherous headlands.

#details
Overlooking the rough waters of the Southern Shore near Cape Broyle Head, a sheer cliff known locally as the "Devil's Stairway" (or Devil's Staircase) has long been the subject of eerie local folklore. Recorded in mid-20th-century oral history collections such as *The Barrelman* broadcasts, the legend tells of a coasting captain who experienced a bizarre and harrowing supernatural encounter along the cliffside. The treacherous terrain and dizzying drops of Cape Broyle Head inspired generations of stories regarding uncanny events and otherworldly forces haunting the jagged coastline.

#location
Cape Broyle Head is a prominent cliffside headland located along the coast of Cape Broyle on the Southern Shore of Newfoundland.

#title
The Empty Box Tomb of Father Burke

#summary
Local legend claims that after the sudden death of Reverend Patrick Burke in 1849, his grieving family sailed from Ireland to Newfoundland and secretly exhumed his body from his cemetery tomb under the cover of night.

#details
Reverend Patrick Burke served as an assistant at Holy Trinity Parish in Ferryland before his death on April 27, 1849. He was buried in the Immaculate Conception Cemetery in Cape Broyle inside a distinctive stone box tomb—notable as the only box tomb in the cemetery and the only monument bearing Latin script. According to community folklore, when news of his death reached his family in Tipperary, Ireland, they chartered a vessel and traveled across the Atlantic. Under the cover of darkness, they reportedly removed the cleric's body from the tomb and carried it back to Ireland, leaving the vault empty.

#location
Immaculate Conception Cemetery is located along the Southern Shore Highway on a hill on the northern side of Cape Broyle, overlooking Cape Broyle Harbour.
