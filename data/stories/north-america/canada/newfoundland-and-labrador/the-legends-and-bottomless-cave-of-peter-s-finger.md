#title
The Legends and Bottomless Cave of Peter's Finger

#summary
The prominent rock outcrop of Peter's Finger is steeped in legends of pirate treasure, a bottomless cave connected to the sea, and lost silver stirrups.

#details
Peter's Finger is a large rock outcrop in New Perlican with a cliff pointing toward Trinity Bay. Local folklore ties the site to the pirate Peter Easton, who allegedly used it as a lookout and left an undiscovered treasure guarded by the cliff. Another prominent legend claims that Peter's Finger sits atop a bottomless cave; this belief gained traction after goats that wandered to the top of the outcrop were later found dead floating in the saltwater of adjacent Vitter's Cove. Adding to the mystery, two 17th-century silver saddle stirrups bearing Potosi mint marks—matching Spanish coins found at a nearby archaeological site—were reportedly discovered by boys who climbed down the treacherous crevices of the rock face. Historically, residents also scaled the dangerous holes in the formation with rope ladders during warmer months to retrieve natural ice and store perishable foods.

#location
Peter's Finger outcrop, located in the southwestern corner of New Perlican, west of Route 80 and south of Vitter's Cove Road, Newfoundland and Labrador.
