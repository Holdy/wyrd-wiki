#title
The Faerie Path of Springfield Road

#summary
Parents in North River strictly warned their children away from the fields and old railway tracks, believing the area was a dangerous faerie path where people could be spirited away.

#details
For generations, outport families passed down frightening warnings about rural landscape features where supernatural forces held sway. In North River, mothers specifically instructed their children never to wander near the fields on Springfield Road or along the old train tracks. These locations were reputed to be active faerie paths where careless wanderers could slip into timeless traps, disappearing into another realm for hours or days.

#location
Springfield Road and the adjacent old railway track path in North River, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#upstream-source
Edited by Lisa Wilson, Foreword by Dale Jarvis, "Folk Beliefs & Legends of Bay Roberts & Area" (Bay Roberts Cultural Foundation and Heritage Foundation of Newfoundland and Labrador, 2014).

#title
The Ghost of the North River Trestle

#summary
An abandoned railway bridge in North River is said to be haunted by the spirit of a girl who died there, posing a deadly threat to anyone who crosses after dark.

#details
Local lore surrounding the old railway trestle crossing in North River warns visitors and locals alike to stay away after nightfall. According to regional folklore, a young girl lost her life on the structure, and her restless ghost now haunts the bridge. Locals recount that she actively tries to attack and harm anyone brave or foolish enough to trespass on the trestle during the night.

#location
The old railway trestle bridge crossing the river in North River, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#upstream-source
Edited by Lisa Wilson, Foreword by Dale Jarvis, "Folk Beliefs & Legends of Bay Roberts & Area" (Bay Roberts Cultural Foundation and Heritage Foundation of Newfoundland and Labrador, 2014).

#title
The Midnight Procession of North River

#summary
A young traveler walking home at midnight in the 1920s witnessed a silent, ghostly funeral procession trudging past the North River cemetery.

#details
In the mid-1920s, a teenager walking home alone around midnight took a shortcut through North River past the Roman Catholic cemetery near the Birch Hills. Under the light of a full moon shining on fresh snow, he spotted a strange, slow-moving line of people carrying lanterns and following a horse-drawn sleigh bearing a coffin. Fearing that the silent, bone-weary phantoms were trapped souls who would force him to join an eternal march, he hid in the snowbank beneath a tree until the ghostly funeral disappeared into the dark.

#location
The road passing through North River near the Roman Catholic cemetery by the Birch Hills, Newfoundland and Labrador.
