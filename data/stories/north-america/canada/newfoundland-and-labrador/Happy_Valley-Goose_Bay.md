#title
The Phantom Dog Team Driver of Labrador

#summary
A phantom trapper and his ghostly white dog team roam the snowy Labrador wilderness, their whip cracks and calls echoing right before fierce winter storms.

#details
Known as Ol' Smoker, the legend tells of the spirit of a solitary man condemned to wander the frozen landscape with his team of white dogs as penance for a misspent life. Residents across the Labrador region, including those living in Happy Valley-Goose Bay, grew up hearing terrifying accounts of the phantom driver used by elders to warn children to get home before dark. Those who claim to have encountered him report hearing the sudden, ghastly crack of a whip and echoing shouts amidst the whistling wind just before whiteout blizzard conditions strike.

#location
The wilderness and interior trails of Labrador, with folklore traditions and accounts actively shared in Happy Valley-Goose Bay.

None.

#title
The Traverspine Gorilla

#summary
Between 1913 and 1915, a towering, hairy wildman with ape-like features and two-toed footprints terrorized a small settlement on the outskirts of Happy Valley-Goose Bay.

#details
Around 1913, a young girl named Michelin playing near her family's cabin in Traverspine—just outside Happy Valley-Goose Bay—encountered a 7-foot-tall, hair-covered creature with long dangling arms and a distinct white mane resembling a Roman centurion's helmet. The creature grinned, baring its white teeth, before the girl fled in terror. Over the next two winters, the entity stalked the woods, left massive 12-inch, two-toed footprints that sank deep into the snow and mud, and drove local sled dogs into a frenzy, occasionally chasing them into the Traverspine River. When the creature later peered into a cabin window, Mrs. Michelin grabbed her shotgun and fired into the brush, hearing a solid thud as lead struck the elusive beast.

#location
The settlement and river of Traverspine on the outskirts of Happy Valley-Goose Bay, Labrador.

#upstream-source
True North: A Journey Into Unexplored Wilderness by Elliot Merrick (1933).
