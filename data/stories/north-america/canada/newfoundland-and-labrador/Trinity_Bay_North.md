#title
The Ghostly Graves and Ruins of Mockbeggar

#summary
Construction work and historical digging in the Mockbeggar neighbourhood unearthed centuries-old coffins and a buried boat, giving rise to persistent local legends of haunted houses and spectral singing.

#details
For over a century, construction and canal excavation in the Mockbeggar neighbourhood of Bonavista repeatedly disturbed mysterious, well-preserved coffins containing bodies dressed in ancient attire, alongside the timbers of an old wooden boat. Early 20th-century reports detailed how locals discovered remains outfitted in clothing resembling Puritan or old French wear buried deep in the acidic bogs. Oral traditions and folklore soon took root, with residents recounting stories of houses built directly atop unmarked graves—including one tale where a home's support post sank directly onto a hidden coffin. Local lore maintains that Mockbeggar is intensely haunted, with neighbours claiming that on quiet nights, eerie foreign singing and ghostly spectral phenomena can still be experienced near the old canal.

#location
The canal area connecting O'Dea's Pond to the ocean in the Mockbeggar neighbourhood of Bonavista, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
The Phantom Light of Trinity

#summary
Beginning around 1916, a brilliant and unexplained ghost light regularly appeared over the water near Trinity narrows, sparking fears of wartime sabotage before becoming a legendary nautical phantasm.

#details
Around 1916, residents and mariners in Trinity began noticing a mysterious, highly brilliant light shining over the water just a few miles off Trinity narrows between 9:00 p.m. and 11:00 p.m. with astonishing regularity. The glow was so convincing that the Fort Point lighthouse keeper once rowed across the harbour in great haste expecting the arrival of the *SS Prospero*, only to discover no ship in sight. During the First World War, frightened locals feared the phenomenon might be a German submarine attempting to tamper with the undersea telegraph cable. Later, fishermen reported trying to row toward the light on the dark fishing grounds, only for it to vanish entirely as they drew close. While some pragmatists theorized it was the headlight from a newly extended railway line, the true nature of the eerie phantom light was never fully explained.

#location
Fort Point Lighthouse overlooking Trinity Narrows in Trinity, Newfoundland and Labrador.
