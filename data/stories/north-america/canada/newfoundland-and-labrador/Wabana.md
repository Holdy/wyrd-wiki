#title
The Marching Men of No. 2 Mine

#summary
Residents near an abandoned mine slope watched in astonishment as nearly a hundred ghostly figures marched out in pairs past the check house before vanishing without leaving footprints in the snow.

#details
At a home located close to the collar of No. 2 Mine—which had been shut down and inactive for some time—a family and their visiting guests were playing cards when someone noticed movement near the mine entrance. Curious, everyone rushed to the window and watched in disbelief as men emerged from the slope two by two, marching past the check house until between ninety and a hundred figures had passed. When witnesses immediately went outside to investigate, they found the slope still securely barred, with zero footprints in the snow and no sign of the men.

#location
The collar and check house of No. 2 Mine, Wabana, Bell Island, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
The Phantom Driver of No. 3 Mine

#summary
While working the graveyard shift alone deep underground, a miner was startled to see the ghost of a deceased driver sitting in a chair along the headway.

#details
In the early 1950s, a miner named Leander Picco took on the graveyard shift in No. 3 Mine on Bell Island. After all the other workers had ascended to the surface, Leander was left completely alone in the dark tunnels. Around 2:30 in the morning, he called his brother George in a state of distress, reporting that as he walked up the headway, he saw the apparition of a driver who had previously been killed working in that exact area sitting in the chair. Leander later discovered that the worker who held the job before him had experienced the exact same supernatural encounter and had immediately requested a transfer out of the mine.

#location
No. 3 Mine headway, Wabana, Bell Island, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
The Woman in White Behind the Arena

#summary
A man walking home across the barrens late at night came face-to-face with a towering, sheet-draped figure known locally as the Woman in White.

#details
During the 1950s, local folklore across Bell Island frequently warned residents about the "Woman in White"—a mysterious, sheet-clad figure said to stalk lonely roads and scare people after dark. One night, a local man named Eric Luffman walked home across the barrens from the West Mines toward Scotia Ridge after having a few drinks. As he neared the water tank behind the arena, he saw a figure over six feet tall standing motionless in the road, resembling a ghost. Though initially terrified, Eric approached the figure and recognized a local prankster waiting to scare someone else.

#location
Near the water tank behind the arena on Scotia Ridge, Wabana, Bell Island, Newfoundland and Labrador.
