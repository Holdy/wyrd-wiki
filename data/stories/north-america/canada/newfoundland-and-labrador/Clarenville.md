#title
The Frozen Embrace of the Bar Water

#summary
During a severe winter in 1870, four travelers crossing the ice on Smith Sound tragically plunged into the treacherous freezing waters known as the "Bar Water" near Shoal Harbour.

#details
In the winter of 1870, amidst a relentlessly harsh freeze, Mr. and Mrs. Hogarth, Mr. William Wiseman, and Mrs. Mary Ann Lenard were returning to Shoal Harbour after visiting Snooks Harbour. While traversing the ice on Smith Sound, a shifting tide and hidden layer of slush concealed a dangerous patch of open water known locally as the "Bar Water." The ice gave way under their feet, plunging all four into the freezing depths. Despite a desperate cry heard by a local resident, Eli Stanley, who rushed to help, all four victims were consumed by the icy abyss, leaving behind a haunting legacy of nature's unforgiving power that is still remembered by locals today.

#location
The Bar Water on Smith Sound near Shoal Harbour, Clarenville, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
Tragedy in the Train Yard

#summary
In the summer of 1937, a fatal poisoning incident at the local asphalt plant and railway yard in Clarenville claimed the lives of several workers under mysterious and tragic circumstances.

#details
In the summer of 1937, the town of Clarenville was shaken when four men fell victim to a deadly poisoning after consuming "Colombian" spirit—a highly refined and odorless methylated spirit used in manufacturing at the local COLAS asphalt plant. Charles Lyons, the plant's chief engineer, and Patrick McHugh, the night watchman, were the first to succumb to the elixir. Shortly after, railway employee Michael Williams also died from the poison, while another worker, Edward Long, barely survived with paralysis and blindness after a delayed reaction. The chilling tragedy left the community haunted by the sudden loss of life and a thorough police investigation into the fatal substance.

#location
The COLAS asphalt plant and railway yard in Clarenville, Newfoundland and Labrador.
