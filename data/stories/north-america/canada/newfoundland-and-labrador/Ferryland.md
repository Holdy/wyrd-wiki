#title
The Ferryland Lighthouse Visitor

#summary
In the early 1970s, an artist and his family living in an isolated lighthouse dwelling on the Avalon Peninsula received a late-night visit from a mysterious old man who later turned out to be a constable who had died twenty years prior.

#details
In the early 1970s, acclaimed Newfoundland artist Gerald Squires, his potter wife Gail, and their two young daughters moved into the remote, long-abandoned Ferryland Head Lighthouse Keeper’s Dwelling in Ferryland, Newfoundland. Just before Halloween, while the family was gathered inside the damp, isolated stone-foundation house, a knock came at the door. Standing on the doorstep was a large, elderly man wearing a long black coat and big boots, whose face was lined with deep wrinkles. Stepping inside, he cheerfully remarked, "Your dog asked me to come in," which seemed entirely plausible as their dog greeted him warmly. 

The stranger made himself entirely at home, regaling the family with colorful local histories and memories of the lighthouse's previous occupants, including an old account of a local boy who had died of pneumonia and whose wake was held in the very house. After hours of storytelling, the visitor expressed approval that the lighthouse was in good hands, saying, "Now I know you’ll be good for this lighthouse," and took his leave. When young Esther looked out to watch him depart down the winding road, the man had completely vanished without a trace into the heavy coastal fog.

A few days later, Gerald and his daughter recounted the encounter to a local neighbor named Arch. After asking several descriptive questions, Arch revealed that the man's appearance, mannerisms, and exact phrasing matched Constable Dick Costello—a former Ferryland policeman who had died twenty years earlier. Years later, when Gerald met Dick Costello's living daughter, she was astonished by the tale, confirming that her father always used that exact phrase whenever entering someone's home.

#location
The Ferryland Head Lighthouse Keeper's Dwelling, located on Ferryland Head at the edge of the Atlantic Ocean in Ferryland, Newfoundland and Labrador.
