#title
Moses Power and the Torbay Ghost

#summary
In August 1877, Moses Power’s home in Torbay was besieged by nightly ghostly disturbances and physical attacks while a crowd of forty men stood watch. Witnesses reported seeing an apparition of a young woman in white muslin as windows were smashed and property damaged.

#details
In August 1877, the home of Moses Power in Torbay, Newfoundland, became the center of a reported haunting. According to an account published in the *Morning Chronicle* on August 11, 1877, the house was under nightly attack from seven o'clock until midnight, with windows being smashed, clapboard torn away, and saltcod disappearing. Even forty men standing watch could not stop the destructive forces. Witnesses, including Power himself, described seeing the apparition of a young woman dressed in white muslin. The newspaper called on Inspector Carty of the constabulary to investigate the fearful disturbances, leaving whether it was truly supernatural or a terrestrial disturbance as part of local history.

#location
The key location is the historic home site of Moses Power located in Torbay, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
The Ghosts and Phantom Dogs of Gallows Cove

#summary
Watson's Cove and Gallows Cove in Torbay are steeped in local legends of a murdered cabin boy guarded by a chain-shaking black dog with glowing red eyes. The coastal area is also said to be haunted by Phil Howlett, a grieving father buried on his property after a tragic household accident.

#details
According to local folklore researched by folklorist Dale Jarvis and Torbay writer Lara Maynard, the coastal area of Watson's Cove and Gallows Cove in Torbay harbors multiple haunting legends. Watson's Cove is said to be haunted by the ghost of a cabin boy murdered by pirates along with his buried loot, accompanied by the phantom of the boy's dog—a large black hound with red, glowing eyes that makes chain-shaking noises. Nearby Gallows Cove, named variously after historical hangings, Devonian roots, or old net-drying frames known as "gallis," is associated with the restless spirit of Phil Howlett. Local legend states that after his young child tragically fell into the open hearth and died, Howlett was denied burial in consecrated ground and was instead interred on his property, where his mournful ghost continues to wander.

#location
The key locations are Gallows Cove and Watson's Cove along the coastal area of Torbay, Newfoundland and Labrador.
