#title
The Hitchhiker of the Humber Valley Highway

#summary
Motorists driving along the highway connecting Deer Lake and Corner Brook through the Humber Valley have reported encountering a mysterious woman in a white dress who either vanishes into thin air or startles drivers by materializing inside their locked cars.

#details
Local legend warns drivers traveling at night along the highway stretch passing through the Humber Valley region—between Deer Lake, Steady Brook, and Corner Brook—to be wary of a roadside hitchhiker. Drivers who slow down to offer assistance to a rain-drenched woman in white find that she abruptly vanishes before they can stop. Conversely, those who drive past without stopping look in their rearview mirror only to discover the same woman silently sitting in the passenger seat beside them. 

#location
The highway stretching between Deer Lake and Corner Brook, passing through Steady Brook, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
The Phantom Guardian of Shellbird Island

#summary
Legend says that pirate Peter Easton buried his stolen gold on Shellbird Island in the Humber River, murdering a crew member to serve as a spectral guardian for the hidden treasure beneath the watchful gaze of the "Old Man in the Mountain" rock face.

#details
According to Newfoundland folklore, pirate Peter Easton sought refuge at the mouth of the Humber River near Corner Brook and Steady Brook, sending his mate to bury three chests of gold on Shellbird Island. To ensure the location remained a secret, the mate shot the accompanying sailor dead and buried him directly with the chest to serve as a ghostly sentinel. On the return trip across the river, the mate drowned in the treacherous waters of the Devil's Dancing Pool, sealing the secret forever. Today, the natural rock formation known as the "Old Man in the Mountain"—best seen when traveling east toward Steady Brook—overlooks the island where the restless spirit of the murdered sailor is said to guard the remaining unrecovered treasure.

#location
Shellbird Island and Breakfast Head along the Humber River, visible from the road traveling toward Steady Brook, Newfoundland and Labrador.
