#title
The Burial at "The Geeze"

#summary
Early exploration folklore from the Avalon Peninsula suggests that Portuguese explorer Gaspar Corte Real landed at Chance Cove in the early 1500s to bury two of his sailors.

#details
Dating back to the earliest European fishing fleets visiting the shores of Newfoundland, local oral history and the Intangible Cultural Heritage Inventory preserve tales of early explorers. According to long-standing folklore surrounding the Western Isthmus, Portuguese explorer Gaspar Corte Real came ashore in the area of Chance Cove—specifically a local spot known as "The Geeze"—during the early sixteenth century. There, in the remote coastal wilderness, he buried two of his sailors who had died during the voyage. The site has long been regarded with an aura of mystery and ghostly reverence by generations of outport fishers, who viewed the isolated burial ground as a liminal and haunted threshold where the spirits of the earliest European mariners linger along the rocky shore.

#location
Chance Cove on the Western Isthmus of the Avalon Peninsula, Newfoundland and Labrador.
