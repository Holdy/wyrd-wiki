#title
Pretty Fanny Goff

#summary
Following her tragic death from typhoid fever on the eve of her wedding in 1823, the renowned beauty Tryphena "Fanny" Goff was buried under an apple tree in her family's garden in Portugal Cove.

#details
Tryphena "Fanny" Goff was celebrated across Newfoundland in the early 19th century for her extraordinary beauty and charm. In March 1823, she was scheduled to marry Mr. Barter, a prominent resident of Brigus. However, less than a fortnight before the wedding, she contracted typhoid fever and passed away. Unaware of the tragedy, Mr. Barter traveled across the bay by ice-sledge to Portugal Cove, stopping along the way at William Squires' home in St. Philip's, where he joyfully declared it the happiest day of his life. Squires sorrowfully broke the news of Fanny's death to him. Devastated, Barter returned to Brigus and died of a broken heart three days later. Fanny was laid to rest beneath an apple tree in the garden of her father's inn in Portugal Cove, where her headstone remained for generations.

#location
The former site of Goff's Inn garden (under the historic apple tree burial site) in Portugal Cove, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#upstream-source
The Treasury of Newfoundland Stories, published by Maple Leaf Mills Limited (1961).

#title
The Bridge of Ill Omen

#summary
A local omen in Portugal Cove held that whenever congregations exiting two different community churches met on a specific footbridge, someone in the community would die within the week.

#details
According to local oral tradition shared in Portugal Cove–St. Philip's, parishioners crossing the footbridge by the monument near the old Anglican church would occasionally exit services at the exact same time as congregants from another church. Whenever these two groups happened to meet precisely on the bridge, it was regarded as an infallible omen of death, signaling that a community member would pass away before the week was out—a belief that persisted until the church was relocated.

#location
The footbridge near the monument by the old Anglican church in Portugal Cove–St. Philip's, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
The Ghosts of Goat Cove

#summary
Local folklore warns that supernatural spirits haunt the area around Goat Cove near Beachy Cove School, where travelers after midnight have reported eerie presences and encounters.

#details
According to oral history recorded from long-time resident Ruth Bugden, the area around Goat Cove near Beachy Cove School was widely considered haunted by local tradition, which held that no one should attempt to pass through the cove at midnight. Bugden also recounted an experience her mother had while staying at a dwelling known as Granny Talker's house, where unseen hands abruptly reached out toward her throat in the dark before an old woman reassured her that the spirit was not meant for her.

#location
Goat Cove, located near Beachy Cove School in Portugal Cove–St. Philip's, Newfoundland and Labrador.
