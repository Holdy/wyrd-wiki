#title
Ms. Tilley's Nightly Return

#summary
A house in Clarke's Beach where a former resident passed away is reportedly haunted by her restless spirit, which routinely opens doors and rearranges furniture.

#details
An elderly woman named Ms. Tilley passed away inside a home in Clarke's Beach. One night, while family members were babysitting and watching a movie in the living room, they heard the back door open and chairs scraping across the kitchen floor. Assuming it was a relative returning home, they checked the kitchen, but found no one there. Shortly afterward, they heard heavy footsteps echoing down the hallway and the bathroom door slamming shut on its own. When recounting the eerie events to their grandmother the next morning, they were told it was the ghost of Ms. Tilley, who routinely visits the house every single night.

#location
A specific residence in Clarke's Beach where Ms. Tilley previously lived and passed away.

#title
The Otterbury Phantom Procession

#summary
Walking home late at night in the 1920s, a young man witnessed a silent, ghostly funeral carriage and a train of weeping mourners marching across a snow-covered hill.

#details
In the mid-1920s around midnight, a teenager known locally as "Farmer Jack" was walking home along a snowy road after visiting his girlfriend. Stopped atop the big hill in Otterbury near Clarke's Beach under a bright full moon, he noticed a strange, silent procession approaching. A horse-drawn sleigh carried a hearse with a coffin atop it, followed by a long line of bone-weary mourners dressed entirely in black. Too terrified to speak or risk being forced to join the phantom marchers, Jack hid behind a tree in the snow until the ghostly funeral train passed silently by and vanished into the night.

#location
The top of the main hill in Otterbury, near Clarke's Beach, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
The Washerwoman of Clarke's Beach Pond

#summary
A tragic drowning at a local pond gave rise to ongoing sightings of a ghostly woman with glowing red eyes washing clothes on the adjacent hillside.

#details
Years ago, a local woman kneeled by the edge of Clarke's Beach Pond to wash her clothes by hand. Losing her balance, she tumbled into the deep water and drowned because she did not know how to swim. According to local legend, anyone standing on the metal bridge overlooking the pond can sometimes spot her apparition on the hillside, relentlessly continuing her laundry with glowing red eyes.

#location
Clarke's Beach Pond, near the metal bridge and adjacent hill in Clarke's Beach, Newfoundland and Labrador.
