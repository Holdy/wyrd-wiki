    Henry the Ghost of Badger's Quay

    Summary

    A newly purchased historic house in Badger's Quay was haunted by a helpful poltergeist named Henry who eventually departed after the homeowner told him he needed to help pay the bills.

    Details

    In 2012, residents Lorraine and Rodney purchased a 120-plus-year-old two-story house that had been floated across the bay from Safe Harbour to Badger's Quay in the 1950s. Shortly after moving in, Lorraine experienced gentle poltergeist activity: oven mitts repeatedly slid off the hot stovetop—acting as a silent warning not to leave them on a hot surface—and a couch cushion dropped precisely behind her neck when she wished for support while on the phone. When her husband Rodney heard about "Henry" the ghost, he jokingly remarked that Henry better be prepared to pay half the bills if he planned on staying. On the very first night Rodney returned home from work, a loud crash at 3:00 a.m. startled the couple awake. Rushing to the dining room, they found a heavy tin sign that had been hanging above a china cabinet full of delicate crystal lying face down on the floor, yet not a single glass or dish was disturbed. Annoyed by the disturbance, Rodney sternly told Henry that if he was going to make noise without contributing financially, he had to pack his bags and leave. The ghostly phenomena ceased entirely after that night.
