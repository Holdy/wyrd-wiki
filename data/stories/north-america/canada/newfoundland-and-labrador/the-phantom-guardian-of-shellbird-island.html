The Phantom Guardian of Shellbird Island

Summary

Legend says that pirate Peter Easton buried his stolen gold on Shellbird Island in the Humber River, murdering a crew member to serve as a spectral guardian for the hidden treasure beneath the watchful gaze of the "Old Man in the Mountain" rock face.

Details

According to Newfoundland folklore, pirate Peter Easton sought refuge at the mouth of the Humber River near Corner Brook and Steady Brook, sending his mate to bury three chests of gold on Shellbird Island. To ensure the location remained a secret, the mate shot the accompanying sailor dead and buried him directly with the chest to serve as a ghostly sentinel. On the return trip across the river, the mate drowned in the treacherous waters of the Devil's Dancing Pool, sealing the secret forever. Today, the natural rock formation known as the "Old Man in the Mountain"—best seen when traveling east toward Steady Brook—overlooks the island where the restless spirit of the murdered sailor is said to guard the remaining unrecovered treasure.
