#title
The Headless Spectre of Queen's Road

#summary
In 1745, a St. John's resident returning home late by carriage was forced to walk the final stretch down a lane off Queen's Road, where he encountered a towering headless phantom.

#details
Around 1745, a man named Samuel Pettyham rented a small house located at the end of a lane branching off Queen's Road in St. John's. After spending a late evening visiting a friend in the west end, his host offered to drive him home in a carriage. As the horse drew near the laneway, it suddenly stopped in its tracks and stubbornly refused to go any further. Opting to walk the remaining distance, Pettyham stepped out, but after walking only twenty yards, a spectral figure stepped into the moonlight. Terrified, Pettyham saw a very tall man missing his head. He fled in absolute horror back up to Queen's Road, bursting into a boarding house and vowing never to spend another night in his house.

#location
A laneway off Queen's Road in downtown St. John's, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
The Phantom Theatergoer of the LSPU Hall

#summary
Patrons and staff at a historic downtown St. John's arts venue have frequently reported sightings of a deceased stagehand who continues to attend performances from the auditorium seats.

#details
The LSPU Hall, located at 3 Victoria Street in St. John's, has a long history as a gathering place and community theater housed in a late 19th-century timber-framed building. Over the decades, staff and visitors have reported unexplained noises, phantom footsteps, and dark shadowy figures roaming the halls. Its most famous spectral resident is Fred Gamberg, a 24-year-old local punk rock musician and hall worker who tragically drowned in July 1995. Following his death, theatergoers reported seeing a young man thoroughly enjoying performances from the audience, only for him to vanish completely into thin air when the house lights came up.

#location
The LSPU Hall is located at 3 Victoria Street in St. John's, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
The Restless Ghost of Catherine Snow

#summary
Following her controversial execution for murder in 1834, the ghost of Catherine Snow has been sighted for nearly two centuries haunting the grounds of former courthouse sites and cemeteries in St. John's.

#details
Catherine Snow was convicted and hanged outside the old courthouse on Duckworth Street in St. John's on July 21, 1834, for the murder of her husband, maintaining her innocence to her dying breath. Ever since her execution—which many citizens believed was a grave miscarriage of justice—her phantom has been spotted walking the grounds and hallways of successive courthouse buildings as well as her burial site. Witnesses have reported seeing her drifting silently down corridors, accompanied by phantom footsteps and elevators moving independently. Though she was symbolically exonerated in a modern mock trial in 2012, reports of her spectral presence persist around the downtown area.

#location
The historic courthouse site and St. Andrew's Presbyterian Church (The Kirk) near Long's Hill and Duckworth Street in St. John's, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
The Spectral Resident of the Four Sisters

#summary
Identical stone houses built by a prominent St. John's stonemason are said to be haunted by a ghostly elderly woman who peers from windows and visits children in the night.

#details
Constructed between 1893 and 1903 on Temperance Street in St. John's by stonemason Samuel Garrett, the four identical stone homes known as the "Four Sisters" were originally intended as wedding gifts for his daughters. Over the years, locals and temporary residents have reported seeing the ghostly apparition of an elderly woman looking out from the windows at passersby, occasionally raising a hand to wave before vanishing. A young girl whose family rented one of the homes reported being visited regularly in the early morning hours by a mysterious lady who appeared in her bedroom. Other witnesses have noted phantom footsteps and figures passing straight through walls where interior connecting doors were once walled over.

#location
The Four Sisters are located at 31 to 37 Temperance Street in St. John's, Newfoundland and Labrador.
