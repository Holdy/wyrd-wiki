#title
The Ghostly Longboat of Baccalieu Tickle

#summary
Local fishermen in Bay de Verde repeatedly heard the phantom sounds of men rowing and calling out in the heavy fog across the water. This auditory apparition invariably manifested just before severe storms struck the coast.

#details
In the waters off the coast of Bay de Verde, local fishers navigating the treacherous fog in Baccalieu Tickle frequently experienced a chilling auditory phenomenon known as the ghostly longboat. On misty days before major gales, men out on the water would clearly hear the rhythmic creaking of oars, the splashing of water, and the voices of men talking and calling to one another as if pulling a heavy boat ashore. Despite the vividness and proximity of the sounds, no boat was ever visible in the fog, serving as an eerie maritime portent tied to the unpredictable weather of the region.

#location
Baccalieu Tickle, the narrow channel of water lying between the community of Bay de Verde and Baccalieu Island off the northeast coast of Newfoundland and Labrador.

#upstream-source
Charis Cotter (editor), *The Ghosts of Baccalieu* (Baccalieu Books, 2013), collecting traditional local ghost stories shared by students and families of Tricon Elementary School in Bay de Verde.

#title
The Shuffling Footsteps of Poor Keye

#summary
During a stormy night in a Bay de Verde home, children and adults heard the familiar, uneven footsteps of a hospitalized neighbor coming down the hallway. The next morning, a telegram arrived confirming that he had passed away at that exact hour.

#details
In the 1920s in the coastal community of Bay de Verde, a family and visiting neighbors sat talking late into the night while children listened quietly from a daybed. The conversation turned to Poor Keye, a local neighbor who was currently receiving medical treatment in a hospital in St. John's, known affectionately for his distinct "step-shuffle-thump" walk due to an injured leg. Suddenly, the front door crashed open and unmistakable shuffling footsteps were heard marching down the hall. When the mother took a lamp to check the hallway, no one was there, and a subsequent crash was heard in the back kitchen. The following morning, news arrived via telegram that Poor Keye had died at the exact time the spectral footsteps echoed through the house, marking a classic Newfoundland forerunner.

#location
A historic family home in the coastal fishing community of Bay de Verde on the Avalon Peninsula of Newfoundland and Labrador.
