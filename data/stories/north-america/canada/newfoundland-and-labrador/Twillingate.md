#title
The Bakers Light of Twillingate Harbour

#summary
A young boy and local fishermen witnessed a terrifying, fast-moving weather light over Twillingate Harbour said to originate from a nineteenth-century shipwreck.

#details
In September 1957, ten-year-old John Gillett climbed Ochre Pitt hill overlooking Twillingate Harbour and witnessed a brilliant light dancing across the water like a ballerina before darting away at impossible speeds. Local elders explained that this was the "Bakers Light," a ghostly weather phenomenon tied to the tragic 19th-century shipwreck of the *Queen of Swansea* on nearby Cape John Gull Island. Local accounts warned that the light preceded fierce storms; one famous local legend described two fishermen whose punt was engulfed by the light, experiencing hurricane-force winds, flashing lightning, and a spinning boat that left one of the men with hair as white as snow.

#location
Ochre Pitt hill and Twillingate Harbour in Twillingate, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
The Mystery Lady and Moving Mop of Long Point Lighthouse

#summary
Long Point Lighthouse is famously associated with an autonomously moving tar mop and the spectral apparition of a lady in white who allegedly rescued a light keeper from a fatal fall.

#details
Constructed in 1875, the Long Point Lighthouse in Twillingate has accumulated distinct supernatural folklore over the decades. One prominent local curiosity involves an old tar mop kept near the bottom of the tower's staircase that inexplicably reverses its position overnight with no human intervention. A darker, earlier legend from the early 1900s tells of a light keeper who slipped while working on the clockwork weights and plunged twelve metres down the center of the tower toward the unforgiving brick floor. Just before impact, he was miraculously caught in mid-air by a woman dressed entirely in white, who vanished into thin air the moment he turned to thank her.

Long Point Lighthouse in Twillingate, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
The Scream at the Orange Lodge

#summary
A worker at the historic Orange Lodge in Twillingate captured eerie, unexplainable sounds on video inside a building long rumored to have once stored caskets and bodies before burial.

#details
In the summer of 2013, Brandon Cooper was working at the Orange Lodge in Twillingate when he began noticing strange, unexplainable sounds echoing from the empty building. Hoping to record whatever was making the noise, Cooper brought a camera inside to film. According to local historian Tony Horwood, the building has a dark history: approximately a century prior, the cold lodge was frequently used to temporarily store caskets and bodies before funerals, leading to local folklore that the spirits of the deceased remained behind long after the physical bodies were buried. Cooper's video documentation captured a sudden, chilling scream from within the empty hall.

#location
The Orange Lodge in Twillingate, Newfoundland and Labrador.
