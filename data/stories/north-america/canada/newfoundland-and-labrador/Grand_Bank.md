#title
The Hidden Horseshoes of College Street

#summary
During renovations of a historic nineteenth-century house in Grand Bank, workers discovered twelve iron horseshoes deliberately concealed within the wall framing around a front window. This hidden collection served as a traditional folk amulet intended to ward off witches, malicious spirits, and the devil.

#details
Constructed around 1890 on College Street in Grand Bank and owned during the early twentieth century by a man named White, a small peaked-gable house held a well-kept secret within its walls. When the front window was removed during later renovations, carpenters pulled twelve iron horseshoes out of the window frame where they had been nailed up and completely hidden from view. While a single horseshoe above a doorway is commonly recognized today as a good luck charm, historical folklore dictates that iron horseshoes—especially in mystical numbers—acted as protective amulets against witchcraft and malevolent forces, preventing any evil entity from crossing the threshold or entering the home.

#location
The house stands on College Street in Grand Bank, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
The Seven Bibles of Evans Street

#summary
When a historic house on Evans Street in Grand Bank underwent renovations, workers found exactly seven copies of the Holy Bible hidden inside the window frame alongside an iron hay fork. This unusual concealment reflected deep-seated regional superstitions and protective rituals against the supernatural.

#details
Built around 1906 on Evans Street in Grand Bank, another historic property revealed strange artifacts during remodeling work in the early 1990s. Tucked away around the inside of a window were seven Bibles, accompanied by a hay fork lodged between the walls. Although some suggested the books were used for insulation, the deliberate choice of exactly seven Bibles—the number seven being long associated in folklore with the supernatural and protective magic—points to a ritual meant to guard the household. Alongside traditional iron tools like hay forks or scissors used to ward off witches and the devil, these hidden items highlight the lingering fears and protective folk customs of turn-of-the-century Burin Peninsula residents.

#location
The house is located on Evans Street in Grand Bank, Newfoundland and Labrador.
