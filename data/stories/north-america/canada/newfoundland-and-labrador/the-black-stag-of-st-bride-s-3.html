    The Black Stag of St. Bride’s

    Summary

    In 1804, a local hunter encountered a mysterious, unkillable black stag on the road near St. Bride's, an eerie meeting that preceded a series of tragic and inexplicable deaths among his family.

    Details

    In the winter of 1804, Thomas Conway of St. Bride’s was traveling by horse and sled toward Point Lance when he encountered an impressive caribou stag with coal-black fur in a roadside clearing. Though an experienced and accurate shot, Thomas fired three times at close range, yet the animal remained completely unharmed and unconcerned. As Thomas lowered his gun, the stag locked eyes with him in an unnatural, soul-piercing gaze before darkness seemed to surge across the snow. Shaken to his core, Thomas rushed home, but later that night his dead body was found slumped over his sled with no marks or signs of struggle. Sometime afterward, Thomas's widow Emily and their infant son—accompanied by two men—traveled out to have the baby christened. They too perished on the road near the same clearing; their bodies were uninjured, and their footprints in the snow showed them frantically scattering in terror from an unseen presence.
