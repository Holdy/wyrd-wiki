#title
The Miraculous Spring of Father Duffy's Well

#summary
During a bitter 19th-century feud between the parish priest of St. Mary's and local merchants, a weary Father Duffy supposedly struck a barren rock with a branch during a long trek through the woods, causing a miraculous freshwater spring to emerge. The site remains a revered local landmark along the highway connecting St. Mary's to the interior.

#details
In the mid-1830s, Father James Duffy served as the fiery first resident parish priest of St. Mary's, where he engaged in a fierce conflict with magistrate and merchant John Martin over beachfront property and church construction. The dispute resulted in riots, criminal charges, and grueling journeys for Father Duffy, who was forced to walk over a hundred miles back and forth between St. Mary's and the Supreme Court in St. John's to face trial. During one such bitter winter trek, Father Duffy and his companions found themselves deep in the wilderness without a drop of water. Exhausted and parched, Father Duffy stepped away from the group, struck a large stone with a small tree branch, and scratched at the earth. Instantly, a steady stream of cool, clear water began to flow from the rock, providing fresh water for their kettles and sustaining them on their arduous journey. The spring became a famous regional legend, later commemorated as Father Duffy's Well.

#location
Father Duffy's Well, located along the Salmonier Line (Route 90) on the Avalon Peninsula leading toward St. Mary's, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
The Phantom Fur Store of St. Mary's Harbour

#summary
In the 1820s, a local merchant spread rumors and orchestrated staged paranormal activity in his trading store to deter thieves from stealing valuable furs. Strange noises, glowing lights, and phantom screams convinced superstitious locals that the building was heavily haunted.

#details
In 1828, young English naturalist Philip Gosse arrived in St. Mary's to work as a clerk for the trading firm Slade, Elson & Company. The establishment maintained a large storehouse specifically for storing valuable animal furs traded from trappers. Realizing that the local Irish Catholic population was deeply superstitious, the company manager, John Wills Martin, sought a clever way to protect the valuable pelts from theft without hiring armed guards around the clock. He intentionally spread rumors throughout the community that the premises were haunted by restless spirits. To give these terrifying stories credibility, Martin had a trusted servant named Ned O'Toole sneak into the building late at night. O'Toole would carry glowing lights and stomp heavily across the wooden floors, while unsettling screams echoed from inside the dark warehouse. The elaborate hoax succeeded brilliantly, keeping superstitious townspeople far away after dark and effectively using a fabricated ghost as an infallible watchman.

#location
The former Slade, Elson & Company fur storehouse located along the waterfront of St. Mary's Harbour in St. Mary's, Newfoundland and Labrador.
