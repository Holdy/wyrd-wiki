#title
Jacob and the Corpse Candle

#summary
A grieving father in St. Lawrence witnesses a mysterious floating flame drift from his home to the local cemetery, foretelling the tragic illness and passing of his young son.

#details
In St. Lawrence, Tom McGrath was mourning the loss of his father when he began hearing unexplained footsteps coming from his late father's abandoned upstairs room. One morning, he watched in astonishment as a floating, candle-like golden flame drifted down the stairs, out the door, and along the path toward the local churchyard and cemetery before fading away. Shortly afterward, his twelve-year-old son Jacob fell ill with a sudden, fatal fever, whispering as he passed away that his grandfather had come to guide him. The account draws upon traditional Newfoundland folklore regarding "corpse candles," spectral lights believed to act as omens of death and spirit guides for the dying.

#location
A saltbox house near the churchyard and cemetery in St. Lawrence, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
The Case of Matterface

#summary
An English clerk who died in St. Lawrence in 1835 was preserved in a barrel of rum that was later unknowingly raided by locals, and his ghost now haunts Turpin's Island where his remains were buried.

#details
Around 1835, an English clerk named Matterface working at Newman and Company's plantation in St. Lawrence passed away. Because embalming wasn't possible, his body was preserved inside a barrel of rum intended to be shipped back to England for burial. However, locals unaware of the barrel's true contents drank the tainted liquor. The barrel—with Matterface still inside—was eventually buried in the cove on Turpin's Island in Little St. Lawrence. Folklore claims that Matterface's ghost haunts the island; years later, cousins camping in a tent on the island heard mysterious footsteps circling them before their campfire was suddenly snuffed out.

#location
Turpin's Island in Little St. Lawrence, Newfoundland and Labrador.
