#title
The Ghost Ship of High Street

#summary
A gruff local resident frequently witnessed a fully rigged phantom ship sailing up the middle of High Street in Trinity.

#details
In a historical account compiled by local historian William White, residents recalled numerous local ghost stories from Trinity, Newfoundland. Among the most enduring was the tale of a man nicknamed "Crusty Harry," who was known for his gruff manner. He repeatedly claimed to witness a full-rigged sailing ship making its way up High Street, passing right by the dwelling house of the late Wm. Kelson.

#location
High Street, near the historic Wm. Kelson property in the town of Trinity, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
The Headless Apparition of Trinity

#summary
Residents of Trinity frequently reported encountering a headless man roaming the local streets.

#details
In William White's 1925 compilation of local folklore and ghost accounts from Trinity, several traditional phantoms were noted as part of the town's oral history. Among these was a spectral figure described as a headless man who was routinely sighted by multiple townspeople near the home of Paddy Murphy.

#location
Near Paddy Murphy's historic home in the town of Trinity, Newfoundland and Labrador.

#title
The Phantom Light of Trinity Narrows

#summary
Beginning in 1916, a brilliant and unexplained light regularly appeared off the narrows of Trinity Harbour, fooling the local lighthouse keeper into believing an unscheduled steamship had arrived.

#details
Documented extensively by local historian William White, a mysterious and blindingly bright light began appearing a few miles outside the narrows of Trinity Harbour around 1916. The apparition occurred with astonishing regularity between 9:00 p.m. and 11:00 p.m. On one occasion, the Fort Point Lighthouse keeper was so convinced that the glow belonged to the arriving steamship *S.S. Prospero* that he frantically rowed across the harbour to the public wharf, only to find empty water. During World War I, the recurring light caused widespread excitement and fear among residents who worried it might be a German submarine tampering with the Atlantic telegraph cables, while a local fisherman later reported the light vanishing right as he drew within a few hundred yards of it.

#location
Fort Point Lighthouse and the public wharf near the narrows of Trinity Harbour, Trinity, Newfoundland and Labrador.
