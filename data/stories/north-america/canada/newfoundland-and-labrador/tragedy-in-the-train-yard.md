#title
Tragedy in the Train Yard

#summary
In the summer of 1937, a fatal poisoning incident at the local asphalt plant and railway yard in Clarenville claimed the lives of several workers under mysterious and tragic circumstances.

#details
In the summer of 1937, the town of Clarenville was shaken when four men fell victim to a deadly poisoning after consuming "Colombian" spirit—a highly refined and odorless methylated spirit used in manufacturing at the local COLAS asphalt plant. Charles Lyons, the plant's chief engineer, and Patrick McHugh, the night watchman, were the first to succumb to the elixir. Shortly after, railway employee Michael Williams also died from the poison, while another worker, Edward Long, barely survived with paralysis and blindness after a delayed reaction. The chilling tragedy left the community haunted by the sudden loss of life and a thorough police investigation into the fatal substance.

#location
The COLAS asphalt plant and railway yard in Clarenville, Newfoundland and Labrador.
