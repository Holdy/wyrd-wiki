#title
The Fairies and Spirits of the Glen

#summary
The winding forest passage known as the Glen near Souris is steeped in generations of folklore concerning woodland fairies, phantom encounters, and uncanny local legends.

#details
Located just northeast of Souris, the scenic forest road and farming hamlet known locally as "the Glen" (Glencorradale) has long held a reputation in Island folklore as a place where the veil between the ordinary world and the supernatural thins. For generations, local children and travelers shared accounts of watching for mischievous fairies among the massive old-growth elms. The area's eerie reputation was further reinforced by figures such as Nellie MacPhee, a lifelong resident of the Glen renowned for her reputed fortunetelling abilities, cementing the wooded road as a site of lingering mystery.

#location
Glen Road (Route 303) in the Glen / Glencorradale area northeast of Souris, Prince Edward Island.

#upstream-source
Frank Harold MacArthur, Legends of Prince Edward Island (1966).

#title
The Flaming Ghost Ship of Souris

#summary
A mysterious, three-masted schooner engulfed in roaring flames has been sighted for over two centuries cruising the Northumberland Strait off the coast of Souris.

#details
For more than 200 years, mariners and coastal residents looking out over the Northumberland Strait near Souris and Wood Islands have reported harrowing sightings of a phantom sailing vessel wrapped in fire. Witnesses consistently describe a full-rigged schooner whose sails, rigging, and hull burn brightly without ever being consumed. Whenever local fishermen or onlookers attempt to row out and rescue any supposed crew members, the spectral ship abruptly fades into the mist or sinks beneath the dark waves.

#location
The coastal waters of the Northumberland Strait off the coast of Souris, Prince Edward Island.

#upstream-source
Roland H. Sherwood, The Phantom Ship of Northumberland Strait: And Other Mysteries of the Sea (1975).
