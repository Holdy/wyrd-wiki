#title
The Ghost of Old Rah at the Carroll Street House

#summary
An elderly Scottish resident who died in his driveway near Alberton became a local legend when his apparition was allegedly sighted in the upstairs bedroom of his former home.

#details
In the town of Alberton, Prince Edward Island, an elderly Scottish resident known by the nickname "Old Rah"—so called because of his frequent greeting, "It's very raw the day"—lived in an old house situated near the corner of Main and Carroll Streets. After he dropped dead in his driveway, the property eventually changed hands and was purchased by a new family. Local lore and tradition soon maintained that Old Rah's ghost haunted the upper floor, where his spectral figure was reportedly witnessed in an upstairs bedroom. Although some skeptics suggested the sighting was merely a whitened chimney flue, the family departed shortly after, cementing the tale in local memory and giving rise to decades of local ghost stories.

#location
The historic Gordon-Dunn-Holm House is located at the intersection of Main Street and Carroll Street in Alberton, Prince Edward Island.

Footprints in the Sands of Time: A History of Alberton, 1980 (pages 265-266)

#title
The Last-Runner Black Dog of Alberton

#summary
When an old house in Alberton was moved from the site where a mistreated boy had mysteriously disappeared, a phantom black dog suddenly bolted out from beneath the structure.

#details
According to local oral history and traditional folklore from the Alberton area, a severe resident named Mr. Brian had an adopted boy who vanished under suspicious circumstances, with neighbors strongly suspecting foul play and abuse. Years later, when the house standing on the property was hoisted onto wooden runners to be relocated, a mysterious black dog bolted out from the exact spot where the building had rested. According to local belief, the apparition served as a supernatural "last-runner" tied to the spirit of the mistreated and vanished boy, matching regional legends of haunting manifestations and unexplained lights shining across nearby fields.

#location
A historic homestead site formerly owned by Mr. Brian in the Alberton area of Prince Edward Island.

#upstream-source
Benevolent Irish Society Oral History Collection, interview with Margaret Kelly, recorded July 11, 1983 (UPEI Library).
