#title
The Curse of Mount Nyangani

#summary
Mount Nyangani is bound by a powerful curse that causes hikers and visitors who break traditional taboos to vanish without a trace into sudden, disorienting mists. Among its most famous victims were two daughters of a government minister who disappeared in 1981 despite massive multi-week searches.

#details
Standing as Zimbabwe's highest peak in the Eastern Highlands, Mount Nyangani is revered by the local VaManyika people as a sacred, sentient landscape governed by powerful ancestral guardians and water spirits. According to local lore, visitors who violate sacred protocols—such as wearing bright red clothing, making noise, pointing at sacred features, or showing disrespect—trigger a curse that blinds them and casts them into a spiritual dimension where they become permanently lost. Over the decades, numerous hikers, tourists, and officials have gone missing on its slopes. The mountain's chilling reputation was cemented in April 1981 when the two young daughters of former Finance Minister Dr. Tichaendepi Masaya disappeared into a sudden, blinding fog during a holiday hike. Despite extensive rescue operations involving hundreds of soldiers, trackers, and thermal imaging equipment, no footprints, clothing, or trace of the sisters was ever found, solidifying the mountain's legend as a place that swallows the living whole.

#location
Mount Nyangani is located within Nyanga National Park in the Eastern Highlands of Zimbabwe, rising to an elevation of 2,593 meters near the border with Mozambique.
