The Healers and Curses of Ukerewe

Summary

In the island communities of Lake Victoria, traditional healers use ritual ceremonies at sacred crossroads and hills to lift potent curses and hexes cast by local sorcerers. Victims of disputes or stolen property seek out these practitioners to cleanse evil spirits and restore spiritual balance.

Details

Across the 28 islands of the Ukerewe archipelago in Tanzania, witchcraft and curses remain deeply embedded in daily life, addressing everything from family disputes and financial ruin to mysterious illnesses. When individuals believe they have been targeted by a witch's hex, modern medicine is often bypassed in favor of local traditional healers, known as waganga. A prominent healer named Dina Charles explains that her calling began after she experienced a sudden mental breakdown, retreated to a hilltop for two weeks, and later discovered a sacred Bible beneath a historical cross commemorating 19th-century missionaries. To cure those afflicted by curses, healers perform nocturnal rituals where patients inhale medicinal fumes under a black muslin cloth while practitioners dance and chant. Afterward, the patient is led to a specific crossroads—a traditional site for warding off malevolent forces—where they are ritually washed to cleanse the hex and invoke benevolent ancestral spirits.
