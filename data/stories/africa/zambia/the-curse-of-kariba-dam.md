#title
The Curse of Kariba Dam

#summary
When the construction of the Kariba Dam forcibly displaced local Tonga people and threatened their sacred river god, a devastating curse brought unprecedented floods and destruction to the project. The wrath of Nyaminyami manifested in catastrophic floods, worker deaths, and lingering threats to the dam's structure.

#details
For centuries, the Tonga people lived peacefully along the banks of the Zambezi River, venerating the river god Nyaminyami, who resided in the Kariba Gorge. In the 1950s, colonial authorities initiated the construction of the massive Kariba Dam, ignoring local warnings and forcibly relocating thousands of villagers. Believing that the dam would permanently separate Nyaminyami from his wife and desecrate his domain, traditional healers and elders invoked a powerful curse against the project. In July 1957, a torrential storm caused a historic flood that breached the temporary dam. The very next rainy season brought an even more catastrophic flood—an event calculated to occur only once every thousand years—which destroyed the coffer dam, access bridge, and parts of the main wall, claiming hundreds of lives. Even after completion, the curse was said to manifest through choking floating weeds and persistent structural threats to the dam walls.

#location
Kariba Dam spans the Zambezi River gorge on the border between Zambia and Zimbabwe.
