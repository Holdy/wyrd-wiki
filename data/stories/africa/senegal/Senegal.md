#title
Drums in the Night: Amina and the Ancestral Spirits of Yoff

#summary
A Lebu woman named Amina suffers years of distress after being inhabited by ancestral spirits known as rabs. Through traditional Ndöp possession ceremonies in Yoff, she eventually learns to integrate and coexist with the possessing entity rather than expel it.

#details
In August 2014, medical anthropologist Angelo Miramonti traveled to Dakar, Senegal, and walked through the sandy alleys of the historic Lebu fishing village in Yoff. There, he witnessed an authentic *Ndöp* possession ceremony where villagers gathered in an enclosed courtyard to invoke ancestral spirits (*rabs*) through thunderous drumming, chanting, and trance-inducing dances. During the rituals, initiated women trembled, shook, and collapsed onto the sand as the spirits took hold of their bodies. Shortly after, Miramonti met Amina, a local woman whose life had been consumed by the presence of ancestral spirits of her lineage. Plagued by psychological turmoil and physical distress interpreted through the lens of animist belief, Amina sought healing outside Western psychiatry and turned to the Ndöp cult. Rather than performing a hostile exorcism, the traditional healers used communal drumming, animal sacrifice, and ritual alliance-building to help Amina tame and reconcile with the possessing spirit, restoring balance to her life.

#location
The enclosed sandy courtyard within the traditional Lebu fishing village in Yoff, Dakar, Senegal, where the Ndöp possession ceremonies take place.

#title
The Curse of the Senegambian Stones

#summary
Local villagers believe that the Earth Spirit guards the ancient megalithic stone circles, placing a curse of misfortune and illness upon anyone who dares to disturb or excavate the sacred monuments. Explorers and excavators who violated the circles suffered sudden house fires, debilitating hand injuries, and mysterious physical ailments as divine retribution.

#details
Deep in the Senegambian region, ancient megalithic monuments—such as the massive stone circles of Sine Ngayene—are revered as the sacred haunts of the Earth Spirit, Banko Sentane. According to traditional folklore, villagers must perform rituals before cultivating nearby fields to ensure the Spirit's neutrality and avoid crop failure. Anyone who desecrates, surveys, or excavates the ancient stone circles invites the wrath of the Spirit. Historical accounts document numerous eerie coincidences and misfortunes striking those who attempted to interfere with the stones: an archaeological commissioner who excavated a circle died shortly thereafter; local workers clearing grass for a survey watched in horror as a sudden gust of wind blew a spark from a hearth fire, destroying a third of their village; a landowner slated to assist with an excavation was suddenly struck by a severe thigh abscess while his brother fell bedridden; and researchers frequently suffered hand lacerations and debilitating finger blisters that hindered their work. These recurring calamities reinforced the local belief that the stone circles are protected by an active, punishing curse.

#location
The Sine Ngayene megalithic stone circle complex is located in central Senegal, approximately 100 kilometers east of Kaolack.
