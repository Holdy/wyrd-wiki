#title
The Curse of the Sacred Forest of Nyama

#summary
When a skeptical villager named Kofi cleared a sacred area belonging to the madebele bush spirits, a punishing curse fell upon the community until proper appeasement was made. 

#details
In the Senufo region of Côte d'Ivoire, the village of Nyama stood near a sacred patch of forest inhabited by the madebele—elusive bush spirits with backward feet. While the villagers traditionally left offerings to keep the peace, a young and ambitious man named Kofi dismissed these customs as mere superstition. Ignoring the warnings of village elders, he led a group of men to clear the sacred forest for agriculture. Once the sacred domain was disturbed, a heavy curse befell the village: tools vanished, paths shifted inexplicably, the local water source dried up, crops withered, and widespread illness took hold. Recognizing that their defiance of the spirits had brought about the disaster, the villagers consulted a diviner and performed rituals of atonement. Kofi, deeply remorseful, led the reconciliation ceremonies, after which the springs flowed once more, the crops recovered, and peace was restored to Nyama.

#location
The village of Nyama, located within the Senufo-inhabited savannah region of northern Côte d'Ivoire.
