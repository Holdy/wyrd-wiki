The Mourning Field Hailstorm

Summary

A strict traditional taboo in Lesotho mandates that no agricultural work may be performed in the fields the day immediately following a burial. Violating this mourning restriction triggers a delayed curse in the form of a devastating hailstorm that destroys the village's crops.

Details

According to traditional Basotho custom, the day immediately following a funeral is a sacred period of mourning and rest during which the community must abstain from all agricultural labor, such as ploughing, hoeing, or reaping. If any individual ignores this taboo and takes tools out to work the fields, a delayed curse is activated. Rather than striking the offender immediately, the transgression invokes a powerful retributive consequence: a fierce hailstorm descends upon the village, punishing the entire community by destroying their crops and livelihood.
