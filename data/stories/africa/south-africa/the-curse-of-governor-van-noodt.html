The Curse of Governor van Noodt

Summary

After unjustly condemning seven soldiers to death for desertion, Governor Pieter van Noodt was cursed from the gallows and died suddenly at his desk hours later, binding his spirit to the fortress in an enduring legend.

Details

In the early 18th century, Pieter Gijsbert van Noodt served as the tyrannical governor of the Dutch East India Company at the Castle of Good Hope. When seven soldiers were captured following an attempted desertion from the garrison, van Noodt coldly sentenced all of them to death by hanging. On the day of the executions, as the men were brought to the gallows, one of the condemned soldiers stared down the governor and pronounced a fierce curse upon him for his ruthless lack of mercy. Unmoved by the warning, van Noodt oversaw the hangings and retired to his office. Later that same day, when servants went to check on him, they found the governor dead at his desk with a look of profound shock and horror frozen on his face. Ever since, folklore maintains that his restless ghost stalks the ancient corridors of the castle, forever trapped under the weight of the soldier's curse.
