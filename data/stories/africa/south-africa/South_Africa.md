#title
The Curse of Governor van Noodt

#summary
After unjustly condemning seven soldiers to death for desertion, Governor Pieter van Noodt was cursed from the gallows and died suddenly at his desk hours later, binding his spirit to the fortress in an enduring legend.

#details
In the early 18th century, Pieter Gijsbert van Noodt served as the tyrannical governor of the Dutch East India Company at the Castle of Good Hope. When seven soldiers were captured following an attempted desertion from the garrison, van Noodt coldly sentenced all of them to death by hanging. On the day of the executions, as the men were brought to the gallows, one of the condemned soldiers stared down the governor and pronounced a fierce curse upon him for his ruthless lack of mercy. Unmoved by the warning, van Noodt oversaw the hangings and retired to his office. Later that same day, when servants went to check on him, they found the governor dead at his desk with a look of profound shock and horror frozen on his face. Ever since, folklore maintains that his restless ghost stalks the ancient corridors of the castle, forever trapped under the weight of the soldier's curse.

#location
The Castle of Good Hope is located at Darling Street and Buitenkant Street, Cape Town, South Africa.

#title
The Demonic Possession of Clara Germana Cele

#summary
In 1906, a sixteen-year-old South African schoolgirl reportedly became possessed by a demonic entity after making a pact with Satan, exhibiting supernatural strength, levitation, and multilingual abilities before undergoing a solemn exorcism.

#details
At St. Michael's Mission in Natal, South Africa, a teenage orphan named Clara Germana Cele allegedly entered into a pact with the devil. Following this, she began displaying terrifying symptoms of demonic possession: speaking languages she had never learned (such as French, German, and Polish), demonstrating clairvoyance regarding hidden sins, possessing immense physical strength that overwhelmed multiple attendants, and levitating up to five feet off the ground. She reacted with extreme violence and revulsion to holy objects, holy water, and relics of the True Cross, emitting inhuman animalistic roars. Two Catholic priests performed a solemn exorcism over a period of two days, during which the entity identified itself as Lucifer and resisted fiercely before finally being cast out amid a foul stench, restoring Clara to peace.

#location
St. Michael's Mission, located approximately twenty miles from the magistracy of Umzinto in KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa.

#title
The Uniondale Hitchhiker

#summary
A young woman who tragically died in a car crash on the N9 highway near Uniondale in 1968 is frequently reported hitchhiking along the road before inexplicably vanishing from moving vehicles.

#details
On Good Friday in 1968, 22-year-old Maria "Ria" Charlotte Roux died when the Volkswagen Beetle driven by her fiancé overturned on the N9 highway near the Barandas turnoff close to Uniondale. Five years later, in 1973, motorists began reporting encounters with a phantom female hitchhiker wearing dark clothing who would accept rides on the lonely stretch of road between Uniondale and Willowmore before disappearing after a few kilometers. Over the years, several drivers and motorcyclists—including police officers and servicemen—encountered the spirit, often experiencing sudden drops in temperature, hearing unearthly sounds, or feeling hands around their waist before she vanished. Witnesses who were later shown photographs of Maria positively identified her as the girl who had vanished from their vehicles.

#location
The N9 highway between Uniondale and Willowmore, near the Barandas turnoff in the Western Cape of South Africa.
