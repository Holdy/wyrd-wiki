#title
The Curse of the Ass-Head

#summary
Following their forced displacement by British settlers, indigenous Koya Temne leaders performed a nocturnal ceremony burying an ass head to curse the land of Freetown. This hex was believed to ensure that the territory would remain politically fractured and unsettled until historical injustices were resolved.

#details
During the expansion of early colonial settlements in Sierra Leone, indigenous populations like the Koya Temne experienced violent territorial displacement. After conflict erupted and British forces established fortifications on contested ground, the dispossessed Koya people retaliated through a fearsome traditional ceremony. They buried the head of an ass at Fort Thornton—the historical site where the modern State House now stands in Freetown—and cast a curse upon the land. According to oral history and local folklore, this hex tied the fate of the city's governance and land to perpetual political strife and societal division until truth and justice regarding the indigenous lands are brought to light.

#location
State House (formerly Fort Thornton) on Tower Hill in Freetown, Sierra Leone.

Mohamed Gibril Sesay (published in 2011).

#title
The Haunting of Wilkinson Road

#summary
Motorists driving along Wilkinson Road at night frequently encounter a sorrowful woman in white named Mama Lungi, who perished in a car crash while searching for her child. Drivers who see her reflection in their rearview mirror are granted safe passage, but those she approaches or knocks upon face misfortune.

#details
According to local legend, Mama Lungi is a restless spirit trapped along Wilkinson Road in Freetown since the 1970s after dying in a tragic car accident while looking for her missing child. At night, motorists traversing the winding road occasionally spot her walking slowly along the roadside in a flowing white gown, her expression filled with sorrow. It is said that drivers who spot her in their rearview mirror receive protection for their journey, but anyone who tries to approach or help her finds she vanishes instantly, and if she knocks on their vehicle's window, great misfortune follows.

#location
The haunting takes place along Wilkinson Road in Freetown, Sierra Leone.

#title
The Ogbanje of Banana Island

#summary
During a turbulent boat journey to Banana Island, a young Sierra Leonean man suffered violent physical seizures as spirit entities attempted to take over his body and drag him back to the spirit world.

#details
Amadu, a young man residing in Freetown, Sierra Leone, became the center of a terrifying occult ordeal after attempting to break his covenant as an ogbanje—a spirit entity inhabiting a human host. When he refused to leave his human family and return to the spirit realm, his spirit siblings initiated vicious metaphysical attacks to sever his connection to his physical body. While crossing the waters toward Banana Island, Amadu suddenly began shaking uncontrollably, losing consciousness, and crawling under benches as unseen forces violently wrenched his body. Upon docking at Banana Island, terrified locals initially blocked their entry before allowing Amadu to be carried into a local hut for urgent healing rituals. Over subsequent months of intense seizures, hallucinations, and dangerous compulsions—such as being pulled toward drowning in the ocean—traditional healers, mullahs, and priests were consulted to combat the possessing entities and help him survive.

#location
A local hut on Banana Island, Sierra Leone.
