#title
The Curse of the Ass-Head

#summary
Following their forced displacement by British settlers, indigenous Koya Temne leaders performed a nocturnal ceremony burying an ass head to curse the land of Freetown. This hex was believed to ensure that the territory would remain politically fractured and unsettled until historical injustices were resolved.

#details
During the expansion of early colonial settlements in Sierra Leone, indigenous populations like the Koya Temne experienced violent territorial displacement. After conflict erupted and British forces established fortifications on contested ground, the dispossessed Koya people retaliated through a fearsome traditional ceremony. They buried the head of an ass at Fort Thornton—the historical site where the modern State House now stands in Freetown—and cast a curse upon the land. According to oral history and local folklore, this hex tied the fate of the city's governance and land to perpetual political strife and societal division until truth and justice regarding the indigenous lands are brought to light.

#location
State House (formerly Fort Thornton) on Tower Hill in Freetown, Sierra Leone.
