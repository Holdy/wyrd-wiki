#title
The Curse of Jinack Island

#summary
According to regional folklore, the remote island of Jinack is protected by an ancestral curse that brings sudden misfortune or supernatural interference upon any outside authorities or law enforcement personnel attempting to exert control over it. For generations, this lingering curse kept officials at bay, turning the island into an isolated sanctuary until modern maritime tragedies eventually forced locals to break the taboo themselves.

#details
Jinack Island, located at the mouth of the River Gambia, has long been shrouded in a reputation of untouchability. Local oral tradition maintains that the land is bound by an ancestral curse designed to repel foreign or government intervention. For decades, whenever outside authorities attempted to establish regular administrative control or raid the island, the curse would supposedly manifest to turn them away—most famously culminating in an incident where a regional anti-drug enforcement raid was abruptly thwarted by a sudden, aggressive swarm of bees, leading to the dismissal of the officials involved. This enduring legend created a supernatural no-fly zone that deterred routine policing, allowing the island to function largely under its own isolated rules. However, in recent years, the defensive isolation protected by the legend collided with the grim realities of modern irregular migration, ultimately prompting villagers to override the ancient taboo to address tragedies at sea.

#location
Jinack Island is situated off the coast of the North Bank Region of The Gambia, precisely where the River Gambia meets the Atlantic Ocean.
