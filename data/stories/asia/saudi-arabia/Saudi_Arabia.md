#title
The Cursed Tombs of Hegra

#summary
Ancient Nabataean rock-cut tombs in the desert of AlUla bear carved inscriptions invoking divine curses upon anyone who dares to disturb, open, or deface them. 

#details
In the ancient caravan city of Hegra (also known as Mada'in Saleh) in northwestern Saudi Arabia, the Nabataeans carved magnificent monumental tombs into sandstone cliffs between the 1st century B.C. and 1st century A.D. To protect these eternal resting places from grave robbers, vandals, and usurpers, the builders engraved legal and divine warnings directly into the stone facades above the entryways. Most famously, Tomb 41 features a chilling inscription that proclaims: "May the lord of the world curse upon anyone who disturb this tomb or open it... and further curse upon whoever may change the scripts on top of the tomb." For centuries, these inscribed curses and local legends regarding the site instilled deep fear in travelers and locals, keeping the ruins largely isolated and untouched.

#location
Tomb 41 is located within the ancient archaeological site of Hegra (Mada'in Saleh) in the AlUla region of northwestern Saudi Arabia.
