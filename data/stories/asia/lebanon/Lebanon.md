#title
The Curse of King Ahiram's Sarcophagus

#summary
An ancient Phoenician king left a dire curse carved upon his royal tomb to punish any ruler who dared to disturb his resting place by stripping away their judicial scepter and overturning their throne. The curse also forewarned that peace would flee from the city of Byblos if the sanctuary was ever uncovered.

#details
Commissioned by his son Ittobaal around 1000 BC, the limestone sarcophagus of King Ahiram was placed deep within the royal necropolis of the ancient Phoenician city of Byblos. To protect his mortal remains from desecration by future rulers, commanders, or rival governors, a powerful protective warning was engraved along the rim and lid using some of the earliest fully developed Phoenician alphabetic characters. The inscription declared that if anyone were to uncover or tamper with the coffin, their judicial scepter would be stripped away, their royal throne overthrown, and peace and tranquility permanently banished from Byblos. Despite this foreboding curse, ancient tomb robbers eventually breached the burial chamber, though the remarkable carved artifact survived for modern discovery following a rain-triggered collapse of the hillside.

#location
The royal necropolis site where the tomb was discovered is located in the ancient coastal city of Byblos (Jbeil), Lebanon, with the sarcophagus itself presently housed in the National Museum of Beirut.

#title
The Possession at the Sabath of Tyre

#summary
A local man was violently attacked and possessed by a female jinni after encountering her at a historic stone archway in Tyre. He suffered eight days of torment before a visiting spiritual healer successfully exorcised the entity.

#details
Hassan Shour, known as Abu Khalil, approached the Sabath—a crumbling historic stone archway located in the old quarters of Tyre—when he was suddenly blocked by a female jinni. She demanded that he carry her to the sea. When Hassan refused and attempted to push her away, she attacked him and took complete control of his body. For eight days, Hassan remained possessed and trapped in severe torment until a spiritual healer from the village of Jouya traveled to assist him, performing rituals that successfully freed him from the spirit's grasp.

#location
The key location of the incident is the Sabath, a crumbling stone archway situated in the old quarters of the coastal city of Tyre, Lebanon.
