#title
The Jinn Possession and Exorcism in Zarqa

#summary
A Jordanian businessman plagued by a female jinn named Johyna underwent recurring exorcism rituals in Zarqa to subdue the entity.

#details
A local businessman in Zarqa, Jordan, became afflicted by spirit possession when a female jinn named Johyna took residence inside his body. Driven by jealousy, the spirit caused domestic conflict with his wife and disrupted his business finances, even leading his partners to force a transfer of family land over unaccounted funds. Unable to afford the thousands of dollars required for rare incense to permanently eradicate the entity, the man had to endure recurring monthly exorcisms performed by local exorcist Mohammed al-Yafawi, who used Quranic verses and physical restraint to weaken and temporarily banish the jinn.

#location
A residence in the city of Zarqa, located just northeast of Amman, Jordan.

#title
The Pharaonic Curse of the Petra Treasury

#summary
According to Bedouin folklore, an Egyptian pharaoh placed a powerful curse upon the Treasury at Petra to protect his magical sanctuary and hidden riches from looters. Anyone who attempts to plunder the monument faces the wrath of this enduring enchantment.

#details
In Bedouin legend, the iconic rock-carved monument known as Al-Khazneh (the Treasury) in the ancient city of Petra was constructed through the magic of an Egyptian pharaoh and his remaining troops who survived the closing of the Red Sea during their pursuit of Moses. Seeking a secure place for their vast wealth before continuing their hunt, they used magic to carve the grand structure into the rose-red sandstone cliffs. To deter any future adventurers or thieves, the pharaoh sealed the monument with a potent curse. Generations of treasure seekers have attempted to break open the stone urn at the top of the facade—evidenced by historical bullet holes left on the stone—failing to breach the magic and risking the vengeful wrath of the curse.

#location
Al-Khazneh (The Treasury) is located at the termination of the Siq gorge within the archaeological city of Petra, Jordan.
