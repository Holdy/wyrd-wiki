#title
Lorenzo Dow's Georgia Curse

#summary
After being abused and nearly lynched by the lawless townspeople of Jacksonboro, an eccentric traveling preacher placed a curse upon the settlement, causing it to wither away while sparing only the home of the kind citizen who had sheltered him. True to the warning, a series of fires, storms, and economic ruin eventually erased the town from the map, leaving only a lone surviving house.

#details
In the early 1800s, the frontier town of Jacksonboro, Georgia, was a rough, lawless settlement driven by lumbering and heavy drinking. When an eccentric, fire-and-brimstone traveling evangelist named Lorenzo Dow arrived to preach against the sins of alcohol, he was pelted with rotten vegetables and mocked by a drunken crowd. Undeterred, Dow smashed open a saloon whiskey barrel, prompting a furious mob to drag him outside with talks of hanging. A pious local resident named Seaborn Goodall intervened, offering Dow shelter for the night under the promise that he would leave by morning. As Dow departed at daybreak, he shook the dust of the town from his shoes, declaring that Jacksonboro's wickedness had brought down a divine curse that would cause the settlement to wither and die, though he explicitly exempted the Goodall house for its hospitality. Over the ensuing years, unexplainable fires, fierce storms, and economic decline devastated Jacksonboro, causing residents to abandon the area until the town entirely vanished into a ghost town, leaving the historic Goodall house standing alone as the sole survivor.

#location
The historic Seaborn Goodall House and the ghost town site of Jacksonboro are located at the end of Dell Goodall Road, approximately 5 miles northwest of Sylvania in Screven County, Georgia.
