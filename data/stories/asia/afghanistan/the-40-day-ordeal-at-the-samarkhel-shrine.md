#title
The 40-Day Ordeal at the Samarkhel Shrine

#summary
Families across Afghanistan bring afflicted relatives to a remote shrine graveyard believing they are possessed by evil spirits or jinns. The patients are chained to trees for forty days under harsh conditions in hopes of a supernatural cure.

#details
At the Mia Ali Sahib shrine located within a dusty graveyard in Samarkhel village, individuals suffering from severe psychological distress—frequently attributed by locals to spirit or jinn possession—undergo a grueling forty-day confinement. Bound by heavy iron chains to trees and surrounded by simple headstones, the afflicted are fed only dry bread, salt, pepper, and water. Managed by descendants of the local saint buried at the shrine, families pay a daily fee in the belief that the saint's supernatural powers will exorcise the malevolent entities and cure the patients. Despite modern medical interventions and the abolition of official institutional shackle practices in the country, hundreds of people continue to be brought to this remote site annually to endure the ritual restraint.

#location
The Mia Ali Sahib shrine is located in the graveyard of Samarkhel village in Afghanistan's eastern Nangarhar province.
