#title
The Curse of Tun Jana Khatib and the Swordfish of Singapore

#summary
An unjustly executed holy man curses the tyrannical ruler of ancient Singapore, unleashing a devastating invasion of swordfish that is temporarily thwarted by a brilliant young boy. Fearing the child's genius, the paranoid ruler orders his execution as well, sealing the doom of the kingdom.

#details
Centuries ago in ancient Singapore (Singapura), a pious missionary named Tun Jana Khatib visited the island and accidentally split a betel palm tree near the royal palace simply by touching it. Mistakenly believing the holy man was displaying dark magic to the queen, the tyrannical ruler Paduka Sri Maharaja ordered his immediate execution at Hujung Pasar (modern-day Kampung Gelam). Before being stabbed to death, Tun Jana Khatib laid a curse on the king and the land, vowing that a cruel ruler would not escape punishment and that a calamity would befall the kingdom. Shortly after, vicious shoals of swordfish swarmed the shores of Singapore, piercing the necks and waists of fishermen and citizens. While the foolish king attempted to form a human shield with his subjects' thighs—causing massive casualties—a seven-year-old boy named Hang Nadim cleverly advised lining the coast with banana tree stems, which trapped the swordfish snouts and saved the populace (naming the area Tanjung Pagar, or "Cape of Stakes"). Terrified that such a brilliant child would eventually usurp the throne, the paranoid ruler had young Hang Nadim executed as well.

#location
Tanjung Pagar (historically associated with Hujung Pasar and Kampung Gelam) is a historic district in central Singapore where the swordfish attack and the execution site of the cursed holy man are located.

#upstream-source
*Sejarah Melayu* (*The Malay Annals*) by Tun Seri Lanang (17th century).

#title
The Novena Church Exorcism Trial

#summary
A woman brought to a Singapore church by her family under the belief that she was possessed by evil spirits subsequently sued the priests and helpers who performed an exorcism rite upon her. Her claims were ultimately dismissed following a high-profile High Court trial.

#details
On August 10, 2004, Amutha Valli Krishnan was brought to church by family members who believed she was possessed by malevolent spirits. Witnesses reported that she crept on the ground like a snake and marched into a private room where Redemptorist priests Simon Tan and Jacob Ong, along with six lay helpers, prayed over her and performed an exorcism rite. Valli later filed a civil lawsuit in the High Court seeking damages, claiming she was forcibly restrained, assaulted, and subjected to a traumatic exorcism without her consent. Following a 32-day trial spanning across 2007 and 2008, Justice Lee Seiu Kin dismissed the lawsuit in February 2009, ruling in favor of the priests and accepting the defense's consistent accounts of the possession and prayer session.

#location
The events took place at the Church of St. Alphonsus, popularly known as Novena Church, located at 300 Thomson Road in Singapore.

#title
The Phantom Passenger of Devil's Bend

#summary
Taxi drivers navigating the winding curves of Old Upper Thomson Road at night have reported picking up a mysterious woman in white who pays with banknotes that turn into hell notes.

#details
Old Upper Thomson Road, once utilized as a racing circuit for the Singapore Grand Prix, contains a notorious sharp V-turn known as Devil's Bend that has claimed numerous lives over the decades. Local taxi drivers working the night shift have shared chilling encounters of picking up a lone female passenger dressed in white along this dark stretch of road. After completing the journey and dropping her off, the drivers discover that the regular cash she handed them for payment has mysteriously transformed into traditional joss paper used for the deceased.

#location
Devil's Bend is a sharp V-turn located along Old Upper Thomson Road in Singapore.
