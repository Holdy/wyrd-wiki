#title
The Jinn Possession at Makunudhoo School

#summary
Students at a remote Maldivian island school were struck by mysterious faintings and violent possessions attributed to a jinn residing in a schoolyard tree.

#details
A decade ago at Makunudhoo School in the Maldives, an alarming series of events began when several students suddenly fainted without explanation. Over the following month, the episodes escalated as some children were seized by a strange, violent force, thrashing with such inhuman strength that groups of four or five men struggled to restrain them. Whispers quickly spread across the island regarding jinn possession and supernatural attacks, with locals pointing blame at a large rain tree (*reethigas*) growing on the school grounds that was believed to be infested with spirits. As school sessions were suspended, both modern psychiatrists and traditional exorcists (*fanditha verin*) were brought to the remote island, and the controversial rain tree was unceremoniously chopped down. While psychiatrists diagnosed nineteen of the children with mass hysteria born of shared terror, four students were considered genuinely afflicted by the spirits, requiring traditional exorcists to trap the jinn in a bottle and sink it into the ocean.

#location
Makunudhoo School, situated on the island of Makunudhoo in the Haa Dhaalu Atoll of the Maldives.

#title
The Origin of the Coconut

#summary
An ancient black magic curse left the early inhabitants dead and the Maldivian islands strewn with hollowed-out human skulls. A wandering sorcerer lifted the curse by transforming the skulls into coconut palms, establishing the lifeblood of the island nation.

#details
According to Maldivian folklore, the very first inhabitants of the islands were all wiped out in a catastrophic stroke of black magic and disease, leaving the land cursed and littered with thousands of empty human skulls. For a long time, the archipelago remained desolate and under this dark hex. Eventually, a young and exceptionally powerful sorcerer traveling through the region took pity upon the afflicted islands. He cast a powerful spell that converted the thousands of hollowed-out human skulls into coconuts and coconut palms, successfully breaking the curse and ensuring sustenance and life for all subsequent generations of settlers. Because of this miraculous origin, coconuts are considered deeply auspicious in Maldivian culture and remain an essential element in weddings and traditional ceremonies.

#location
The islands of the Maldives archipelago in the Indian Ocean.

Hasan Ahmed Manik, Maldivian Myths.

#title
The Spectral Passengers of the Sinamale' Bridge

#summary
Taxi drivers and motorists crossing a major maritime bridge in the Maldives late at night have reported unsettling encounters with spectral entities and unseen hitchhikers. These modern ghostly legends intertwine state-of-the-art infrastructure with traditional island folklore.

#details
After the construction of the Sinamale' Bridge connecting Malé to the surrounding islands, drivers traversing the multi-kilometer asphalt span after dark began experiencing bizarre supernatural phenomena. Taxi drivers and moped riders frequently reported feeling invisible or phantom passengers hitchhiking on the backs of their vehicles, while others witnessed shadowy figures appearing unexpectedly along the roadway. These haunting accounts reflect how traditional Maldivian beliefs surrounding spirits and jinn adapted to modern engineering and transit routes.

#location
The Sinamale' Bridge spans the waters of the Indian Ocean, connecting the capital city of Malé to the islands of Hulhulé and Hulhumalé in the Maldives.
