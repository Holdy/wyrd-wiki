#title
The Clal Center Urban Legend

#summary
The Clal Center in Jerusalem became burdened by an enduring urban legend that it was cursed after a popular myth claimed a rival was buried in its wet concrete foundations. Decades of structural failures, confusing floor plans, and economic decline seemed to fulfill the curse, though the site remains a vibrant hub for resilience.

#details
Conceived in the 1970s as a futuristic, gleaming shopping and commercial paradise on a prominent plot in central Jerusalem, the Clal Center was plagued by misfortune almost from the start. Its avant-garde architectural design featuring a labyrinth of half-floors and disjointed levels proved to be a baffling navigational nightmare for shoppers and tenants alike. Compounding its structural quirks, a popular urban legend emerged claiming that a mafia-style murder victim had been buried deep within the building's wet concrete foundations during construction. As the years went on, the center suffered a series of heavy blows, including declining retail fortunes, tragedies, and a reputation for being cursed. Despite its gloomy atmosphere, parts of the building have been creatively revitalized by urban pioneers, including a thriving rooftop garden and community project.

#location
The Clal Center is located at a prominent multi-street plot between Jaffa Road and Agrippas Street in downtown Jerusalem, Israel.

#title
The Fróðá Wonders

#summary
Following the death of a traveler who left forbidden bed linen at an isolated farm, a terrifying wave of fatal hauntings and a supernaturally animated seal terrorized the household. The malevolent entities were finally banished when a priest conducted formal door-doom exorcisms and burned the cursed textiles.

#details
Around 1000 AD, a woman named Þórgunna arrived at the Fróðá farm on the Snæfellsnes peninsula carrying valuable brocade bed linen. Before dying of a sudden fever, she explicitly requested that her bed linens be burned, warning of terrible consequences if her wish was ignored. The housewife, Þuríður, ignored the warning and kept the textiles. Soon after, the shepherd died and returned as a hostile ghost, followed by a series of mysterious deaths among the household staff, who also rose as undead ghouls alongside drowned crewmen. During the winter hauntings, a seal surfaced through the home's hearth, becoming unnaturally invulnerable and aggressive until it was beaten to death with a sledgehammer. Finally, Chieftain Snorri goði sent a priest who burned the cursed linens, conducted door-doom legal trials to sentence the spirits, and successfully banished the entities from the farm.

#location
Fróðá farm is located near the Fróðárheiði heath on Road 54 along the Snæfellsnes peninsula in western Iceland.
