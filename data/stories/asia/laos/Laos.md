#title
The Curse of Sri Khottabong

#summary
A powerful mythical hero cursed the kingdom of Vientiane after being betrayed and murdered in a palace latrine, dooming the realm to perpetual decline.

#details
According to local borderland folklore, a powerful lord named Sri Khottabong came to the aid of the King of Vientiane to rid the land of a destructive herd of elephants and was rewarded with the hand of the king's daughter in marriage. Fearing the hero's immense magical abilities and invulnerability, the king conspired with his daughter to discover Sri Khottabong's only vulnerable spot—his anus. Enticed to use a specially designed latrine pit in the royal palace, Sri Khottabong was pierced from below by a sharp stake. As he writhed in agony, he placed a powerful curse upon the Vientiane kingdom and its ruler, declaring that the realm would never truly flourish, or would only do so fleetingly like an elephant folding its ears or a snake sticking out its tongue.

#location
The royal palace site of ancient Vientiane, Laos, along the Mekong River basin where the historical events and folklore are rooted.

#title
The Exorcism and Curing of Nakasang's Possessed Hosts

#summary
In the riverside village of Nakasang in southern Laos, individuals believed to be possessed by ravenous *phi pop* spirits are banished from their homes to undergo rigorous biannual exorcism and water-cleansing rituals. These ceremonies serve to purge malevolent entities and restore community harmony.

#details
Rooted in ethnic Lao folklore, individuals accused of hosting malevolent, organ-devouring *phi pop* spirits are exiled from their home communities and sent to Nakasang Village in southern Laos for cultural healing and spiritual treatment. Upon arriving, the afflicted must formally acknowledge their possession and endure a mandatory three-year rehabilitation program. During biannual ceremonies at the village's sacred *ta ho* spirit house, spirit mediums channel ancestral entities while sanctified herbal water is poured through the floorboards onto the crouching hosts below. Following this, the possessed individuals must dash through the muddy *nong cheut* pond and swim downstream in the Mekong River to completely expel the possessing spirit.

#location
Nakasang Village, Khong District, Champasak Province, southern Laos, specifically centered around the village's *ta ho* spirit house and *nong cheut* ritual pond adjacent to the Mekong River.

#upstream-source
Ian G. Baird, "Where Do the Ravenous Spirits (Phi Pop) Go? Nakasang Village in Southern Laos as a Place of Cultural Healing", *Southeast Asian Studies*, Vol. 13, No. 1 (2024).

#title
The Nightly Cry of Mueng Kao

#summary
Following her sudden illness and cremation, a young girl named Thong returns as a ghost, haunting the road next to her former village home. Her chilling cries and the accompanying chorus of howling dogs terrorize the residents of Mueng Kao throughout the night.

#details
While living in Mueng Kao, a young girl named Thong fell severely ill and passed away after three days. Her body was cremated at the local temple, Wat Sone Suk Sith. That very night, the entire town heard Thong's distinctive, haunting cries traveling along the road next to the author's childhood home—a path well-known for ghost traffic because it led directly to the village cemetery. Accompanied by a chorus of howling stray dogs, Thong's spirit walked back and forth along the road, unable to enter her family home due to sacred protective white rope blessed by monks. Neighbors who encountered her apparition were paralyzed with fear, and the haunting continued repeatedly until dawn.

#location
The events take place along the road adjacent to Wat Sone Suk Sith in Mueng Kao, Laos, which leads directly to the local cemetery.
