#title
The Counting Stones of San-Tash

#summary
To account for his enormous invading army, the conqueror Tamerlane commanded every soldier to deposit a single stone upon arrival, and later instructed that all survivors remove theirs, leaving behind a permanent monument of stone mounds representing those lost in battle.

#details
In the northeastern valley of Kyrgyzstan near the border, the military commander Tamerlane (Timur) prepared to march his massive army into foreign campaigns. Needing an accurate account of his troop numbers, he ordered every single soldier in his vast host to pick up a stone and place it in a designated spot before proceeding. When the army eventually returned from their grueling campaigns, Tamerlane required every surviving soldier to pick up one of the stones they had previously left and take it away with them. The stones that remained behind in the heaps could not be reclaimed; they formed massive, permanent burial mounds—most notably the monumental San-Tash mound, standing four meters high and fifty-six meters wide—which silently recorded the exact toll of the soldiers who had perished and never returned from the war.

#location
The San-Tash burial mound complex is located in the Kakyra Valley near the Tyup River in the Issyk-Kul Region of northeastern Kyrgyzstan.

#title
The Encounter with the Albarsty

#summary
In the village of Gaur, a local resident named Khaireddin Khashimbekov narrowly escaped a malevolent demonic shapeshifter by striking it with his riding whip. The folkloric entity, known as an albarsty, had approached him disguised as a family member with backward-facing feet.

#details
According to early 20th-century ethnographic records of Kyrgyz folklore, a man named Khaireddin Khashimbekov was riding a horse through the village of Gaur in the Burana Region when he encountered a terrifying supernatural entity. A figure resembling his aunt approached him, but Khaireddin noticed immediate signs of the demonic albarsty: her feet were turned entirely backward and her facial features were incomplete. Having previously heard of these malevolent shapeshifters that prey on humans, he quickly straightened up and swung his riding whip at her, upon which the albarsty instantly vanished into thin air. Local accounts from the surrounding district similarly describe albarstys infiltrating homes at night to oppress sleeping victims by pressing heavily on their chests or stealing souls during vulnerable moments.

#location
The story took place in the village of Gaur, situated in the Burana Region near the historic Burana Tower archaeological complex in the Chuy Region of Kyrgyzstan.
