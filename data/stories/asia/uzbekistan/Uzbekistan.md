#title
Curse of Timur

#summary
The disturbance of the tomb of the conqueror Timur in Samarkand is said to have unleashed a devastating invasion and catastrophic war upon the Soviet Union.

#details
In June 1941, Soviet anthropologists led by Mikhail Gerasimov set out to open the tomb of the Turco-Mongol conqueror Timur (Tamerlane) at the Gur-e-Amir mausoleum in Samarkand, ignoring warnings from local elders who presented a 19th-century manuscript detailing a dark curse against disturbing the grave. When the coffin was opened on June 20, 1941, it allegedly emitted a foul odor, caused unexplained technical malfunctions, and was associated with a legendary inscription warning that any invader who opened the tomb would unleash a disaster worse than him. Just two days later, on June 22, 1941, Nazi Germany launched Operation Barbarossa against the Soviet Union. The devastating war continued until November 1942, when Stalin ordered Timur's remains to be returned and reburied with Islamic rites, shortly after which the Red Army achieved its turning-point victory at the Battle of Stalingrad.

#location
The Gur-e-Amir mausoleum is located in Samarkand, Uzbekistan.
