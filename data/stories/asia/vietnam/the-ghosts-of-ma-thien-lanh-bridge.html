The Ghosts of Ma Thiên Lãnh Bridge

Summary

Built through the forced labor of hundreds of prisoners who died during its construction, the unfinished Ma Thiên Lãnh Bridge in Côn Đảo is haunted by the lingering spirits of those who perished. Villagers frequently report sighting various apparitions around the jungle span, including a man in a white shirt, a woman in a white dress, and young boys.

Details

During the French colonial era in 1930, prisoners from the notorious Côn Đảo Prison were forced to carry heavy rocks through rugged jungle terrain to build Ma Thiên Lãnh Bridge to facilitate infrastructure transport toward Ong Dung Beach. Out of the prisoners assigned to the arduous project, approximately 356 lost their lives due to starvation, poisonous drinking water, brutal abuse, and harsh climate conditions before construction was ultimately abandoned in August 1945. Today, locals recount chilling paranormal encounters near the crumbling structure, such as a man in a white shirt and black trousers watching travelers from a distance before abruptly vanishing, a woman in a white dress standing upon the bridge at dawn, and the spirits of two shirtless boys demanding dessert.
