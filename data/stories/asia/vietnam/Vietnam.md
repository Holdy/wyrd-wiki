#title
The Ancestral Trance of Panduranga

#summary
During an annual spring ritual at a family home in Panduranga, a designated young medium is overtaken and possessed by ancestral spirits to judge and guide living descendants.

#details
In the ancestral homelands of Panduranga, modern descendants of the ancient Cham civilization preserve their living heritage through sacred spring rituals held shortly after the Cham New Year. During this ceremony, when the boundary between the living and the dead is believed to grow thin, a young female family member chosen as the household's designated spirit medium kneels before a candlelit altar. As traditional *baranưng* and *ginăng* drums beat a hypnotic rhythm accompanied by piercing *saranai* music, the medium enters a deep trance and is completely possessed by ancestral entities. Brandishing blue hand fans, she leaps and spins in an animated dance, summoning family members forward to prostrate themselves, receive spiritual judgment, and pay proper homage to their lineage.

#location
The ceremony takes place at a family home located just outside the city of Phan Rang-Tháp Chàm in Ninh Thuận Province, Vietnam.

*Heritage Conservation and Tourism Development at Cham Sacred Sites in Vietnam: Living Heritage Has a Heart* by Quảng Đại Tuyên (Isvan).

#title
The Ghosts of Ma Thiên Lãnh Bridge

#summary
Built through the forced labor of hundreds of prisoners who died during its construction, the unfinished Ma Thiên Lãnh Bridge in Côn Đảo is haunted by the lingering spirits of those who perished. Villagers frequently report sighting various apparitions around the jungle span, including a man in a white shirt, a woman in a white dress, and young boys.

#details
During the French colonial era in 1930, prisoners from the notorious Côn Đảo Prison were forced to carry heavy rocks through rugged jungle terrain to build Ma Thiên Lãnh Bridge to facilitate infrastructure transport toward Ong Dung Beach. Out of the prisoners assigned to the arduous project, approximately 356 lost their lives due to starvation, poisonous drinking water, brutal abuse, and harsh climate conditions before construction was ultimately abandoned in August 1945. Today, locals recount chilling paranormal encounters near the crumbling structure, such as a man in a white shirt and black trousers watching travelers from a distance before abruptly vanishing, a woman in a white dress standing upon the bridge at dawn, and the spirits of two shirtless boys demanding dessert.

#location
Ma Thiên Lãnh Bridge is located deep in the jungle of Côn Đảo National Park on Côn Sơn Island in Ba Ria - Vung Tau Province, Vietnam.

#title
The Love Curse of Thien Mu Pagoda

#summary
Unrequited sorrow from a historical tragedy birthed a supernatural curse claiming that unmarried couples who visit the ancient pagoda together will inevitably suffer a separation. 

#details
Centuries ago, a young couple deeply in love faced insurmountable opposition from their families due to differing social classes. Overcome by despair, they journeyed to the Perfume River near Ha Khe Hill to make a final pact, but only the young man perished while his lover survived and was forced into another marriage. Imbued with the lingering grief and resentment of this broken romance, a curse settled upon the site, dictating that any unmarried couple entering the sacred grounds will face relationship discord and eventual breakup.

#location
Thien Mu Pagoda is situated on Ha Khe Hill, overlooking the Perfume River approximately five kilometers west of the center of Hue, Vietnam.
