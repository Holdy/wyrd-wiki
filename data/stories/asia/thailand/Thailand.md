#title
The Curse of King Ue-Lue

#summary
A grieving and vengeful serpent king cursed a mortal ruler to transform into stone and remain bound to a mountain cave for eternity after a bitter deception.

#details
In the ancient kingdom of Rattappa Nakhon, Prince Fahoong fell in love with a mysterious woman named Nakkarintrani, who eventually revealed herself to be a Naka serpent in human form. When her true nature was exposed to the royal court, King Ue-Lue banished her in anger, prompting her father—the Naka King—to wage war, submerge the kingdom beneath the waters of what is now Khong Long Lake, and place a fierce curse upon King Ue-Lue. The monarch was transformed into a giant stone serpent imprisoned forever in the mountains, where his scaled rock formation can still be seen today at Naka Cave.

#location
Naka Cave is located within Phu Langka National Park in Bueng Kan Province, northeastern Thailand.

#title
The Ghost of Miss Wan on Chak Phra Road

#summary
A narrow road in Bangkok's Thonburi district is haunted by the spirit of a pregnant woman who was murdered by her husband. Her terrifying apparition frequently manifests to motorists near the Wat Taling Chan temple.

#details
Dating back to the late 1970s, a grim urban legend surrounds Chak Phra Road in the Thonburi district of Bangkok, which runs alongside the Khlong Chak Phra canal. According to the tale, a local woman named Miss Wan—referred to as Phi Yai Wan—was brutally murdered by her husband while she was pregnant. Her restless, vengeful spirit now haunts the roadway in various disturbing ways. In particular, her ghost is frequently encountered by passersby directly in front of the Wat Taling Chan temple that the road passes through. Fearing paranormal encounters with her spirit, many local taxi and tuk-tuk drivers strictly avoid driving down Chak Phra Road after sunset.

#location
Chak Phra Road runs alongside the Khlong Chak Phra canal and passes directly in front of the Wat Taling Chan temple in the Thonburi district of Bangkok, Thailand.

#title
The Possession of Ban Songyae

#summary
In 1908, multiple families in the northeastern Thai village of Ban Songyae fell victim to an outbreak of spirit possession by the cannibalistic Pop entity. The afflicted villagers were ultimately delivered when two Catholic priests intervened with religious rites at the village church.

#details
In 1908, panic gripped the remote northeastern village of Ban Songyae in Yasothon province when members of five local families were overcome by a malevolent, visceral-eating entity known in Isan folklore as a Pop. The possessed individuals displayed alarming and erratic behavior, plunging the community into terror. Desperate for deliverance, the villagers appealed to the local clergy. Two Catholic priests, Father Desaval and Father Ambrosio, intervened and performed solemn prayers, blessings, and religious rites centered around the settlement's wooden church, successfully subduing the entity and restoring peace to the community.

#location
The key location is St. Michael's Church, situated in the village of Ban Songyae within Yasothon province, Thailand.
