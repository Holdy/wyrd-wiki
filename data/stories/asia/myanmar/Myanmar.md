#title
Curse of Turin Cave

#summary
Queen Oh Pyae Thin inscribed a potent curse upon the Turin Cave in Bagan to damn anyone who desecrates her sacred offerings to endless torment and starvation.

#details
During the historic Bagan era, Queen Oh Pyae Thin established a sacred shrine at Turin Cave and sealed it with a fearsome stone inscription curse. She decreed that anyone who maliciously destroys or interferes with her pious deeds would be cast into the depths of the Avici hell, rendered unable to ever witness the Buddhas of the universe, and condemned to perpetual, agonizing starvation and thirst where they wish to eat and drink but can never do so.

#location
Turin Cave is situated in the ancient archaeological temple zone of Bagan, Mandalay Region, Myanmar.

#title
The Vengeful Spirits of Mount Popa

#summary
Following their brutal execution by a suspicious king, a blacksmith and his sister became powerful animist spirits that possess mediums and guard the sacred mountain.

#details
In ancient Myanmar, a master blacksmith named Maung Tint De possessed such extraordinary strength that the reigning monarch grew fearful of his influence. To neutralize him, the king married Maung Tint De's sister, Daw Phyu, and then lured the blacksmith to the royal palace under false pretenses. Guards seized Maung Tint De, tied him to a giant Champak tree, and burned him alive. Overcome with grief and betrayal, his sister threw herself into the blazing fire to die alongside him. Following their violent deaths, their spirits transformed into vengeful entities known as Min Mahagiri and Hnamadawgyi, who haunted the slopes of Mount Popa and devoured anyone who approached. Eventually placated and canonized among Myanmar's 37 lords, their spirits continue to take possession of human mediums during trance rituals at the mountain shrines.

#location
Mount Popa is a prominent, extinct volcanic mountain located in central Myanmar, rising above the Myingyan plain in the Mandalay Region.
