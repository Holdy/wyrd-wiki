#title
Otora Gitsune

#summary
A one-eyed, three-legged fox spirit named Otora Gitsune possessed numerous villagers in Japan, starting with a young woman named Otora. Victims exhibited strange physical ailments and endlessly recounted battle memories they never experienced until the spirit was eventually slain by a hunter.

#details
After its home at the Inari shrine grove in Nagashino Castle was destroyed following the Battle of Nagashino, an enraged fox lost its left eye and leg, becoming a vengeful spirit known as Otora Gitsune. The entity began possessing local people, most notably the daughter of a wealthy family named Otora. Those possessed by Otora Gitsune suffered from left eye discharge, inexplicable left leg pain, and an obsessive compulsion to talk endlessly about the Battle of Nagashino. While priests could sometimes perform exorcisms, stubborn cases required visiting the wolf-dedicated Yamazumi Shrine in Shizuoka Prefecture to frighten the fox away. The possessing spirit terrorized the region until it was finally killed by a hunter on the banks of the Sai River.

#location
The ruins of Nagashino Castle are located in the city of Shinshiro, Aichi Prefecture, Japan.

#title
The Specter of Kiyotaki Tunnel

#summary
Motorists driving through the Kiyotaki Tunnel in Kyoto frequently encounter a terrifying ghostly woman who appears in rear-view mirrors or plummets onto the hoods of moving cars. Local urban legends warn that seeing her or ignoring mysterious omens inside the tunnel will bring sudden tragedy.

#details
Constructed in the 1920s with forced labor under brutal conditions where many exhausted workers died and were allegedly buried within its walls, the 444-metre Kiyotaki Tunnel in Kyoto has earned a dark reputation for supernatural phenomena. Over the decades, drivers and pedestrians navigating the tunnel have shared harrowing accounts of paranormal activity. The most prominent apparition is a ghostly woman who startles drivers by materializing in their rear-view mirrors or dropping directly onto the hoods of passing cars. Those who venture through are also met with inexplicable occurrences, including ghostly handprints pressing against car windows, disembodied whispers echoing through the damp passageway, and traffic signals that mysteriously turn red to block entry.

#location
The Kiyotaki Tunnel is a passageway located between northern Arashiyama and Sagakiyotaki in Kyoto, Japan.

#title
The Vengeful Head of Taira no Masakado

#summary
Following his defeat, the severed head of the rebel samurai Taira no Masakado flew across Japan and became a wrathful spirit, bringing swift and mysterious deaths to anyone who attempts to disturb or disrespect his burial mound.

#details
Taira no Masakado was a powerful provincial samurai who led a rebellion against the emperor in the 10th century before being defeated in battle. His decapitated head was taken to Kyoto to be put on public display, but it refused to decompose, crying out nightly in anger until it finally launched itself into the sky and flew back to the Kantō region, landing at a site in what is now modern Tokyo. A burial mound, known as the *kubizuka*, was built to enshrine the head, but the site became heavily feared because any disturbance, neglect, or disrespect shown to the area unleashes a severe curse. Over the centuries, attempts to demolish or pave over the mound have been repeatedly halted after mysterious accidents, illnesses, and sudden deaths befell the ministers, workers, and occupants involved in the projects. To this day, the small shrine is maintained with deep reverence, surrounded by offerings and stone frog figures, as local businesses and workers take great care never to slight the resting place of the formidable warrior.

#location
The shrine and head-mound (Masakado-zuka) is located in the Ōtemachi financial district of Chiyoda, Tokyo, just a short walk from Tokyo Imperial Palace.
