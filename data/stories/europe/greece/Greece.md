#title
The Bridge of Arta

#summary
Forty-five master masons laboured day and night to build a bridge, but every night its foundations collapsed until the chief builder's wife was entombed within it, prompting her to lay a fierce curse on all who would cross. Realizing her brother might one day pass, she altered the curse into a stabilizing condition that linked the safety of the bridge to the permanence of the surrounding mountains and skies.

#details
According to Greek folklore, a crew of forty-five masons and sixty apprentices spent years attempting to build a stone bridge across the Arachthos River, but every day's labour would mysteriously collapse by nightfall. Desperate for a solution, a speaking bird told the master builder that the construction could only succeed if he sacrificed his own wife by walling her up alive inside the foundations. When the tragic deed was carried out, the doomed woman cursed the bridge—and anyone crossing it—to tremble and fall like leaves. Remembering that her own brother might one day use the bridge, she softened her dying words, transforming the curse into a binding condition: the bridge would only shake when the wild mountains trembled, and travellers would only fall when the high birds dropped from the sky.

#location
The Bridge of Arta crosses the Arachthos River on the western outskirts of the city of Arta in northwestern Greece.

#title
The Exorcism of the Student at the Monastery of Saint Gerasimos

#summary
A seventeen-year-old student afflicted by an unclean spirit was brought to a historic island monastery in Greece where he was successfully exorcised before the relics of a revered ascetic. The account was documented in a mid-twentieth-century Greek newspaper.

#details
In late September 1949, a seventeen-year-old boy from the village of Vathy who attended high school in Lefkada became severely troubled by an unclean spirit. Suffering from the possession, he was taken to the Monastery of Saint Gerasimos on the island of Kefalonia, Greece, which is renowned for healing the afflicted and demon-possessed. While attending the Divine Liturgy chanted by Father Spyridon Linardatos, the boy suddenly began screaming in terror, crying out that he was "Legion" and questioning where the saint would cast him. As the priest attempted to perform an exorcism using the Honorable Cross, the young man bit down on the crucifix, declaring it to be a powerful weapon. The following day, as he knelt before the coffin holding the incorrupt relics of Saint Gerasimos, the boy shouted that he had been silenced and defeated before suddenly rising completely cured and healthy.

#location
The Monastery of Saint Gerasimos is located in the Omala valley on the island of Kefalonia, Greece.
