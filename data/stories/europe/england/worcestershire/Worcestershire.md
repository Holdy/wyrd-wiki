#title
The Curse of Raggedstone Hill

#summary
An embittered monk cast a lasting curse from Raggedstone Hill, dooming anyone touched by its shadow or crossing the path of his wrath to an untimely end.

#details
According to local Worcestershire folklore, a monk from Little Malvern Priory was severely punished and made to climb Raggedstone Hill on his knees daily until his spirit broke. In his old age and deep bitterness, he turned toward the surrounding landscape and cursed the hill, proclaiming that anyone upon whom the shadow of its peak falls would suffer a tragic fate before their time. Another version tells of a dispute between a monk and the landowner of Birtsmorton Court over common land; when the monk was ordered away, he pronounced that whenever the shadow of Raggedstone Hill touched the moated manor, disaster and early death would follow the household. Over the centuries, several notable figures connected to the estate—including Cardinal Thomas Wolsey and William Huskisson, who became the world's first railway casualty—have been superstitiously linked to the terrifying reach of the curse.

#location
Raggedstone Hill forms a prominent peak at the southern end of the Malvern Hills range in Worcestershire.

#title
The Ghostly Tale of Old Buckle

#summary
This story recounts the legend of Old Buckle, a headless horseman who is said to haunt the road between Throckmorton and Bishampton in Worcestershire.

#details
Generations of children from Bishampton have been both frightened and delighted by the tale of Old Buckle, a headless horseman who was said to roam the road between Throckmorton and Bishampton. The story gained newfound credibility when a researcher at the Hive in Worcestershire discovered the will of John Frederick Buckle, a tenant farmer from Throckmorton.

According to an inquest reported in the Berrows Journal on January 25th, 1879, John Buckle traveled to Alcester to pay his rent and arranged to meet a business associate in a local inn. When his associate was delayed, John had to spend the day at the inn, leaving at 11:10 PM. He was well when he left but was found dead shortly after midnight. The publican at the Bull’s Head Inn in Inkberrow reported hearing a horse and cart careering up the drive next to his pub. Upon investigation, he found the cart overturned on top of John Buckle, who had died with "his head turned under his body."

The road was narrow and strewn with loose stones, leading the publican to speculate that John had fallen asleep and the horse had automatically made for the stables at the back of the Bull. The driver waking overturned the vehicle in trying to turn it around.

#location
The road between Throckmorton and Bishampton is a key location in this story.
