#title
The Ruskington Horror

#summary
The Ruskington Horror is a ghostly apparition that has been reported along the A15 road near Ruskington, Lincolnshire, for decades. The sightings include various spectral figures, such as a phantom cyclist and a little girl in a pink gown.

#details
The Ruskington Horror has been a subject of intrigue and fear for many years, with numerous reports of ghostly encounters along the A15 road near Ruskington, Lincolnshire. The apparition is often described as a figure that appears suddenly in front of drivers, sometimes with a raised arm as if to warn them. One driver reported seeing a face appear from around the front pillar of his car's windscreen, with dark hair and olive-green skin.

Other sightings include a white shape that turns into a man who fades away into mist, and instances where drivers thought they had hit someone but found no trace of a body. The area is also known for other ghostly sightings, such as horses running along the road and a little girl in a pink gown.

The field from which these figures are seen emerging was once a plague pit, adding to the eerie atmosphere. Additionally, a nearby church built by the Knights Templar has links with mysticism, further enhancing the mysterious aura of the location. Despite attempts at cleansing the area, sightings continue to be reported.

#location
The A15 road near Ruskington, Lincolnshire

#title
The Vengeance of Tiddy Mun

#summary
When Dutch engineers drained the Lincolnshire wetlands in the seventeenth century, the offended bog spirit Tiddy Mun inflicted a persistent curse of pestilence upon the local population. The plague of sickness and flooding only lifted after the villagers gathered at twilight to offer public apologies and appease the spirit.

#details
In the seventeenth century, when Dutch engineers led by Cornelius Vermuyden began draining the fens and marshes of the Ancholme Valley, they deeply disturbed the ancient ecology and violated the domain of Tiddy Mun, a tiny, white-bearded bog spirit. Enraged by the destruction of his sacred wetlands, Tiddy Mun cast a curse of pestilence and flooding upon the local communities, causing children and the elderly to sicken and die in droves. The affliction persisted as a delayed consequence of the landscape's alteration until the desperate villagers finally sought to make amends. Gathering at twilight during the new moon, they poured water into the dykes and offered formal apologies to appease the spirit, after which the curse was lifted and the waters receded.

#location
The Ancholme Valley in North Lincolnshire, where the wetlands were drained by seventeenth-century engineers.
