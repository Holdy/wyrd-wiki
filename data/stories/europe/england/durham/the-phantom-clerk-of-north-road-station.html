The Phantom Clerk of North Road Station

Summary

A 19th-century nightwatchman guarding a railway level crossing in Darlington encountered a ghostly former clerk and his dog inside a station cellar. When the apparition struck at him, the watchman punched back, his fist passing straight through the ghost to strike the stone wall.

Details

On a freezing winter night in the 19th century, nightwatchman James Durham was on duty guarding the level crossing where the Stockton and Darlington Railway met the Great North Road in Darlington. Seeking warmth, he walked to the nearby North Road station and descended into the cellar to eat his supper by the fire. Suddenly, a strange man accompanied by a large black retriever emerged from the coalhouse. The figure wore a cutaway coat with gilt buttons and a Scotch cap. When the man struck aggressively toward James, James threw a punch in retaliation, but his fist passed entirely through the phantom and smashed into the stone wall above the fireplace, badly grazing his knuckles. The apparition and dog retreated into the coalhouse and vanished into thin air. Local inquiries later revealed that the description perfectly matched a former station clerk named Winter, who had committed suicide with a pistol in the room above and whose body had been temporarily laid out in that exact cellar.
