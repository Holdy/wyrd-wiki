#title
The Curse of the Lambton Worm

#summary
After skipping church to fish in the River Wear, a rebellious young heir catches a monstrous creature and throws it down a well, inadvertently unleashing a beast that terrorizes the countryside and dooms nine generations of his family to violent, restless deaths.

#details
Young John Lambton skipped church one Sunday to fish in the River Wear, where he hooked a strange, eel-like creature with nine holes on each side of its head. Dismissing warnings that he had caught the devil, he tossed the creature into a local well. Years later, after John departed for the Crusades, the creature grew into a colossal, tree-uprooting worm that wrapped itself around Penshaw Hill, devouring livestock and terrorizing local villages while subsisting on a daily offering of milk. Upon returning from the Crusades, John sought the advice of a wise woman, who instructed him to cover his armor in spearheads and battle the beast in the River Wear. She warned him that after slaying the monster, he must kill the first living thing he saw, or his family would be cursed for nine generations so that none would die peacefully in their beds. Although John successfully slew the worm, his overjoyed father rushed out to meet him before the designated hunting hound could be released; while John later killed the dog, the curse was sealed, leading subsequent generations of the Lambton family to meet tragic and violent ends.

#location
Penshaw Hill, topped by the historic Penshaw Monument, overlooks the River Wear in County Durham and marks where the giant worm coiled itself.

#upstream-source
Sir Cuthbert Sharpe, *The Worme of Lambton* (1830); William Henderson, *Notes on the Folk-Lore of the Northern Counties of England and the Borders* (1866).

#title
The Phantom Clerk of North Road Station

#summary
A 19th-century nightwatchman guarding a railway level crossing in Darlington encountered a ghostly former clerk and his dog inside a station cellar. When the apparition struck at him, the watchman punched back, his fist passing straight through the ghost to strike the stone wall.

#details
On a freezing winter night in the 19th century, nightwatchman James Durham was on duty guarding the level crossing where the Stockton and Darlington Railway met the Great North Road in Darlington. Seeking warmth, he walked to the nearby North Road station and descended into the cellar to eat his supper by the fire. Suddenly, a strange man accompanied by a large black retriever emerged from the coalhouse. The figure wore a cutaway coat with gilt buttons and a Scotch cap. When the man struck aggressively toward James, James threw a punch in retaliation, but his fist passed entirely through the phantom and smashed into the stone wall above the fireplace, badly grazing his knuckles. The apparition and dog retreated into the coalhouse and vanished into thin air. Local inquiries later revealed that the description perfectly matched a former station clerk named Winter, who had committed suicide with a pistol in the room above and whose body had been temporarily laid out in that exact cellar.

#location
The events took place in the cellar of North Road Station, located beside the historic level crossing of the Stockton and Darlington Railway and the Great North Road in Darlington, County Durham.
