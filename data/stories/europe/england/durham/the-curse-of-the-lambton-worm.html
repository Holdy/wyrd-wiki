The Curse of the Lambton Worm

Summary

After skipping church to fish in the River Wear, a rebellious young heir catches a monstrous creature and throws it down a well, inadvertently unleashing a beast that terrorizes the countryside and dooms nine generations of his family to violent, restless deaths.

Details

Young John Lambton skipped church one Sunday to fish in the River Wear, where he hooked a strange, eel-like creature with nine holes on each side of its head. Dismissing warnings that he had caught the devil, he tossed the creature into a local well. Years later, after John departed for the Crusades, the creature grew into a colossal, tree-uprooting worm that wrapped itself around Penshaw Hill, devouring livestock and terrorizing local villages while subsisting on a daily offering of milk. Upon returning from the Crusades, John sought the advice of a wise woman, who instructed him to cover his armor in spearheads and battle the beast in the River Wear. She warned him that after slaying the monster, he must kill the first living thing he saw, or his family would be cursed for nine generations so that none would die peacefully in their beds. Although John successfully slew the worm, his overjoyed father rushed out to meet him before the designated hunting hound could be released; while John later killed the dog, the curse was sealed, leading subsequent generations of the Lambton family to meet tragic and violent ends.
