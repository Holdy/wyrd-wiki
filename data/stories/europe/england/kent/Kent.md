#title
The Curse of the Kentish Longtails

#summary
Following an act of desecration against his horse by locals, Thomas Becket placed a generational curse upon the inhabitants of Strood. Ever since, all children born in the town were said to carry a physical appendage as punishment.

#details
While traveling through the town of Strood in Kent, the Archbishop Thomas Becket was subjected to a cruel insult when local inhabitants—or members of the hostile de Broc family—cut off the tail of his horse. Deeply angered by the disrespect shown to him and his mount, Becket pronounced a curse upon the settlement. From that day onward, every subsequent generation of children born in Strood would be cursed to come into the world with a tail, earning the locals the enduring nickname "Kentish Longtails".

#location
The town of Strood in Kent, situated along the historic route near Rochester.

#title
The Ghost of Blue Bell Hill

#summary
The ghost of Judith Lingham, a young woman who died in a 1965 car crash on the A229, is said to appear as a hitchhiker or figure on the road before vanishing, with sightings reported for decades.

#details
On November 19, 1965, Judith Lingham died at the scene of a collision between a Ford Cortina and a Jaguar on the A229 near Blue Bell Hill, while her friends Suzanne Browne and Patricia Ferguson later succumbed to injuries. Local lore claims Lingham's spirit haunts the road, with accounts from the 1960s–70s describing a young woman in white appearing to drivers, vanishing mid-conversation or as cars approach. Film director Sonya Roseman documented the legend in her 2014 movie *The Ghost of Blue Bell Hill*, citing paranormal activity detected during filming.

#location
The A229 road near Blue Bell Hill, Kent, where the 1965 car crash occurred.
