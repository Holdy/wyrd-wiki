    Tresmarrow skull

    Summary

    Royalist Sir Hugh Piper is said to have set a Roundhead's skull in a niche at Tresmarrow House, and whenever it was buried the household suffered nightly knocks, voices and trampling until it was dug up again. After a spell in London being exhibited as Oliver Cromwell's skull, it returned to Launceston in 2001 and is now in the town museum.

    Details

    Sir Hugh Piper, a Royalist and Governor of Launceston Castle under Charles I, owned Tresmarrow House, which Roundheads razed in 1646. When he rebuilt it, local tradition says he placed a Roundhead's skull in a wall niche as a reminder of the King's fate; a skull is even carved on Piper's memorial in Launceston parish church. In 1882 Sabine Baring-Gould recorded a farmer who buried the skull, only to suffer nightly noises, voices, knocks and trampling until he dug it up and put it back. Later in the 19th century a member of the Dawe family buried it in the garden, and the disturbances began at once, so it was returned to its niche. When the Dawes emigrated to Canada in 1908 and the house was demolished, the skull was rumoured to have gone with them, but it later surfaced in London being shown as the skull of Oliver Cromwell. It came back to Launceston in 2001, went on display at Lawrence House Museum, and seems to have been quiet ever since.
