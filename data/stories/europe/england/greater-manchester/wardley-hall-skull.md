#title
Wardley Hall skull

#summary
The skull at Wardley Hall is most likely that of Father Ambrose Barlow, a Catholic priest hanged, drawn and quartered in 1641, whose head was taken from Lancaster Castle and hidden in the hall's walls. When a servant threw it into the moat a terrible storm broke, and the owner had the moat drained to bring it back.

#details
Two identities have been suggested for the Wardley Hall skull. One story says it belonged to Roger Downes, a disgraced member of the owning family who escaped a murder trial and was later beheaded with a rapier by a watchman he had drunkenly attacked, though this is thought improbable. The likelier owner is Father Ambrose Barlow, a Catholic priest hanged, drawn and quartered in 1641, whose head was displayed at Lancaster Castle before a Catholic sympathiser stole it and hid it in the walls of Wardley Hall, where it was rediscovered in the 18th century. When a servant of the owner, Matthew Moreton, mistook it for an animal skull and threw it into the moat, a violent storm broke that night. Moreton had the moat drained and the skull returned, and it is now kept behind glass in a niche beside the main staircase, out of the public's reach.
