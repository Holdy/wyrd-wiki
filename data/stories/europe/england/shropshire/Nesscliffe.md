#title
Wild Humphrey and the Devil's Steed

#summary
The notorious 15th-century outlaw Sir Humphrey Kynaston evaded capture by the authorities with the help of his legendary horse, Beelzebub, which was said to be the devil incarnate.

#details
Sir Humphrey Kynaston was a nobleman turned highwayman who made his home in a sandstone cave on Nesscliffe Hill after being outlawed for debts and violence. Operating along the road between Shrewsbury and Oswestry, he became a legendary figure of resistance who robbed the wealthy and aided the poor. According to local folklore, his uncanny ability to vanish without a trace and perform impossible escapes—such as leaping across wide river gaps and soaring over the heads of armed sheriffs' men—was granted through a dark bargain with the devil, who transformed into his faithful, backward-shod black steed, Beelzebub. 

#location
Kynaston's Cave is situated high upon the red sandstone cliffs of Nesscliffe Hill, overlooking the village of Nesscliffe in Shropshire, UK.
