#title
The Devil's Leap at Montford Bridge

#summary
When lawmen attempted to trap the notorious outlaw Sir Humphrey Kynaston by dismantling Montford Bridge, his satanic steed Beelzebub cleared the gaping chasm in a single supernatural bound.

#details
Sir Humphrey Kynaston, a notorious 16th-century Shropshire highwayman living as an outlaw in the caves of Nesscliffe, was reputed to ride a fearsome horse named Beelzebub—a beast said to have been given to him by the Devil himself or embodying the Devil incarnate. Frustrated by his repeated escapes, the local Sheriff devised a trap at Montford Bridge, removing several wooden planks across the span to create a perilous gap. Under the cover of night, Kynaston arrived at the bridge, recognized the trap, and spurred his steed forward. With supernatural strength, the devilish horse leaped cleanly over the gaping hole, leaving his pursuers utterly baffled.

#location
Montford Bridge crosses the River Severn along the B5061 in the parish of Montford, Shropshire.
