#title
The Fiend of the Roman Ruins

#summary
When the courageous knight Payn Peverel spent the night in the ruined Roman city of Uriconium at Wroxeter, he confronted and vanquished a fiery fiend that guarded subterranean treasures.

#details
According to the 13th-century border chronicle *The History of the Fitz-Warines*, King William encountered the burnt and ruined walls of an ancient fortified town near Wroxeter. Local lore told how the spirit of the giant Geomagog had possessed the ruins and that a devil guarded the subterranean treasures collected within. Unfazed by the legend, the brave knight Payn Peverel resolved to spend the night in the highest palace of the ruined city. That night, amidst a terrifying tempest of lightning and thunder that paralyzed his attendants with fear, Peverel invoked the cross. A monstrous fiend appeared brandishing a club and vomiting fire and smoke, but Peverel boldly attacked him with his sword under the protection of the Holy Cross. Overpowered by the sacred sign, the vanquished fiend yielded and confessed that he was a diabolical spirit guarding the golden cattle, animals, and golden bull of the ancient giant.

#location
The highest palace within the ruined Roman city of Uriconium (modern Wroxeter) in Shropshire, England.

#upstream-source
The 13th-century Anglo-Norman chronicle *The History of the Fitz-Warines*.
