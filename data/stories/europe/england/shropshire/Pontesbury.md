#title
The Legend of the Devil's Chair

#summary
While carrying stones in his apron across Britain to fill a nearby valley, the Devil dropped his load when his apron strings snapped, forming the rocky outcrop known as the Devil's Chair.

#details
According to Shropshire folklore, the Devil—often called 'Owd Scratch'—was traveling across Britain from Ireland carrying a large load of rocks in his apron. He intended to use the stones to fill up a valley known as Hell's Gutter in the hills south of Pontesbury. Growing tired from his journey, he paused to rest on the ridge of the Stiperstones. When he stood up, his apron strings suddenly snapped, causing the heavy rocks to tumble out and scatter across the landscape. The Devil left them where they fell, and locals claim the stones still smell of brimstone on hot summer days. Additionally, the Devil is said to return on the longest night of the year to sit upon the rocky throne of the Devil's Chair, summoning witches and evil spirits to judge their misdeeds and elect the King of Evil.

#location
The Devil's Chair rock formation on the Stiperstones ridge in the hills south of Pontesbury, Shropshire.
