#title
The Devil's Causeway

#summary
The Devil built a remote stretch of road in a single night to aid his travels and still haunts the path at midnight to judge belated travelers. Those deemed wicked are savagely beaten by the fiend.

#details
Folklore tells of a 30-yard stretch of cobblestone road known as the Devil's Causeway, which branches off the ancient Watling Street route between Acton Burnell, Ruckley, and Cardington. According to tradition, this rugged path was constructed by the Devil in a single night to facilitate his nefarious journeys across the region. Still haunted by Owd Scratch, the Devil roams the road at midnight appearing as a towering figure with goat or cow horns and hooves, riding a white horse. He intercepts anyone unfortunate enough to be out late, passing harmlessly by those who have lived good lives while brutally beating wretched souls into a bloodied pulp.

#location
The Devil's Causeway is a historic stretch of road branching off the south-western arm of Watling Street (at National Grid Reference NGR 530 033) running towards Acton Burnell and Cardington in Shropshire.
