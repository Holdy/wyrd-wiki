The Puritan's Curse of Moreton Corbet

Summary

A banished religious zealot placed a vengeful curse upon a powerful family and their grand Elizabethan manor, decreeing that neither the lord nor his descendants would ever inhabit the uncompleted halls. True to his words, the terrified family fled the estate, leaving it to fall into permanent ruin and desolation.

Details

During the reign of King James I, Sir Vincent Corbet sheltered a Puritan neighbour named Paul Holmyard to save him from religious persecution. However, as Holmyard grew increasingly fanatical, Sir Vincent felt compelled to ask him to leave for his own safety and peace. Evicted and forced to survive in the local woods, a bitter and hardened Holmyard eventually emerged, confronted Sir Vincent, and laid a fierce curse upon the family and their magnificent new Elizabethan manor. He declared that neither Sir Vincent nor his children, nor any generation of their children, would ever live in the halls, and that the grand building work would remain forever unfinished and become a desolate refuge for snakes and unclean beasts. Terrified by the chilling prophecy, Sir Vincent and his heirs refused to dwell in the estate, leaving the buildings to fall into ruin and hauntings by the vengeful phantom of Holmyard himself.
