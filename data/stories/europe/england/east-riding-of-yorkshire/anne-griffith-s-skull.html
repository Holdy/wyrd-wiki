    Anne Griffith's skull

    Summary

    Anne Griffith, youngest of the sisters said to have built Burton Agnes Hall, was attacked by robbers and asked as she died that her head be kept in the hall. Her sisters buried her whole, and the house was plagued by noises until her skull was brought inside, where it is thought to remain, walled up.

    Details

    Tradition says Burton Agnes Hall was built in Elizabeth I's reign by three sisters. The youngest, Anne, was attacked while out walking, perhaps by robbers after her ring, and fell ill and died. She begged that her head be kept in the Hall, warning that her spirit would trouble the house if it were removed, but her sisters buried her body whole. Noises soon troubled the house, and when the body was exhumed the head was found detached and fleshless; once it was placed on a table inside, all was quiet. When a servant threw it onto a wagon, the horses reared and the house shook until it was brought back, and when a Boynton buried it in the garden, wailing filled the house until it was dug up. The skull was eventually walled up in a niche, and its exact location is unknown. The Hall was actually built for Sir Henry Griffith, so whether the skull is really Anne's is uncertain.
