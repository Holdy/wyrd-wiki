#title
Sowood House skull

#summary
In 1968 workmen restoring Sowood House found a human skull in an iron box hidden in the brickwork, probably a 17th-century man's and perhaps placed there to guard against witches. An elderly local then wrote that it had been found once before and buried in Coley churchyard, and the house was haunted by cries of "Where's my head?" until it was brought back.

#details
Sowood House, near Coley, was built in 1631 for John and Grace Whitley. In 1968, while Frank Drury was restoring it, workmen found an iron box hidden in the brickwork behind a chimney, and inside it was a human skull. Archaeologists judged it to be an adult man's from the 17th century; the crown had been removed after death and letters, now unreadable, were engraved on the bone. Experts suggested it had been built into the house as protection against witches, though some wondered if it came from a victim of the Halifax Gibbet. An 80-year-old woman from Southowram then wrote to the local paper to say that her great-grandfather, a churchwarden at Coley, had seen the skull found once before in the early 19th century. On the vicar's advice it was buried in the churchyard, but the house was then haunted by cries of "Where's my head?" until it was dug up and returned. After 1968 the skull went to the University of Leeds, and its whereabouts today are unknown.
