The Curse of Milner Field

Summary

A lavish Victorian mansion near Bingley gained a notorious reputation as a cursed estate after a succession of its owners and their family members suffered sudden tragedies, scandals, and premature deaths. Within decades, the unmarketable grand house was entirely abandoned and ultimately demolished.

Details

Built between 1871 and 1873 for Titus Salt Junior, son of the famous industrialist Sir Titus Salt, Milner Field was a magnificent Gothic mansion that hosted royalty. However, its history soon became overshadowed by misfortune. Titus Salt Jr. died suddenly in the mansion's billiard room in 1887. The estate was subsequently purchased by wool baron Sir James Roberts, whose family was plagued by an extraordinary run of grief: three of his four sons died young under tragic circumstances, another was badly wounded in the First World War, and a daughter's husband was imprisoned following a sensational murder. Following the Roberts family, subsequent occupants suffered similarly grim fates—Ernest Gates lost his wife shortly after moving in and died two years later from blood poisoning, while the final owner, Arthur Remington Hollins, lost his wife to pneumonia within a year and died himself shortly thereafter. Left uninhabitable and unsellable due to its ghoulish reputation, the mansion was abandoned in 1930 and dismantled in the 1950s.
