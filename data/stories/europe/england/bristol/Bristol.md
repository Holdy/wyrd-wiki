#title
The Goblin's Curse

#summary
When a fearless Bristol captain defies local warnings to explore a subterranean cavern, he and his companion are cursed by a malevolent goblin who foretells their deaths. Within a year, both men perish exactly as the apparition predicted.

#details
Captain Sturmey, a bold sailor from Bristol, scoffed at the warnings of townsfolk regarding the dreaded Goblin of Pen Park Hole. Accompanied by a miner named Dick, he descended deep into the dark, winding abyss of the cave until they reached an underground lake. There, perched upon a stone, sat a hideous goblin. Mocking the men, the goblin used a fishing rod to pull a shroud out of the water for the captain and a coffin for Dick before vanishing behind a stone door. True to the curse, both explorers met their fatal ends within a single year.

#location
Pen Park Hole is a subterranean limestone cave system located in the Southmead and Brentry district of northern Bristol.

#title
The Highwayman of Gallows Acre Lane

#summary
An 18th-century highwayman who terrorized travelers and was executed on his crime grounds continues to haunt the road junction where he was buried. His restless spirit is said to roam the area where he met his grisly end.

#details
During the 18th century, a ruthless highwayman named Jenkins Protheroe plagued travelers along the isolated tracks of Clifton. Eventually caught for his crimes, Protheroe was sentenced to death, hanged directly at the site of his exploits, and buried in the grass junction at the top of what was then known as All Gallows Lane. Today, people traversing the junction where Pembroke Road meets Clifton Down associate the spot with lingering supernatural activity tied to the executed thief.

#location
The key location is the grassy junction at the top of Pembroke Road where it intersects with Clifton Down in Clifton, Bristol.
