The Highwayman of Gallows Acre Lane

Summary

An 18th-century highwayman who terrorized travelers and was executed on his crime grounds continues to haunt the road junction where he was buried. His restless spirit is said to roam the area where he met his grisly end.

Details

During the 18th century, a ruthless highwayman named Jenkins Protheroe plagued travelers along the isolated tracks of Clifton. Eventually caught for his crimes, Protheroe was sentenced to death, hanged directly at the site of his exploits, and buried in the grass junction at the top of what was then known as All Gallows Lane. Today, people traversing the junction where Pembroke Road meets Clifton Down associate the spot with lingering supernatural activity tied to the executed thief.
