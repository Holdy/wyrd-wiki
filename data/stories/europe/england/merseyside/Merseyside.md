#title
The Knight of Smithdown Lane

#summary
A man encountered a spectral knight in armor on Smithdown Lane during a dense fog in 1987, later realizing he had witnessed a timeslip to medieval times.

#details
On Christmas Eve 1987, Patrick walked along Smithdown Lane in foggy Edge Hill, heading home to his mother's house. As he entered a thick fog, he saw a knight in armor on horseback demanding his identity. Terrified, Patrick fled and recited the Lord's Prayer, but the knight's hooves faded as he encountered a local named Johnno, who heard the approaching horses. The incident occurred on Smithdown Lane, Liverpool's oldest road, which historically connected to Toxteth Forest and the manor of Esmedune.

#location
Smithdown Lane, Edge Hill, Liverpool (specifically near the junction with Lodge Lane)

#title
The Upright Gambler of Rodney Street

#summary
An eccentric wealthy contractor allegedly made a fatal pact with the devil and arranged to be buried sitting upright in a pyramid tomb to prevent Satan from ever claiming his soul.

#details
William Mackenzie was a successful 19th-century civil engineering contractor who amassed a vast fortune. According to local folklore, he was also an avid and reckless gambler who entered into a high-stakes poker game with the devil, ultimately losing his soul. Fearing that the devil would come to claim his payment once he was laid horizontally beneath the earth, Mackenzie arranged a clever loophole. Upon his death, he was interred inside his tomb in an upright, seated position at a table clutching a winning hand of cards. Because he was never traditionally buried flat in the ground, the devil could not take him, leaving his restless ghost to wander the surrounding streets at night.

#location
The pyramid tomb of William Mackenzie is located in the old churchyard on Rodney Street in Liverpool, Merseyside.
