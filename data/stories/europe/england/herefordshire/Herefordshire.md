#title
The Curse of Ragged Stone Hill

#summary
An embittered monk cast a lasting curse upon Ragged Stone Hill, dooming anyone touched by its shadow to an untimely end. Over the generations, this dread fate became linked to the male heirs of a nearby manor and high-profile figures connected to the estate.

#details
According to local legend, an aggrieved 15th-century monk from Little Malvern Priory was ordered off common land on Raggedstone Hill by a local landowner expanding his estate. Furious at the altercation, the monk pronounced a curse from the peak, declaring that anyone upon whom the shadow of the hill fell would die before their time. Over subsequent centuries, unexpected tragedies, premature deaths among family heirs, and the downfalls of prominent historical figures associated with the nearby Birtsmorton Court—such as Cardinal Thomas Wolsey and the statesman William Huskisson—became superstitiously attributed to the lingering shadow of the curse.

#location
Ragged Stone Hill forms part of the southern Malvern Hills, straddling the border area near Ledbury in Herefordshire.

#title
The Phantom Driver of A465

#summary
Locals in Herefordshire attribute repeated accidents on the A465 near Bromyard to the ghost of a woman who died in a 1920s road collision after a struggle over her steering wheel.

#details
In the early 2000s, drivers repeatedly lost control on a specific stretch of the A465 near Bromyard, crashing into a farmer's fence without skid marks. A county councillor met a mysterious psychic at a local pub who claimed a woman had died in the 1920s or 1930s after a violent struggle over her steering wheel. The councillor later confirmed the accidents consistently occurred in the same location, with drivers reporting steering wheels suddenly "pulled from their hands" despite no visible road hazards.

#location
A465 road near Bromyard, Herefordshire, specifically at the location where multiple vehicles struck a farmer's fence between 2000–2002.
