#title
The Curse of Blackmere

#summary
Blackmere, near Brereton Hall in Cheshire, is a lake shrouded in legends of curses and omens. The Brereton family's tragic history is intertwined with eerie apparitions seen at the mere.

#details
Close to historic Brereton Hall lies Blackmere, a dark pool of water surrounded by trees and steeped in old stories. For locals, it has long been associated with bad omens, drowned spirits, and a hallside curse that persists. The lake is linked to the tragic history of the Brereton family, who once resided at Brereton Hall.

One legend tells of a Brereton lord who rode off to war, and whose death was foretold by strange activity at Blackmere. Some versions of the tale describe an armored figure riding across the mere's surface or a ghostly head appearing in the water shortly after the lord’s fatal blow in battle. These apparitions were seen as signs that the hall would never escape tragedy, and that Blackmere would always reflect the family’s darkest moments.

Modern visitors to Blackmere report different but equally chilling experiences. Some describe seeing a dark shape rising from the water before dissolving into mist; others mention the silhouette of a mounted figure at the tree line at dusk, vanishing instantly when they blink or look away. There are also tales of sudden, unnatural stillness—birds falling silent, wind dropping, and the mere’s surface becoming eerily flat before ripples appear from no visible source.

#location
Blackmere is located near Brereton Hall in Cheshire, UK.

#title
Woman of Kendal Road

## Summary
The story tells of a ghostly figure, known as the White Woman, who appears on Kendal Road near Macclesfield. Drivers and pedestrians report seeing her suddenly appear in their path before vanishing.

## Details
On the outskirts of Macclesfield, Kendal Road is known for a stretch near "the Birdy" where drivers and pedestrians have reported encountering a pale woman who appears at the roadside or in the path of cars, only to vanish without a trace. This ghostly figure, dubbed the White Woman of Kendal Road, has become one of Cheshire’s most striking modern ghost tales.

The Paranormal Database notes repeated reports of a white-clad female figure seen near the Birdy, often described as wearing light clothing and standing unnaturally still before disappearing. No one seems certain who she is meant to be, but theories range from a victim of a road accident to a much older spirit linked to paths that pre-date the current road layout.

Witness accounts describe startlingly similar experiences. Drivers say a woman suddenly steps into view at the edge of their headlights, sometimes directly in front of the car, causing them to brake hard – only to find no one there when they get out to check. Pedestrians walking near the Birdy after dark speak of a figure ahead that seems to glide rather than walk, or stands with her back to the road before melting into the night.

## Location
The story is set on Kendal Road in Macclesfield, Cheshire, specifically near a spot known locally as “the Birdy.”
