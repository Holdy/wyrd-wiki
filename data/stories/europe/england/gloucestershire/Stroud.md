#title
The Tragedy of the Ragged Cot

#summary
In 1760, a desperate innkeeper who attempted to rob a passing stagecoach accidentally caused the deaths of his wife and infant child, leading to his execution and a lasting spectral haunting.

#details
In December 1760, Bill Clavers, the landlord of the Ragged Cot coaching inn near Minchinhampton and Stroud, faced severe financial hardship due to unseasonably harsh winter weather. Seeking money, he resolved to rob the London-bound midnight stagecoach. Intoxicating himself with brandy for courage, he was confronted by his wife, who held their infant child and begged him not to go. In a rage, Clavers shoved her aside, causing her to fall violently down the stairs before he rode out to commit the robbery. Returning triumphant with stolen gold, he discovered to his horror that both his wife and child were dead at the bottom of the staircase. In a panic, he concealed their bodies in a wooden trunk. When local authorities and soldiers later investigated the dark inn after noticing strange, flickering blue lights, they confronted Clavers alongside the glowing spectral apparition of his wife and child. Clavers was subsequently tried at the Gloucester Assize Court, convicted, and hanged, and reports of the ghostly mother and child haunting the premises have persisted ever since.

#location
The Ragged Cot is an historic coaching inn located in Hyde, near Minchinhampton and Stroud in Gloucestershire.
