#title
The Haunting and Tumultuous History of the Trouble House Inn

#summary
Built in the mid-18th century in the parish of Cherington, the isolated roadside inn known as the Trouble House became a magnet for tragic misfortune, violent riots, and the apparition of a phantom "Lady in Blue." Over the centuries, successive owners and patrons encountered supernatural disturbances, moving objects, and dark legends stemming from its turbulent past.

#details
Originally constructed around 1754 by a Tetbury carpenter named John Reeve and initially known as the Wagon & Horses, the inn gained a reputation for misfortune early on. Early landlords suffered through the early deaths of wives and the forcible abduction of regular customers by press gangs. During the agricultural riots of 1830, rioters chasing a hidden threshing machine converged on the inn, setting fire to the wagon outside before stopping there for a drink prior to being arrested by local cavalry. 

Local tradition also preserves darker tales of financial ruin: one nineteenth-century owner allegedly hanged himself from a ceiling beam after bankruptcy during a botched refurbishment, while a subsequent innkeeper drowned himself in a nearby pond. The property was eventually purchased, repaired, and renamed the "Trouble House" in honor of its chaotic history. Following major renovations in 1931, a spectral entity known as the "Lady in Blue"—described as a lifelike woman with long dark hair and sometimes a hood—began haunting the back rooms, rearranging objects, placing matches into startled guests' hands, and carrying trays of bottles. Despite subsequent changes in ownership and modernization, occasional sightings of the Lady in Blue have persisted into recent years.

#location
The Trouble House Inn stands along the Cirencester Road (A433) within the parish of Cherington, approximately two miles north-east of Tetbury in Gloucestershire.
