#title
The Dragon of Tredington and Deerhurst

#summary
According to local Gloucestershire legend, a monstrous serpent once ravaged the vale between Deerhurst and Tredington before being slain by a clever labourer with the aid of milk and an axe.

#details
Centuries ago, a gigantic serpent or dragon covered in impenetrable scales emerged to terrorize the local parishes of Deerhurst and Tredington, poisoning the inhabitants and slaughtering their livestock. Desperate to rid the land of the beast, the local ruler offered a fine estate of land to anyone brave enough to slay it. A labourer named John Smith devised a cunning plan. Knowing the creature's habits, he left a massive quantity of milk at its favorite haunt. The dragon eagerly drank the milk until it grew stupefied and fell into a deep slumber. Seizing the moment, Smith advanced with his axe, struck between the dragon's scales, and severed its head, earning the estate for his descendants. To this day, physical reminders of the legend endure locally, including the dragon-headed stone carvings framing the ancient north doorway of St John the Baptist Church in Tredington and the ancient fossilized reptile bones embedded in the floor of the church's south porch.

#location
St John the Baptist Church in the village of Tredington, Gloucestershire.
