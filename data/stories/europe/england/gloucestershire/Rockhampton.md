#title
The Wandering Standing Stone of Rockhampton

#summary
An ancient limestone standing stone marking the junction of three historic Gloucestershire hundreds vanished during road works in the 1930s, only to be miraculously rediscovered and re-erected decades later.

#details
For generations, an ancient limestone monolith stood at the junction of the old Thornbury and Berkeley road and the lane leading east toward Falfield in the village of Rockhampton, Gloucestershire. Serving as an old Saxon boundary marker and the convergence point for three historic Gloucestershire hundreds—Berkeley, Langley, and Swyneshead—the stone carried deep local significance as an ancient meeting and court site. During the 1930s, however, the standing stone seemingly vanished, believed to have been buried beneath tarmacadam when the Berkeley road was paved over. For nearly fifty years it remained lost to history. Then, in 1984 and 1985, during road-widening operations along the narrow route, workers struck stone beneath the surface. Archaeologists confirmed it was the missing ancient boundary marker. The stone was successfully re-erected within feet of its original position, where it continues to be cared for by locals today.

#location
The Rockhampton Standing Stone stands at the road junction of the old Thornbury-Berkeley road and the lane leading east toward Falfield in Rockhampton, Gloucestershire (Grid Reference ST65679378).
