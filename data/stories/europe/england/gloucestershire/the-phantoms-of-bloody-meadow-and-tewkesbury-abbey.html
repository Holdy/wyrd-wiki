    The Phantoms of Bloody Meadow and Tewkesbury Abbey

    Summary

    Centuries after the brutal 1471 Battle of the Wars of the Roses, the water meadows and ancient abbey of Tewkesbury remain haunted by phantom soldiers, a grieving woman in white, and the spirit of the young Prince of Wales.

    Details

    On May 4, 1471, Tewkesbury hosted one of the most savage and decisive battles of the Wars of the Roses, where the forces of York clashed with the House of Lancaster. Thousands perished in the water meadows beside the River Severn—a blood-soaked terrain since known as Bloody Meadow. According to local legend, the spectral sounds of clashing steel, war cries, and dying groans still echo across the grass on quiet nights.

    The tragedy left a profound supernatural legacy throughout the town, particularly around Tewkesbury Abbey and its historic surroundings. Among the most frequent apparitions is a young man dressed in a velvet doublet, believed to be 17-year-old Edward of Westminster, the Lancastrian Prince of Wales, who met a violent end during the conflict. Additionally, witnesses have reported a weeping woman in white wandering near the abbey grounds, traditionally thought to be his mother, Queen Margaret of Anjou. Down on the High Street, at Ye Olde Black Bear pub, a ghostly soldier wearing a dented and tarnished breastplate is occasionally seen standing by the window, gazing out toward the river as if awaiting an escape that never came.
