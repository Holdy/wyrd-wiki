#title
The Dragon Stops and Dovecote of Elkstone Church

#summary
Perched nearly 1,000 feet above sea level in the Cotswolds, the 12th-century Church of St John the Evangelist in Elkstone is rich in medieval architectural folklore, featuring dragon-head stops, a rare chancel dovecote, and stone carvings reflecting ancient legends of St John.

#details
Constructed around 1160, the Church of St John the Evangelist in Elkstone is celebrated for its remarkable Norman architecture and local legends embedded in its stonework. Situated nearly 1,000 feet above sea level—making it one of the highest churches in the Cotswolds—the building contains unusual architectural features that inspired local lore, including a hidden upper chamber built above the chancel vaulting to serve as a medieval dovecote (columbarium). The interior Norman chancel arches terminate in distinct dragon-head stops, while the south doorway features a carved tympanum depicting Christ in Majesty surrounded by the symbols of the Evangelists. Parish tradition connects the church's dragon and serpent motifs to medieval accounts of St John the Evangelist, such as the tale from the *Golden Legend* where St John drinks safely from a poisoned chalice from which a green dragon emerges. Furthermore, local heritage associates the village name with early Saxon settlers and an ancient pagan carved stone preserved within the church vestry.

#location
The Church of St John the Evangelist stands in the village of Elkstone, Gloucestershire (Grid Reference SO 968123).
