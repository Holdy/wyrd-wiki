    The Yatton Demoniac

    Summary

    In 1788, a tailor from Yatton became the center of a notorious national sensation after claiming to be possessed by seven demons for eighteen years. Following a public appeal, he was taken to the vestry of Temple Church in Bristol where an exorcism successfully cast out the evil spirits.

    Details

    George Lukins, a 44-year-old tailor and common carrier from Yatton, claimed that his strange malady began around 1770 when he was struck by an invisible hand while participating in a Christmas pageant or mumming play. For the next eighteen years, he suffered from violent fits, made barking and animal-like noises, sang an inverted Te Deum, and occasionally declared himself to be the devil. Despite being examined by eminent surgeons in Wrington and spending twenty weeks at St. George's Hospital in London, medical professionals could find no physical cure, leading local villagers to suspect he was bewitched. Lukins himself maintained he was possessed by seven demons who could only be driven out by the united prayers of seven clergymen.

    In May 1788, a visiting woman named Sarah Barber brought his plight to the attention of the Rev. Joseph Easterbrook, vicar of Temple Church in Bristol. On June 13, 1788, Easterbrook and six Methodist ministers gathered with Lukins in the vestry of Temple Church. During the intense prayer and hymn-singing session, as a crowd gathered outside, the clergymen commanded the evil spirits to depart in the name of the Holy Trinity. Witnesses reported hearing strange, unearthly voices arguing before the demons were cast out, after which Lukins immediately became calm, praised God, recited the Lord's Prayer, and returned to Yatton in peace. While contemporaries and later medical writers intensely debated whether the case was genuine demonic possession, epilepsy, hysteria, or elaborate imposture, the incident went down in history as England's most famous 18th-century exorcism.
