    The Devil's Door of St Andrew's

    Summary

    At St Andrew's Church in Miserden, local folklore attributes supernatural significance to an ancient north doorway and an adjacent yew tree. Parishioners historically believed that malevolent entities passed through the church, while tradition holds that the threshold is used by the souls of the dead for their final journey.

    Details

    St Andrew's Church in the Cotswold village of Miserden features an ancient Anglo-Saxon architectural detail known locally as the "Devil's Door." According to traditional beliefs held by generations of parishioners, evil forces or the devil would enter the sacred building through the main entrance and exit through this specific north-facing portal. Alongside this legend, tradition maintains that the doorway serves as the exit point for the souls of the dead as they depart the church on their final journey. Standing immediately outside this threshold is a large, ancient yew tree, whose dark, poisonous branches cast a shadow regarded by locals as a transitional boundary between the mortal world and the afterlife.
