    The Bread and Cheese Dole of St Briavels

    Summary

    Every Whit Sunday, bread and cheese are tossed from the walls of St Briavels Castle to the crowds below in an ancient custom. Local tradition holds that these pieces possess protective properties, once carried by Forest of Dean miners as charms against underground accidents.

    Details

    Dating back centuries—with written records stretching back to at least 1779—the Bread and Cheese Dole is an annual scrambling custom held every Whit Sunday at St Briavels Castle. Following the church service, small pieces of bread and cheese are thrown from the castle ramparts to the local villagers and visitors who scramble to catch them. According to local folklore, the custom originated from a medieval arrangement where villagers paid a penny to the lord of the castle for the right to gather firewood in Hudnalls Wood. Over generations, people came to believe that the distributed food carried good luck and protection, prompting local miners to keep fragments as lucky charms to protect them against accidents in the mines.
