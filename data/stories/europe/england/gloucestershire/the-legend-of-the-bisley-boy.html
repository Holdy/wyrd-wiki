The Legend of the Bisley Boy

Summary

According to local folklore, young Princess Elizabeth died while staying in Bisley during a plague outbreak and was secretly replaced by a local boy who went on to reign as Queen Elizabeth I.

Details

In the sixteenth century, as plague ravaged London, young Princess Elizabeth was sent to stay at Over Court in Bisley. According to local legend, the princess suddenly fell ill and died during her visit. Terrified of the wrath of King Henry VIII, the villagers searched frantically for someone of similar age and appearance, ultimately finding a red-headed local boy who resembled her in face and stature. They dressed him in the princess's clothes and sent him back to the royal court in her place. This deception supposedly explains why the monarch reigned as the "Virgin Queen" from 1558 to 1603, never marrying for fear that the secret would be discovered. The tale gained widespread notoriety in the nineteenth century after the local vicar, Thomas Keble, popularized the legend following the discovery of a medieval stone coffin containing a child's bones during excavations near Over Court.
