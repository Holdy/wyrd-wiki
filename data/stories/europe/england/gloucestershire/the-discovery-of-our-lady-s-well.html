    The Discovery of Our Lady's Well

    Summary

    According to local tradition, the Virgin Mary discovered the ancient spring after her boat was washed ashore by the Severn Bore during a journey to visit Joseph of Arimathea at Glastonbury. The well's waters were historically sought for their miraculous healing properties and pilgrimage cures.

    Details

    Standing on the hillside overlooking the Severn Valley in Hempsted, Our Lady's Well is a 14th-century limestone well house featuring a stone sculpture of Saint Anne, the Virgin Mary, and an angel carved into its eastern gable. Local folklore holds that the spring was originally discovered by the Virgin Mary herself when her boat was carried up the River Severn by the Severn Bore as she traveled to visit Joseph of Arimathea at Glastonbury. Climbing the steep slope from the riverbanks, she came upon the cascading spring. Over the centuries, the well became a revered site of pilgrimage where the waters were believed to possess medicinal virtues, curing various ailments of those who washed within them—earning the site the alternative folk moniker "Our Lady's Washhouse."
