#title
The Bishop's Curse at Blockley Church

#summary
When a visiting bishop discovered a local priest neglecting his duties, his sudden curse struck the man dead before divine mercy brought him back to life as a reformed character.

#details
Around 1060 or 1070, St. Wulfstan, the Bishop of Worcester, journeyed to the settlement of Blockley to celebrate Easter Mass. Upon inspecting the local parish, he found the resident priest to be indolent, disrespectful, and utterly neglectful of his sacred responsibilities. Filled with righteous wrath, the bishop rebuked and cursed the priest. Instantly struck down by the force of the curse, the unfortunate man collapsed and appeared dead. Overcome with sudden remorse, Wulfstan knelt and prayed fervently for divine forgiveness, whereupon the priest miraculously revived, instantly transformed into a deeply pious and devoted servant of the church.

#location
St Peter & St Paul's Church, situated on a prominent knoll in the village of Blockley, Gloucestershire.

#title
The Corpse Road of Blockley

#summary
For centuries, the medieval corpse road linking the hamlet of Aston Magna to St Peter and St Paul's Church in Blockley was walked by grieving villagers, generating enduring folklore that restless spirits and phantom funeral processions still traversed the route.

#details
In the medieval parish system, outlying hamlets such as Aston Magna possessed only small chapels without burial rights, meaning all deceased parishioners had to be carried along a specific, direct path—known as a corpse road—to the mother church of St Peter and St Paul in Blockley for burial. Because folklore dictated that the dead traveled straight and low to the ground like crows, these paths were laid out as directly as possible across fields and streams. Superstition held that running water could prevent a spirit from finding its way back home, so corpse roads were deliberately routed across brooks and fords, such as Blockley Brook near the old silk mill, where bearers could rest the coffin on flat stones. Over generations of frequent use, locals whispered tales of phantom funeral processions moving silently along the route in the dark, and spectral watchers keeping vigil over the ancient pathway.

#location
The corpse road running between Aston Magna and St Peter and St Paul's Church in Blockley, Gloucestershire.
