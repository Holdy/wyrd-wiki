    The Specter of Brockeridge Common

    Summary

    In January 1934, multiple witnesses on Brockeridge Common reported terrifying encounters with a glowing, white-clad female apparition whose eyes emitted a bright light.

    Details

    In January 1934, the Tewkesbury Register and Agricultural Gazette published accounts of a ghostly woman haunting Brockeridge Common in the parish of Twyning. Intrigued by widespread local rumors, two friends decided to investigate the common on a Sunday night, waiting near some sheds until 11:45 PM. Hearing a piercing, woman-like cry, they witnessed a bright light emanating from a nearby wooded area known as Vauxhall Cover. The light approached them, revealing a figure of a woman dressed in white with brilliant light shining directly from her eyes. The apparition glided past them silently toward the main road before returning to the cover and entering the cellar ruins of a large house. After uttering another terrible cry, the ghost vanished across ploughed and green fields, ultimately ascending Fowborough Hill, where it circled several times as if searching for something. Subsequent inquiries by newspaper reporters confirmed that local cyclists and groups of men keeping watch had also encountered the alarming spook on the common.
