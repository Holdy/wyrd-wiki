#title
The Hermit Princess of Chapel Rock

#summary
According to local legend, a 4th-century Welsh princess who retired as a hermit to a tidal islet off Beachley Point was brutally murdered by sea raiders in her cell.

#details
In the 4th or 5th century, a princess from Gwynedd named Tecla abandoned her father's royal court to seek a solitary religious life as an anchoress. She settled upon Chapel Rock, a tiny, isolated tidal island in the Severn Estuary just off Beachley Point. There, she lived a life of devotion, but her peaceful retreat ended tragically when sea pirates or raiders attacked the islet and murdered her in her cell. Centuries later, the ruins of a 13th-century chapel—and an earlier dark-age sanctuary—still mark the site, where a holy well was once celebrated for its healing properties and where visitors faced treacherous tidal waters to remember the martyred princess.

#location
Chapel Rock, a tiny tidal island in the Severn Estuary approximately half a mile south-west of Beachley Point, Gloucestershire (OS Grid Reference ST5484 9002).

#title
The Royalist Ferryman of Beachley

#summary
During the English Civil War, a hostile ferryman deceitfully marooned two Royalist Cavaliers on a Severn Estuary sandbank where they drowned in the rapidly rising tide. Today, their spectral figures are said to haunt the shores near the old crossing.

#details
During the English Civil War, Parliament's control of the Severn bridge at Gloucester forced Royalists traveling between Gloucestershire and South Wales to use the ancient ferries across the estuary. At the Old Passage connecting Aust and Beachley, two Cavaliers sought transport across the turbulent waters to reach King Charles's supporters in Wales. Annoyed by their aristocratic entitlement—or driven by Parliamentarian sympathies—the ferryman took vengeance under the cover of thick sea mist and darkness. Instead of landing them safely, he deposited the unsuspecting soldiers upon a submerged sandbank. Trapped as the fierce Severn tide rapidly surged around them, the Cavaliers perished in the floodwaters. According to local legend, their spectral figures still wander the mud and reedbeds by night, desperately seeking a path to safety as the phantom tide rises.

#location
The Old Passage ferry slipway and peninsula at Beachley where the River Severn and River Wye converge in Gloucestershire.
