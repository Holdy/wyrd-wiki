#title
The Great Boddington Oak

#summary
For centuries, the parish of Boddington was famed for a colossal ancient oak tree in the manor grounds whose hollow base measured eighteen yards in circumference before it was destroyed by fire in 1790.

#details
For generations, the grounds of Boddington Manor in Gloucestershire harbored a natural marvel known simply as the Boddington Oak. Renowned across the region for its immense scale, the ancient tree became a celebrated local landmark. By 1783, its massive trunk had grown hollow with age, yet it still stood proud, boasting a circumference of eighteen yards around its base. Villagers and travelers alike regarded the giant oak with awe as a silent witness to centuries of local history. Tragically, this living monument's long reign came to an end in 1790, when the ancient tree caught fire and was completely consumed by the flames, leaving behind only memories and local renown.

#location
The site of the ancient Boddington Oak is located within the grounds of Boddington Manor in the parish of Boddington, Gloucestershire.
