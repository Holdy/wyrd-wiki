    The Moving Stones and Screaming Wall of Nympsfield Long Barrow

    Summary

    Local folklore tells of the neolithic Nympsfield Long Barrow as a shunned leper hospice where the stones mysteriously roll down into the valley at night, and recounts the terror of a villager who stole a slab for his wall.

    Details

    For generations, villagers around Nympsfield held a superstitious fear of the ancient Nympsfield Long Barrow, believing the site to have once been a hospice for lepers. Beyond this dark reputation, the monument was surrounded by bizarre legends regarding the megaliths themselves. It was widely believed that the heavy stones composing the structure possessed a life of their own, capable of rolling down the hill into the valley on certain nights before returning to the ridge. In one specific tale, a local man disregarded the taboos surrounding the ancient barrow and removed a stone slab to use as building material in his own wall. However, every night the stone remained in his masonry, he was tormented by terrifying, unearthly screams, prompting him to quickly tow the slab back and re-erect it at the monument.
