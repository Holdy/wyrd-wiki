#title
The Little Rissington UFO Encounter

#summary
In October 1952, two military pilots flying a training aircraft from RAF Little Rissington encountered three saucer-shaped objects at high altitude, an event corroborated by military and civilian radar stations.

#details
On the afternoon of October 21, 1952, Flight Lieutenant Michael Swiney and Royal Navy student Lieutenant David Crofts took off in a Gloster Meteor trainer jet from the RAF Little Rissington air base in Gloucestershire for a high-level navigation flight. Upon punching through a layer of cloud at around 15,000 feet, Swiney was startled to see three white, circular, saucer-shaped objects positioned ahead of them. Initially mistaking them for parachutes, the pilots climbed to 35,000 feet to investigate while maintaining steady radio communication. The objects, emitting an iridescent glow without any visible means of propulsion, remained stationary before crossing to the starboard side of the aircraft. After roughly ten minutes of observation, the objects abruptly vanished. Ground radar stations in southern England and civilian air traffic control at Gloucester confirmed tracking the anomalous targets moving at high speed, prompting an official Air Ministry debriefing and keeping the account shrouded in military secrecy for decades.

#location
The skies above the former RAF Little Rissington airfield (now the village of Upper Rissington) in the Cotswold District of eastern Gloucestershire, situated just west of the A424 road.
