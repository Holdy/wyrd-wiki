#title
The Ghostly Coach of Chavenage House

#summary
Following his role in the trial and execution of King Charles I, Parliamentarian Colonel Nathaniel Stephens was cursed by his daughter, and upon his death a spectral black coach driven by a headless coachman was seen carrying away his soul.

#details
During the English Civil War, Chavenage House was owned by Colonel Nathaniel Stephens, a Member of Parliament and relative of Oliver Cromwell. Cromwell pressured Stephens into supporting the trial and execution of King Charles I, prompting Stephens to remark that the best cure for a sick plant was cutting off its head. Horrified by her father's complicity, Stephens's daughter placed a curse on the family. Shortly afterward, Nathaniel fell ill and died. At the exact moment of his death, a spectral black coach and horses, driven by a headless coachman, drew up at the entrance of Chavenage House. The ghost of Nathaniel, dressed in his burial shroud, emerged from the house, boarded the carriage, and was driven away. Legend maintains that every subsequent owner who dies at Chavenage departs in this same supernatural fashion.

#location
Chavenage House is located approximately 2 miles northwest of Tetbury in Gloucestershire, accessible via local country roads off the B4014.
