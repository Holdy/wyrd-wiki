#title
The Legend of Benedict's Pool

#summary
In the hamlet of Grafton near Ashton under Hill, a deep pool bears the name of a wicked monk who threw his ill-gotten wealth and himself into its waters, resulting in centuries of hauntings and poltergeist activity until clergy intervened.

#details
According to local tradition, a monk named Benedict robbed the helpless and committed numerous misdeeds before plunging himself and his stolen treasures into a deep, bottomless pool at the foot of Bredon Hill. His body was never recovered, and his perturbed spirit began walking the area, frequently startling country folk. Over the centuries, whenever attempts were made to clear or fill in the overgrown pool, violent poltergeist disturbances erupted at an adjacent mansion built on the site of the former monastery, causing furniture, china, and pictures to crash to the ground and shatter. To put an end to the supernatural turmoil, twelve priests carrying twelve lighted candles processed to the pool to perform a solemn laying ceremony. Although the spirit was supposedly quieted and the wealth restored, villagers continued to avoid the dark, withy-darkened pool, particularly on moonlit nights.

#location
Benedict's Pool in the hamlet of Grafton, near Ashton under Hill at the foot of Bredon Hill.

#upstream-source
The Cheltenham Looker On (August 6, 1910), with later recounting in Fred Archer's *Benedict's Pool*.
