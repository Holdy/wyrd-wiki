#title
The Legend of Cromwell's Cannon at Haresfield Beacon

#summary
Local legend maintains that during the English Civil War siege of Gloucester in 1643, Oliver Cromwell used Haresfield Beacon as a vantage point to fire a cannon at the cathedral tower, prompting defenders to hang protective wool sacks around it.

#details
According to local Cotswold tradition and oral history, Oliver Cromwell surveyed his armies and military positions from the commanding heights of Haresfield Beacon during the Parliamentarian siege of Gloucester in 1643. Local legend vividly recounts that Cromwell fired a cannon shot from the beacon toward the distant tower of Gloucester Cathedral. To protect the stonework from the bombardment, the townspeople and defenders reportedly hung wool sacks around the tower to absorb the impact. Although military historians note the extreme distance makes direct artillery fire from the hill improbable, the tale became deeply embedded in local folklore and is commemorated on Haresfield Hill by the Cromwell Stone.

#location
The Cromwell Stone on Haresfield Hill (Haresfield Beacon), Haresfield, Gloucestershire.
