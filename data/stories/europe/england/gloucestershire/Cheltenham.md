#title
The Cheltenham Ghost

#summary
Beginning in 1882, multiple members of the Despard family and their household staff repeatedly witnessed the apparition of a veiled woman in black within their home on Pittville Circus Road.

#details
In April 1882, Captain Despard and his family moved into a house known as 'Donore' (formerly 'Garden Reach') on Pittville Circus Road in Cheltenham. Soon after, in June, the captain's daughter Rosina (writing under the pseudonym 'Rose Morton') became the first to encounter a mysterious figure: a tall woman dressed in soft black woolen material with her face partially hidden in a handkerchief and a widow's cuff visible on her wrists. Over the next few years, seventeen different people—including family members and domestic servants—saw the apparition, often noting how lifelike and physical it appeared before vanishing. The haunting became one of the earliest and most thoroughly investigated cases by the Society for Psychical Research, documented extensively by Frederic Myers and Rosina Despard, and remains one of Britain's most celebrated ghost accounts.

#location
Pittville Circus Road, Cheltenham, Gloucestershire (specifically at the house formerly known as 'Donore' or 'Garden Reach').

#upstream-source
R.C. Morton, *Record of a Haunted House* (Proceedings of the Society for Psychical Research, 1892).

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cheltenham_Ghost

#title
The Cheltenham Ghost of Pittville Circus Road

#summary
Between 1882 and 1889, residents of a house in Cheltenham repeatedly witnessed the apparition of a tall woman dressed in black widow's weeds with her face concealed by a handkerchief. This exceptionally well-documented haunting became one of the foundational cases investigated by the Society for Psychical Research.

#details
In April 1882, Captain F.W. Despard and his family moved into a newly rented house on Pittville Circus Road in Cheltenham, known variously as 'Garden Reach' or 'Donore'. Shortly thereafter, in June 1882, the Captain's nineteen-year-old daughter Rosina encountered a strange figure standing at the head of the stairs. The apparition appeared as a tall woman dressed in black woollen garments, wearing widow's cuffs on her wrists, and holding a handkerchief to her face so that only her forehead and hair were visible. Over the next several years, the figure was seen by at least seventeen different witnesses within the household, who frequently heard her footsteps pacing the hallways. Despite attempts to speak to, corner, or photograph the apparition, it remained elusive, giving only a slight gasp when addressed directly before fading away. Frederic Myers of the Society for Psychical Research investigated the phenomena, concluding it was one of the best-authenticated hauntings on record, and tentatively identified the figure from old photographs as Imogen Swinhoe, the troubled second wife of a former resident who had died years prior.

#location
Garden Reach (later St. Anne's House) on Pittville Circus Road in Cheltenham, Gloucestershire.
