#title
Gladys' Leap

#summary
To avoid a lengthy two-mile detour on her postal round, a Gloucestershire postwoman routinely jumped across a woodland brook, leading to the spot being officially named in her honor.

#details
For 35 years, from 1942 to 1977, Gladys Hillier served as the village postwoman for Cranham in Gloucestershire. Rather than taking a long, winding two-mile detour to deliver mail across the rural landscape, she chose a daring shortcut: leaping across a three-foot-wide brook, which is a tributary of the Painswick Stream located within Cranham Woods. Her daily athletic feat became a beloved local anecdote, eventually prompting the Ordnance Survey to officially map and name the crossing "Gladys' Leap" in 1977. In 2005, a permanent footbridge was constructed across the brook to spare future mail carriers the muddy leap, cementing the local legend that also famously inspired the title of folk-rock band Fairport Convention's 1985 studio album.

#location
Gladys' Leap is located over a tributary of the Painswick Stream in Cranham Woods, near Cranham, Gloucestershire (51°48′26″N 2°09′36″W).
