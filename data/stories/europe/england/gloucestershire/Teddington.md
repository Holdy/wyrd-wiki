#title
The Giant of Dixton Hill and the Tibblestone

#summary
A giant attempting to hurl a massive stone at pirates on the River Severn slipped and dropped it near Teddington. The resulting monolith, known as the Tibblestone, remains a landmark on the local landscape.

#details
According to local folklore, a giant living on Dixton Hill sought to attack pirates navigating up the River Severn. He picked up a massive standing stone and wound up to hurl it toward the river. However, as he threw, his foot slipped, causing him to stumble and drop the stone far short of his target near Teddington. The giant's slip left a noticeable scar on the hillside, while the hurled boulder landed by the roadside, becoming known as the ancient Tibblestone. Centuries later, after briefly disappearing, the prehistoric monolith was rediscovered during landscaping work at a local garage forecourt, where it continues to stand as a historic boundary marker.

#location
The Tibblestone stands on a garage forecourt off the A46 near the Teddington roundabout in Gloucestershire.
