#title
The Half-Flesh Spectre of Toddington Manor

#summary
Two robbers fleeing from the grounds of Toddington Manor were terrified by a nightmarish apparition of a man who was half flesh and half skeleton. This spectral ghoul is widely believed to be the tormented spirit of Sir William de Tracy, one of the knights who murdered Thomas Becket.

#details
Toddington Manor in Gloucestershire was long held by the Tracy family, descendants of Sir William de Tracy, one of the four knights who assassinated Thomas Becket in Canterbury Cathedral in 1170. According to local legend, Sir William met a gruesome end, dying of leprosy during a crusade of repentance in the Holy Land after reportedly tearing the rotting flesh from his own bones in agony. A local story recounts a night when two robbers broke into the estate's grounds and were confronted by this horrific phantom figure—a man appearing half flesh and half skeleton—causing them to run screaming in terror from the property.

#location
Toddington Manor is a 19th-century Gothic country house situated near the village of Toddington in Gloucestershire, England.
