    The Murder of Thomas Roberts

    Summary

    In April 1750, a fifteen-year-old boy named Thomas Roberts from Laverton was stabbed to death during an altercation with a local youth named Richard Cull, whose mother allegedly encouraged the attack. A memorial tombstone preserving the tragic verse detailing the event stands inside St Michael's Church in neighbouring Buckland.

    Details

    In April 1750, fifteen-year-old Thomas Roberts, son of Isaac and Mary Roberts from the Gloucestershire village of Laverton, was playing innocently when he became embroiled in a dispute or affray with Richard Cull, another local youth from Laverton. During the conflict, Richard Cull drew a knife and fatally wounded Thomas. According to local accounts recorded on his memorial, Richard’s mother stood by and actively encouraged the tragic act. A pieced-together stone tombstone in the south aisle of St Michael's Church in Buckland preserves the verse commemorating young Thomas and warning against blood guilt.
