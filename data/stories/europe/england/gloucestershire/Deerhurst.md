#title
The Dragon of Walton Hill

#summary
A ferocious serpent once terrorised the parish of Deerhurst until a daring labourer named John Smith plied the beast with milk and decapitated it with a single blow of his axe, earning the estate of Walton Hill as his reward.

#details
According to local tradition recorded by early historians, a massive dragon or serpent of prodigious bigness once made its home in the parish of Deerhurst, poisoning the villagers with its breath and devouring their livestock. To rid the land of this deadly pest, a royal decree was issued promising the estate of Walton Hill to anyone brave enough to slay the monster. A labourer named John Smith devised a cunning plan: knowing the dragon's habits, he left a large quantity of milk where the beast was accustomed to rest. The creature drank its fill, grew heavy and stupefied, and fell fast asleep. Seizing the moment, Smith crept up and struck the dragon between its impenetrable scales with an axe, severing its head in one mighty stroke. As promised, Smith received the lands at Walton Hill, and stone carvings of dragons can still be seen in Deerhurst Church today.

#location
Walton Hill lies just to the east of Deerhurst parish and north-west of Walton Farm in Gloucestershire.

#upstream-source
Originally recorded by the 17th-century Gloucestershire historian Robert Atkyns in 1712, and later detailed by Rev. George Butterworth in *Deerhurst: a Parish in the Vale of Gloucester* (Tewkesbury 1887).
