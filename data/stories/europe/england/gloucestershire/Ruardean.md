#title
The Bears of Ruardean

#summary
In 1889, an angry mob in the Forest of Dean brutally attacked and killed two performing Russian bears after false rumors spread that they had harmed villagers. The slain animals were allegedly buried in the garden of the local police station, giving rise to an enduring local taunt that still follows residents today.

#details
In the autumn of 1889, four Frenchmen arrived in the Forest of Dean leading two Russian performing bears. As the men and their animals traveled toward Ruardean, a panic-fueled rumor spread that the bears had mauled a woman and killed a child in the village, supposedly fed on human flesh. Enraged and fearful residents launched a violent attack, slaughtering the innocent bears and beating their handlers. Although other villagers stepped in to rescue and nurse the injured men, and the attackers were later heavily fined, the village became unfairly stigmatized. To this day, locals are frequently teased with the mocking refrain, "Who killed the bears?" while local folklore suggests the unfortunate animals were buried beneath the garden of the village's former police station.

#location
The former police station located in the village of Ruardean, Gloucestershire.
