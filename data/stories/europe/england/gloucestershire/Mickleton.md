#title
The Mickleton Hooter

#summary
A terrifying unearthly shrieking and howling known as the "Mickleton Hooter" has long been reported to echo around midnight through Mickleton Hollow, a deep wooded glen running up from the village.

#details
In the parish of Mickleton, deep within Mickleton Hollow (sometimes referred to as Weeping Hollow)—a wooded glen running up towards the hills—locals for centuries reported hearing a strange, unearthly booming, shrieking, and howling sound around midnight, attributed to a ghostly entity known as the "Mickleton Hooter" or "Belhowja." Because the sound could never be visually traced to any living animal, numerous local legends sprang up to explain its origin. One tradition connected the noise to the ghostly lowing of the monstrous Dun Cow, which was hunted across the Midlands and wounded near Mickleton by Guy of Warwick in the 13th century. Another local version maintained that the haunting sounds were the lamenting ghost of Sir Edward Greville's only son, who was accidentally shot and killed in Mickleton Hollow. Alternatively, folklorists noted that "hooter" is an old regional word for a ghostly hound, linking the sound to phantom packs of hounds, spectral coaches, and black dogs said to roam the surrounding borders of Mickleton and Meon Hill.

#location
Mickleton Hollow, a deep wooded glen running from Mickleton towards the Ilmington Hills on the Gloucestershire-Warwickshire border.
