#title
The Ghostly Guardian of the Frocester Bells

#summary
Four men attempting to steal church bells from the abandoned St Peter's Church in Frocester were thwarted by the ghostly apparition of a devout woman who had cast her own jewels into the bell metal in 1639. To this day, her phantom continues to haunt the lonely church tower and graveyard.

#details
In the 18th century, four men from nearby Coaley decided to steal the church bells from the crumbling, abandoned St Peter’s Church in Frocester. Dragging a donkey and cart down a dark lane, they forced open the sagging doors of the ruined church, lowered a bell, and inspected it by the dim light of rushlights. The bell was cast in 1639 by William Whitmore and contained jewels that a pious woman had thrown into the molten metal during its creation. 

As the thieves prepared to load the heavy bell onto their cart, they discovered an old woman squatting silently upon it, glaring at them. When they attempted to move the bell with her on it, it refused to budge. Realising through her semi-transparent form that she was a phantom, the terrified men fled empty-handed. Though the church was eventually demolished in 1952—leaving only the isolated tower standing—the ghostly woman is still occasionally sighted in the churchyard, eternally guarding the historic site.

#location
The solitary tower of the ruined St Peter's Church standing in its historic graveyard in Frocester, Gloucestershire.
