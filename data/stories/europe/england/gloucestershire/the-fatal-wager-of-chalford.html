The Fatal Wager of Chalford

Summary

In February 1766, a gentleman visiting Chalford to play cards uttered a terrible oath during a dispute over the game, after which his flesh mysteriously began to rot until he died. This eerie account was documented in contemporary parish records and the Annual Register.

Details

In February 1766, a Minchinhampton gentleman named Richard Parsons visited a friend's private house in Chalford to play cards. The evening proceeded pleasantly until late at night, when a heated dispute arose over the state of the game. Parsons insisted that they were six, while his opponents maintained they were five. Furious, Parsons swore with fearful curses, wishing that he might never enter heaven and that his flesh might rot upon his bones if they were not six in the game. Shortly after, a bystander blew out the candle out of shock at his blasphemy. The men subsequently moved to another house to continue playing, during which Parsons began complaining of a severe pain in his leg. Over the following days, a mortification rapidly spread across his limbs, causing his flesh to discolor and rot. Despite medical attention and the ministrations of the parish clergyman who administered the sacrament, Parsons deteriorated quickly amidst delirium and fearful trembling, dying within the month. When he passed away on March 4, 1766, his eyes remained stubbornly open, and he was buried the following day at the parish church of Bisley.
