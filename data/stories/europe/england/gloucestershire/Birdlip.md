#title
The Prehistoric Apparitions of West Tump

#summary
Following the excavation of ancient burial mounds near Birdlip, locals reported encountering strange, tattooed figures in leather cloaks near the West Tump long barrow. These eerie encounters fueled local belief that prehistoric spirits had been awakened from their graves.

#details
After late 19th-century archaeological excavations disturbed the ancient long barrows and burial mounds scattered through the woods around Birdlip and Cranham, rumours of uncanny occurrences quickly spread among local villagers. It was widely believed that disturbing these ancient resting places had prevented the dead from finding peace. 

The most prominent encounter happened when a young woman was walking along the path from Birdlip toward Cranham. As she passed West Tump deep within the woods, she suddenly spotted figures unlike anyone she had ever encountered. Wearing long leather cloaks with faces marked by intricate tattoos and carrying spears tipped with leaf-shaped stones, the figures resembled prehistoric people. Terrified by the sight, she fled through the bracken, solidifying a lasting local legend that the ancient inhabitants of the barrows still wander the wooded ridges of Birdlip.

#location
West Tump long barrow, situated within the woodland hills between Birdlip and Cranham in Gloucestershire.

Lesley Grinsell, "Gloucestershire Barrows" (Bristol and Gloucestershire Archaeological Society Journal, Volume 79)

#title
The Shepherd and the Devil of Birdlip Hill

#summary
A resourceful shepherd walking up Birdlip Hill outwits the Devil by tricking him into drinking a foul-tasting sheep medicine disguised as spiced ale. Consequently, the enraged Devil roars in agony and flees the area permanently.

#details
A shepherd walking up Birdlip Hill after fetching medicine for a sick ewe knew the Devil was lying in wait to catch travelers on the road. Knowing that horses refused to travel that route due to the fiend's presence, the shepherd went on foot, carrying a drinking horn filled with ale and his parcel of pungent sheep tonic. As he neared the turn by Black Dog's Lane, he took a sip of ale, poured the rest of the boiled sheep medicine into the horn, and continued onward. The Devil promptly sprang out and demanded the ale. Handing over the horn while taking to his heels, the shepherd watched as the Devil eagerly gulped down the concoction. The vile sheep tonic caused the Devil to let out a roar heard all the way in Cheltenham, and he has never dared go near Birdlip Hill since.

#location
The events take place on Birdlip Hill along the road near Black Dog's Lane in Birdlip, Gloucestershire.
