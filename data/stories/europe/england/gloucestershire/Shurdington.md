#title
The Legend of the Devil's Chimney

#summary
Provoked by the Christian churches in the region, the Devil hurled rocks from Leckhampton Hill at Sunday churchgoers, only for the stones to rebound and trap him underground, leaving his fiery chimney protruding above. Locals long placated him by leaving coins atop the rock formation to prevent him from escaping and causing mischief.

#details
Standing prominently on Leckhampton Hill near Shurdington, the bizarre limestone rock formation known as the Devil's Chimney has captured the imagination of generations. According to local folklore, the Devil was deeply angered by the proliferation of Christian churches built across the area. Seeking vengeance, he would sit upon the heights of Leckhampton Hill every Sunday and hurl massive stones down at pious villagers making their way to prayer. However, divine justice intervened, causing the hurled rocks to rebound back upon him. The barrage buried the Devil deep beneath the ground and piled heavy stones upon him so he could never escape. Trapped in his subterranean lair, he was forced to use the rock pillar as his chimney to vent the smoke and fumes of hell. In past centuries, superstitious visitors climbing the hill would leave a coin on top of the rock as payment to the Devil, ensuring he would remain quiet in his underground prison and refrain from wreaking havoc on the surrounding countryside.

#location
The Devil's Chimney is located on the western edge of Leckhampton Hill, immediately south of Cheltenham and overlooking Shurdington in Gloucestershire.
