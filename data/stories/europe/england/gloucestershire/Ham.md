#title
The Origin of the Spotted Swine

#summary
According to local tradition, the distinctive black spots of the Berkeley Vale's famous pigs were caused by heavy cider apples falling from orchard trees onto their backs. This rustic phenomenon became an enduring piece of folklore across the parish of Ham and the surrounding Berkeley Vale.

#details
In the rural Gloucestershire parish of Ham, near Berkeley, cider production was once central to agricultural life, with orchards surrounding local farmsteads and taverns. According to long-standing local folklore, the unique appearance of the Gloucestershire Old Spot pig—known for its characteristic black spots—originated from the abundance of heavy cider apples growing in the local orchards. As free-roaming pigs foraged beneath laden fruit trees during harvest season, heavy apples would frequently drop from the branches and strike them squarely on the back. The repeated bruising from these falling apples supposedly left permanent marks on the animals' hides, creating the iconic spotted pattern that came to define the breed throughout the Berkeley Vale.

#location
The orchards and rural lanes surrounding the village of Ham, near Berkeley in Gloucestershire.
