    The Despicable Dunsdons of Icomb and Wychwood

    Summary

    In the 18th century, three notorious brothers known as the Dunsdon gang used a secret cottage in Icomb as their base to terrorize the Cotswolds with highway robbery and burglary, culminating in a gruesome botched heist where one brother's arm was severed.

    Details

    During the latter half of the eighteenth century, the Cotswolds were plagued by the Dunsdon brothers—Tom, Dick, and Harry—who became Gloucestershire's most notorious highwaymen and burglars. Hailing from nearby Fulbrook, the trio used a cottage in the Gloucestershire village of Icomb as one of their primary secret bases and hideouts. From this refuge, they launched raids across the region, most notably holding up the Oxford to Gloucester stagecoach and making off with a massive haul of cash.

    Their criminal career met a grisly turning point when they targeted Tangley Hall between Stow-on-the-Wold and Burford. Unbeknownst to the brothers, the household had been forewarned after the gang discussed their plans in a local tavern. When Dick reached his arm through the house's Judas hole to unlatch the door, the waiting butler and constable slipped a noose around his hand and tied it tightly to the inner handle. As Dick shrieked in terror for help, his desperate brothers outside swung a sword at the door, hacking off Dick's arm entirely to free themselves while leaving the severed limb trapped behind. Dick bled to death from his wounds shortly afterward, while Tom and Harry continued their criminal exploits until they were finally captured following a botched shooting at an inn in 1784, leading to their trial, execution, and enduring place in local folklore.
