#title
Betty's Grave

#summary
A rural crossroads near Poulton is named Betty's Grave after a mysterious figure of folklore variously remembered as a hardworking reaper, an executed witch, or a persistent beggar.

#details
Betty's Grave is a quiet rural crossroads located a quarter of a mile from the Gloucestershire village of Poulton, appearing on Ordnance Survey maps as early as 1830. Local tradition preserves several conflicting accounts of who Betty was and how she came to be buried at the crossroads. One legend portrays her as a virtuous and hardworking girl who wagered she could cut an acre of corn with a sickle faster than any man, dropping dead from exhaustion upon completion. Another version describes her as a mute woman suspected of witchcraft who was attacked and hanged by local townspeople; when her ghost repeatedly rose from the ground, the villagers vowed to lay flowers on her grave to keep her at rest. A further variant tells of a troublesome beggar who repeatedly froze to death under village hedges, eventually being buried at the crossroads after multiple resurrections convinced locals of her uncanny nature.

#location
Betty's Grave is a crossroads located a quarter of a mile outside Poulton, Gloucestershire, reached via Bell Lane.
