#title
The Last Tree of Kingswood Forest

#summary
Standing beside the old workhouse on the ridge between Yate and Chipping Sodbury, a massive hollow oak tree was long held by local tradition to be the final surviving remnant of the ancient royal Kingswood Forest.

#details
For centuries, the royal Kingswood Forest dominated the landscape of South Gloucestershire, stretching across the borders of Yate and Bristol as a vast royal hunting ground and woodland. As civilization encroached and the great forest was gradually cleared for agriculture, industry, and settlements, local oral tradition maintained that one ancient sentinel remained standing. Situated right next to the 19th-century workhouse (now the Ridgewood Centre) on the edge of Ridge Wood in Yate, a colossal, hollow oak tree became a prominent local landmark. Well into the 20th century, tramps and travelers passing through the area would use its vast hollow interior to store their possessions before entering the confines of the workhouse. Passed down through generations, local legend steadfastly referred to this specific oak as the very last living tree of the great Kingswood Forest, serving as a tangible, living link to the medieval wilderness that once defined the region.

#location
The Ridgewood Centre, located on the boundary between Yate and Chipping Sodbury, South Gloucestershire.
