#title
The Midnight Drink of the Whittlestone

#summary
According to local legend in Lower Swell, the ancient megalith known as the Whittlestone comes alive at midnight when it hears the church clock strike in nearby Stow-on-the-Wold, making its way down the hill to Lady's Well to drink.

#details
In the village of Lower Swell in Gloucestershire, an ancient monolith called the Whittlestone (or Whistlestone)—believed to be the last surviving remnant of a prehistoric burial chamber—is the subject of enduring local folklore. Village tradition dictates that when the stone hears the church clock from a mile away in Stow-on-the-Wold strike twelve, the massive boulder rouses itself and travels down the hill to Lady's Well to take a drink. Tales from the village also recount that the stone possessed an unyielding, almost supernatural resistance to human interference; past attempts to move or cast it down were met with local awe, as old villagers maintained that neither all the king's horses nor all the king's men could truly conquer its ancient spirit.

#location
The Whittlestone, a prehistoric standing stone located in the village of Lower Swell, Gloucestershire.
