#title
The Murder of King Edmund at Pucklechurch

#summary
During a feast celebrating the Feast of St. Augustine in 946 AD, King Edmund I of England was stabbed to death by an exiled robber after intervening in a brawl at his royal villa. The bloody confrontation ended the young king's reign and left a dark, enduring legend in the village.

#details
On May 26, 946 AD, King Edmund I and his court gathered at the royal hunting lodge and villa in Pucklechurch to celebrate the Feast of St. Augustine of Canterbury. During the festivities, the king noticed an uninvited guest seated among the company: an outlaw and thief named Leofa (or Liofa), whom Edmund had banished years earlier for his crimes. While the rest of the revellers were flushed with wine and failed to notice, the king flew into a sudden rage. Leaping from the table, Edmund grabbed the intruder by the hair and threw him to the ground. As the king struggled with him, Leofa drew a concealed dagger and plunged it deep into Edmund's chest, inflicting a fatal wound. The king's attendants immediately fell upon the assassin, hacking him to pieces, but not before Leofa had wounded several members of the royal household. King Edmund died from his injuries at the age of twenty-five and was subsequently buried at Glastonbury Abbey.

#location
The site of the ancient royal villa and hunting lodge where King Edmund was murdered is located directly behind the former Star Inn in the village of Pucklechurch, South Gloucestershire.
