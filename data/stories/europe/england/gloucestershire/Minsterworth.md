#title
The Prisoners of the Apple Tree

#summary
In the eighteenth century, the seventeenth-century farmhouse now known as the Apple Tree Inn served as a young offenders institute where troubled youths were said to be kept in chains. Local legend maintains that the gloomy atmosphere and echoes of these captive boys left a lasting imprint on the ancient structure.

#details
Constructed originally in the seventeenth century as a farmhouse belonging to the local Lord of the Manor, the historic building in Minsterworth underwent a grim change of purpose by the eighteenth century when it was repurposed into a young offenders institution. According to local folklore and longstanding oral tradition, young boys were held within its stone walls under harsh penal conditions, frequently kept bound in iron chains. Long after the institution closed and the building transitioned through use by the prominent Harvey family, years of hospitality, and eventually operating as a licensed public house in the 1950s, local tales continued to recount the presence of the captive youths and the dark history of the site.

#location
The Apple Tree Inn stands prominently alongside the A48 road in the village of Minsterworth, Gloucestershire.
