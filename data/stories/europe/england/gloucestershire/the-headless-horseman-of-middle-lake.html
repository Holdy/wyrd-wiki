    The Headless Horseman of Middle Lake

    Summary

    According to local legend, a treacherous knight who murdered his cousin in a bloody duel was cut down near the waters of Woodchester Park, where his headless specter still rides today.

    Details

    Centuries ago, the secluded valley of Woodchester Park was the stage for a bitter family feud. Sir Rupert de Lansigny murdered his young cousin in a duel in a ruthless bid to claim ownership of the estate. When the boy's grieving father stepped forward to seek justice, a man named John de Tresthaye intervened to fight in his stead. Realizing he was overmatched by Tresthaye's swordsmanship, Sir Rupert panicked and attempted to flee on horseback. He was pursued down to the edge of the Middle Lake, where he was cut down and slain. Ever since, locals and visitors have reported sightings of Sir Rupert's headless apparition galloping on a phantom horse alongside the waters of the lake and the grounds of the estate.
