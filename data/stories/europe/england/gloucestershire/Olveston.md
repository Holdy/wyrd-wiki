#title
The Great Storm and the Fall of the Steeple

#summary
During a violent tempest in 1604, a lightning strike shattered the soaring spire of the village church, an event recorded in contemporary accounts as fearful news. The ruined steeple was subsequently dismantled and never rebuilt, leaving the church with the truncated tower seen today.

#details
In 1604, a fierce storm swept across Gloucestershire, bringing torrential rain and violent gales. During the height of the tempest, a bolt of lightning struck the towering stone spire of St Mary the Virgin Church in Olveston. The lightning tore through the upper masonry, setting the steeple ablaze and causing catastrophic structural damage. This harrowing event was documented in contemporary broadsheets and pamphlets under the title *"Fearful Newes"*. Rather than rebuilding the soaring spire, the parishioners repaired the remaining structure into a truncated tower, which stands to this day. Stone salvaged from the rebuilding of the church tower was later utilized in the mid-seventeenth century to enlarge the nearby White Hart Inn.

#location
St Mary the Virgin Church is situated at the heart of Olveston village in South Gloucestershire, sharing the main crossroads with Church Hill and The Green.

#upstream-source
*Fearful Newes* (contemporary pamphlet/account), referenced in the Olveston Parish Historical Society publication *Yesteryear*.
