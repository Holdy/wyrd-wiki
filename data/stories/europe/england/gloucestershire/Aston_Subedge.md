#title
The Burning of Burnt Norton

#summary
Following a descent into grief and madness after his son's death, 18th-century baronet Sir William Keyt set fire to his grand mansion at Aston Subedge, perishing in the flames alongside his dark obsessions.

#details
In the early 1700s, Sir William Keyt, master of Norton House in Aston Subedge, Gloucestershire, suffered profound tragedy when his youngest son died in a tragic accident. Consumed by grief, Keyt withdrew into his fortune and embarked on extravagant, reckless building projects on his estate, erecting a second vast mansion that plunged him deep into debt. As his mind deteriorated further, Keyt became obsessed with a young maidservant named Molly Johnson, attempting to claim her for himself amid a household already fractured by jealousy and unrequited desires. Driven entirely to madness, on a fateful night in 1741, Sir William Keyt locked himself inside the mansion and set it ablaze, burning the magnificent house and himself to the ground. The scorched ruins of Burnt Norton—later immortalized in local lore and literature—remained as a haunting testament to the baronet's fiery demise.

#location
Burnt Norton estate, located in the civil parish of Aston Subedge, Gloucestershire.
