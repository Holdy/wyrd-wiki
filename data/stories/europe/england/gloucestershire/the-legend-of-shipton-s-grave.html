The Legend of Shipton's Grave

Summary

Shipton's Grave is a lonely roadside burial site at a crossroads near Barton End, named after a tragic local man executed for stealing sheep to feed his starving family. His memorial at the intersection has long remained a haunting landmark along the ancient Cotswold ridgeway tracks.

Details

Local legend and regional history record the tale behind Shipton's Grave, situated at the crossroads near Upper Barton End and Tetbury Lane. According to accounts passed down through generations, the grave marks the final resting place of a local man who was caught and executed for stealing sheep in order to provide food for his starving family during desperate times. Because executed criminals were traditionally barred from burial in consecrated churchyards, he was interred directly at the remote roadside crossroads. Stagecoaches and travelers navigating the old pack-horse tracks between Tiltups End, Barton End, and Chavenage frequently passed the lonely gravesite, cementing its solemn place in local folklore.
