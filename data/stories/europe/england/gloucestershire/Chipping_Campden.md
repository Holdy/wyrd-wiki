#title
The Campden Wonder

#summary
In 1660, an estate steward mysteriously vanished during a walk from Chipping Campden, leading to the wrongful execution of his servant and family for murder before the steward unexpectedly returned two years later with a wild tale of abduction and slavery.

#details
On August 16, 1660, William Harrison, an estate steward and rent collector in Chipping Campden, set out on foot to collect rents in Charingworth and never returned. When search parties found his slashed hat, collar, and blood-stained shirt near Ebrington, suspicion fell on his servant, John Perry. Under interrogation, John gave rambling and contradictory accounts before eventually accusing his mother, Joan, and his brother, Richard, of robbing and murdering Harrison. Despite the complete absence of a body and frantic protestations of innocence from Joan and Richard, all three Perrys were convicted of murder at the Gloucester Assizes and hanged on Broadway Hill in March 1661. Two years later, in 1662, William Harrison reappeared in Chipping Campden alive, claiming he had been waylaid by swordsmen, sold into slavery in the Ottoman Empire by Barbary pirates, and eventually escaped. The tragic miscarriage of justice remains one of England's most famous historical folklore mysteries and later contributed to legal reforms regarding murder convictions without a corpse.

#location
Chipping Campden High Street is located in the Cotswold district of Gloucestershire, England, where William Harrison lived and ultimately returned after his disappearance.

#upstream-source
_A true and perfect account of the examination, confession, trial, condemnation and execution of Joan Perry, and her two sons, John and Richard Perry, for the supposed murder of William Harrison_ by Sir Thomas Overbury.

#title
The Campden Wonder

#summary
A steward's mysterious disappearance in 1660 led to a false murder confession, the execution of an entire family for witchcraft and murder, and the steward's inexplicable return two years later.

#details
In August 1660, seventy-year-old William Harrison, steward of the manor in Chipping Campden, set out to collect rents in nearby Charingworth and failed to return. When his servant, John Perry, went out to look for him, he also vanished. Suspicion soon fell upon John Perry, who ultimately confessed under interrogation that he, his brother Richard, and his mother Joan had waylaid, robbed, and murdered Harrison near the Coneygree on the edge of Chipping Campden, disposing of his body in a cesspool. Despite maintaining their innocence and the total absence of a corpse, all three family members were convicted at the Gloucester assizes and hanged on Broadway Hill, with Joan widely believed to be a witch. Two years later, William Harrison miraculously reappeared in Chipping Campden, claiming he had been abducted by horsemen, sold into slavery in Smyrna (modern-day Izmir, Turkey), and eventually escaped via Portugal.

#location
The banqueting houses of old Campden House and the parish church in Chipping Campden, Gloucestershire, as well as Broadway Hill.

#upstream-source
_The Campden Wonder_, edited by Sir George Clark.
