    The Penance of Stone Church

    Summary

    In 1388, four churchwardens from Stone were convicted of perjury after a bitter legal dispute with the Vicar of Berkeley and were sentenced to walk semi-nude around their village church carrying heavy wax candles.

    Details

    In the late fourteenth century, the parishioners of Stone sought independence from the mother church in Berkeley so they could have sacraments and services administered locally. Four local churchwardens—John Sergeant, John Guliane, Stephen Hykedon, and William Chapman—brought a successful case against the Vicar of Berkeley, John Trevisa, at the Worcester Consistory Court. However, subsequent inquiries ordered by the Bishop of Worcester and Lord Berkeley revealed that the churchwardens had fabricated their claims and lied under oath during the proceedings. As punishment for their perjury, the four men were condemned to walk in procession around All Saints' Church in Stone for four consecutive Sundays at noon, clad only in their shifts and carrying one-pound wax candles while loudly proclaiming their guilt, followed by two further Sundays of public penance in Berkeley.
