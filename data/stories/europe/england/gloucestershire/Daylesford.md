#title
The Grey Geese of Adlestrop

#summary
An old woman driving her geese across Adlestrop Hill refused a witch's request for money, prompting the witch to curse the flock and turn them into stone. These petrified geese were later transported by Warren Hastings to adorn the grounds of Daylesford House.

#details
According to local tradition, an old woman was driving a flock of geese across Adlestrop Hill when she encountered an old witch who demanded money for one of the birds. When the woman stubbornly refused, the witch cast a curse over the entire flock, instantly transforming every goose into solid stone. The petrified birds remained scattered across the hillside for generations until the late 18th century, when Warren Hastings—the former Governor-General of India—acquired the neighboring Daylesford estate. Fascinated by the legend, Hastings had several of these legendary stone geese uprooted from Adlestrop Hill and brought down to Daylesford House to serve as ornamental rockwork in his newly landscaped gardens.

#location
Adlestrop Hill rises in the Cotswold district of Gloucestershire, directly adjacent to the village of Adlestrop and the Daylesford estate.

#upstream-source
A humorous ballad published in 1808 in the Gentleman's Magazine.
