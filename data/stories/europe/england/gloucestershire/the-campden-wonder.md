#title
The Campden Wonder

#summary
In 1660, an estate steward mysteriously vanished during a walk from Chipping Campden, leading to the wrongful execution of his servant and family for murder before the steward unexpectedly returned two years later with a wild tale of abduction and slavery.

#details
On August 16, 1660, William Harrison, an estate steward and rent collector in Chipping Campden, set out on foot to collect rents in Charingworth and never returned. When search parties found his slashed hat, collar, and blood-stained shirt near Ebrington, suspicion fell on his servant, John Perry. Under interrogation, John gave rambling and contradictory accounts before eventually accusing his mother, Joan, and his brother, Richard, of robbing and murdering Harrison. Despite the complete absence of a body and frantic protestations of innocence from Joan and Richard, all three Perrys were convicted of murder at the Gloucester Assizes and hanged on Broadway Hill in March 1661. Two years later, in 1662, William Harrison reappeared in Chipping Campden alive, claiming he had been waylaid by swordsmen, sold into slavery in the Ottoman Empire by Barbary pirates, and eventually escaped. The tragic miscarriage of justice remains one of England's most famous historical folklore mysteries and later contributed to legal reforms regarding murder convictions without a corpse.

#location
Chipping Campden High Street is located in the Cotswold district of Gloucestershire, England, where William Harrison lived and ultimately returned after his disappearance.
