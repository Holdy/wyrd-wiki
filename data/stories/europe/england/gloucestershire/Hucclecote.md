#title
The Hucclecote Interjection at Churchdown Church

#summary
During a church service at Churchdown, a parishioner from neighboring Hucclecote famously interrupted the liturgy to ask what the people of Hucclecote had done, cementing a long-standing local rivalry and naming tradition.

#details
According to local tradition recorded in nineteenth-century folklore collections, Churchdown Church—situated atop Churchdown Hill near Gloucester—was the setting for a famous local jest. During the church service, when the congregation recited the customary response, "And make Thy chosen people joyful," a man from the nearby village of Hucclecote stood up and retorted, "And what have the Hucclecote people done?" This witty interjection is traditionally said to be why Churchdown became vulgarly known among country folk as "Chosen." Later local recollections preserved in county notes identified the parish clerk credited with this distinction as William Ursell, who formerly resided at Zoon's Farm in Hucclecote.

#location
Churchdown Church is located on the summit of Churchdown Hill, near Gloucester and Cheltenham in Gloucestershire.
