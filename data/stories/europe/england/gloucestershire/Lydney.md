#title
The Curse of the Stolen Ring and the God of Dwarf's Hill

#summary
Excavated from an ancient Roman temple on a Gloucestershire hillside, a lead curse tablet calling upon the god Nodens to punish a ring-thief helped inspire generations of folklore about dwarves, hobgoblins, and magical rings.

#details
At the ancient Roman temple site atop Dwarf's Hill in Lydney Park, archaeologists uncovered a remarkable lead curse tablet dedicated to the healing god Nodens. Written centuries ago, the inscription revealed that a man named Silvanus had lost a golden ring and vowed half its value to the deity, asking Nodens to withhold health from anyone named Senicianus until the stolen ring was returned to the temple sanctuary. Years later, a heavy gold ring bearing the name Senicianus was indeed unearthed in a Hampshire field. Following the departure of the Romans, local generations whispered that the ruins and surrounding hill were inhabited by dwarves, hobgoblins, and the "little people." When author J.R.R. Tolkien worked on the archaeological excavation of the site in 1929, these ancient local legends and the shadowed ruins of Lydney Park deeply stirred his imagination, weaving into the fabric of his legendary mythos.

#location
The Roman temple of Nodens is located on Dwarf's Hill within the Lydney Park Estate in Lydney, Gloucestershire.
