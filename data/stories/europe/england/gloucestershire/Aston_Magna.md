#title
The Corpse Road of Aston Magna

#summary
For centuries, the hamlet of Aston Magna lacked burial rights, requiring villagers to carry their dead along a strict two-mile corpse road across streams and fields to the mother church in Blockley under ancient folk superstitions.

#details
During the medieval period and continuing into the nineteenth century, the chapel at Aston Magna held only chantry status rather than full parish rights, meaning local residents could not be buried within the hamlet. Consequently, funeral parties were bound by custom and tradition to carry their deceased along a dedicated corpse road stretching over two miles to St Peter & St Paul’s Church in Blockley. This sombre journey gave rise to enduring folk beliefs; rural superstition held that spirits travelled in straight lines and were unable to cross running water. Because of this, the path was routed across small streams and bridges to prevent restless souls from finding their way back to their former homes. Bearers would also utilize flat resting stones along the way to ease the heavy burden of the coffin before making the final ascent up the hill to the churchyard.

#location
The historic corpse road running from Aston Magna to St Peter & St Paul's Church in Blockley, Gloucestershire.
