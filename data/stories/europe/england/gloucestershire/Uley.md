#title
The Gray Lady of Owlpen Manor

#summary
Queen Margaret of Anjou is said to haunt Owlpen Manor in Uley after spending the night there on the eve of the Battle of Tewkesbury in 1471.

#details
Owlpen Manor, a historic Tudor manor house located in the parish of Uley, Gloucestershire, is reputed to be haunted by Queen Margaret of Anjou, the wife of King Henry VI. According to family tradition and local legend, Margaret spent the night of May 2, 1471, at the manor house en route to the fateful Battle of Tewkesbury. The following day, her son, Prince Edward, was killed. The shock and tragedy of her son's death are said to anchor her spirit to the manor. She is frequently described as a benevolent "grey lady" wearing a medieval fur-trimmed gown and wimple, who drifts through the upper rooms and the Great Chamber. During World War II, young evacuee children staying at the house reportedly saw her walking the halls in her medieval attire, mistaking her ghost for a living resident.

#location
Owlpen Manor is located in the parish of Uley, near Dursley, Gloucestershire (GL11 5BZ).

#title
The Roman Soldier of Hetty Pegler's Tump

#summary
A spectral Roman soldier in a plumed helmet is frequently seen standing in the mist around the ancient Uley Long Barrow.

#details
Uley Long Barrow, also known locally as Hetty Pegler's Tump, is a 5,000-year-old Neolithic multi-chambered burial mound situated on a ridge overlooking the Severn Valley near Uley, Gloucestershire. Alongside the ancient neolithic human remains discovered within the barrow over the centuries, excavations also revealed the burial of a Roman man. Local folklore maintains that the restless spirit of a Roman soldier haunts the site, often appearing in the mist standing guard around the mound with his plumed helmet and shield clearly visible.

#location
Uley Long Barrow (Hetty Pegler's Tump) is located on a ridge above the village of Uley in Gloucestershire.
