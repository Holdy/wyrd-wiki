#title
The Legend of the Twin Churches of Eastleach

#summary
Local folklore attributes the construction of the two medieval churches facing each other across the River Leach to a bitter feud between two sisters who refused to share a place of worship.

#details
According to traditional local legend, two wealthy sisters residing along the River Leach fell out over a fierce family dispute and could no longer bear to share a single parish church. Rather than reconcile their differences, each sister stubbornly decided to commission her own grand place of worship. Consequently, two magnificent 12th-century churches—St Andrew's Church in Eastleach Turville and St Michael and St Martin's Church in Eastleach Martin—were built on opposite banks of the river, standing just 180 yards apart. Although historical records indicate the separate parishes and churches were actually established by distinct lords of the manor for their respective families and retainers, the legend of the warring sisters persists as a charming local explanation for this rare architectural juxtaposition in the Cotswolds.

#location
St Andrew's Church and St Michael & St Martin's Church, located on opposite banks of the River Leach in Eastleach, Gloucestershire.
