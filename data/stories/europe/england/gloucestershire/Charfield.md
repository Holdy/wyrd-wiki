#title
The Ghost Children and the Woman in Black of Charfield

#summary
Following a horrific 1928 railway disaster in Charfield, two unidentified child victims and a mysterious woman in black visiting their mass grave became the subjects of enduring local folklore. Locals have long reported sightings of spectral children standing hand-in-hand near the tracks, patiently waiting to be identified.

#details
On October 13, 1928, a Bristol-bound mail train collided with a shunting goods train at Charfield station in Gloucestershire, igniting gas cylinders and causing a massive inferno that killed 16 people. Amid the charred wreckage, two children's bodies were discovered that remained entirely unclaimed and unidentified. Designated as victims "Number 9 and Number 10," they were laid to rest in a mass grave at the village churchyard. In the decades following the crash, a frail elderly woman dressed entirely in black was frequently seen arriving in a chauffeur-driven limousine to leave flowers on their memorial, fueling rumors that she possessed a secret connection to the children. Although her visits ceased in the early 1960s, local legend holds that the ghostly figures of two young children still haunt the area near the railway, silently watching the tracks.

#location
St James' Church graveyard in Charfield, South Gloucestershire, holds the memorial and mass grave of the railway disaster victims where the mysterious woman in black once left her tributes.

#title
The Ghost Children of Charfield Graveyard

#summary
Following a tragic 1928 train crash that left two young victims unidentified and buried in St James's graveyard, ghostly figures of children and a mysterious woman in black have been reported haunting the area.

#details
At 5:30 am on 13 October 1928, a Leeds to Bristol express train crashed in Charfield, Gloucestershire, resulting in a devastating collision and fire that killed fifteen people. Among the dead were two children whose bodies went entirely unclaimed and unidentified, leading to their burial in St James's graveyard under gravestones inscribed simply with "two unknown." For decades following the disaster, local tradition maintained that a mysterious woman dressed entirely in black visited the children's graves late into the 1950s. Since then, witnesses have reported seeing two lonely, lost ghostly railway children walking hand-in-hand and hovering near the churchyard and crash site.

#location
St James's Church graveyard in the village of Charfield, Gloucestershire.
