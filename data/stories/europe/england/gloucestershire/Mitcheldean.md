#title
The Curse of Eli Hatton

#summary
After being hanged in a gibbet for murder in 1723, a condemned man named Eli Hatton reportedly placed a curse on Mitcheldean that brought plagues of flies to the town.

#details
In the early 18th century, a carpenter named Tom Twibervile was battered to death in his shop in Mitcheldean. Eli Hatton was convicted of the murder and sentenced to death. In 1723, Hatton was hanged at Pingry Tump at Wigpool, a high spot overlooking the town, and his body was left hanging in a gibbet as a grim signpost and warning to others. According to local legend and historical accounts, Hatton placed a curse upon Mitcheldean before his death, which was long blamed for recurring plagues of flies descending upon the settlement.

#location
Pingry Tump at Wigpool, overlooking the town of Mitcheldean in Gloucestershire.

#title
The Haunting of the George Hotel

#summary
Following his tragic suicide at Mitcheldean's George Hotel in 1833, Trooper Joe McGurk's restless spirit haunted the ancient coaching inn for generations until the building was destroyed by fire in 2019.

#details
In October 1833, Trooper Joe McGurk of the Eighth Lancers was travelling from Abergavenny to Gloucester to face a court martial following a drunken altercation over a bottle of grog. While staying the night at the George Hotel in Mitcheldean, and fearing the severity of military punishment, McGurk loaded a carbine, pointed it at his chest, and pulled the trigger, passing away a few hours later. For the next 175 years, guests and staff at the 400-year-old coaching inn reported paranormal activity, frequently gasping as bottles and glasses moved on their own across the bar, only to be assured by regulars that it was simply "Old Joe" at work. The historic building ultimately burned down in a devastating fire in December 2019.

#location
The former site of the George Hotel in the conservation zone opposite the parish church in Mitcheldean, Gloucestershire.
