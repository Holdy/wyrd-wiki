#title
The Underground Chambers of Torbarrow Hill

#summary
Two gravel diggers near Cirencester discovered hidden underground chambers beneath a prehistoric mound guarded by a hostile automaton, only for the subterranean complex to collapse upon their return.

#details
According to an account published in 1685, two workmen digging for gravel near Cirencester sank four yards deep into Torbarrow Hill and discovered an entrance leading into the belly of the earth. Exploring inside, they found a series of rooms and tunnels containing furniture and carpets that crumbled to dust upon touch, along with numerous urns filled with ashes and ancient Roman coins. In the deepest chamber, they encountered the life-sized image of a man in armor holding a truncheon, illuminated by a burning glass lamp. When the men approached, the figure struck out, smashing the lamp and extinguishing the light amid hollow groans before the hill caved in and buried the entire subterranean discovery.

#location
Tar Barrows (Torbarrow Hill), located on the high ground approximately half a mile north-east of Cirencester, Gloucestershire.

#upstream-source
"A Strange and Wonderful Discovery Newly made of Houses Under Ground at Colton’s Field in Gloucester-Shire" (London broadsheet, 1685; preserved in the Bodleian Library among Dr. Rawlinson's papers).

#title
The Underground Chambers of Torbarrow Hill

#summary
Two workmen digging near Cirencester stumbled upon subterranean rooms filled with ancient treasures and urns, only to be violently attacked by an armed guardian figure. When they returned with an antiquarian the next day, the subterranean halls collapsed and vanished forever.

#details
According to a 1685 account, two workmen digging gravel at the foot of Torbarrow Hill (also known as Tar Barrow) on the edge of Cirencester discovered an opening leading into the belly of the mound. Exploring inside, they found a series of underground rooms and tunnels furnished with carpets and furniture that crumbled to dust upon touch, alongside Roman urns containing ashes and precious coins. In the deepest chamber, they encountered a lifelike figure of a man holding a truncheon, illuminated by a glass lamp. When one worker approached, the figure struck out at him, causing them both to flee with handfuls of coins. Returning the following day with an antiquarian, they found the guardian figure again; as it lashed out with its truncheon, it shattered the lamp and plunged the room into darkness amidst a deep groan. Instantly, the hill caved in, sealing the entrance and burying the underground chambers permanently.

#location
Tar Barrow (Torbarrow Hill) is a prehistoric round barrow situated on high ground just northeast of Cirencester, near Burford Road.

#upstream-source
An anonymous broadsheet published in London in 1685 entitled: “A Strange and Wonderful Discovery Newly made of Houses Under Ground at Colton’s Field in Gloucester-Shire.”
