#title
The Servant of Littledean Hall

#summary
According to local legend, Littledean Hall is haunted by the audible spirit of an enslaved black servant who murdered his young master, Charles Pyrke, in revenge for a grievous betrayal. The tale is immortalised by an 18th-century portrait hanging in the hall known as *The Red Boy*.

#details
Local tradition holds that during the 18th century, Thomas Pyrke of Littledean Hall brought a young enslaved black servant and his sister to Gloucestershire from his West Indies plantation. The servant was assigned to young Charles Pyrke, wearing a silver collar around his neck as a badge of servitude. Years later, after Charles allegedly dishonoured the servant's sister, the young man discovered the betrayal, crept into his sleeping master's room, and strangled him before fleeing. Although the servant was subsequently tracked down and killed, his restless spirit is said to still haunt the ancient corridors and dining room of Littledean Hall, heard rather than seen as he paces the rooms of his lifelong servitude, love, and hate.

#location
Littledean Hall stands in the village of Littledean within the Forest of Dean district of Gloucestershire (Grid Reference SO672130).
