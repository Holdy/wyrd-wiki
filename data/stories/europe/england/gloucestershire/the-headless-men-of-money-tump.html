The Headless Men of Money Tump

Summary

Two men walking home from a village feast late at night near Chalford encountered a group of figures standing by an ancient Bronze Age round barrow. Upon drawing closer, they were horrified to discover that the men had no heads.

Details

Sometime before the enclosure of the Bisley Commons in 1869, two men from Chalford were walking home after attending the annual Bisley Feast. As they made their way along the track connecting Nash End to France Lynch, they passed Money Tump, an ancient Bronze Age round barrow. In the dim light, they noticed a group of figures ahead of them and, assuming they were friends returning from the same festivities, hurried to catch up. To their absolute terror, when they drew near, they realized the figures were entirely headless. The profound fright of the encounter left a lasting, abiding effect on the two men for the remainder of their lives.
