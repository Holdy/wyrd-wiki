    The Standing Stones of Odo and Dodo

    Summary

    Two ancient standing stones near Stanway, named after the legendary Saxon nobles Odo and Dodo, are said to have been positioned with a tree between them to keep the quarrelling lords apart.

    Details

    Local folklore in the Gloucestershire countryside near Stanway and Nottingham Hill centers on two solitary upright monoliths known as "Odo and Dodo." Standing at six and seven feet tall respectively, the stones are named after Odo and Dodo, early figures linked in local tradition and 15th-century chronicles to the origins of Tewkesbury Abbey and the surrounding manorial estates. According to accounts collected by antiquarians, the stones were originally erected within the ancient camp atop Nottingham Hill before being moved around 1860 to the grounds of Prescott House near the Stanway boundary. Popular lore maintains that a tree was intentionally planted or left standing directly between the two stone pillars so that the quarrelsome spirits of the two figures would be kept apart and prevented from continuing their legendary arguments.
