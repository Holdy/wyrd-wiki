#title
The Three Kings of Chestles Field

#summary
An ancient megalithic burial chamber in Wick is traditionally believed to mark the graves of three British kings who fell in battle against the Saxon chieftain Ceaulin in 577 AD.

#details
In the village of Wick, a field historically known as "Chestles" or "Castles" near Coldharbour Farm contains a weathered prehistoric burial chamber composed of ancient pinkish megalithic stones rising from a low sepulchral mound. According to local folklore and historical tradition, this field was the site of a fierce battle fought around 577 AD between the advancing Saxon chieftain Ceaulin and three British kings. All three British monarchs were slain during the encounter and were said to be buried right there beneath the ancient mound, with the standing stones serving as their lasting monumental markers. Alongside this martial legend, traditional accounts from the surrounding district also whisper of a hidden golden calf buried near the ancient pagan stones.

#location
The Chestles field and megalithic burial chamber are located near Coldharbour Farm in the village of Wick, South Gloucestershire.
