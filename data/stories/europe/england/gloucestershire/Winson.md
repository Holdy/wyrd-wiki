#title
The Votive Crosses and Devil's Door of St Michael's Church

#summary
At the historic St Michael's Church in Winson, local folklore and medieval tradition preserved the custom of carving protective votive crosses into the porch before embarking on journeys, alongside the folk belief that the northern entrance served as the "devil's door."

#details
Nestled in the Cotswold village of Winson beside the River Coln, St Michael's Church (dating back to before the mid-12th century) holds deep roots in medieval folk belief and local custom. Carved directly into the stone shafts of the church's south porch are numerous votive crosses. In traditional local lore, these marks were carved by villagers about to set out on arduous journeys or pilgrimages. Acting as a spiritual pledge, the traveler would carve the cross as a promise to make an offering or gift to the church upon their safe return. Furthermore, the parish maintained the ancient folk belief that evil and malevolent forces entered from the north, designating the church's north door as the "devil's door." Consequently, the north door was permanently blocked up, and parishioners strictly avoided burying their dead on the northern side of the churchyard.

#location
St Michael's Church, situated on Ditch Lane in the village of Winson, Gloucestershire (OS grid reference SP090086).
