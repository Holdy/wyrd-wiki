#title
The Royal Ferryman of Ashleworth Quay

#summary
Local tradition holds that following his defeat at the Battle of Worcester in 1651, the future King Charles II was covertly ferried across the River Severn by a member of the Jelf family. In gratitude for this clandestine passage, the Crown allegedly granted the family the permanent right to operate a ferry and sell ale from their riverside home.

#details
According to local folklore surrounding The Boat Inn at Ashleworth Quay, the historic tavern owes its origins to a royal rescue. Following the disastrous defeat of Royalist forces at the Battle of Worcester in 1651, the future King Charles II was fleeing for his life across the English countryside. Seeking a safe crossing over the River Severn, he was aided by an ancestor of the Jelf family, who ferried him across the water to safety. In appreciation of this vital assistance, the King later rewarded the family at the Restoration by granting them perpetual rights to run the river ferry and vend ale from their farmhouse. An alternative, older strand of the local legend suggests the connection dates even further back to the 15th century, when King Edward IV was similarly aided by the Jelfs while fleeing the Lancastrians. To this day, these enduring royal tales remain deeply woven into the history of the remote Severnside settlement and its centuries-old pub.

#location
The Boat Inn stands on the west bank of the River Severn at Ashleworth Quay in Gloucestershire.
