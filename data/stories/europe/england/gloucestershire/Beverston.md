#title
The Phantom Coach of Chavenage

#summary
Following his reluctant agreement to the execution of King Charles I during the Civil War, Parliamentarian colonel Nathaniel Stephens was cursed by his daughter and died shortly after, leading to a ghostly phantom coach and the spectre of the martyred King appearing at his funeral.

#details
During the English Civil War, Colonel Nathaniel Stephens served as Lord of the Manor of Chavenage in Beverston and fought on the side of Parliament against King Charles I. In December 1648, Oliver Cromwell’s son-in-law, General Henry Ireton, visited Chavenage to persuade Stephens to support the regicide of the King. After a night of intense discussion, Stephens reluctantly agreed. Shortly afterward, his daughter Abigail condemned his betrayal and laid a curse upon him. Stephens fell terminally ill and died within months. When mourners gathered for his funeral, a hearse driven by a headless figure drew up to the manor house. Stephens reportedly rose from his coffin and entered the carriage, which then assumed the shape of King Charles I before departing at breakneck speed. According to local folklore, whenever the head of the Stephens family subsequently died, the ghost of the King would return to carry them away.

#location
Chavenage House is a late-16th-century country house located in the civil parish of Beverston, near Tetbury in Gloucestershire.
