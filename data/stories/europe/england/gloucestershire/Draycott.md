#title
The Draycott Corpse Road and the Water-Bound Spirits

#summary
According to local folklore along the historic corpse road connecting Draycott to St Peter & St Paul's Church in Blockley, carrying the dead across running water was essential because flowing streams prevented restless spirits from finding their way back home.

#details
For centuries, the small hamlet of Draycott in Gloucestershire lacked its own burial rights, requiring villagers to carry their deceased relatives over a two-mile corpse road to the mother church of St Peter & St Paul in Blockley. Local custom and folklore strictly dictated how this solemn journey was conducted to keep the dead from returning. The deceased were always carried out of their homes feet first so that their souls, looking back over their shoulders, would not recognize the path behind them. Furthermore, because traditional belief held that running water could stop a spirit from following the living, the route was deliberately planned to cross local streams and brooks. Bearers crossed these watercourses—such as the stream beneath Draycott Road near the old silk mill—confident that the running water acted as a barrier, preventing wandering spirits from tracking the living back to Draycott before the coffin was finally laid to rest in the terraced churchyard.

#location
The historic corpse road and footpath running between Draycott and St Peter & St Paul's Church in Blockley, Gloucestershire.
