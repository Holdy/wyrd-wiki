#title
The Wandering Summer-House of Alkerton Grange

#summary
Local tradition maintains that the ornate 18th-century baroque summer-house nestled in the gardens of Alkerton Grange was mysteriously transported from an entirely different location and re-erected on its present site.

#details
At the rear of the garden belonging to Alkerton Grange in the Gloucestershire hamlet of Alkerton stands a striking, highly ornate early 18th-century baroque summer-house, featuring red brickwork, stone dressings, Ionic pilasters, and a swept gable surmounted by carved urns and a broken pediment. While historians have noted how unusually grand and out-of-character this architectural style is for the grounds of a comparatively modest historic property, generations of local residents have passed down a persistent tradition explaining its presence. According to this village lore, the exquisite little pavilion did not originally belong to Alkerton Grange at all, but was dismantled elsewhere and brought in to be re-erected where it now stands.

#location
Alkerton Grange, located in the hamlet of Alkerton, Gloucestershire.
