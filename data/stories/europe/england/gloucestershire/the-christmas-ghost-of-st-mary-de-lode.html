    The Christmas Ghost of St Mary de Lode

    Summary

    A fourteenth-century priest who drowned at sea while returning from Rome kept his solemn vow to his congregation by appearing as a phantom to conduct the Christmas morning service. To this day, his hooded apparition is said to return to oversee the church's holiday worship.

    Details

    During the fourteenth century, the parish priest of St Mary's in Gloucester travelled to Rome to petition the Pope for permission to translate the Bible into English. Before his departure, he promised his parishioners that he would return in time to lead the Christmas morning service. When Christmas Day arrived and he still had not been seen, his faithful congregation nonetheless prepared the church. Right on time, the priest entered, conducted the entire service without speaking or greeting anyone, and then suddenly vanished. When the startled parishioners spilled into the churchyard calling his name, a monk from the nearby abbey brought tragic news: the priest had perished at sea when his ship sank in the English Channel. Believed to have honored his promise from beyond the grave, his ghostly hooded figure is said to return on dark winter mornings to ensure the parish Christmas services are properly maintained.
