#title
The Murder of Lorenzo Beha on the Tittleshall Road

#summary
In November 1853, traveling watchmaker Lorenzo Beha was brutally murdered on the roadside between Wellingham and Tittleshall by local labourer William Thompson.

#details
On November 18, 1853, Lorenzo Beha, a Norwich watchmaker and jeweller who travelled across Norfolk selling his wares on installment, was walking toward Wellingham. That afternoon, travellers along the road between Wellingham and Tittleshall discovered a trail of blood leading to a hedge and ditch. In the ditch lay Beha's body, his head nearly severed by a timber hatchet left covered in blood and hair. Although his box of valuable jewellery remained unopened by his side, his pockets had been rifled. William Thompson, a local labourer seen hiding in the nearby plantation earlier that day, was arrested after stolen watches bearing Beha's name and blood-stained garments were found in his possession. Thompson was convicted at the Norfolk Assizes and executed at Norwich Castle Hill.

#location
The incident took place along the road and adjacent ditch situated between the villages of Wellingham and Tittleshall in Norfolk, England.
