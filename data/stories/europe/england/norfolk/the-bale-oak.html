The Bale Oak

Summary

Standing just west of All Saints Church in Bale, the ancient Bale Oak was a massive tree steeped in local folklore of pre-Christian worship whose cavernous hollow trunk famously served as a cobbler's shop.

Details

Located in the North Norfolk village of Bale, immediately west of All Saints Church, the Bale Oak was a legendary tree of extraordinary proportions, measuring 36 feet in circumference with branches spanning over 70 feet. Local folklore maintained that the ancient oak had originally served as a site for pre-Christian pagan worship within an ancient sacred grove. By the 18th century, the tree's interior had hollowed out so extensively that local historian Rev. Blomfield recorded that ten or twelve men could stand inside it. During this period, the hollow trunk was actively utilized as both a cobbler's shop and a pigsty. As the centuries passed, the heavily damaged and rotting tree became a hazard, leading the Lord of the Manor, Sir Willoughby Jones, to order its removal in 1860 despite widespread local grief.
