#title
The Great Stone of Lyng

#summary
Hidden within a shadowed woodland in Lyng, a massive glacial boulder known as the Great Stone is said to bleed when pricked with a pin and is haunted by the spectral remnants of an ancient battle.

#details
The Great Stone of Lyng is a large glacial erratic resting in a secluded woodland coppice near Lyng Easthaugh, steeped in dark local legends. According to tradition, the stone will bleed if pricked, echoing its supposed use as a sacrificial altar or marking the site of a bloody battle between King Edmund and the invading Danes. Generations ago, village schoolchildren were routinely dismissed early in the winter months so they could pass the haunted grove and Hogsback Hill before darkness fell, fearful of the unexplainable dread and phantom apparitions lingering in the trees. Local lore also claims that treasure is buried beneath the stone—which defied the efforts of a landowner who tried to move it with a dozen horses—while phantom convent bells are said to ring from beneath the nearby River Wensum.

#location
The Great Stone of Lyng is located in the wooded King's Grove near Lyng Easthaugh and Hogsback Hill in the parish of Lyng, Norfolk.

#upstream-source
A March 13, 1939 newspaper article detailing accounts given by Reverend E. C. Weddall, the Rector of Lyng.
