#title
The Black Hound of Blickling Hall

#summary
Following a fatal duel on Cawston Heath, the restless spirit of Sir Henry Hobart manifested from a cooked eel as a giant black hound. This spectral dog cursed the Blickling Hall estate before being sealed in a tower and occasionally glimpsed by witnesses over the centuries.

#details
In 1698, Sir Henry Hobart and local lawyer Oliver Le Neve fought a sword duel on Cawston Heath, resulting in Hobart receiving a fatal stomach wound. After Hobart's death, his body was repeatedly disturbed from its burial sites and ultimately sunk into a lake. When a gamekeeper caught a monstrous eel from these waters and attempted to cook it, the eel vanished in an explosion of steam and transformed into an enormous black dog bearing Hobart's eyes. The apparition brought a curse of misfortune upon the Blickling Hall estate until a visiting wizard summoned the beast and locked it away in the South East tower. Over the centuries, the spectral hound has been reportedly sighted by witnesses roaming the estate grounds.

#location
The site of the fateful 1698 duel on Cawston Heath is marked by a stone plinth and urn located just off the Norwich Road in Cawston, Norfolk.

#title
The Devil in the Boot

#summary
A holy medieval rector famously captures and imprisons the Devil inside a leather boot. This legendary feat is immortalized in painted woodwork at a historic Norfolk church.

#details
According to English folklore, the 13th-century rector and miracle worker Sir John Schorne performed his most famous act of exorcism by conjuring the Devil and trapping him securely within a boot. This legendary triumph over evil became widely celebrated across East Anglia and beyond, leading to his enduring depiction holding a boot containing the imprisoned fiend. At St Agnes Church in Cawston, Norfolk, this iconic scene is preserved on the 15th-century painted rood screen, though the panel bears historic signs of damage from superstitious practices associated with the legend.

#location
St Agnes Church is located in the village of Cawston in Norfolk, England.

#title
The Last Duel on Cawston Heath

#summary
In August 1698, local lawyer Oliver Le Neve and Sir Henry Hobart met on Cawston Heath for a fateful sword duel that ended in Hobart's death and became the last duel fought in Norfolk.

#details
Driven by bitter political and personal rivalries, Witchingham lawyer Oliver Le Neve and Sir Henry Hobart of nearby Blickling Hall arranged a duel to settle their disputes. On August 20, 1698, the two men met upon Cawston Heath, a wild expanse of gorse and heather east of the village. During the brutal encounter, Le Neve struck a fatal blow against Hobart. Sir Henry succumbed to his wounds shortly thereafter, leaving behind a dark local legacy. Today, a small stone near the Woodrow Inn marks the historic site of what is remembered as the final duel ever fought in the county of Norfolk.

#location
Cawston Heath, located adjacent to the Woodrow Inn on the eastern side of Cawston in Norfolk.

#title
The Phantoms of Cawston Heath

#summary
Following a fatal 17th-century duel on Cawston Heath, the spirit of Sir Henry Hobart and spectral manifestations are said to haunt the location where the bloody clash took place.

#details
In 1698, a bitter dispute over political defeat and rumors of cowardice led Sir Henry Hobart of Blickling Hall and Oliver Le Neve of Great Witchingham Hall to fight an illegal duel on Cawston Heath. As a servant girl watched in terror from the bushes, Hobart was mortally wounded by Le Neve in a swift and brutal encounter and died the following day. According to local folklore, on clear nights the phantom clashing of swords can still be heard on Cawston Heath as the sworn enemies endlessly reenact their fatal fight.

#location
The Duel Stone plinth and urn, located just off the Norwich Road/Holt Road on Cawston Heath in Cawston, Norfolk.
