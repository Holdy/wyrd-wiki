#title
The Drummer Boy of Potter Heigham

#summary
During a bitterly cold winter in 1815, a young soldier on leave drowned while skating across the frozen river to visit his sweetheart, and his phantom is said to still haunt the waters. On winter evenings, his spectral figure can be heard or seen drumming across the ice as a warning to travelers.

#details
In the winter of 1815, a drummer boy home on leave fell in love with a local village girl despite the disapproval of her family. Because the Broads were frozen thick with ice, the young soldier would skate across the water each evening to meet her at a hut near Hickling Broad, beating a tattoo on his kettle drum to announce his arrival. On a bitterly cold night in February, he ventured across once too often and fell through the thin ice near the wherry channel, drowning before he could be rescued. His body was recovered days later when the ice began to thaw, identifiable by his distinctive yellow and scarlet uniform. According to local tradition, his ghostly figure continues to skate across the icy waters of Potter Heigham on winter nights, with the beat of his drum serving as an eternal search for his lost love and a ghostly warning about dangerous ice.

#location
Potter Heigham Bridge crosses the River Thurne in the village of Potter Heigham, Norfolk.
