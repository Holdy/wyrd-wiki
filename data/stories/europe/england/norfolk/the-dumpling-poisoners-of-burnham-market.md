#title
The Dumpling Poisoners of Burnham Market

#summary
Two 19th-century women committed a series of murders by slipping arsenic into food and drink. Following their public execution, their vengeful spirits were said to haunt North Street in Burnham Market.

#details
During the 1830s in Burnham Market, friends Frances Billing and Catherine Frary engaged in illicit affairs and plotted to kill those who stood in their way. Utilizing arsenic purchased from the local pharmacist, they poisoned a baby, a husband, and a rival named Mary Taylor via tainted food and drinks like dumplings and porter. Their crimes were exposed following autopsies and eyewitness accounts of tampering, leading to their arrest, trial, and public execution in Norwich. Ever since, locals have reported that the spirits of the two poisoners continue to haunt North Street, leaving an eerie atmosphere behind.

#location
North Street in the village of Burnham Market, Norfolk, marks where the murderers once lived and where their ghosts are reported to roam.
