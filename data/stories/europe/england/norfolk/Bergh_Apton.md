#title
The Black Shuck of Cooke's Lane

#summary
A late-night cyclist returning home from a darts match in 1944 was terrified when a large, snarling black hound leaped out from a roadside pond in Bergh Apton and vanished directly into a solid stone wall.

#details
In late 1944 or 1945, shortly before midnight, a member of the Thurton George & Dragon darts team was cycling along Cooke's Lane—the road connecting Thurton and Bergh Apton—after finishing an away match. As he approached the pond situated opposite Washingford House, a massive black dog, roughly the size of a large Labrador with a smooth back and shaggy hair hanging from its body, suddenly bounded out from the side of the water. Growling and snapping with large, visible teeth, the beast charged directly at crossbar height. Expecting to be knocked from his bicycle and bitten, the terrified cyclist braced for impact, only to watch in horror as the creature abruptly vanished right into the stone wall on the right-hand side of the road. Deeply shaken, he pedalled home as fast as he could, forever marked by his encounter with Norfolk's legendary phantom hound.

#location
The pond opposite Washingford House on Cooke's Lane (the Thurton to Bergh Apton road) in Bergh Apton, Norfolk (grid reference TG31100128).
