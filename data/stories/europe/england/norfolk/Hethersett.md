#title
The Faines

#summary
Calf-sized spectral creatures with glowing saucer eyes and giant silent black dogs haunt the roads and playing fields of Hethersett. Witnesses have reported these phantom hounds padding alongside cyclists before vanishing into thin air.

#details
Historical records and modern accounts document uncanny canine apparitions in Hethersett. In the early 20th century, antiquary Walter Rye noted calf-sized creatures with saucer-like glowing eyes frequenting Mill Road and Gravel Pit Lane, with one witness describing an entity that shed light like a bicycle lamp. Decades later, a local worker cycling along Henstead Road was shadowed by a silent, huge rough-coated black dog that vanished at a bend. Another witness recounting an experience at the Hethersett Memorial Playing Field described a similar towering hound that paced alongside him before disappearing completely behind a brick toilet block despite clear visibility.

#location
Hethersett Memorial Playing Field in Hethersett, South Norfolk, can be located via Recreation Road or Canns Lane.

Walter Rye, Recreations of a Norfolk Antiquary (1920)

#title
The Faines of Hethersett

#summary
Calf-sized phantom beasts with glowing saucer eyes and silent, massive phantom dogs have long been reported haunting the lanes and playing fields of Hethersett.

#details
For generations, local folklore in Hethersett has spoken of strange phantom creatures known as the Faines. In his 1920 work *Recreations of a Norfolk Antiquary*, antiquarian Walter Rye recorded accounts of these calf-sized beasts with abnormally large, glowing saucer eyes frequenting specific parts of the village, including Mill Road and Gravel Pit Lane. Later twentieth-century accounts expanded on these sightings when local residents reported being shadowed by enormous, silent, rough-coated phantom dogs while walking across the Hethersett Memorial Playing Field or cycling along Henstead Road, with the spectral hounds pacing alongside witnesses before vanishing completely into thin air.

#location
Mill Road and Gravel Pit Lane in Hethersett, Norfolk.

#upstream-source
Walter Rye, Recreations of a Norfolk Antiquary (1920).
