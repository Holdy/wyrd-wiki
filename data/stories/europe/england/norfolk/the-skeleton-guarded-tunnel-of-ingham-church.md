#title
The Skeleton-Guarded Tunnel of Ingham Church

#summary
Nineteen skeletons were discovered piled beneath the altar of Holy Trinity Church in Ingham during Victorian renovations. These remains formed a grim guard before the entrance of a legendary subterranean passage linking the old priory to a nearby manor house.

#details
According to local tradition and 19th-century accounts recorded by Ernest R. Suffling, Holy Trinity Church in Ingham—built upon the grounds of a 13th-century Trinitarian priory—held a chilling discovery beneath its chancel. During Victorian restoration work in the 1860s, workers dismantling the altar for repairs uncovered nineteen human skeletons piled together beneath the stonework. The bones appeared to act as a strange guard blocking the entrance to a subterranean tunnel that allegedly ran half a mile from the old priory to a local manor house. Alongside the skeletal sentries, local folklore also notes sightings of a "transparent lady" apparition haunting the old gravel pit on the north side of the churchyard behind The Swan public house.

#location
Holy Trinity Church is situated in the village of Ingham, Norfolk, located directly next to The Swan public house.
