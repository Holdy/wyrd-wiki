#title
The Pig-Faced Monk of West Dereham

#summary
A spiteful ghost of a monk with a pig-like face is said to haunt West Dereham after resenting the construction of a secret tunnel built to hide his facial deformity. The subterranean passage allegedly connected St. Andrew's Church to the 12th-century abbey.

#details
According to local folklore, a subterranean tunnel was constructed between St. Andrew's Church and the 12th-century abbey in West Dereham for a monk whose head and face resembled that of a pig. Because he was deemed too hideous to be seen walking openly through the village, the passage was built so he could travel between the church and the abbey unseen. Rather than appreciating this gesture, the monk grew resentful and spiteful, and his ghost continues to haunt West Dereham to this day.

#location
St. Andrew's Church stands about a mile north of the site of the 12th-century abbey in West Dereham, Norfolk (TF66740210).
