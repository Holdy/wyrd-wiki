#title
The Banningham Rectory Spirits

#summary
Banningham Rectory was once home to an antiquarian who housed both a spectral George III armchair that summoned a flame-haired ghost when placed near a fireplace and a haunted portrait of a woman who tragically died at Yaxley Hall.

#details
For decades, antiquarian Bryan Hall lived at Banningham Rectory alongside two remarkable and uncanny items. The first was a George III armchair acquired decades earlier by a Nottinghamshire rector's wife after she witnessed a ghostly flame-haired woman sitting in it. True to legend, whenever the chair was placed close to a fireplace, the spectral woman would appear, and those standing near it reported feeling an unexplainable chill even on hot days. The second item was an oil portrait of Henrietta Nelson, a former resident of Yaxley Hall who tragically fell down the stairs in 1816 and whose spirit reportedly haunted her portrait and the grounds of wherever the painting resided—including the gardens of Banningham Rectory. Both items remained in Mr Hall's care until a massive auction held on the property prior to his passing in 2004.

#location
Banningham Rectory in Banningham, Norfolk.

#title
The Banningham Snake Pit

#summary
According to local legend, Richard Cromwell installed a 14-foot deep snake pit on the Banningham village green during the 17th century where men could test the marital fidelity of their wives.

#details
During the turbulent era of the 17th century, when witch hunts and strict moral codes swept East Anglia under the influence of figures like Matthew Hopkins and Oliver Cromwell, a dark local legend took root in Banningham. It was said that Richard Cromwell, the son of the Lord Protector, commissioned a 14-foot deep pit on the village green and filled it with venomous snakes. According to village lore, any husband harboring suspicions regarding his wife's conduct could throw her into the pit; if she emerged unharmed, her virtue and innocence were proven, whereas failing the ordeal meant a grim fate.

#location
The Banningham village green in the village of Banningham, Norfolk.

#title
The Haunted Portrait and Armchair of Banningham Rectory

#summary
Antiquarian Bryan Hall acquired a haunted portrait of Henrietta Nelson and a spectral George III armchair, both of which brought paranormal manifestations to his home.

#details
Norfolk antiquarian Bryan Hall collected numerous curiosities throughout his life, including two items accompanied by spirits. One was a portrait of Henrietta Nelson, a woman who died after falling down the stairs at Yaxley Hall; after her mausoleum was destroyed, her ghost began haunting wherever her portrait was kept, leading to sightings of her walking in the grounds of Banningham Rectory. The other item was a George III armchair from Newstead Abbey that was haunted by a flame-haired woman who would appear if the chair was placed near a fireplace, causing Hall to keep it safely distanced.

#location
Banningham Rectory is situated in the village of Banningham, located in North Norfolk, England.
