#title
The Visions and Pranks of a Gissing Weaver

#summary
In early nineteenth-century Gissing, a young weaver named William Garrard divided his time between leading local village pranks and experiencing profound spiritual visions and signs in the stars. His turbulent youth and inner awakening culminated after the untimely death of his young wife, leading him away from the village to become a lifelong preacher.

#details
In the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries, William Garrard lived in the South Norfolk village of Gissing, where his family ran a hemp and flax weaving business. As a young man, William was known as the ringleader of local mischief and youthful folly. On one occasion, while playing ten-pin bowling at the village pub, The Chequers, he was suddenly struck by an overwhelming wave of emotional love and weeping. Despite his rowdy reputation, he frequently engaged in elaborate pranks with his companions, such as playing tricks on an irritable local resident named Tom Sparrow—including sneaking into Sparrow's cottage to cut his bed-cords, causing the bed to collapse beneath him. 

Seeking answers to his troubled conscience and his forbidden romance with Sarah Norman, William wandered down Tibenham Long Row on a starlit night and asked the heavens for a sign, whereupon shooting stars appeared before him. He eventually married Sarah, but her health rapidly declined due to tuberculosis, and she died in February 1820, leaving him with an infant son. Struck with intense grief and remorse over his past behavior, William experienced powerful spiritual transformations and visions in his Gissing home before eventually leaving the weaving trade to embark on a fifty-year career as a Baptist minister.

#location
The Chequers pub and the surrounding historic village of Gissing lie in the South Norfolk district, approximately five miles north-east of Diss.
