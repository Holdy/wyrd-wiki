#title
The Fairy Cow of South Lopham

#summary
During times of severe hardship and drought, a magical cow would appear in the Norfolk village of South Lopham to provide free milk to the locals before stamping its hoof into a stone slab and vanishing.

#details
According to local legend recorded as early as 1736 by historian Francis Blomefield, a benevolent fairy cow would magically manifest in South Lopham whenever the community faced times of distress or drought, offering her milk freely to anyone in need. When the period of hardship finally ended, the creature would stamp her hoof firmly onto a slab of sandstone in a meadow known as Oxfootpiece, burning the permanent imprint of her hoof into the rock before disappearing. Other variants of the legend attribute the hoofprint to an ordinary cow that was attacked or milked through a sieve by an intoxicated man or witch until she kicked out in pain, or to a rampaging ox with a thorn in its foot. The stone slab itself, known as the Ox-Foot Stone, was historically preserved at Oxfootstone Farm in the parish.

#location
The former site of Oxfootstone Farm in South Lopham, Norfolk.

#upstream-source
Francis Blomefield, *History of Norfolk* (1736); W.A. Dutt, *The Ancient Mark-Stones of East Anglia* (1926); Michael W. Burgess, *The Standing Stones of Norfolk and Suffolk* (1978).

#title
The Legend of the Ox-Foot Stone

#summary
A weathered sandstone slab in South Lopham bears a mysterious indentation said to be the hoofprint left by a magical fairy cow that vanished after a period of village hardship. In an alternative local variant, an ordinary cow kicked the rock in agony after being milked with a sieve by a drunken villager.

#details
The Ox-Foot Stone is an oblong slab of weathered sandstone situated in South Lopham. According to traditional folklore, during a severe period of dearth, a magical fairy cow would regularly appear in the village to provide free milk to the inhabitants. Once the drought and hardship ended, the creature stamped its hoof onto the stone and vanished. Another version of the legend claims the stone was kicked by a village cow when a drunken local attempted to milk her using a sieve, causing the distressed animal to lash out and leave a permanent hoofprint on the rock.

#location
The Ox-Foot Stone is located at Oxfootstone House on Brickkiln Lane in South Lopham (Grid Reference TM05178098).
