#title
The Cursed Goat's Head of Strumpshaw

#summary
For decades, a mounted goat's head known as "Old Capricorn" hung in a Strumpshaw pub, drawing tales of poltergeist activity, sudden tragedies, and mysterious reappearances after being thrown into a river.

#details
In 1908, Mrs. Newton, the landlady of The Goat Inn in Strumpshaw, purchased a white goat from an itinerant pedlar for half a crown. Years later, local resident Harry Thompson slaughtered the animal and mounted its head behind the pub's bar. Featuring long horns and unsettling black-and-hazel eyes, the trophy became known as "Old Capricorn" and soon gathered a dark reputation.

When Frank Walpole took over the inn in 1967, a series of bizarre and terrifying occurrences plagued the building: mirrors flew off walls, the piano played by itself with the lid closed, and a 17-year-old patron tragically died in a car crash the day after touching the head. Believing the relic was malevolent, Walpole removed it, but after regulars demanded its return, it was reinstated—only for farm animals and pets to suffer mysterious fates. 

In February 1972, Walpole weighted the head down and threw it into the River Yare to rid the pub of the curse. Weeks later, it was discovered floating miles away in Rockland Dyke. The head was briefly handed over to a local farmer, whose family immediately suffered bizarre misfortunes before the relic was once more returned to the area and eventually found buried in a shallow grave at the Strumpshaw gravel pit. Although the pub was renamed The Huntsman in 1984, the chilling legend of the cursed goat's head remains a staple of Broadland folklore.

#location
The Goat Inn (now operating as The Huntsman), located in Strumpshaw, Norfolk.

#title
The Cursed Goat's Head of Strumpshaw

#summary
A mounted goat's head brought to a Norfolk pub in 1908 unleashed decades of poltergeist activity, tragic accidents, and bizarre hauntings that persisted even after repeated attempts to destroy or banish the relic.

#details
In 1908, Mrs. Newton, the wife of the landlord at The Goat Inn in Strumpshaw, bought a magnificent white goat from an itinerant pedlar. After the goat was slaughtered, its preserved head—complete with long horns and piercing black-and-hazel eyes—was mounted behind the bar. Decades later, under landlord Frank Walpole, the pub became plagued by inexplicable phenomena, including mirrors flying off walls, a piano playing by itself, phantom figures, and a fatal car crash suffered by a teenager who had touched the head. Believing the relic was cursed or haunted, Walpole repeatedly removed it, throwing it into the River Yare and burying it in a gravel pit, but the head continually resurfaced or caused new misfortunes wherever it went, cementing its chilling legacy in local folklore.

#location
The Goat Inn (subsequently renamed The Huntsman), located in the village of Strumpshaw in Broadland, Norfolk.
