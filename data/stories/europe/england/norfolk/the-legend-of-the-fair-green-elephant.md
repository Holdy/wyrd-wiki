#title
The Legend of the Fair Green Elephant

#summary
Generations of residents in Diss passed down a persistent tale that a circus elephant was buried beneath Fair Green, a local legend later supported by historical records, eyewitness accounts, and archaeological surveys.

#details
For decades, local folklore in the market town of Diss maintained that a large circus elephant had been buried secretly beneath the turf of Fair Green after dying while visiting with a travelling troupe. While some local records pointed back to the 1867 death of Madame Abdella—a 20-year-old Burmese elephant belonging to Edmonds’ Menagerie that fell ill and died on the green—other residents insisted a similar burial took place in the 1940s. An eyewitness named Colin Lond recalled that as a boy playing on the green, he watched an elephant being hoisted into a deep pit without its trunk before workers covered the carcass in white lime powder. In 2019, ground-penetrating radar surveys conducted by geophysics students from the University of East Anglia revealed subterranean soil anomalies in the corner of the green near the playground, providing physical evidence that corroborated the long-standing town legend.

#location
Fair Green in Diss, Norfolk, specifically in the corner near the playground.
