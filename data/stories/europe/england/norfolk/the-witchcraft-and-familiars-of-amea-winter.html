    The Witchcraft and Familiars of Amea Winter

    Summary

    In 1627, a resident of Grimston named Amea Winter was formally accused of witchcraft after allegedly bewitching a local man, prompting villagers to burn her supposed animal familiars in an act of sympathetic magic.

    Details

    In 1627, tensions in the Norfolk village of Grimston reached a boiling point when local resident Alexander Turner accused Amea Winter of witchcraft and bewitchment. According to historical witness statements submitted to local justices, during the period of the alleged affliction, two strange creatures resembling a frog and a toad—widely believed to be Winter's demonic familiars—appeared. Seizing one of the creatures, a villager named John Piper threw it into a fire and held it there until it was entirely consumed. Contemporaneous depositions noted that this fiery act of sympathetic folk magic caused Amea Winter to immediately become lame, mirroring the physical torment inflicted upon her familiar.
