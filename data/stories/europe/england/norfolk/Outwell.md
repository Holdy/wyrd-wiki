#title
The Bathing Eagles of Beaupré Hall

#summary
A pair of decorative stone eagles atop the gate pillars of Beaupré Hall were said to leave their perches every New Year's Eve under a full moon to bathe in the nearby River Nene.

#details
The 16th-century country house known as Beaupré Hall once stood in Outwell, featuring a pair of carved stone eagles adorning its entrance gate pillars. According to local legend, on the last night of the year whenever there was a full moon, these stone birds would miraculously come to life, descend from their pedestals, and bathe in the adjacent River Nene before returning. Before the historic hall was demolished in 1966, the stone eagles were removed from the property and eventually relocated to crown the gate piers at Fulbourn Manor in Cambridgeshire.

#location
The site of the former Beaupré Hall is located at grid reference TF51510456 in Outwell, Norfolk, near the River Nene.
