#title
Nobb's Corner

#summary
Following suspicions that he murdered his son in 1785, a Norfolk brickmaker hanged himself and was buried at a parish crossroads. This roadside grave established a local landmark known as Nobb's Corner, steeped in macabre folklore.

#details
In 1785, Richard Knobbs, a brickmaker living in the Norfolk village of Hempnall, fell under grave suspicion of murdering his son. Overwhelmed by the situation, Knobbs took his own life by hanging himself from a tree. In accordance with historical English penal practices for suicides, he was denied burial in consecrated churchyard ground and was instead interred anonymously at a remote parish crossroads on the boundary line. The junction where he was laid to rest became locally known as Nobb's Corner, enduring in regional folklore as a haunted and liminal site.

#location
Nobb's Corner is a crossroads located on a parish boundary in the Norfolk village of Hempnall.

#title
The Naming of Nobb's Corner

#summary
Following his suicide after being suspected of murdering his son, brickmaker Richard Nobbs was buried at a remote crossroads in Hempnall, forever renaming the junction Nobb's Corner.

#details
In 1785, the grim discovery of a young man's skeletal remains half-buried in a ditch at Tasburgh pointed suspicion toward his father, a local brickmaker named Richard Nobbs. Overcome by the accusation, Nobbs took his own life by hanging himself in Pope's Wood. Under the harsh penal laws and customs regarding suicides at the time, he was denied burial in consecrated church ground and was instead interred at the local crossroads where the parishes of Hempnall, Topcroft, and Woodton meet—a junction previously known as Sisland Cross. Ever since his roadside burial, the spot has been known locally as Nobb's Corner.

#location
Nobb's Corner is a staggered crossroads located at grid reference TM264939 where the parishes of Hempnall, Topcroft, and Woodton meet.

#title
The Vanishing Hound of Market Hole

#summary
A young cyclist returning home late at night nearly collided with a massive spectral dog that suddenly materialized on a country road before vanishing into thin air.

#details
Mrs. Sophia Wilson, a native of Hempnall, recounted an unsettling encounter experienced by her 24-year-old son. As he was cycling home from Norwich along the stretch of road known as Market Hole, he suddenly spotted a large black dog about to cross directly in front of his bicycle. Expecting a violent crash, he braced for impact, but the creature abruptly vanished. Stopping to inspect the dark road, he found nothing at all, returning home pale and deeply shaken by the paranormal encounter.

#location
Market Hole, also known as Field Lane, is a stretch of road located in Hempnall, Norfolk.
