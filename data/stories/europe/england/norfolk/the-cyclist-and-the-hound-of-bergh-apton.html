The Cyclist and the Hound of Bergh Apton

Summary

Returning home from a darts match late at night near the end of the Second World War, a cyclist was ambushed by Black Shuck near a roadside pond. The snarling hound lunged at crossbar height before miraculously vanishing into a solid wall.

Details

In late November of 1944 or 1945, S.W. Gilbert was cycling home around midnight after playing in an away darts match for the Thurton George and Dragon team. As he rode along the road from Thurton towards Bergh Apton, near the pond opposite Washingford House, a large black dog suddenly leaped out from the roadside. Described as being as large as a Labrador with a smooth back and shaggy fur hanging below, the beast bared its large teeth, growled menacingly, and lunged at him at crossbar height. Expecting to be knocked from his bicycle and attacked, the terrified cyclist was astonished when the hound abruptly vanished straight into the wall on his right-hand side, leaving him shaking with fear and pedalling home as fast as he could.
