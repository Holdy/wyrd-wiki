#title
The Stolen Tower of West Walton

#summary
The Devil and a band of laborers attempted to steal the massive stone bell tower of St. Mary's Church, only to drop it on the edge of the churchyard when they found it too heavy and cumbersome to move. Another local tradition attributes the feat to the giant Tom Hickathrift.

#details
In the village of West Walton, the 13th-century bell tower of St. Mary's Church stands completely detached, situated some sixty or seventy feet away from the main nave on open arches at the entrance to the churchyard. According to local folklore, the Devil took a fancy to the magnificent structure and resolved to steal it. Rather than using his own imps, the Devil enlisted a crew of human laborers. Although the men were strong enough to lift the heavy tower onto their shoulders, they lacked intelligence; they could neither fit it through the narrow gate nor clear the churchyard wall. After marching in vain around the perimeter looking for an exit, they gave up and dropped the massive stone tower right where it now stands. In an alternative version of the legend, the famous Norfolk giant Tom Hickathrift picked up the tower on a bet, but discarded it because it was far too heavy for him.

#location
St. Mary's Church is located in West Walton, Norfolk (grid reference TF471133), where its detached bell tower stands over open arches at the churchyard entrance.
