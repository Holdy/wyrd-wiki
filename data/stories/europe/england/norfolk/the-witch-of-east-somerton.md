#title
The Witch of East Somerton

#summary
According to local legend, an accused witch was buried alive inside the ruined St Mary's Church in East Somerton, where her wooden leg sprouted into a mighty oak tree and her restless spirit continues to haunt the grounds.

#details
Local tradition holds that during the height of England's witch trials, villagers dragged an accused woman to the medieval St Mary's Church in East Somerton and buried her alive in the nave on consecrated ground. Because she had a wooden leg, it is said that she enchanted the limb before her death so that an oak tree—now known as "The Witch's Leg" or "The Witch's Finger"—would sprout from her grave and tear apart the church above. On cold, dark nights and at twilight, the witch's spirit drifts across the skeletal ruins. Furthermore, ghostly monks are said to patrol the shattered walls to prevent curious visitors from releasing her spirit by circling the tree three times.

#location
The ruins of St Mary's Church, located in the woodland near Burnley Hall Estate in East Somerton, Norfolk.
