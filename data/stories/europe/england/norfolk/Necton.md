#title
Old Mother Fyson of the Necton Border

#summary
Elizabeth Fyson, an eighteenth-century local healer and reputed witch, operated out of the Jolly Farmers Inn where its position straddling the Necton parish boundary allowed patrons to evade drinking hour restrictions. Legend holds that thirsty locals could gain an extra hour of drinking simply by stepping across the room into the Necton half of the building.

#details
Elizabeth Robinson, born in 1717, settled in Holme Hale after marrying Thomas Fyson. Following his death in 1765, she spent decades living alone and built a formidable reputation across the region as a cunning woman, doctress, and witch known as "Old Mother Fyson." Villagers and travelers alike sought her out for medical cures, herbal remedies, fortune-telling, and love potions. Her residence, the Jolly Farmers Inn, served as a prominent coaching house uniquely positioned across the parish line between Holme Hale and Necton. Because Holme Hale and Necton enforced different licensing hours, tavern-goers exploited this geographical quirk; when the landlord called time under Holme Hale jurisdiction, patrons could simply step across the indoor boundary into the Necton side of the inn to continue drinking for another hour. In her old age at 82, Elizabeth married a young man named Richard Parfrey, who eventually squandered her accumulated savings, leaving her in poverty before her death in 1803.

#location
The Jolly Farmers Inn was situated on the parish boundary between Holme Hale and Necton in Norfolk.
