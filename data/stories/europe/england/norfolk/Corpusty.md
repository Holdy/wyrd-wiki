#title
The Devil's Dish

#summary
After human remains were improperly covered up rather than given a Christian burial in 1717, divine wrath caused the ground in Mossymere Wood to open up, swallowing ancient oak trees and creating a deep sinkhole claimed by the Devil.

#details
In July 1717, human remains and ancient relics were discovered within Mossymere Wood in the parish of Corpusty. Rather than taking the bones to a consecrated site for a proper Christian burial, those who found them hastily covered them up on the spot. This illicit act was viewed as a grave crime against God, invoking severe divine displeasure. On July 23, 1717, a thunderous crack echoed through the woods as the earth suddenly gave way; several large oak trees were swallowed by the sinking ground, and bubbling water rose up in their place. Once the water drained away, it left behind an 18-metre-wide depression known as the Devil's Dish, which local folklore maintains was seized by the Dark One as his own domain.

#location
The Devil's Dish is an 18-metre depression located on the southern side of Mossymere Wood within Corpusty parish, near Saxthorpe (grid reference TG130309).

#title
The Organ of Corpusty

#summary
When a long-silenced organ in a village church is restored against the dying wishes of its 18th-century builder, a terrifying supernatural reckoning is unleashed from beyond the grave.

#details
In 1748, Sir Roger Kettle, lord of the local manor, commissioned a magnificent organ to be built at St. Peter's Church to please his music-loving wife, Lady Henrietta. Following her sudden and accidental death, a grieving Sir Roger issued a strict deathbed decree that the instrument must never be played again to prevent a dark reckoning from beyond the grave. More than a century later, as the organ sits in deep decay, the local vicar Cedric Fox-Bright decides to restore it to working order for church services. Despite warnings of the curse, the restoration moves forward, leading to a horrifying supernatural manifestation when the forbidden instrument sounds once again.

#location
St. Peter's Church, situated in the village of Corpusty, Norfolk.

#title
The Rising Corpse of Corpusty

#summary
In 19th-century Corpusty, a grieving sister bribed a gravedigger to open her brother's coffin at midnight, resulting in a terrifying encounter when the corpse unnaturally swelled, floated, and groaned.

#details
A macabre 19th-century tale from the village of Corpusty tells of a man who died and was buried while his sister was away. Unable to attend the funeral services, she became desperate for one last glimpse of her brother and bribed the local gravedigger to unearth and open the coffin at the stroke of midnight. When the gravedigger prized the coffin lid aside, the dead man's body inexplicably grew very large, floated upward like a ghost, uttered a hoarse groan, and then collapsed back down into the coffin.

#location
St Peter's Church graveyard located in the village of Corpusty, Norfolk.
