#title
The Haunted Squash Courts of Bircham Newton

#summary
Ghostly footsteps, phantom airmen, and inexplicable audio recordings of wartime activity have long haunted the disused squash courts at the former RAF Bircham Newton airfield.

#details
Established in 1916 as a training base for the Royal Flying Corps, RAF Bircham Newton played a vital role through two world wars before closing in 1960. Over 530 servicemen lost their lives during its operational history. Years later, when the site was repurposed for construction training, the station's recreation squash courts gained a dark reputation for spectral phenomena. In the 1970s, a film crew member playing squash alone in the courts heard footsteps in the viewing gallery and turned to witness the apparition of an RAF serviceman dissolving into thin air. Later paranormal investigations and audio recordings captured chilling sounds inside the right-hand court—including metallic clanks, heavy doors, and the hum of aircraft engines—while students and visitors in subsequent decades reported being pinned to their beds by unseen forces and chilled by dramatic, sudden drops in temperature.

#location
The disused squash courts located within the grounds of the former RAF Bircham Newton airfield (now the National Construction College) in Bircham Newton, Norfolk.

#title
The Haunted Squash Courts of Bircham Newton

#summary
While playing alone in the disused squash courts at the former RAF base in Bircham Newton, a film crew member encountered the spectral figure of an airman. Subsequent audio recordings made inside the building captured phantom footsteps, aircraft engine noises, and wartime chatter.

#details
In December 1971, assistant soundman Kevin Garry was working with a film crew at the former RAF Bircham Newton airbase when he decided to play a game in the base's disused squash courts. Armed with the only key, he entered the second court, where he noticed a sudden drop in temperature. Hearing footsteps and a sigh from the viewing gallery, he turned to see the ghostly apparition of an RAF serviceman before it vanished into thin air. Terrified, he fled, accidentally locking himself inside before escaping. Later that night, he and technician Peter Clark returned with a tape recorder, left it running in the right-hand court for 25 minutes, and captured phantom footsteps along with muffled voices and aircraft engine sounds. The recording was later broadcast on the BBC, sparking years of similar paranormal accounts from students and staff experiencing poltergeist activity, physical pushes, and ghostly encounters across the airfield.

#location
The disused squash courts located on the grounds of the former RAF Bircham Newton airbase (now the National Construction College) in Bircham Newton, Norfolk.
