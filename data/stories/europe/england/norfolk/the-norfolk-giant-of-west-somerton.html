    The Norfolk Giant of West Somerton

    Summary

    Robert Hales, a celebrated Victorian giant who stood over seven and a half feet tall and toured the world with P.T. Barnum, was laid to rest in his home village, where his massive stone tomb remains a legendary local landmark.

    Details

    Born in West Somerton in 1820, Robert Hales grew to an astonishing height of seven feet six inches and weighed over thirty stone. Known widely as the "Norfolk Giant," Hales became a major Victorian celebrity, traveling across America, joining P.T. Barnum’s famous travelling exhibition, and even being presented to Queen Victoria at Buckingham Palace, where he famously gifted the monarch a pair of giant gloves. Despite his international fame and travels, Hales remained deeply attached to his roots in West Somerton. Following his death from bronchitis in 1863 at the age of 43, his body was returned to his home village. He was buried in the churchyard of St Mary's Church, where a large, imposing sarcophagus-style tomb was erected in his honor. Local lore and tradition have preserved his extraordinary story across generations, making his monumental grave beneath the church's ancient round tower a famous point of interest.
