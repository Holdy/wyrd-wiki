#title
The Babes in Wayland Wood

#summary
In the sixteenth century, an avaricious uncle abandoned his orphaned niece and nephew in a Norfolk forest to inherit their fortune, inspiring the dark local legend immortalized on the Watton town sign.

#details
According to local Norfolk folklore, two young orphaned children—a brother and sister—were left in the care of their uncle at Griston Hall. Eager to inherit their late father's wealth, the villainous uncle took the children deep into nearby Wayland Wood in the dead of winter and abandoned them to perish. When the children ultimately succumbed to starvation and the freezing cold, compassionate robins covered their lifeless bodies with leaves. Local tradition holds that the ghostly wails of the murdered children still echo through the ancient trees of the wood, and the historic oak beneath which they supposedly huddled together was struck by lightning in 1879. The tragic account, which later evolved into a popular nursery fable and pantomime, is permanently commemorated on the official town sign of Watton.

#location
Wayland Wood, located just south-east of Watton in Norfolk, England, directly off the A1075.

#upstream-source
Thomas Millington, *The Norfolk gent his will and Testament* (1595).

#title
The Ghosts of Wayland Wood

#summary
The spirits of two young orphaned children, abandoned in a dark Norfolk forest by their treacherous uncle seeking an inheritance, are said to wander the trees wailing in the dark.

#details
An orphaned brother and sister were placed in the care of their uncle at Griston Hall near Watton. Eager to seize their father's fortune, the villainous uncle lured the children deep into the murky expanse of Wayland Wood during the bitter winter and left them to die. As the children succumbed to the cold, a robin famously covered their frozen bodies with leaves. Local folklore maintains that their little ghosts continue to wander the woodland, heard wailing in the darkness. The tragic legend inspired the famous tale of "Babes in the Wood," and is commemorated on the Watton town sign, which depicts the two children beneath an oak tree.

#location
Wayland Wood is an ancient woodland situated on the edge of Watton in south Norfolk, England.
