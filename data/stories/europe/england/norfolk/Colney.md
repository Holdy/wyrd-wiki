#title
The Lions of Colney Hall

#summary
In December 1911, two pet African lions kept in a private menagerie at Colney Hall mauled and fatally injured the estate owner's 29-year-old son, Lieutenant Terence Barclay.

#details
In the late 19th century, Hugh Gurney Barclay of the prominent banking family acquired Colney Hall, a grand estate featuring man-made Pulhamite caves and a private menagerie. During a safari in Kenya, Barclay was gifted two African lions—named Mitzi and Fritzi—by a German count. The lions were brought back to Norfolk and kept as pets on the estate. Although treated like domestic cats, tragedy struck in December 1911. Twenty-nine-year-old Lieutenant Terence Barclay, having handled raw meat earlier that day, went near the enclosure where the lions attacked him. Though a groundskeeper intervened by firing a gun to scare them off, Terence ultimately died from his injuries. He is buried in the churchyard of St Andrew’s Church in Colney, where his headstone famously understatement-notes that he died "from the result of an accident."

#location
Colney Hall and St Andrew's Church in the parish of Colney, located west of Norwich, Norfolk.
