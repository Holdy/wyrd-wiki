#title
The Secret Tunnels of North Elmham

#summary
Local legends in North Elmham speak of hidden subterranean passages, including a tunnel from St. Mary’s Church to the medieval Bishop's Palace and an attempted exploration of a legendary eleven-mile tunnel to Little Walsingham.

#details
According to local folklore and historical records in North Elmham, two secret underground passages are said to connect the village's historic ecclesiastical sites. One traditional belief maintained that a subterranean passage ran directly from beneath the altar of St. Mary's Church north toward the ruins of Bishop Despencer’s Manor House, commonly known as the Bishop's Palace. Another prominent local legend claimed that a much longer tunnel once linked the ancient church grounds—built upon the site of a Saxon cathedral—all the way to the priory at Little Walsingham, eleven miles away. Local accounts from the 1940s tell of youngsters who attempted to explore this subterranean passage, only for the entrance to collapse after just a few metres, narrowly allowing them to escape unharmed.

#location
St. Mary’s Church and the Bishop’s Palace ruins in North Elmham, Norfolk.

#upstream-source
Samuel Lewis's *A Topographical Dictionary of England* (1848); local folklore records compiled in East Anglian archive collections.
