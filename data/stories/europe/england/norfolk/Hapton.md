#title
The Haunting of Hapton House

#summary
While working upstairs alone in Hapton House, a local cleaner experienced phantom footsteps, doors repeatedly slamming and opening on their own, and flowers moving across shelves.

#details
During her years working across various homes in the village, Joan Sculfor spent time cleaning at Hapton House. While working alone upstairs in the building, she heard unexplained footsteps pacing along the passageway outside. When she checked, nobody was there. Immediately afterward, a door slammed shut by itself, then creaked open, repeating the cycle despite the complete absence of any breeze or draughts. During her time there, she also witnessed flowers mysteriously shifting positions on the shelves.

#location
Hapton House is a residence located within the rural parish of Hapton in the South Norfolk district of Norfolk, England.

#title
The Protector's Visit to Hapton

#summary
A persistent local legend maintains that Oliver Cromwell stayed in the village of Hapton during the English Civil War, a tradition once commemorated by a plaque on the local stables.

#details
For generations, word-of-mouth tradition in Hapton held that Oliver Cromwell visited and stayed in the village during the turbulent years of the English Civil War, with older residents remembering a plaque mounted on the village stables that cited the event. Historical records reveal deep, complex ties between the area and the Parliamentary leader: Cromwell was a distant relative and correspondent of Thomas Knyvett III, the Royalist Lord of the Manor whose family jointure-home was Hapton Hall. Although Knyvett was captured by Cromwell at Lowestoft early in the conflict and faced aggressive land sequestration by local Parliamentary committees, influential family connections ultimately spared him. Years later, in 1646, Cromwell even wrote to Knyvett asking him to protect the "poor men of Hapton"—a group of local Independents facing religious suppression. While definitive proof of Cromwell physically sleeping in the village remains lost to time, the enduring oral tradition and the tangible web of wartime friction between Cromwell and the local gentry have cemented the legend in village history.

#location
Hapton Hall and the historic village stables in Hapton, Norfolk.
