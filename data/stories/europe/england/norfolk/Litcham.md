#title
The Crossroads Burial of Litcham

#summary
An 1815 parish road survey documents a remote three-way junction in Litcham where a man was interred with a stake driven through his body. Such deviant roadside burials were historically practiced to deter restless spirits and deny suicides consecrated ground.

#details
Historical records and regional road surveys highlight a grim piece of folklore and local history associated with the Norfolk parish of Litcham. An 1815 parish road survey explicitly references a remote junction known as "the three crossways" within the parish boundaries, noting it as the site where an unnamed man was buried directly in the public highway with a stake driven through his corpse. In accordance with traditional English penal and folkloric customs of the period, individuals who took their own lives (*felo de se*) or suffered execution were forbidden from being buried within hallowed churchyards. Instead, authorities and superstitious villagers interred them at lonely crossroads and highway boundaries, believing that the confusing geography would prevent wandering ghosts from finding their way back to inhabited settlements.

#location
The location is centered at the historic three-way crossroads within the parish of Litcham, Norfolk.

Robert Halliday: 'The Roadside Burial of Suicides: An East Anglian Study' in 'Folklore' Vol.121, No.1 (2010), p.86 (referencing an 1815 road survey of Litcham parish).

#title
The Stake at the Litcham Crossways

#summary
An 1815 parish road survey records a lonely three-way crossroads in Litcham where a suicide was buried with a stake driven through his body. This grim wayside grave became a focal point for local folklore and ghostly dread.

#details
Recorded in an 1815 road survey of Litcham and discussed in historical studies of East Anglian folklore, a desolate three-way crossroads in the parish was the site of a traditional deviant burial. An unnamed man who had taken his own life was interred directly in the highway at the junction, complete with a wooden stake driven through his corpse to prevent his spirit from wandering. The lonely spot became deeply ingrained in local fears, remembered as a site where the restless dead lingered.

#location
The three-way crossroads located within the parish of Litcham, Norfolk.
