#title
The Apparition of Sandy Lane

#summary
A woman walking home late at night encountered a transparent, floating entity that appeared to scale invisible steps before vanishing into the dark. This marked the first documented sighting of a haunting that repeated along the lane throughout the late 1960s and early 1970s.

#details
During the late 1960s, a woman was walking home along Sandy Lane in Dereham just before midnight following an evening out with friends. As she made her way through the darkness, a chilling apparition materialized before her. The nearly transparent, floating figure appeared to climb a flight of invisible stairs suspended in the air before dissolving completely into the night. This bizarre encounter stands as the first recorded report of the Sandy Lane ghost, which went on to manifest on multiple occasions over the following few years.

#location
Sandy Lane is a road located in the town of Dereham in Norfolk, England.

#title
The Stalking Black Hound

#summary
In November 1945, a cyclist named John Harries was stalked by a ghostly black hound along the road towards the former RAF base at Swanton Morley before it vanished into thin air.

#details
In November 1945, John Harries was cycling through the Norfolk countryside towards the former RAF base at Swanton Morley when he encountered a mysterious black hound. The spectral dog tracked his movements, maintaining an unnerving presence just out of sight and stopping whenever Harries stopped. As he continued towards the base, the hound vanished completely.

#location
The road leading towards the former RAF Swanton Morley near East Dereham, Norfolk.

#title
The Stolen Saint and the Miraculous Spring of Dereham

#summary
Following the death of the Anglo-Saxon princess St. Withburga, her miraculously incorrupt body was enshrined in Dereham until monks from Ely cunningly stole her relics after plying the villagers with a great feast. In the wake of her original burial, a clear holy spring emerged from the ground, which became a celebrated site of pilgrimage and healing.

#details
In the 7th century, St. Withburga, daughter of King Anna of East Anglia, founded a convent in Dereham. Upon her death in 743, she was buried in the churchyard. Roughly fifty years later, her coffin was opened and her body was discovered to be entirely incorrupt, prompting her translation to a shrine inside the church. According to local legend, a crystal-clear spring miraculously welled up on the exact site of her original tomb. In 974, the ambitious monks of Ely—seeking to unite Withburga's remains with those of her saintly sisters—travelled to Dereham and hosted a lavish feast for all the local townspeople. Once the villagers were sufficiently intoxicated, the monks slipped into the church, took the still-flexible body of St. Withburga, and carried it across the fens to Ely Cathedral, leaving behind a mocking receipt of the theft. Despite the removal of her physical relics, her holy well persisted as a revered site of local wonder and therapeutic bathing through the centuries.

#location
St. Nicholas Church in East Dereham, Norfolk, where the historical holy well and old bath site lie just outside the west door of the church.
