#title
The Witch's Leg at St Mary's Church

#summary
Local folklore tells of a suspected witch who was buried alive in the nave of St Mary's Church, cursing her wooden leg to sprout into an oak tree that would eventually destroy the building. Legend warns that walking around the tree three times will release her vengeful spirit.

#details
In the woodland near Winterton-on-Sea at East Somerton stand the atmospheric, roofless ruins of St Mary's Church, which fell into disuse in the seventeenth century after its parish was absorbed by Winterton. Growing right out of the centre of the church's ruined nave is a striking oak tree. According to local legend, during the height of England's witch trials, villagers brutally buried a suspected witch alive inside the church. Possessing a wooden leg, the woman supposedly placed a curse upon it before her death, causing it to take root and grow into the tree now known as "The Witch's Leg," with the supernatural intent of tearing down the church above her. It is said that anyone foolish enough to circle the tree three times will awaken and release her trapped spirit, though ghostly monks are said to haunt the ruins to prevent intruders from doing so.

#location
The ruined St Mary's Church is located in a wooded grove near Burnley Hall in East Somerton, accessible via walking routes leading from Winterton-on-Sea, Norfolk.
