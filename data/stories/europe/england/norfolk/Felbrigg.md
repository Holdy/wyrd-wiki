#title
The Bibliophile of Felbrigg Hall

#summary
The spirit of former owner William Windham III is said to haunt the Gothic library at Felbrigg Hall, where he is occasionally spotted reading or sitting by the table. His eternal attachment to his books stems from a fatal injury sustained while rescuing valuable volumes from a burning building in London.

#details
William Windham III, an avid reader and politician born in 1750, met his tragic end following a heroic act in July 1809. Upon spotting a house on fire near his friend's valuable library, he rushed in to help rescue precious books, falling and badly injuring his hip in the process. The injury eventually developed into a tumor that required surgery, leading to his death in 1810. Since then, staff and visitors at Felbrigg Hall have reported encountering his ghostly apparition in the estate's Gothic library, often appearing seated at a table or relaxing in an easy chair when his favourite volumes are laid out.

#location
The Gothic library inside Felbrigg Hall, situated on the Felbrigg estate near Cromer in North Norfolk, England.

#title
The Phantom Bibliophile of Felbrigg Hall

#summary
William Windham III, a passionate book collector who died following a library fire rescue in 1810, is said to haunt the Gothic library at Felbrigg Hall in Norfolk, where his apparition appears among his beloved volumes.

#details
Felbrigg Hall, a country house and estate in North Norfolk, is reputed to be haunted by several spirits, the most famous of whom is William Windham III (1750–1810). Known in life as an enthusiastic statesman, sportsman, and fervent bibliophile, Windham met his tragic fate after rushing to rescue valuable books and manuscripts from a burning house on Conduit Street in London in 1809. During the rescue, he suffered a severe hip injury that eventually led to a fatal tumor and his death following surgery in 1810. He was buried in the family vault at Felbrigg Church, just a short distance from the hall.

Ever since, his spectre has frequently been encountered within the estate's Gothic library—often described as standing near a table where his favorite volumes are laid out or relaxing in an armchair by the hearth. Alongside the phantom bibliophile, local lore surrounding the hall includes accounts from former parlour maids who experienced mysterious candle-snuffing outside a vacant chamber accompanied by a disembodied voice, as well as legends of "Mad Windham" driving a phantom coach through the dark surrounding woods.

#location
Felbrigg Hall, situated in the village of Felbrigg, near Cromer, Norfolk, England.

#title
The Spectral Hound of Felbrigg Woods

#summary
Black Shuck, the legendary devil dog with glowing red eyes, is said to roam the ancient woods and grounds surrounding Felbrigg House as a fatal omen. Encountering the spectral hound in this North Norfolk estate forewarns of death before the year is out.

#details
Local folklore surrounding the historic Felbrigg estate tells of the haunting presence of Black Shuck, the spectral devil dog of East Anglia. Roaming the deep, ancient beech and oak woods planted centuries ago to shelter the grand house from North Sea gales, the phantom hound is described as a terrifying apparition with glowing red eyes. According to tradition, catching sight of this monstrous hell-hound is not merely a startling experience, but an ominous prophecy guaranteeing that the witness will meet their death before the year comes to an end.

#location
Felbrigg Hall and its surrounding historic parkland and woods in North Norfolk.
