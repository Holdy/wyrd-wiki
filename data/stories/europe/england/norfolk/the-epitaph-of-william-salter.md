#title
The Epitaph of William Salter

#summary
William Salter, a Yarmouth stage-coachman killed in a coaching accident on the steep hill in Haddiscoe in 1776, is commemorated by a celebrated rhyming epitaph built into the churchyard wall of St Mary's Church.

#details
William Salter worked as a stage-coachman driving the Yarmouth stage during the eighteenth century. On October 9, 1776, at the age of 59, his life came to a tragic end when his coach overturned on the steep hill running against St Mary's Church in the Norfolk village of Haddiscoe. He was laid to rest in the village churchyard, and a stone wall monument was set into the churchyard wall bearing a famous rhyming epitaph praising his honesty, dependability, and care for his horses, concluding with the poetic wish that he might drive straight up to heaven like Elijah. Over the centuries, the memorial has become a celebrated local landmark and piece of regional folklore, widely recorded in historical chronicles of the coaching age.

#location
The monument is set into the churchyard wall of St Mary's Church on Church Lane in Haddiscoe, Norfolk.
