#title
The Black Shuck of Weeting Castle

#summary
Visitors walking around the moat of Weeting Castle have reported encountering the infamous Black Shuck, a noiseless giant black hound with a single fiery eye.

#details
While Weeting Castle experienced a largely peaceful history free from sieges or conflict, its 12th-century ruins are occasionally disturbed by ghostly phenomena. In addition to reports of strange mists and disembodied voices, a couple walking around the castle's moat had a chilling encounter with the legendary Black Shuck. Known across Norfolk as a silent black dog as large as a calf with a single fiery eye in the middle of its head, encountering this spectral beast is traditionally believed to portend death before the year's end.

#location
Weeting Castle is a ruined 12th-century manor house located in the village of Weeting in Norfolk, England.

#title
The Cannon of Pepper Hill

#summary
A round barrow in Weeting parish received its name from the local tradition that Oliver Cromwell positioned his cannon upon it to bombard the nearby medieval manor.

#details
Near the medieval church and the ruins of Weeting Castle in Norfolk, a round barrow known as Pepper Hill (or Pepper High Hill) stands about a mile away. Local folklore attributes the barrow's name to the belief that during the English Civil War, Oliver Cromwell stationed his cannon atop the mound so that he could "pepper" the castle with artillery fire.

#location
Pepper Hill is a round barrow located in the parish of Weeting, Norfolk, at grid reference TL787882.

#upstream-source
W. G. Clarke: 'In Breckland Wilds' (Robert Scott, 1925), p.128.

#title
The Devil's Footstep at Fossditch

#summary
The ancient linear earthwork known as the Fossditch is said in local legend to have been gouged out by the Devil dragging his foot across the landscape.

#details
The miles-long linear earthwork dividing the parish of Weeting from Hockwold, known as the Fossditch or Fendyke, is the subject of old Breckland folklore. According to the tradition, the deep trench was created when the Devil dragged his foot along the ground, and a clod of soil that he scraped off his boot subsequently fell to form the earthworks at Thetford Castle.

#location
The Fossditch is an early Saxon linear earthwork running along the western boundary of Weeting parish in Norfolk, around grid reference TL771953.

Mark Knights: 'Peeps at the Past' (Jarrold & Sons, 1892), p.42.

#title
The Phantoms of Weeting Castle

#summary
Visitors to the medieval ruins of Weeting Castle have reported paranormal phenomena, including unexplained mists, disembodied voices, and encounters with the spectral hound Black Shuck.

#details
Weeting Castle, built as a grand manor house in the 12th century by Hugh de Plais, has a peaceful history free from conflict, yet its tranquil setting is frequently disturbed by otherworldly entities. Visitors exploring the ancient ruins have experienced eerie patches of strange mist and heard disembodied voices echoing across the site. Furthermore, a couple walking around the castle's ancient moat reported a terrifying encounter with the infamous Black Shuck, a noiseless spectral hound described as large as a calf with a single fiery eye.

#location
The medieval ruins and surrounding moat of Weeting Castle located in the village of Weeting, Norfolk, UK.
