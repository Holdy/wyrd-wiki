#title
The Hateful Thing of Geldeston

#summary
While walking home along a lane at night, a woman and her daughter encounter a terrifying, shapeshifting apparition known as the Hateful Thing, which trails them until it finally heads toward the local churchyard.

#details
As recorded in 19th-century East Anglian folklore, a local woman named Mrs. S set out for an evening walk from Gillingham back to Geldeston with her daughter and future son-in-law. As they crossed a stile into the road, the daughter—who had been born during the chime hours and possessed the sight to perceive hidden things—shouted in terror that a dog was blocking their way. Although neither Mrs. S nor the young man could initially see anything, the daughter watched in horror as the entity expanded in size until it was larger than a horse. When the daughter grabbed hold of her mother's arm, Mrs. S was suddenly granted the sight to witness the beast: a black, shapeless, ever-shifting entity that accompanied them down the road. The trio kept pace with the thumping apparition until it reached the sandy lane leading toward St. Michael's churchyard, where it finally disappeared.

#location
St. Michael's Church stands in the village of Geldeston, Norfolk, situated near the north bank of the River Waveney.

#upstream-source
Margaret Helen James, *Bogie Tales of East Anglia* (1891).

#title
The Hateful Thing of Geldeston

#summary
A woman walking back to Geldeston along the market path with her daughter and her companion encounters a terrifying, shapeshifting beast. The entity trails them before finally disappearing up the sandy lane leading to the churchyard.

#details
While returning to Geldeston from Gillingham along the market path at night, a mother and her daughter—who possessed the gift of second sight from being born during chime hours—encountered a mysterious entity. Initially perceived as a dog, the creature grew larger and shifted shapes as they walked, becoming visible to the mother the moment her daughter touched her. Although the daughter's companion could only hear its heavy thumping footsteps, the two women watched the black, shapeshifting beast pace ahead of them for half a mile until it turned into the sandy lane leading up to St Michael's churchyard.

#location
The sandy lane leading up to St Michael's Churchyard in Geldeston, Norfolk.

#upstream-source
Margaret Helen James, *Bogie Tales of East Anglia* (1891).

#title
The Highway Phantom of Geldeston

#summary
A terrifying spectral black dog as large as a horse once stalked lone travellers along the road from Geldeston to Bungay, growling fiercely and dragging victims by their clothing.

#details
At Geldeston in Norfolk, a monstrous black hound was historically known to prowl the highway leading toward Bungay. Described as being as large as a horse with fiery eyes and foaming jaws, the spectral beast would target pedestrians travelling alone at night. It stalked its victims closely, growling menacingly whenever anyone attempted to turn back, and occasionally seizing terrified travellers by their clothes to drag them along the road.

#location
The highway leading from Geldeston towards Bungay in Norfolk.
