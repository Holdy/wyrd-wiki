#title
The Swallowed Church of Dilham

#summary
According to local legend, a second medieval church in the Norfolk village of Dilham was entirely swallowed by the earth into a deep depression. This mysterious geological hollow, known as Seager-ma-hole, was long regarded by villagers as a supernatural site.

#details
Local folklore in the parish of Dilham tells of a lost church that once stood in the area before being swallowed up by the earth into a deep solution hole or depression called 'Seager-ma-hole', which was also traditionally held to be a fairies' bay where oxen or wandering livestock could vanish. The legend persisted through generations, reinforced by the eerie nature of the hollow. Decades later, in the mid-1990s, a large circular depression measuring three meters across and 0.6 meters deep suddenly opened up on the north side of the graveyard at the current St Nicholas Church, providing a striking physical phenomenon that mirrored the ancient tale of the swallowed church and kept the legend firmly rooted in local memory.

#location
The churchyard of St Nicholas Church in the village of Dilham, North Norfolk, near where the depression known as Seager-ma-hole is situated.
