#title
Heard's Holde and the Jack o' Lantern

#summary
Before the 1810 enclosure of Irstead, a phosphorescent spirit known as Jack o' Lantern haunted Heard's Holde in Alder Carr Fen Broad, said to be the ghost of a wicked man named Heard who drowned there. Locals were warned never to mock the light or keep their own lanterns lit, as the vengeful spirit would attack travellers and haunt their homes.

#details
Recorded by the rector of Irstead, Rev John Gunn, and published in the 1849 edition of *Norfolk Archaeology* based on the accounts of local resident Mrs Lubbock and her father, the legend centres on Heard's Holde in Alder Carr Fen Broad. According to tradition, the marshy spot was named after a man named Heard who was guilty of unmentionable crimes and drowned there. On misty nights, a phosphorescent light—known locally as "Jack"—would rise and fall, mimicking a candle in a lantern. 

Superstition dictated that travellers must extinguish their own lanterns when the light appeared to avoid having them smashed to pieces. Mrs Lubbock recounted an incident where her father and a companion were followed all the way home by Jack after the companion mocked and jeered at the phantom, culminating in the glowing light blazing up outside their windows. In another local attempt to rid the parish of the nuisance, three learned gentlemen tried to exorcise Heard's spirit by reading scripture to shrink and bottle him, but the clever ghost stayed one verse ahead of them until a boy distracted him with two pigeons, enabling the men to successfully bind his spirit.

#location
Heard's Holde is located in Alder Carr Fen Broad within the parish of Irstead in Norfolk.

#upstream-source
Rev John Gunn, *Norfolk Archaeology*, Vol. 2 (1849).

#title
The Lantern Man of Irstead

#summary
On foggy nights prior to 1810, a malignant spectral light known as Jack o' Lantern haunted Heard's Hole in Irstead, smashing lanterns and harassing travellers until its restless spirit was bound.

#details
Before the Irstead Enclosure in 1810, a ghostly dancing light resembling a candle in a lantern—known as Jack o' Lantern or the Lantern Man—was frequently seen on foggy nights at Heard's Hole in Alder Carr Fen Broad. Legend held that this intelligent, malevolent apparition was the spirit of a criminal named Heard who had drowned there. The spectral light actively attacked people carrying lanterns, knocked a mocking gentleman off his horse in the neighboring parish of Horning, and tormented scoffers by hovering outside their windows. Eventually, villagers sought to lay the spirit to rest; although the ghost kept ahead of scripture-reading men, a boy laid down a pair of pigeons which distracted the entity, allowing the men to successfully bind its spirit.

#location
Heard's Hole is situated in Alder Carr Fen Broad within the parish of Irstead, near the border with Neatishead.

#upstream-source
*Norfolk Archaeology. Or Miscellaneous Tracts Relating to the Antiquities of Norfolk* (1849) by Reverend John Gunn, recording the accounts of county wise woman Mrs Lubbock.
