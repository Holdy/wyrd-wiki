#title
The Quarrelling Sisters of Antingham

#summary
According to local tradition, the twin medieval churches standing side-by-side in a single Antingham churchyard were built by two feuding sisters who refused to worship together.

#details
In the Norfolk village of Antingham, visitors have long been intrigued by the presence of two separate medieval churches situated within the very same churchyard: the 14th-century St Mary’s Church and the ancient, roofless ruins of St Margaret’s Church. Local folklore explains this unusual architectural duplication through a tale of two sisters. According to the legend, the sisters fell into a bitter family quarrel. Being unwilling to reconcile their differences or share a place of worship, each sister independently commissioned her own church in the exact same parish churchyard so that they could practice their faith apart from one another. Although historians point out that St Margaret’s is actually a pre-Norman foundation while St Mary’s was constructed centuries later—making a simultaneous sisterly feud historically impossible—the legend persisted among villagers and local writers for centuries.

#location
St Mary's and St Margaret's churches share a single churchyard off Church Lane in the village of Antingham, Norfolk (NR28 0NL).
