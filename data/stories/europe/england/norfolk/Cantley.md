#title
The Callow Pit Coffer

#summary
A sunken iron chest filled with treasure lies hidden in a tree-shrouded pond at a remote crossroads, jealously guarded by supernatural forces. Attempts to haul the coffer ashore are thwarted when a single spoken word breaks the required silence, causing the chains to snap and the treasure to vanish forever into the depths.

#details
At a lonely crossroads where the parishes of Cantley, Southwood, and Moulton meet lies Callow Pit, a deep and murky pond shrouded by ancient trees. According to local folklore, a heavy iron coffer containing immense treasure—believed to be hidden by outlaws or smugglers—rested at the bottom of the pit. Determined to claim the wealth, local villagers gathered with a team of white oxen and heavy iron chains to drag the chest from the water. They successfully hooked the coffer and began hauling it toward the surface. However, magical tradition dictated absolute silence during the retrieval; the moment the chest broke near the surface, someone spoke out in excitement. Instantly, the spell was broken, the chains snapped with a deafening crack, and the coffer plunged back down into the dark abyss, never to be seen again.

#location
Callow Pit is located at the crossroads on the parish boundaries of Cantley, Southwood, and Moulton in Norfolk.

#title
The Devil and the Callow Pit Treasure

#summary
Two men attempting to salvage a chest of gold from Callow Pit are thwarted by the devil after boasting that Satan himself could not take it from them. The ensuing supernatural struggle causes the treasure to sink back into the depths forever, leaving behind only the chest's iron ring.

#details
Many years ago, two adventurous men took advantage of unusually low water levels at Callow Pit—located near the border of the Norfolk parishes of Southwood, Moulton, and Cantley—to haul up an ancient iron chest filled with gold. Using a platform of ladders across the pit, they inserted a staff through the ring on the lid and successfully raised the prize. As they celebrated, one of the men arrogantly proclaimed that not even the devil himself could take the treasure from them. Instantly, the pit was enveloped in a sulphurous cloud of steam, and a monstrous black hand emerged from the water to seize the chest. A fierce tug-of-war ensued between the men and the devil until the chest broke apart under the immense strain, sinking back into the water never to be seen again. The thwarted men escaped with nothing more than the heavy iron ring, which was subsequently fastened to the door of Southwood Church. Additionally, local folklore holds that a headless horseman rides at midnight from Callow Pit toward Cantley Spong.

#location
Callow Pit is situated on the parish boundaries near Southwood, Moulton, and Cantley in Norfolk.

#title
The Treasure of Callow Pit

#summary
Two adventurous men attempting to haul a chest of gold from a deep pit near Cantley lost their prize to the Devil, leaving behind only the iron ring from the chest which was affixed to a local church door.

#details
On the boundary of the parishes of Southwood and Moulton, near Cantley in Norfolk, sits a deep, tree-shrouded hollow known as Callow Pit (or Caller Pit), which village tradition whispered contained an iron chest filled with gold. Many years ago, two adventurous men took advantage of an unusually low water level to try to retrieve the treasure. Constructing a platform of ladders across the pool, they managed to insert a staff through the ring on the chest lid and heave it to the surface, placing the staff onto their shoulders to carry it away. 

In a moment of supreme hubris, one of the men shouted triumphantly, "We've got it safe, and the devil himself can't get it from us!" Instantly, a thick cloud of sulphurous steam enveloped the pit, and a black hand and arm surged from the water to grab the chest. A fierce tug-of-war ensued between the men and the unseen entity until the chest could no longer bear the strain and broke apart, sinking back beneath the waters forever. The only remnant the thwarted men walked away with was the iron ring from the lid, which was subsequently fastened to the door of Southwood Church. To this day, local folklore adds that the area is patrolled by a headless horseman riding at midnight from Callow Pit toward Cantley Spong.

#location
Callow Pit, situated on the parish boundary between Southwood and Moulton near Cantley, Norfolk, UK.
