#title
The Devil and Brograve Mill

#summary
Sir Berney Brograve made a fateful wager with the devil to mow a beanfield and, after losing, locked himself in Brograve Mill where he was terrorised through the night by cloven hooves. Today, his flaming-horsed ghost still haunts the Horsey marshes.

#details
In the 18th century, Sir Berney Brograve of Waxham Hall entered into a dangerous wager with the devil, betting his soul for the rights to drain the local fenlands if he could mow a beanfield faster than his dark challenger. Berney lost the contest and, refusing to surrender his soul without a fight, fled to seek refuge inside Brograve Mill, near the village of Horsey. 

All through the night, the devil pounded relentlessly on the mill's stout wooden door with his cloven hooves. By morning, the pounding ceased, but when Berney emerged, the door was hideously pitted with deep hoof marks, and the entire structure of the mill was left permanently tilting toward the west. Sir Berney eventually passed away, but his spirit never found rest; his phantom is frequently reported galloping across the Brograve Level near Horsey and Waxham upon a spectral horse that breathes fire from its nostrils.

#location
Brograve Mill, standing beside the Waxham Cut near the village of Horsey in Norfolk.
