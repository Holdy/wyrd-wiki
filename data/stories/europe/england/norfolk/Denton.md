#title
The Legends of Misery Corner and Hangman's Hill

#summary
Misery Corner and Hangman's Hill in Denton are local landmarks steeped in dark folklore, associated with tragic suicides, Civil War executions, and medieval gallows.

#details
In the Norfolk parish of Denton, two interconnected sites—Misery Corner and Hangman's Hill—are the subjects of grim local legend and traditional accounts. Misery Corner, situated at the junction where Manor Farm Road and Darrow Green Road meet, acquired its sorrowful name through several competing folk traditions. According to one enduring account, a pregnant servant girl working at Ivy Farm at the corner was overcome with shame and hanged herself from the rafters—or drowned herself in the farm pond—during the Civil War. Another local tradition claims the name stems from the execution of a soldier by firing squad or gallows at the site, or that "Misery" is a corruption of *Miserere*, the psalm sung by condemned soldiers as they were led to their deaths. 

Just a short distance away near Darrow Wood and Castle Hill lies Hangman's Hill, a small tree-covered mound traditionally believed to have been the site of executions where a gallows once stood. Together, these landmarks preserve centuries of dark local folklore regarding justice, tragedy, and death in the rural landscape of south Norfolk.

#location
Misery Corner is located at the junction of Manor Farm Road and Darrow Green Road (Grid Reference TM269898) in Denton, Norfolk, adjacent to Hangman's Hill.
