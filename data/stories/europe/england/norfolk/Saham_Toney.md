#title
The Crossroads Burial of Saham Toney

#summary
Following an act of ostracism after he poured wine and beer into the river, a local man took his life around 1790 and was interred at a village crossroads.

#details
Around 1790, an unnamed resident in Saham Toney began pouring wine and beer into the local river. This eccentric behavior outraged the villagers, who ostracized him by sending him to "Coventry." Distressed by the community's rejection, the man died by suicide and was consequently buried at the crossroads in accordance with post-medieval custom for self-murderers.

#location
The crossroads where Bell Lane, Ovington Road, Cley Lane, and Chequers Lane meet in Saham Toney, Norfolk (Grid Reference TF907019).

#upstream-source
Norfolk Historic Environment Record (Record MNF13171).

#title
The Sudden Appearance of Saham Toney Mere

#summary
According to local tradition, the circular mere in the center of Saham Toney miraculously materialized overnight.

#details
Local folklore records that the shallow freshwater mere in Saham Toney was said to have appeared overnight. The village sign famously depicts the mere alongside historical figures, reflecting local tales that monks from Castle Acre Priory were granted rights to fish in its waters twice a year, and early lords of the manor such as Sir Roger Toni were closely tied to the location.

#location
Saham Toney Mere, situated in the center of the village of Saham Toney in Norfolk.

#upstream-source
Ursula Bourne, East Anglian Village & Town Signs (1986).
