#title
The Bear of Bessingham Manor

#summary
At Bessingham Manor in Norfolk, the eccentric Edwardian landowner Edmund Denham Spurrell kept a pet bear in the cellars that occasionally escaped to terrorize local villagers before meeting a tragic end.

#details
Following the death of his father in 1906, Edmund Denham Spurrell returned from India to Bessingham Manor in North Norfolk, bringing with him an unusual companion: a live bear. The animal was housed in the outbuildings and the brick barrel-vaulted cellars of the Victorian manor, and was frequently brought out to entertain visiting guests. However, the bear became notorious around the surrounding countryside for escaping its enclosure, leading to stories of unsuspecting villagers finding the wild animal rummaging through their kitchens. The bear's escapades ultimately concluded in tragedy when it broke loose once more and attacked a member of the household, leaving Denham with no option but to shoot it. Local tradition also maintains that Denham's beloved horse was subsequently buried standing upright within the grounds of the manor.

#location
Bessingham Manor, situated on the edge of the village of Bessingham in North Norfolk, UK.
