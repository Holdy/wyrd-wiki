#title
The Black Hound of Blickling Hall

#summary
Following his death, the corpse of an unpopular Blickling baronet was repeatedly rejected by the consecrated earth before transforming into a terrifying spectral black dog that plagued the estate. The beast was eventually subdued by a London wizard and imprisoned within the south-east turret of Blickling Hall.

#details
After Sir Henry Hobart died from wounds sustained in a 1698 duel, his burial at Blickling church was interrupted when the earth repeatedly cast out his corpse, forcing villagers to sink it into the estate lake with heavy stones. A few days later, a gamekeeper caught a monstrous eel from the lake and brought it home, where it suddenly transformed into a giant black dog possessing the dead man's eyes and unleashed a dark curse across the entire Blickling estate. A famous wizard summoned from London eventually tamed the beast with seven whistles and led it into the south-east turret of Blickling Hall, where it was sealed behind a bricked-up door—only for sightings of the phantom hound to resume after the room was reopened in the 1860s.

#location
Blickling Hall is a historic Jacobean stately home situated in the village of Blickling, just north of Aylsham in Norfolk, UK.

#upstream-source
F. J. Meyrick, *Round About Norfolk and Suffolk* (1926).

#title
The Bound Hound of Blickling Hall

#summary
Following his death in a duel, the wicked Sir Henry Hobart found his corpse repeatedly rejected by consecrated earth, returning as a demonic black hound possessed by his evil soul. The beast was eventually subdued by a London wizard and sealed inside the south-east turret of Blickling Hall.

#details
After Sir Henry Hobart died from wounds sustained in a late 17th-century duel, his burial at Blickling church was immediately interrupted when his corpse was mysteriously cast out of the ground overnight. Re-buried in a woodland copse within the park, his body was once again rejected by the earth, prompting locals to weight it with heavy stones and sink it deep into the estate lake. A week later, after an unusual eel was caught from the lake, a spectral black dog with the dead man's eyes manifested on the estate, unleashing a dark curse over the grounds. A wizard from London was eventually summoned who, using his wand and seven whistles, tamed the demonic hound and led it to the south-east turret of Blickling Hall, where the village mason bricked up the doorway to imprison it forever.

#location
The south-east turret of Blickling Hall is situated within the Blickling Estate, located just north of Aylsham in Norfolk, England.

#title
The Haunting of Blickling Hall by Black Shuck and the Wicked Squire

#summary
Following the death of the indebted and cruel Sir Henry Hobart in 1698, his corpse was repeatedly rejected by consecrated earth and water before a demonic black dog bearing his eyes appeared and was sealed within the hall's turret.

#details
After Sir Henry Hobart died following a duel in 1698, his burial at Blickling church was disrupted when the earth seemingly rejected him, casting his corpse out of its lead casket. When reburied in a copse, the body was once again forced out of the ground. Desperate, the local clergy had the corpse weighted with heavy stones and dropped into ten feet of water in the estate's lake. Shortly after, a monstrous eel was caught, and a supernatural black dog—Black Shuck, possessing the dead man's wicked eyes—manifested at the hall. Unable to be trapped, killed, or poisoned, a London wizard eventually summoned the beast with seven whistles, leading it to the south-east turret of Blickling Hall where the door was permanently bricked up. Over a century later, in the 1860s, residents in the hall's drawing room once again witnessed a mysterious black dog running through the rooms just as the old bricked-up turret was reopened.

#location
Blickling Hall, situated in the village of Blickling near Aylsham in north Norfolk.

#title
The Headless Queen of Blickling Hall

#summary
On the anniversary of her execution, the ghost of Queen Anne Boleyn arrives at her former home in a headless horse-drawn carriage. Carrying her severed head, she wanders the ancient corridors until dawn.

#details
Every year on May 19th, the anniversary of her execution in 1536, the spirit of Anne Boleyn returns to Blickling Hall. She is said to arrive in a carriage driven by a headless coachman and pulled by four headless horses. Stepping out of the coach dressed all in white, the headless apparition carries her own severed head on her lap. She then glides silently through the ancient rooms, staircases, and countless corridors of the estate until daybreak. 

#location
Blickling Hall is a historic stately home and estate located in the village of Blickling, just north of Aylsham in Norfolk, United Kingdom (NR11 6NF).

#title
The Spectral Return of the Accused Queen at Blickling Hall

#summary
Born at Blickling Hall, Anne Boleyn was executed after being accused of witchcraft and treason, and her headless ghost is said to return to her birthplace every year on the anniversary of her death.

#details
Anne Boleyn, the second wife of King Henry VIII, was born at Blickling Hall in Norfolk. When Henry grew desperate for a male heir and sought to discard her, charges of treason, adultery, incest, and witchcraft were levied against her to ensure her downfall. In Tudor England, witchcraft was a capital crime often invoked against powerful or unpopular figures. Following her execution by beheading on May 19, 1536, local folklore maintained that her spirit could not rest. On the anniversary of her death every year, Anne's headless apparition returns to Blickling Hall. Legend describes her arriving in a carriage driven by a headless coachman, holding her severed, blood-stained head in her lap, before gliding through the darkened rooms of the hall until dawn.

#location
Blickling Hall, situated near Aylsham in Norfolk, UK.

Alison Weir, The Lady in the Tower (Random House, 2009).
