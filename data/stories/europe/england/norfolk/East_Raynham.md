#title
The Brown Lady of Raynham Hall

#summary
According to legend, Raynham Hall in Norfolk is haunted by the spirit of Lady Dorothy Walpole, an eighteenth-century noblewoman who famously manifests as an ethereal figure in a brown brocade dress.

#details
Lady Dorothy Walpole, sister of Great Britain's first Prime Minister Robert Walpole, was married to Charles, 2nd Viscount Townshend. Local legend holds that after her husband discovered her infidelity, she was locked away in her rooms at Raynham Hall until her death in 1726. Over the following centuries, the apparition—dubbed the "Brown Lady"—was witnessed by numerous guests and residents, including King George IV, Colonel Loftus, and Captain Frederick Marryat, who reported being confronted by a figure with empty, dark eye-sockets before firing a revolver through its phantom form. The haunting achieved global renown in September 1936 when photographers Captain Hubert Provand and Indre Shira captured what is claimed to be a photograph of the ghost descending the hall's main staircase.

#location
Raynham Hall, situated in the village of East Raynham, Norfolk, England.

#upstream-source
Florence Marryat, *There Is No Death* (1891).

#title
The Brown Lady of Raynham Hall

#summary
Lady Dorothy Walpole haunts her former home after allegedly being locked away by her jealous husband until her death in 1726. Her spectral form, famously captured on camera in 1936, continues to wander the corridors and staircase wearing a distinctive brown dress.

#details
Lady Dorothy Walpole married Charles Townshend in 1713, but rumor and suspicion plagued their marriage. According to legend, after her husband discovered perceived infidelity or was deceived by political rivals, Dorothy was imprisoned within Raynham Hall until her death from smallpox in 1726. Following her demise, guests and residents began reporting encounters with a ghostly figure in a brown brocade dress with glowing, empty eye sockets. In 1836, Captain Frederick Marryat encountered the apparition in the corridors, famously firing a pistol through the phantom's face before it vanished. Decades later, on September 19, 1936, photographers Captain Hubert C. Provand and Indre Shira captured what became one of the most famous and widely debated ghost photographs in history while documenting the hall's staircase.

#location
Raynham Hall is a historic country house situated in the village of East Raynham, near Fakenham in Norfolk, England.
