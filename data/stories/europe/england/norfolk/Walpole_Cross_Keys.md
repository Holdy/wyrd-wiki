#title
The Lost Treasury of King John

#summary
In October 1216, King John's baggage train and royal treasury attempted to cross the treacherous tidal marshlands between Walpole Cross Keys and Sutton Bridge, only to be swallowed by the advancing tide. To this day, the royal possessions remain lost beneath the silt of the Fenlands.

#details
In October 1216, amid the turmoil of the First Barons' War, King John traveled through Lincolnshire toward Bishop's Lynn. Stricken with dysentery, the King altered his own route while directing his baggage train—carrying the royal wardrobe, church relics, and the kingdom's treasury—to take what was thought to be a faster shortcut across the salt marshes and tidal mudflats of the Wash. As the carts, horses, and thousands of attendants attempted the crossing between Walpole Cross Keys and Sutton Bridge at low tide, the waters rose far faster than anticipated. The heavy wagons sank deep into the treacherous mud, and despite desperate efforts by soldiers and drivers to save the cargo, the advancing tide and coastal whirlpools engulfed the royal possessions entirely. Shortly after hearing of the disaster, King John died at Newark Castle, leaving his legendary treasure permanently buried beneath the silt of the Fenlands.

#location
The historical crossing point across the tidal estuary and marshlands between Walpole Cross Keys and Sutton Bridge.
