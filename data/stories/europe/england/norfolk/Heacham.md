#title
The Legend of the Golden Gates

#summary
Local folklore maintains that a pair of magnificent golden gates lies buried in a field near Eaton Farm just outside Heacham. These fabled treasures are said to be hidden close to the ancient route of the Icknield Way.

#details
According to regional tradition, a set of golden gates was hidden or buried in the earth near Eaton Farm, situated just north of the small bridge crossing the Heacham River roughly a mile outside the village. The site is noted as being close to the historic course of the Icknield Way and adjacent to a field marked on Tudor maps as 'Cattell Wong'. Despite generations of local speculation, the golden gates have never been uncovered.

#location
The legend centers on a field near Eaton Farm, located just north of the small bridge crossing the Heacham River about one mile outside the village of Heacham in Norfolk.

#title
The Phantom of the Orange House

#summary
The former home of Count Bentinck in Heacham is said to be haunted by his lingering presence, characterized by the smell of shag tobacco and spectral party sounds.

#details
When Lois Carrick and her family purchased the Orange House on the corner of Malthouse Crescent and Staithe Road in Heacham in 2007, the previous owners—who had lived there for nearly four decades—revealed that the property was haunted by a former resident, Count Bentinck. Residents noted the distinct smell of his shag tobacco lingering in the upstairs bedroom he once occupied, an identification later confirmed by his visiting daughter. Additionally, phantom sounds resembling an ongoing gathering, complete with the clinking of glasses and the tinkling of a piano, were occasionally heard coming from the drawing room.

#location
The Orange House, located on the corner of Malthouse Crescent and Staithe Road in Heacham, Norfolk.
