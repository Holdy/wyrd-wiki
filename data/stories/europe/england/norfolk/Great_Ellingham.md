#title
The Witches of Penhill Road

#summary
In the early 20th century, local children and residents of Great Ellingham feared a mother and daughter living in a wooden shack on Penhill Road whom they whispered were witches. Their accounts were later preserved in local historical recollections of the village.

#details
According to local memory recorded in William R. Lebbell's *A Little History of Great Ellingham*, two women—a mother and daughter—lived halfway up Penhill Road inside a wooden hut on the south side of the road, just off the track leading to Homelea Farm. During the 1920s, young children from local farming families who walked down Penhill Road from Poplar Farm to work in the fields were frightened of the two women, whom villagers regarded with superstitious fear as witches. While the women were likely merely eccentric or reclusive outsiders, their isolated dwelling and unusual lifestyle left a lasting impression of fear and folklore among the children who passed by their shack.

#location
The wooden shack stood on the south side of Penhill Road, just off the track leading to Homelea Farm in Great Ellingham, Norfolk.
