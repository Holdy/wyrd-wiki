The Haunted Squash Courts of Bircham Newton

Summary

Ghostly footsteps, phantom airmen, and inexplicable audio recordings of wartime activity have long haunted the disused squash courts at the former RAF Bircham Newton airfield.

Details

Established in 1916 as a training base for the Royal Flying Corps, RAF Bircham Newton played a vital role through two world wars before closing in 1960. Over 530 servicemen lost their lives during its operational history. Years later, when the site was repurposed for construction training, the station's recreation squash courts gained a dark reputation for spectral phenomena. In the 1970s, a film crew member playing squash alone in the courts heard footsteps in the viewing gallery and turned to witness the apparition of an RAF serviceman dissolving into thin air. Later paranormal investigations and audio recordings captured chilling sounds inside the right-hand court—including metallic clanks, heavy doors, and the hum of aircraft engines—while students and visitors in subsequent decades reported being pinned to their beds by unseen forces and chilled by dramatic, sudden drops in temperature.
