#title
The Phantom Light of Runton Mill

#summary
A mysterious glowing light periodically crosses the fields near Runton Mill and vanishes into a nearby copse where ancient human bones were discovered.

#details
Recorded in nineteenth-century accounts of local folklore, a strange phantom light has been witnessed travelling across the fields from Runton Mill in Norfolk. The luminous apparition moves from the mill area over the fields and finally sinks into a fir-spinney or copse. According to local tradition, the light buries itself directly into the earth at the exact location where human skeletal remains were once unearthed. While some skeptics, including antiquarian writer Walter Rye, attributed the phenomenon to light reflections from the distant Cromer lighthouse upon atmospheric vapor, residents insisted upon its supernatural nature, linking the nocturnal light to the forgotten graves.

#location
Runton Mill stands in the agricultural fields immediately south of the villages of West Runton and East Runton in Norfolk.

#upstream-source
Walter Rye, *A History of Norfolk*

#title
The Shrieking Pits

#summary
A spectral woman in white haunts the ancient iron-working hollows between Aylmerton and West Runton Heath, weeping and wailing as she searches every pit for her murdered baby. According to local legend, her husband killed both their child and her, leaving her spirit to wander the depressions in the ground in eternal despair.

#details
Between the 9th and 12th centuries, extensive iron-working activity across the wooded slopes between Aylmerton and West Runton Heath left behind numerous circular depressions and hollows in the earth. Over time, these pits became the center of a tragic local legend. It is said that a man, convinced that his infant child was not his own, murdered the baby and buried it in one of the pits near St John's Church before returning home to kill his wife as well. Ever since, the ghostly figure of the woman has been witnessed wandering through the hollows on dark nights. Clad in white, she peers desperately into each pit, shrieking in anguish when she discovers each one empty, trapped forever in her hopeless search for her lost child.

#location
The shallow iron-working hollows known as the Shrieking Pits, situated on the wooded slopes between Aylmerton and West Runton Heath in Norfolk.

#title
The Silent Phantom of West Runton Slipway

#summary
In December 1972, a fisherman on the beach at West Runton encountered a gigantic, silent black dog with a broad head and small ears that paced along the base of the cliffs under the full moonlight. Despite coming within fifteen feet, the spectral beast made no sound on the shingle and cast no shadow.

#details
In December 1972, a 52-year-old fisherman was fishing on the beach near the foot of the West Runton slipway between 2:00 AM and 3:00 AM. Noticing movement about 100 yards away near the cliffs on the Cromer side, he watched as a massive black dog—comparable in size to a Great Dane or Mastiff—approached. The creature possessed a notably broad head, a short muzzle, and small, bent-over ears. Assuming it was a domestic dog from the village, the fisherman whistled and called out to it, but the beast completely ignored him, keeping its head lowered as if tracking a scent. It reached the slipway, turned back along the foot of the cliff, and walked within fifteen feet of him over a span of fifteen minutes. Remarkably, the dog made no sound whatsoever on the loose shingle and cast no shadow in the full moonlight before the chilly witness packed up and returned home.

#location
The beach at the foot of the West Runton slipway on the North Norfolk coast, situated near the cliffs toward Cromer.
