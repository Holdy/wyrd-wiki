#title
The Gentleman of Foulsham Hall

#summary
A milk collector arriving early at Foulsham Hall encountered a white-haired gentleman in a dressing gown walking the grounds. This sighting sparked enduring local legends of a ghostly resident or a secret recluse linked to a subterranean tunnel.

#details
According to local tradition recorded in Thomas Quarles' historical accounts, Foulsham Hall is haunted by a mysterious white-haired gentleman. A poor local woman who routinely collected milk from the hall each morning arrived unusually early one day and spotted the figure wandering the grounds in a dressing gown. Speculation long surrounded whether the apparition was supernatural or Major-General Philip Skippon living in hidden seclusion following the Restoration. Adding to the mystery, local lore speaks of a rumored underground passage connecting the hall to the village church a quarter of a mile away.

#location
Foulsham Hall is located in the historic village of Foulsham in North Norfolk, England, situated approximately a quarter of a mile from the local parish church.

#upstream-source
Thomas Quarles, The History and Antiquities of Foulsham, in Norfolk

#title
The Secret Tunnel and Ghost of Foulsham Old Hall

#summary
Foulsham Old Hall is said to be haunted by a white-haired gentleman in a dressing gown, and linked to the parish church by a mysterious underground tunnel that reveals itself during droughts and cave-ins.

#details
Local legend holds that Foulsham Old Hall—historically associated with the Parliamentarian commander Major-General Philip Skippon—harbors a haunting specter. According to local accounts recorded in the 19th century, a poor milkwoman who arrived early one morning to collect milk from the Hall caught sight of a white-haired gentleman wandering the grounds in a dressing gown. Furthermore, tradition long maintained the existence of a subterranean passage running from the Hall's medieval moat all the way to the porch of the village church, a quarter of a mile away. This hidden tunnel was said to be traceable during long dry summers by the parched, discolored grass directly above it. The legend gained startling local credence when parish labourers working in the pasture north of the Hall suddenly fell through the collapsing ground into a brick archway, allowing a local bricklayer to explore sections of the ancient tunnel before being blocked by accumulated earth and debris.

#location
Foulsham Old Hall is situated within its medieval moated enclosure located at grid reference TG035247 in the parish of Foulsham, Norfolk.
