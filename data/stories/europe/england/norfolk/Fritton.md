#title
The Golden Plough of Bell Hill

#summary
In 1528, a rogue treasure-hunting monk named William Stapleton was drawn to Fritton by local folklore claiming a priceless golden plough was buried within an ancient mound. Guided by local accounts, he searched Bell Hill for the hidden treasure, though nothing of the sort was ever discovered.

#details
In 1528, William Stapleton, a monk who had abandoned St. Benet's Abbey to pursue treasure hunting, travelled to Fritton after being informed by a local woman connected to Calkett Hall. Local legend maintained that immense wealth, specifically a legendary golden plough, was hidden deep within Bell Hill. Stapleton and his companions investigated the mound—believed by locals to be an ancient barrow or earthwork—in an attempt to unearth the riches, but their efforts ultimately yielded nothing.

#location
Bell Hill mound (TG466014) in the parish of Fritton and St Olaves, Norfolk.

#upstream-source
L. V. Grinsell: 'Folklore of Prehistoric Sites in Britain' (David & Charles, 1976), p.137.

#title
The Long-Necked Entities of Fritton Woods

#summary
Two people in a parked car after dark encountered two tall, human-like figures with elongated necks standing near a tree. When the car's headlights were switched on, the strange entities vanished behind the trunk.

#details
In July 1997, a couple resting in their car in Fritton Woods during the hours of darkness noticed two strange figures standing quietly next to a tree. Straining to see in the dim light, they realized the figures were only human-like rather than fully human, featuring tall statures, elongated necks, and slim, unusually long hands. Terrified, the driver started the engine and switched on the headlights, flooding the surrounding woodland with bright light, at which point the mysterious entities simply slipped behind the tree and vanished without a trace.

#location
The sighting occurred among the trees within Fritton Woods (Waveney Forest) in Norfolk.
