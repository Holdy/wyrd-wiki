#title
The Ghost Runner of Morston

#summary
In the late 19th century, world-record-breaking sprinter Charles George Wood earned the local moniker "the ghost-runner" after startling residents by suddenly materializing out of thick coastal mists while training on the local marshes. His legacy is commemorated at his former home at Morston Hall and his grave outside All Saints Parish Church.

#details
During the 1880s, Charles George Wood (known as C.G.) lived in Morston and reached the pinnacle of global athletics, setting multiple British, European, and world sprinting records. To maintain his peak physical condition, Wood trained rigorously at dawn and dusk along the marsh footpaths connecting Morston and Stiffkey. Because thick sea fogs frequently rolled across the North Norfolk coastline during these hours, local walkers and travelers were often startled when Wood would suddenly and silently materialize at high speed out of the mist, earning him the enduring local nickname "the ghost-runner." Later in life, he also contributed to the establishment of the modern Olympic Games alongside Pierre de Coubertin. Wood passed away in 1937, and today his resting place is marked by a simple stone bearing the inscription "Athlete" outside the east window of Morston's 12th-century All Saints Parish Church, while his former family residence is preserved as Morston Hall.

#location
All Saints Parish Church overlooks the village of Morston, adjacent to the marsh footpaths stretching towards Stiffkey along the North Norfolk coast.
