#title
The Legend of Saint Walstan

#summary
Born into nobility in Bawburgh, Prince Walstan renounced his wealth at age twelve to work as a humble farm labourer, inspiring centuries of folklore about his miraculous oxen and holy wells.

#details
Born around 975 AD in Bawburgh, Walstan was the son of noble parents with royal connections. At the age of twelve, he chose to renounce his privileged background, leaving home to walk seven miles to Taverham to work as a farm labourer. Throughout his life, he remained deeply pious and charitable, giving away his wages and even his shoes to the needy. When the farmer’s wife forced him to work barefoot among thorns as punishment for his generosity, the thorns miraculously emitted a pleasant scent rather than scratching him. Nearing the end of his life, Walstan received a vision foretelling his death and instructed that upon his passing, his body should be placed on a cart pulled by two oxen, allowing the animals to wander freely to determine his burial place. Following his death on May 30, 1016, the oxen journeyed through the countryside, causing springs of water to gush forth wherever they paused, culminating in the valley below Bawburgh Church where St. Walstan's Well now stands. The oxen then climbed the hill to the church, where Walstan was buried, establishing Bawburgh as a major medieval pilgrimage site.

#location
St. Mary and St. Walstan’s Church is located on Church Street in the village of Bawburgh, Norfolk.
