#title
The Lantern Man of Alderfen Broad

#summary
In nineteenth-century Irstead, the wise woman Mrs. Lubbock and local villagers reported being haunted by a malevolent ghost light known as the Lantern Man, believed to be the spirit of a drowned criminal named Jack Heard. The entity terrorized travellers and notoriously attacked anyone who mocked him or carried a lit lantern near Heard's Hole and Alderfen Broad.

#details
During the nineteenth century, the marshland village of Irstead in Norfolk was said to be plagued by a terrifying ghost light known as the Lantern Man. According to Mrs. Lubbock, a renowned local wise woman who frequently witnessed the phenomenon, the spectral light was the restless spirit of a criminal named Jack Heard, who had committed terrible deeds during his life and ultimately drowned in nearby Alderfen Broad. 

Locals described the entity as appearing on misty nights around Heard's Hole, rising, falling, and twisting about like a candle enclosed in a lantern. The spirit was fiercely hostile: if travellers walked along the road carrying a lit lantern and failed to extinguish it, the Lantern Man would rush at it and dash it to pieces. On one notable occasion, a gentleman riding horseback through the adjoining parish of Horning mocked the entity by calling him "Will o' the Wisp," prompting the spectral light to attack him and knock him violently from his horse.

#location
Alderfen Broad and Heard's Hole lie within the marshy wetlands of the parish of Irstead in Norfolk, England.

#title
The Lantern Man of Alderfen Broad

#summary
A malicious ghost light known as the Lantern Man haunts the marshes and waters of Irstead, originating from the restless spirit of a drowned criminal named Jack Heard. The glowing entity terrorizes travelers by shattering their lanterns and attacking those who mock it.

#details
In 19th-century Irstead, a local wise woman named Mrs Lubbock frequently witnessed the eerie Lantern Man—a floating, candle-like ghost light that rose, fell, and twisted over the local marshes and Alderfen Broad. Local lore held that the entity was the ghost of Jack Heard, a criminal who had committed terrible deeds before drowning in the broad. The spirit was notoriously hostile; if travelers walked past with lit lanterns without putting them out, Jack would dash them to pieces. Once, when a gentleman mocked the entity and called him "Will o' the Wisp" while riding in nearby Horning, the Lantern Man attacked and knocked him straight off his horse.

#location
Alderfen Broad is a wetland nature reserve located within the parish of Irstead in Norfolk, England.
