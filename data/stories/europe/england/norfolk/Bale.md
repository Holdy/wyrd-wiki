#title
The Bale Oak

#summary
Standing just west of All Saints Church in Bale, the ancient Bale Oak was a massive tree steeped in local folklore of pre-Christian worship whose cavernous hollow trunk famously served as a cobbler's shop.

#details
Located in the North Norfolk village of Bale, immediately west of All Saints Church, the Bale Oak was a legendary tree of extraordinary proportions, measuring 36 feet in circumference with branches spanning over 70 feet. Local folklore maintained that the ancient oak had originally served as a site for pre-Christian pagan worship within an ancient sacred grove. By the 18th century, the tree's interior had hollowed out so extensively that local historian Rev. Blomfield recorded that ten or twelve men could stand inside it. During this period, the hollow trunk was actively utilized as both a cobbler's shop and a pigsty. As the centuries passed, the heavily damaged and rotting tree became a hazard, leading the Lord of the Manor, Sir Willoughby Jones, to order its removal in 1860 despite widespread local grief.

#location
The site of the ancient Bale Oak is located immediately west of All Saints Church in the village of Bale, North Norfolk.

#upstream-source
Archie Miles, *Hidden Trees of Britain*, and the historical accounts of Rev. Blomfield.

#title
The Ghostly Shuck Dog of Clip Street Lane

#summary
A grandmother witnessed a ghostly Shuck Dog just outside her bungalow on a green lane in Bale. The spectral hound's appearance was preserved as a vivid family memory.

#details
Passed down through family lore, a grandmother's encounter with a spectral 'Shuck Dog' occurred just a few yards from the gate of her newly built bungalow on a green lane in Bale. Although the primary witnesses have since passed away, the childhood recollection of this phantom hound remained a deeply real and enduring part of the family's oral tradition.

#location
The ghostly hound was encountered just outside a bungalow located on Clip Street Lane in Bale, Norfolk.

#title
The Phantom Hound of Clip Street Lane

#summary
A grandmother encountered the legendary Black Shuck just outside the gate of her bungalow on a green lane in Bale. This eerie sighting of the spectral hound was passed down through family recollections across generations.

#details
A resident's grandmother experienced a chilling encounter with Black Shuck, the demonic phantom dog of East Anglian folklore, only a few yards from the gate of her bungalow. The sighting took place on Clip Street Lane in Bale. Although the direct witnesses passed away, the story remained vivid within the family after being recounted from early childhood by an aunt who remembered the event.
