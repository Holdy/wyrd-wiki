#title
The Tragic Legend of Billy and Sally Grimes on Mulbarton Common

#summary
During a severe winter, an elderly couple perished in separate snow-filled hollows on Mulbarton Common after venturing out to visit a local public house during heavy blizzards. Their bodies were discovered weeks later when the snow finally thawed.

#details
The legend was originally related by the Reverend Richard Spurgeon, Rector of Mulbarton from 1812 to 1843, and later recorded by Ella Collier. It tells of an old couple, Billy Grimes and his wife Sally, who lived in a small cottage on the edge of Mulbarton Common. During a harsh winter marked by heavy snowfall and severe blizzards, the couple was confined indoors for days, interrupting Billy's daily habit of walking across the common to The World's End for an evening glass of beer. 

One evening, insisting that the snow had stopped and the moonlight would guide him, Billy grabbed his walking stick and set out across the common. When he failed to return after two hours, a worried Sally wrapped herself in a hood and cloak to search for him. The next day, a neighbour found their cottage empty and unlocked. Approximately six weeks later, when a rapid thaw cleared the deep snowdrifts from the common, Billy and Sally were found in separate hollows, having tragically blundered into the deep snow and suffocated.

#location
Mulbarton Common and The World's End public house in Mulbarton, Norfolk.

#upstream-source
Written by Ella Collier, as related by the Rev. Richard Spurgeon, Rector of Mulbarton 1812-1843, and preserved among the Rich's Charity papers.
