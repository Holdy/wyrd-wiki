#title
The Grey Lady of Hunstanton Hall

#summary
After a treasured Persian carpet gifted by the Shah was cut into pieces by a descendant, the ghost of Dame Armine le Strange returned to haunt Hunstanton Hall until the remaining fragments were recovered.

#details
Dame Armine le Strange, an 18th-century heiress and mistress of Hunstanton Hall, deeply cherished a magnificent, hand-woven Persian carpet gifted to her. On her deathbed in 1768, she made her son swear a solemn oath that her precious carpet would never be sold or destroyed, warning that if he broke her trust, she would return from the grave to haunt the estate. Decades later, an unsuspecting family member discovered the rug rolled up in the attic and cut it into smaller pieces to be distributed as household rugs. Immediately after the destruction of the carpet, Dame Armine's pale apparition was sighted peering angrily from the window of the Gatehouse. Upon realizing the terrifying consequence of their actions, the family frantically tracked down and reunited the surviving fragments at the Hall, where the Grey Lady has since been seen keeping watch over the estate.

#location
Hunstanton Hall is a historic country house situated in the village of Old Hunstanton, Norfolk.

#title
The Smugglers' Phantom Hound of Old Hunstanton

#summary
Smugglers in Old Hunstanton exploited the local legend of the spectral dog Old Shuck by attaching lanterns to black rams to frighten away curious villagers during contraband runs.

#details
In the coastal parish of Old Hunstanton, 18th-century smugglers routinely moved illegal contraband across the land. To deter nosey locals and inquisitive villagers from interfering with their operations, they capitalized on the terrifying regional folklore of Old Shuck—an enormous phantom hound described as having glowing eyes and fiery breath. The smugglers tied lanterns around the necks of black rams and drove them across the fields and tracks to mimic the demonic spectral dog, frightening people into staying safely indoors. This supernatural ruse complemented a violent rivalry with revenue authorities, whose fallen officers are buried within the parish churchyard.

#location
The key location is the churchyard of St Mary the Virgin in Old Hunstanton.
