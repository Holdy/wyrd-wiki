    The Grinning Demons of St Peter's Church

    Summary

    Carved into the timber chancel roof of St Peter's Church in Upwell are grotesque effigies of demons, which local tradition maintains represent the malignant spirits of the Fens that furiously glared down in hatred as the sacred building rose from the marshland.

    Details

    Standing alongside the ancient Well Creek in the Norfolk village of Upwell, St Peter’s Church has long been renowned not only for its striking medieval architecture and soaring timber roof, but also for the macabre details hidden in its woodwork. While the nave and aisles are supported by beautifully carved wooden angels bearing the insignia of the crucifixion and adorned with swans, the chancel roof tells a much darker visual tale. Interspersed among the structural timbers are the detailed effigies of horned, snarling demons, their faces twisted into hideous, grinning expressions of rage and torment. According to local lore passed down through generations of fenland parishioners, these grotesque carvings were created to portray the malvolent spirits that haunted the damp, mist-shrouded fens before the parish was tamed and consecrated. As the stonework and timber of St Peter’s were raised against the wilderness, these dark entities were said to have gathered at twilight, howling and grinning in bitter fury at the holy encroachment, eventually becoming immortalized in the oak roof timbers as a permanent warning of the struggle between sacred sanctuary and the wild, untamed fenland darkness.
