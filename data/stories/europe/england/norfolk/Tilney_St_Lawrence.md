#title
The Legend of Tom Hickathrift and the Fenland Giant

#summary
In the marshland village of Tilney, a prodigiously strong laborer named Tom Hickathrift defeated a terrifying giant with a makeshift cartwheel weapon and left his legendary mark across the local churches.

#details
According to East Anglian folklore, Tom Hickathrift was a gigantic and immensely strong workingman who lived in the marshlands between Wisbech and King's Lynn. When a ferocious giant terrorized the common lands of the Fenland villages—including Tilney St Lawrence and Terrington—Tom confronted the beast armed only with a wooden axletree and a cartwheel. After a brutal battle, Tom slew the giant and claimed his land for the local commoners. In another well-known episode of the legend associated with Tilney St Lawrence, Tom kicked a massive stone ball across the Fens to determine his final resting place; the stone flew roughly two miles from Tilney St Lawrence and struck the wall of Tilney All Saints Church, leaving an enduring crack. For centuries, chroniclers and villagers pointed to a stone coffin and grave slab in the churchyard carved with representations of a cartwheel and axletree as the final resting place of the Fenland hero.

#location
The churchyard of Tilney All Saints Church, near Tilney St Lawrence, Norfolk, UK.
