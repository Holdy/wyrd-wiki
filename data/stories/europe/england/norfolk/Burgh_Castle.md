#title
Old Scarfe and the Demon Dog of Burgh Castle

#summary
A wicked 13th-century mercenary knight named Baron Rudolph Scarfe returned from the dead as a fiery-eyed, chain-dragging hellhound that prowled between Great Yarmouth and the ancient Roman fort of Burgh Castle. Known as Old Scarfe, the beast terrorized travelers until local clergy eventually subdued and bound him in the cellar of a local inn.

#details
According to local tradition, Baron Rudolph Scarfe was a notorious mercenary knight from the Hartz mountains of Germany who fled to the Norfolk village of Burgh Castle after being excommunicated for his wicked and depraved deeds. He continued his evil lifestyle in England until his death, whereupon the Devil transformed him into a ferocious black dog laden with heavy chains and possessing blazing, fiery eyes. 

Known as Old Scarfe, the phantom hound was said to lope nightly between Great Yarmouth, Southtown Road, and Burgh Castle. He appeared in various terrifying forms—sometimes as a giant goat or a monstrous dog that breathed foul air upon unfortunate witnesses. On dark wintry nights, travelers along the turnpike road heard him rushing past, making sorrowful moanings and dragging a clanking chain. Eventually, local clergy intervened, performing solemn rites to banish and imprison him in the cellars of the Duke's Head inn, where he was doomed to remain until ritual candles thrown into the river burned out.

#location
The Roman fort and surrounding parish of Burgh Castle in Norfolk, situated near the confluence of the rivers Waveney and Yare.

#title
Old Scarfe's Haunt

#summary
The ancient stone walls of a Roman fort are haunted by a demonic one-eyed hellhound named Old Scarfe. This spectral beast is said to be the reincarnation of a deeply wicked 13th-century knight.

#details
After a life defined by depraved and wicked acts, a 13th-century mercenary knight named Baron Rudolf Scarfe sought refuge in the village of Burgh Castle. Upon his death, his misdeeds caught up with him when the devil transformed his soul into a massive black dog laden with chains. Sent back to earth to wreak havoc, the one-eyed hound known as Old Scarfe continues to stalk the ancient Roman fort at Burgh Castle, striking terror into anyone who hears its ghostly howls.

#location
The ruins of the 3rd-century Roman fort located in the village of Burgh Castle, Norfolk.

#title
The Demon Hound of Burgh Castle

#summary
Baron Rudolph Scarfe, a notoriously wicked 13th-century knight who fled to Burgh Castle, was transformed by the Devil into a demonic black dog upon his death and cursed to roam the area. Known as Old Scarfe, the spectral beast drags heavy chains and haunts the ruins of the Roman fort and surrounding roads.

#details
According to local folklore, Baron Rudolph Scarfe was a 13th-century mercenary knight who fled to the village of Burgh Castle in Norfolk after being excommunicated from Germany for his wicked and depraved acts. He continued his evil lifestyle in Burgh Castle until his death, whereupon the Devil took his soul, changed him into a fierce black hound laden with clanking chains, and sent him back to earth to create further havoc. The phantom beast, commonly referred to as Old Scarfe, was said to lope to Burgh Castle every night and roam the Southtown Road, terrifying locals and travellers with his fiery eyes and terrifying presence.

#location
The ruins of the 3rd-century Roman fort located in the village of Burgh Castle, Norfolk, overlooking the Waveney valley.

#title
The Specter of the South Wall

#summary
An eerie apparition of an unknown figure wrapped in a white flag is reportedly seen being hurled over the ruined south wall of the ancient Roman fort.

#details
Burgh Castle, a 3rd-century Roman fortress also known as Gariannonum, is steeped in centuries of folklore and paranormal legends. One of its most striking spectral occurrences involves the apparition of an unidentified person tightly wrapped in a white flag. Local lore describes this spectral figure being forcefully thrown over the south wall of the fortress—a chilling echo of historical punishments once inflicted upon individuals convicted of severe wrongdoing within the fort's bounds.

#location
The key location is the ruined south wall of the Roman fort at Burgh Castle in Norfolk, England.
