Sir Thomas Boleyn’s Penance Across Wroxham Bridge

Summary

Every year on the anniversary of his daughter's execution, the ghost of Sir Thomas Boleyn drives a carriage across twelve Norfolk bridges, including the one at Wroxham. Doomed for eternity, the phantom coach is pulled by four headless horses as it completes its cursed journey.

Details

According to local Norfolk folklore, Sir Thomas Boleyn—father of the ill-fated Tudor queen Anne Boleyn—is condemned to perform an annual penance following his daughter's execution. Each year on May 19th, his restless spirit must drive a ghostly coach pulled by four headless horses across twelve distinct bridges in Norfolk. This nocturnal journey of torment takes him from Blickling Hall toward Wroxham, traversing ancient crossings at Aylsham, Burgh, Buxton, Coltishall, Meyton, Belaugh, Oxnead, Braydeston, and Wroxham Bridge itself. Those unfortunate enough to cross paths with the spectral carriage on its midnight voyage risk witnessing the terrifying apparition as it thunders across the river.
