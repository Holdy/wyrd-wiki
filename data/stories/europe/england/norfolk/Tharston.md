#title
The Haunted Council House of Tharston

#summary
In the winter of 1937, a newly built council house in Tharston became a local sensation when it was plagued by relentless, loud knocking sounds that baffled investigators and drew crowds of curious onlookers.

#details
In November 1936, the Brown family—consisting of a mother, father, four-year-old daughter, and 13-year-old son named Herbert—moved into a sturdily-built semi-detached council house at Meadow View in the Norfolk village of Tharston, near Long Stratton. Within weeks of settling in, the household was disturbed by inexplicable phenomena. Loud, aggressive rapping noises began echoing through the structure on a daily basis, occurring every single day of the week with the bizarre exception of Thursdays. 

The knocking grew so violent and resonant that it could be heard clearly from outside, prompting crowds of curious locals to gather in the street. By January and February 1937, the strange case caught the attention of the regional press and local investigators. A team of four investigators visited the Tharston property in February 1937; at around 10:45 PM, they sat in the house and personally heard the heavy knocking commence in one of the upstairs bedrooms, continuing uninterrupted until past 1:00 AM. Despite thorough inspections of the walls, floors, and surrounding property, no physical cause or prankster could be found to explain the spectral disturbance.

#location
A semi-detached council house at Meadow View in Tharston, near Long Stratton, Norfolk, UK.

#upstream-source
The Eastern Daily Press (January–February 1937 reports, later featured in Weird Norfolk).
