#title
Bodger's Gap

#summary
According to village folklore, a hedgerow at Cross Green in Edgefield remains barren of plant life where a murderer named Bodger allegedly fled after a bloody rampage.

#details
In the village of Edgefield, local legend tells of a violent incident where a young man named Mr. Bodger used an axe to kill his father and a neighbour named Mr. Ford who tried to intervene. According to folklore recorded by local schoolchildren during the 1986 Domesday Project, Bodger fled through a specific gap in a hedge at Cross Green. Because of the immense amount of blood spilled during his escape, it is said that nothing has ever been able to grow at that spot since. In reality, the tale stems from a genuine double murder committed in June 1907 by labourer William Jacobs, who attacked his neighbour and killed his elderly father before fleeing through a hedge, eventually becoming garbled into local legend.

#location
A hedgerow at Cross Green on the corner of Briston Road and Ramsgate Street in Edgefield, Norfolk.

Maurice Morson: 'Norfolk Mayhem & Murder: Classic Cases Revisited' (Pen & Sword Books, 2008).

#title
The Smugglers' Secret Tunnel

#summary
Placing an ear to the ground at Cross Green is said to reveal the phantom singing of smugglers echoing from a hidden underground passage.

#details
According to local tradition, a secret tunnel once used by smugglers runs for several miles from the former White Horse inn at Cross Green northwards to the coast at Cley. Generations of villagers have claimed that if you press your ear against the earth near the old inn, you can hear the ghostly voices and singing of the smugglers reverberating beneath the surface.

#location
Cross Green in Edgefield, located on the corner of Briston Road and Ramsgate Street at the site of the former White Horse inn (TG090342).
