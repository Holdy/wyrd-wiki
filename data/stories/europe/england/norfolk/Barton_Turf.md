#title
The Face in Barton Broad

#summary
The ghostly face of a tragic medieval woman materializes and floats across the surface of Barton Broad on early August mornings.

#details
During the Crusades, a knight returned from the Holy Lands and unknowingly fell in love with his own daughter, who had been raised by another family. When she rejected his advances in favor of another suitor, a confrontation ensued where the knight fired a crossbow bolt intended for the lover, but tragically struck and killed his daughter instead. Ever since, under specific calm conditions on the morning of August 5th, her spectral face or her apparition with her lover in a sinking boat can be witnessed floating upon the waters of Barton Broad or drifting in the mist above the lake. Historical accounts note that aviators in 1916 and the Bishop of Wentworth in 1781 have witnessed this eerie phenomenon.

#location
Barton Broad is a 130-acre lake located along the River Ant within the parish of Barton Turf, Norfolk, UK.

#upstream-source
The poem *Barton Broad* (1781) by the Bishop of Wentworth.

#title
The Face of Lady Edyth in Barton Broad

#summary
During the Crusades, a returning knight tragically killed his unrecognized daughter with a crossbow, causing her ghostly face to appear on the misty waters of Barton Broad every August.

#details
According to local tradition, a knight returned from fighting in the Crusades and failed to recognize his own daughter, Edyth, who had been fostered out as an infant. Smitten by her beauty and unaware of their familial relation, the knight flew into a jealous rage when she spurned his romantic advances in favor of another man. During a confrontation on the water, the knight fired a crossbow bolt that struck and killed Edyth. Overcome with grief upon discovering he had slain his own child, the knight returned to the Holy Land forever. Since that time, on the morning of August 5th, the serene face of Lady Edyth is said to manifest across the surface of Barton Broad, a spectral phenomenon later witnessed by a military pilot flying overhead in September 1916.

#location
Barton Broad, a 130-acre expanse of water situated on the edge of Barton Turf in the Norfolk Broads.

#upstream-source
*Ghosts of the Broads* by Dr Chas Sampson.
