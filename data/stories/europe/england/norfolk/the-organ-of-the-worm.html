    The Organ of the Worm

    Summary

    An organ historian investigating a remote Norfolk parish church uncovers a cursed instrument containing ancient pipes and becomes haunted by terrifying recurring nightmares of a legendary subterranean beast. After ignoring warnings not to inspect the organ, he discovers a dark history of fatal accidents and a deathbed prohibition linked to ancient local folklore.

    Details

    While researching historic organs in Norfolk, an architectural historian visits a remote village church featuring a round stone tower, ancient yew trees, and a 1907 organ donated by a local baronet. Intrigued by hidden medieval pipework and carved wooden dragon shades within the instrument, he climbs onto the organ bench to examine it further, only to slip and suffer a concussion. Waking at the rectory, the historian is sternly warned by the rector that the baronet died in delirium shortly after the organ's installation, leaving strict deathbed instructions that the instrument must never be unlocked or played. The rector's wife lends him a nineteenth-century pamphlet titled The Roundhay Worm, detailing an ancient legend about a blind dragon that plagued the village during musical celebrations. That night—and on subsequent full moons—the historian suffers a vivid, terrifying nightmare of the organ dissolving into a bottomless abyss from which a massive, blind white worm emerges with rows of gaping teeth. Later conversations with an elderly organ builder confirm that the original installation was plagued by fatal accidents and that a technician dropped dead immediately after tuning the ancient pipes.
