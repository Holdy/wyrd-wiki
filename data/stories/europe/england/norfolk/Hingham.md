#title
The Grey Lady of the Dog Inn

#summary
A spectral grey lady frequently haunted a young child's bedroom at the Dog Inn in Hingham, sitting at the foot of the bed while the family dog refused to enter the room.

#details
During the late 1920s when her family ran the Dog Inn in Hingham, a young girl experienced nightly visits from a ghostly figure known as the Grey Lady. The apparition would walk into the child's bedroom and sit quietly on the end of the bed. Adding to the eerie atmosphere, the family dog steadfastly refused to cross the threshold into the room. Local tradition connected the haunting to the tragic death of a serving girl at the inn years prior.

#location
The Dog Inn was a historic public house located in the Market Place adjacent to the churchyard in the Norfolk village of Hingham.

#title
The Maiden Window of Hingham Church

#summary
Local tradition maintains that the Trinity chapel window within the parish church was funded and glazed entirely through the contributions of the unmarried young women of Hingham.

#details
According to local historical record and tradition, the Trinity chapel within the grand church of St. Andrew in Hingham was established and adorned through the collective efforts of the town's maidens. The medieval window in the chapel was glazed at their sole expense, accompanied by an ancient inscription reading "Thys Windowe ys ye Mayden cost of Nengham" (This window is the maidens' cost of Hingham), commemorating their devotion and financial sacrifice to the parish church.

#location
The Trinity chapel window is located within St. Andrew's Church in the market town of Hingham, Norfolk.
