#title
Mother Chergrave and the Graveyard Love Charm

#summary
Mother Chergrave was a 19th-century self-professed witch and charmer in southeast Norfolk who sold graveyard love charms and attempted to pass demonic familiars to her daughter. Her story illustrates the persistence of traditional witchcraft beliefs into the Victorian era.

#details
Operating in the Loddon and Chedgrave area until her death in the early 1870s, Mother Chergrave made a living as a service magician and charmer, notably selling a verse charm that promised to reveal the name of a future husband at the cost of sacrificing the user's final year of earth-life. She had a single daughter, Mara, whom she declared was bound to Satan. On her deathbed, Mother Chergrave handed Mara a box containing bat-winged imps, instructing her to let them bite her breasts to become an even greater witch. However, immediately after her mother's death, Mara threw the unopened box onto the fire and subsequently married.

#location
Holy Trinity Church and its churchyard in the Loddon and Chedgrave area of southeast Norfolk.

#upstream-source
*Bogie Tales of East Anglia* (1891) by Margaret Helen James.

#title
The Witch of Chedgrave and her Familiar Imps

#summary
In 19th-century Chedgrave, a notorious wise woman known as Mother Chergrave practiced magic alongside her bat-winged imps until her dramatic death, after which her adopted daughter cast the familiars into the fire.

#details
In the 19th century, Mother Chergrave operated as a renowned wise woman in the Chedgrave and Loddon area, openly practicing magic, palmistry, and creating herbal remedies. After one of her long absences, she brought back a young girl named Mary, whom she claimed was bound to Satan. Mary was rescued and raised by a kindly neighbor, Mrs Crab, while carrying protective charms. When Mother Chergrave lay on her deathbed, she commanded Mary to take her box of bat-winged imps and embrace witchcraft rather than marry. As the old woman died shrieking, villagers crowded around the room clamoring to see the legendary familiars. Hearing the music of a fiddle played by a local pedlar known as "Lucky Chance" outside, Mary placed the sealed box of imps directly onto the fire, destroying them before she and Chance married and left the village.

#location
Mother Chergrave's former cottage situated near the border of Chedgrave and Loddon along the River Chet in south Norfolk.

#upstream-source
Margaret Helen James, Bogie Tales of East Anglia (1891).

#title
The Woman in Red of Cross Stone Road

#summary
A phantom woman dressed in scarlet is said to appear and sit upon the post-medieval boundary stone at the road junction near Chedgrave. Locals and travelers traversing the rural lanes have passed down the legend of this spectral figure for generations.

#details
Along the narrow, winding country route leading from Chedgrave toward Hardley lies Cross Stone Road. At this specific road junction stands an old boundary stone or the remnants of a historic cross. According to local legend, on certain nights, the ghostly apparition of a woman in red can be seen sitting quietly upon the stone. 

#location
The junction and post-medieval boundary stone located on Cross Stone Road along the route leading from Chedgrave to Hardley.
