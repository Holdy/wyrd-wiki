#title
The Haunting of Syderstone Parsonage

#summary
In 1833, the occupants of the parsonage in the Norfolk village of Syderstone were tormented by an inexplicable poltergeist that produced violent hammering and moaning sounds, with local lore linking the haunting to either a dissolute former clergyman or the lingering spirit of Tudor noblewoman Amy Robsart.

#details
In 1833, the Rector of Syderstone, Revd J. Stewart, found his home transformed into a "House of Mystery" plagued by relentless and terrifying disturbances. Household staff and visitors heard chilling sounds ranging from low moans reminiscent of a whipped soldier to deafening hammer blows on an anvil, clashing iron, and rattling glass. The terrified maids were rendered immobile by fear, and local clergy alongside a surgeon stayed overnight to investigate, ultimately attesting that the phenomena were entirely genuine and defied any rational explanation. Local tradition and subsequent historical inquiries attributed the haunting either to Revd Mantle—a former 18th-century vicar of Syderstone who had descended into debauchery and drunkenness—or to Amy Robsart, the daughter of local landowner Sir John Robsart of Syderstone Hall, whose tragic 1560 death at Cumnor Place in Oxfordshire fueled centuries of scandal and ghost stories that reportedly followed her spirit back to her ancestral home and later into the parsonage.

#location
Syderstone Parsonage (the old rectory building) located in the village of Syderstone, Norfolk, UK.

#upstream-source
1833 contemporary newspaper articles (including 'The Real Ghost'), letters by Revd J. Stewart, and the research papers and manuscripts of Revd N. Fourdrinier held in the Norfolk Record Office.

#title
The Syderstone Parsonage Poltergeist

#summary
In 1833, the rector of Syderstone and multiple visiting clergymen were terrorized by inexplicable moaning, clashing, and hammering noises throughout the local parsonage. Local accounts attributed the haunting to the troubled spirit of a disgraced former vicar.

#details
In 1833, Reverend J. Stewart, the Rector of Syderstone, found his home—known locally as the "House of Mystery"—plagued by bizarre and terrifying disturbances. Household staff were so frightened by eerie sounds, ranging from low moans and clashing glass to the rattling of iron, that some fled in terror. On one particular night, four local priests and a surgeon stayed overnight to investigate and witnessed loud, anvil-like hammer blows and scratching noises that persisted from late evening until after sunrise, leaving all witnesses completely bewildered. Reverend Stewart traced the haunting back some 60 years to a former vicar, Reverend Mantle, who had lived a life of heavy drinking and debauchery before dying in a miserable state right before the mysterious noises first began.

#location
The former parsonage in the village of Syderstone, located near Fakenham in Norfolk.

#upstream-source
Manuscripts and unpublished writings of Revd. N. Fourdrinier (Norfolk Record Office, NRO MC 5), citing 1833 local newspaper articles.
