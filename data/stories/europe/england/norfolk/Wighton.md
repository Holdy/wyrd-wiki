#title
The Fiddler of Fiddler's Hill

#summary
An adventurous violinist and his dog vanished underground while exploring a secret medieval tunnel connecting Walsingham and Binham, with his final notes abruptly falling silent beneath a roadside burial mound. Years later, road workers unearthed the skeletons of a man and a dog at the exact spot.

#details
Centuries ago, a legend arose of a secret underground passage connecting Walsingham Abbey and Binham Priory. An intrepid fiddler and his faithful dog decided to investigate the subterranean route by entering the tunnel while playing his violin, allowing friends and villagers above ground to track his progress by the sound of his music. As he marched along beneath the countryside, the strains of the fiddle were heard moving steadily toward a Bronze Age earthwork mound at a rural crossroads. Suddenly, the music stopped completely. No one dared venture into the dark tunnel after him, but after a long wait, the fiddler's dog emerged alone, shivering and terrified with its tail between its legs. The fiddler was never seen alive again. Decades later, in April 1933, Norfolk County Council road workers digging at the mound where the Wighton-Stiffkey road crosses the Binham-Warham road made a startling discovery: the skeletons of a man and a dog buried together.

#location
Fiddler's Hill is located at the crossroads where the Wighton to Stiffkey road intersects with the Binham to Warham road in Norfolk.
