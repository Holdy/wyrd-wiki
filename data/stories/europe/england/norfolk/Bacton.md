#title
The Haunting of Gibbet Piece

#summary
Following his execution for the 1797 murder of his lover, William Suffolk's body was displayed in a gibbet cage in Bacton Wood, where his restless ghost is said to still linger. Locals report hearing the ghostly creaking of the cage and witnessing unexplained phenomena near the site.

#details
In 1797, William Suffolk, a married father from North Walsham, had an affair with a local woman named Mary Beck, resulting in a secret pregnancy. Fearing exposure, the couple conspired against the infant, but tension mounted when Mary's brother discovered the relationship. On February 3, 1797, during a meeting in the woods, an argument broke out and Suffolk brutally attacked Mary with a stick, leaving her body near a cart track to make her injuries look like an accident. Mary regained just enough consciousness before dying to name Suffolk as her attacker. Suffolk was arrested, tried at Norwich Castle, and sentenced to death by hanging. Following his execution on March 25, 1797, his body was coated, placed inside an iron cage, and suspended from a gibbet in Bacton Wood (historically known as Witton Wood) as a grim public deterrent. His corpse remained hanging for years until the post was eventually removed. Ever since, the woodland—particularly the area known as Gibbet Piece—has been reputed to be haunted, with visitors and locals reporting eerie creaking sounds reminiscent of a swaying iron cage, horses and dogs refusing to pass the spot, and phantom skeletal remains appearing on the moss only to vanish moments later.

#location
Bacton Wood (also known as Witton Wood), located near Bacton and North Walsham in Norfolk, England.

#title
The Long Coastguardsman

#summary
A betrayed coastguardsman, hacked to pieces by smugglers, eternally patrols the lonely cliff-edge between Bacton and Mundesley.

#details
In the late 18th century, a coastguardsman patrolling the lawless Norfolk coastline at Bacton sought to catch smugglers with the help of a local informer. However, the boy betrayed him, leading a gang of smugglers directly to his position instead of summoning military back-up. The coastguardsman was attacked, hacked to pieces, and his remains were flung over the cliff into the sea. Bound to the exact spot where his life was brutally ended, his ghostly figure continues to stride purposefully along the dark, stormy cliff-edge, venting his fury into the wind.

#location
The lonely cliff-edge between Bacton and Mundesley on the Norfolk coast.

#title
The Shipwreck Hound of Bacton Abbey

#summary
A colossal, shaggy spectral dog said to originate from an ancient shipwreck paces back and forth along the coast road between Bacton Abbey and Beeston Regis with glaring, luminous eyes. Local tradition connects the phantom hound to men buried at the historic abbey ruins.

#details
According to local folklore and 19th-century accounts, a massive black shaggy dog with glaring eyes haunts the coastal route stretching from Bacton to Beeston Regis. Legend maintains that the spectral beast emerged from a shipwreck off the coast, following which some men were buried at Bacton Abbey and others at Beeston Abbey. Ever since, the ghostly hound has been seen travelling back and forth along the coastal road between these two historic locations.

#location
The ruins of Bacton Abbey are situated on the Norfolk coast at Ordnance Survey grid reference TG348331, near the coastal road.
