#title
The Imps of Attleborough

#summary
An elderly 19th-century woman in Attleborough accused of witchcraft jokingly named her supposed "imps" leading a skeptical horse dealer to regret mocking her when beasts bearing the same names met bizarre fates.

#details
During the 19th century in the Norfolk market town of Attleborough, an elderly woman lived under persistent local accusations of being a witch who commanded three demonic imps. Embracing the rumors with dark humor, she gave her supposed familiars the lighthearted names Pug, Lightfoot, and Bluebell. A local horse dealer, highly skeptical of these witchcraft claims, decided to mock the old woman by naming three newly acquired horses after her alleged imps. His skepticism was severely tested when misfortune struck: the horse named Bluebell was killed in an accident, Lightfoot began viciously kicking anyone who approached, and Pug inexplicably went lame. Terrified and superstitious, the horse dealer only saw Pug recover his health after he finally renamed the animal. To the superstitious villagers, these uncanny mishaps served as undeniable proof that the old woman was indeed a witch.

#location
The Norfolk market town of Attleborough, located along the A11 between Norwich and Thetford.
