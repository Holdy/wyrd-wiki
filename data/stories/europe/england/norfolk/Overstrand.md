#title
The Phantom Hound of Overstrand Churchyard

#summary
At twilight every evening, a terrifying spectral hound known as Black Shuck or Old Shuck is said to pass under the wall of Overstrand churchyard. The coal-black, headless beast travels along Shuck's Lane from Beeston, leaving behind scorched ground and the distinct smell of brimstone.

#details
In January 1829, while staying nearby at Northrepps Cottage, writer Amelia Opie recorded the persistent local belief that at dusk each evening, the ghost of a dog passes beneath the wall of Overstrand churchyard after starting its journey from Beeston. Unlike human phantoms, this four-footed apparition was noted by locals as being both visible and tangible. Known variously as "Old Shuck" or "Shock," the legendary hellhound is described as coal-black with glowing saucer eyes, and sometimes depicted as headless with a white handkerchief tied where its head should be. Folklore relates that the beast prowls along a dreary local route appropriately named Shuck's Lane, where the spots it visits are reportedly found scorched and heavily impregnated with the scent of brimstone. For decades, traditional accounts maintained that the creature made its lair within the abandoned ruins of St. Martin's Church in Overstrand before restoration work commenced in the early 20th century.

#location
The events and sightings take place around the churchyard wall of St. Martin's Church and Shuck's Lane in the coastal parish of Overstrand, Norfolk.

#title
The Twilight Prowler of Overstrand Churchyard

#summary
At twilight each evening, a massive spectral hound with glowing saucer eyes and a tangible shaggy coat paces along the coastal route toward Overstrand churchyard. A local gamekeeper once swore the phantom creature brushed directly beneath his hand, leaving behind scorched earth and the scent of brimstone.

#details
In local tradition recorded as early as 1829, a terrifying spectral hound known as Old Shock or Shuck makes a nightly journey starting from Beeston toward Overstrand. This ghostly quadruped is said to pass directly beneath the stone wall of Overstrand churchyard at twilight. Unlike traditional phantoms, the hound is startlingly solid; a gamekeeper who encountered the beast maintained until his dying day that he felt its rough, hard, and shaggy coat as it brushed past his hand. Described as headless with blazing saucer eyes—or occasionally appearing with a handkerchief tied where its head should be—the creature's sudden appearances have also been associated with scorched ground and an acrid smell of brimstone. A local lane in the parish of Overstrand remains named Shuck's Lane in memory of its frequent nocturnal visits.

#location
The stone wall surrounding the churchyard in the coastal village of Overstrand, Norfolk.
