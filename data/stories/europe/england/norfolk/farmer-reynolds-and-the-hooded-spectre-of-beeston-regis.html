Farmer Reynolds and the Hooded Spectre of Beeston Regis

Summary

A frustrated Norfolk farmer repeatedly harassed by a grey-hooded ghost at sunset ensured his final resting place would crush the spirit's hiding spot by having his grave marker replaced with the very glacial erratic the spectre used for cover.

Details

At Beeston Regis, farmer James Reynolds frequently drove his horse and cart past All Saints Church, where ancient glacial erratic stones stood near the gateway. At sunset, a hooded apparition dressed in grey robes would repeatedly leap out from behind one of the stones to frighten his horses and attempt to grab their reins. Rather than being intimidated, Reynolds was infuriated by the disturbances. Before his death in 1941, he gave strict instructions for his grave to be marked by the large stone the ghost used as a hiding place, believing the heavy stone's weight and removal of its cover would permanently trap or put the spirit to rest. Sixteen years later, the stone was temporarily moved so his wife, Ann Elizabeth, could be buried alongside him before being placed back over their shared resting place.
