#title
The Devil's Chest of Callow Pit and the Limpenhoe Church Ring

#summary
Two men attempting to salvage a chest of gold from a deep Norfolk pit lost their prize to a supernatural black hand after boasting that even the Devil could not take it from them. The iron ring salvaged from the chest eventually found its home on the door of Limpenhoe's parish church.

#details
According to local tradition, an iron chest filled with gold lay submerged at the bottom of Callow Pit, a deep pool between the parishes of Southwood and Moulton. When the water level dropped, two adventurous men laid ladders across the pit to form a platform and managed to hook a heavy staff through the ring on the chest's lid, hoisting it up to their shoulders. Just as one of the men boasted, "We've got it safe, and the Devil himself can't get it from us," a cloud of sulphurous smoke erupted, and a dark arm reached out from the water to wrestle the chest back into the depths. The men lost the treasure entirely, escaping with only the heavy iron ring from the chest's lid. They originally attached this ring to the door of Southwood Church; when that building fell into ruin in the 1880s, the relic was transferred to St Botolph's Church in Limpenhoe, where it was affixed to the north door.

#location
St Botolph's Church is located in the village of Limpenhoe in the Broadland district of Norfolk, England.

#title
The Devil's Doorknocker of Callow Pit

#summary
Two men attempting to salvage a sunken treasure chest from a deep woodland pool managed to snag its iron ring handle before it slipped back into the depths, leaving behind a relic that eventually became a church door knocker. This heavy ring, carrying a legacy of folklore, now hangs on the entrance of Limpenhoe's parish church.

#details
On the parish boundary of Southwood and Moulton in Norfolk lies Callow Pit, a deep pool long rumored to hold buried treasure. Two young men determined to claim the hidden riches laid a ladder across the water and probed the depths with long sticks until they hooked a ring-like object. As they hauled the heavy chest toward the surface, one of the men managed to grasp the iron ring handle. At the very last moment, the ring snapped off in his hand, and the chest plunged back into the dark water, never to be seen again. The salvaged iron ring was subsequently fitted as a door handle on Southwood church and later transferred to St Botolph's Church in Limpenhoe, where the ominous relic remains today.

#location
St Botolph's Church is situated in the small village of Limpenhoe in Norfolk, south of the River Yare.
