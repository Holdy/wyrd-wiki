#title
The Cursed Foundations of Kirkescrofte

#summary
Following a vow of gratitude to St. Faith, medieval nobles attempted to build a monastery at Kirkescrofte in Horsford, only for the masonry to collapse nightly until the project was relocated.

#details
After returning from a pilgrimage to Rome, Norman nobleman Robert Fitzwalter and his wife Sybil de Cheney believed they were miraculously delivered from a gang of robbers by the intervention of St. Faith. In gratitude, they vowed to erect a monastery dedicated to the saint on a plot of land in Horsford known as Kirkescrofte. Construction commenced, but every wall and pillar raised during daylight hours mysteriously tumbled into ruin overnight. Interpreting this nightly destruction as a sign that divine will opposed the site, the couple abandoned Kirkescrofte and successfully re-established the monastery further away at Horsham St. Faith.

#location
The historic site of Kirkescrofte located near the village of Horsford in Norfolk.

#title
The Demonic Demolition of Kirkescrofte

#summary
Twelfth-century monastic building work in Horsford was repeatedly destroyed overnight by devils until the project was relocated.

#details
After believing they were miraculously saved from robbers by St. Faith while returning from a pilgrimage to Rome, Robert Fitzwalter and his wife Sybil de Cheney vowed to erect a monastery in Horsford. They commenced building their first foundation on a plot of land known as Kirkescrofte. However, supernatural interference thwarted their efforts; every wall and structure erected by workmen during the day was mysteriously thrown down and destroyed by devils during the night. Concluding that the site was cursed and that neither God nor the saint wished for the monastery to stand there, the founders abandoned Kirkescrofte and successfully relocated the priory to Horsham St. Faith.

#location
The historic site of Kirkescrofte in Horsford, Norfolk.

Sir William Dugdale: 'Monasticon Anglicanum' (Longman, Hurst, Rees, Orme & Brown, 1817-30), Vol.3, p.636; Pamela Brooks: 'Norfolk Ghosts & Legends' (Halsgrove, 2008), p.69.
