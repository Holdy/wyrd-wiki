#title
The Devil's Doorknocker of Callow Pit

#summary
Two men attempting to salvage a sunken treasure chest from a deep woodland pool managed to snag its iron ring handle before it slipped back into the depths, leaving behind a relic that eventually became a church door knocker. This heavy ring, carrying a legacy of folklore, now hangs on the entrance of Limpenhoe's parish church.

#details
On the parish boundary of Southwood and Moulton in Norfolk lies Callow Pit, a deep pool long rumored to hold buried treasure. Two young men determined to claim the hidden riches laid a ladder across the water and probed the depths with long sticks until they hooked a ring-like object. As they hauled the heavy chest toward the surface, one of the men managed to grasp the iron ring handle. At the very last moment, the ring snapped off in his hand, and the chest plunged back into the dark water, never to be seen again. The salvaged iron ring was subsequently fitted as a door handle on Southwood church and later transferred to St Botolph's Church in Limpenhoe, where the ominous relic remains today.

#location
St Botolph's Church is situated in the small village of Limpenhoe in Norfolk, south of the River Yare.
