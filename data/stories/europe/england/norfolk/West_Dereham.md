#title
The Pig-Faced Monk of West Dereham

#summary
A secret underground tunnel was supposedly built between St. Andrew's church and the abbey in West Dereham to hide a monk with a pig-like face from public view, but his spiteful ghost still haunts the area today.

#details
Local folklore in West Dereham tells of a subterranean passage constructed between St. Andrew's church and the nearby 12th-century abbey. The tunnel was allegedly built specifically for a monk whose head and face so closely resembled a pig that he was deemed too hideous to walk openly through the village streets. However, the monk proved ungrateful for this considerate arrangement, and his vengeful spirit now haunts West Dereham out of spite.

#location
St. Andrew's church (TF66740210) stands in the village of West Dereham, Norfolk, approximately a mile north of the site of the 12th-century West Dereham Abbey.

Paul Lee: 'The Ghosts of King's Lynn and West Norfolk' (2nd ed, 2023) and the West Dereham Heritage Group report (April 2012).

#title
The Pig-Faced Monk of West Dereham

#summary
A spiteful ghost of a monk with a pig-like face is said to haunt West Dereham after resenting the construction of a secret tunnel built to hide his facial deformity. The subterranean passage allegedly connected St. Andrew's Church to the 12th-century abbey.

#details
According to local folklore, a subterranean tunnel was constructed between St. Andrew's Church and the 12th-century abbey in West Dereham for a monk whose head and face resembled that of a pig. Because he was deemed too hideous to be seen walking openly through the village, the passage was built so he could travel between the church and the abbey unseen. Rather than appreciating this gesture, the monk grew resentful and spiteful, and his ghost continues to haunt West Dereham to this day.

#location
St. Andrew's Church stands about a mile north of the site of the 12th-century abbey in West Dereham, Norfolk (TF66740210).
