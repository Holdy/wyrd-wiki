    King Edmund's Earthwork at Bunn's Bank

    Summary

    Local legend attributes the ancient earthwork of Bunn's Bank in Attleborough to King Edmund, who supposedly constructed it in the ninth century to defend his royal town against marauding Danes.

    Details

    Running for about two miles along the south and east of the parish of Attleborough, Bunn's Bank is a fragmentary earthwork whose exact age has long been debated by historians, with origins potentially dating back to early Saxon times. However, local tradition firmly maintains that the embankment was built by King Edmund in the 9th century as a defensive fortification to protect his royal town from invading Danish raiders.
