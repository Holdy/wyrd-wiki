#title
Mary Bone's Well

#summary
An ancient well in East Rudham is said to be named after a local woman who was brutally drowned in its waters by a medieval priest from the nearby priory.

#details
Located a short distance south-east of the ruins of the Augustinian St. Mary's Priory between Coxford and Broomsthorpe in East Rudham, the ancient Mary Bone's Well carries a grim local legend. According to village tradition, the well gets its moniker from a local woman named Mary Bone, who met a tragic end when she was drowned in its depths by a corrupt priest hailing from the nearby priory. The historic site continues to be marked on Ordnance Survey maps.

#location
Mary Bone's Well, situated south-east of St. Mary's Priory ruins between Coxford and Broomsthorpe in East Rudham, Norfolk (Grid Reference TF847287).

#title
The Vicar Apparition of East Rudham

#summary
On Boxing Day in 1908, multiple witnesses in East Rudham reported seeing their vicar walking the church grounds, unaware he was recovering from a train crash 1,700 miles away in Algeria.

#details
On December 26, 1908, word reached East Rudham that Reverend Dr. Astley and his wife had been severely injured in a train derailment while traveling to Biskra, Algeria. While Dr. Astley was recovering in bed from a head injury far overseas, parishioners and staff at East Rudham experienced a baffling phenomenon. The vicar's housekeeper, Mrs. Hartley, spotted him sitting at his usual outdoor reading desk on the vicarage lawn. When she called upon Reverend Brock to witness the sight, he too observed the figure in clerical garb. Furthermore, the maid, Florence Breese, reported seeing the vicar on the church grounds earlier that same day. Although journalists investigated and debated whether it was an instance of a stress-induced apparition or psychic double, Dr. Astley himself was amused to confirm he was confined to his bed in North Africa when the sightings occurred.

#location
St. Mary's Church and the vicarage lawn in East Rudham, Norfolk.

#upstream-source
Originally documented in contemporary Norfolk newspapers following the incident.

#title
The Vicar's Apparition of East Rudham

#summary
On Boxing Day in 1908, the acting vicar and housekeeper of St. Mary's Church in East Rudham witnessed a spectral double of the parish vicar sitting outside the vicarage window, shortly before news arrived of his tragic death abroad.

#details
In December 1908, Reverend R. Brock was serving as the Acting Vicar of St. Mary's Church in East Rudham while the regular vicar, Reverend Dr. Astley, was travelling in Algeria. On the afternoon of Boxing Day (December 26), the vicarage housekeeper rushed to fetch Reverend Brock to the study window, pointing outside to an alcove by the garden wall. There, both of them clearly saw a full apparition of Dr. Astley seated at a desk reading, complete with his distinctive clerical collar and gold watch chain across his waistcoat. Knowing the vicar was thousands of miles away in North Africa, they watched in astonishment as the manifestation lingered before eventually fading away. Sometime later, word reached the village that Dr. Astley and his wife had died in a railway accident in Algeria around the exact date and time of the ghostly visitation.

#location
The vicarage of St. Mary's Church in the village of East Rudham, Norfolk.
