    The Rescue of Rector Christopher Barnard

    Summary

    During the English Civil War, the devoted parishioners of Dickleburgh courageously pursued and fought off Parliamentary soldiers to rescue their arrested rector.

    Details

    In 1643, amidst the intense religious and political upheavals of the English Civil War, Rector Christopher Barnard of All Saints' Church in Dickleburgh steadfastly refused to take the Covenant demanded by the Earl of Manchester's forces. Consequently, soldiers seized him and dragged him away toward Norwich Castle. Viewing his loss as a terrible judgment upon their community, his parishioners banded together, followed the armed party, and successfully rescued Barnard, hiding him safely within the village for a long period. When later threats arose of a second plundering of his home, the villagers—including women and children—voluntarily and at great personal risk secured his remaining goods in their own houses. Barnard survived the Cromwellian era and was triumphantly restored to his rectory twenty years later following the Restoration of the Monarchy.
