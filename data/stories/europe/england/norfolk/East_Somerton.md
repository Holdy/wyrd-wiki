#title
The Witch of East Somerton

#summary
According to local legend, an accused witch was buried alive inside the ruined St Mary's Church in East Somerton, where her wooden leg sprouted into a mighty oak tree and her restless spirit continues to haunt the grounds.

#details
Local tradition holds that during the height of England's witch trials, villagers dragged an accused woman to the medieval St Mary's Church in East Somerton and buried her alive in the nave on consecrated ground. Because she had a wooden leg, it is said that she enchanted the limb before her death so that an oak tree—now known as "The Witch's Leg" or "The Witch's Finger"—would sprout from her grave and tear apart the church above. On cold, dark nights and at twilight, the witch's spirit drifts across the skeletal ruins. Furthermore, ghostly monks are said to patrol the shattered walls to prevent curious visitors from releasing her spirit by circling the tree three times.

#location
The ruins of St Mary's Church, located in the woodland near Burnley Hall Estate in East Somerton, Norfolk.

#title
The Witch's Leg

#summary
A suspected witch buried alive within the ruins of a medieval church in East Somerton enchanted her wooden leg to sprout an oak tree that would destroy the building above. Walking around this tree three times is said to release her spirit, though spectral guardians keep intruders at bay.

#details
Deep in the woodlands of East Somerton lie the romantic ruins of the 15th-century St Mary’s Church, where nature has reclaimed the tumbling stone walls and open-sky tower. According to local legend, during the height of England's witch trials, a suspected witch possessing a wooden leg was brutally buried alive in the church's nave. In her final moments of suffering, she cast a curse, enchanting her wooden appendage to grow into an oak tree that would tear down the sanctuary. Folklore warns that anyone brave enough to walk around the tree three times will awaken and release the witch's spirit, though ghostly figures are believed to haunt the ruins and prevent anyone from doing so.

#location
The ruins of St Mary's Church are situated in the woodlands of East Somerton, Norfolk.

#title
The Witch's Vengeance of East Somerton

#summary
An accused witch buried alive within a ruined church enchanted her wooden leg to sprout an oak tree, forever preventing her soul from joining Satan and binding her restless spirit to the ruins. Visitors who circle the tree three times at twilight may awaken her presence.

#details
During the height of witch persecution in Norfolk, villagers dragged an accused woman to the isolated St Mary's Church in East Somerton and buried her alive in consecrated ground in an attempt to keep her soul from descending to join Satan. Because she possessed a wooden leg, a mighty oak tree miraculously sprouted from the spot where she was buried. According to local legend, this tree—often called the witch's leg—keeps her restless spirit trapped within the ruined nave. On dark nights, her ghost paces the skeletal ruins, and those bold enough to walk around the tree three times while whispering her name risk waking the dead.

#location
The ruins of St Mary's Church are located in a secluded woodland grove on the Burnley Hall Estate in East Somerton, Norfolk.
