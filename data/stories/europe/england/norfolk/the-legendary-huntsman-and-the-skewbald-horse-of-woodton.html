    The Legendary Huntsman and the Skewbald Horse of Woodton

    Summary

    Edward "Ned" Baldry rose from a humble stable boy at Woodton Hall to become a master huntsman who hunted before the King of France, leaving behind a celebrated local legend and a poetic epitaph in the village churchyard.

    Details

    Born in Woodton in 1705, Edward "Ned" Baldry began working in the stables of Woodton Hall under the Suckling family at the age of thirteen. Through dedication and skill, he rose through the ranks to become the chief huntsman of the Woodton Hunt. According to local tradition, Baldry once purchased a wretched old horse at Bungay fair merely to feed his hounds, but later changed his mind and bred her. The following year, she produced a striking skewbald foal affectionately known as 'Shell'.

    When Denzil Suckling passed away in 1747, he left Baldry the fine pack of hounds, the beloved 'shelled horse', and ample funds for their maintenance. Baldry subsequently took the pack to Ireland and even hunted for the King of France at Versailles. Eventually returning to his home parish, Baldry died in 1759 and was buried in the north-east corner of Woodton churchyard beneath a famous rhyming epitaph commemorating his unmatched skill in hunting fox and fallow deer across England, Ireland, and France. His faithful horse Shell was buried nearby in the park beyond the church wall.
