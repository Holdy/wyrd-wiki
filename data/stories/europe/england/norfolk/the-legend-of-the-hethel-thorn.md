#title
The Legend of the Hethel Thorn

#summary
The ancient Hethel Thorn in Norfolk is said to have grown from the staff of Joseph of Arimathea and served as a secret meeting place for rebel peasants during the reign of King John.

#details
Standing in a meadow north-west of All Saints Church in the village of Hethel, the Hethel Old Thorn is recognized as one of the oldest and largest hawthorns in Britain, with historical records dating back to the early 13th century when it was already noted as a boundary marker. Over the centuries, local folklore attached rich legends to the gnarled, weather-beaten tree. Locally referred to as the "Witch of Hethel" due to its curiously convoluted branches and crown of mistletoe, tradition claims that the thorn miraculously sprouted from the staff of Joseph of Arimathea. Furthermore, historical folklore maintains that the sprawling tree served as a clandestine gathering place for rebel peasants during the reign of King John.

#location
The Hethel Thorn stands in a meadow north-west of All Saints Church in the Norfolk village of Hethel (grid reference TG171006).
