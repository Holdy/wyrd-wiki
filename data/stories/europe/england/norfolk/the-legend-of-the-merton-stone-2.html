    The Legend of the Merton Stone

    Summary

    A massive ancient boulder lies half-buried in a Norfolk marl pit with the enduring warning that if it is ever removed, a great deluge will rise and cover the entire earth.

    Details

    Situated near the western boundary of Merton parish with Threxton, just off Peddar’s Way near an area known as Capp’s Bush, lies a massive twenty-tonne boulder of sandstone embedded in a marl pit. According to local folklore, this stone serves as a cosmic plug; should anyone ever succeed in removing or unearthing it entirely, all the waters of the deep would rise up and submerge the whole world. Despite being recognized by geologists as a glacial erratic transported during the last ice age, the stone's mythical reputation inspired several real-world attempts to move it. Notably, the 5th Lord Walsingham once gathered local laborers with ropes and beer to try and shift the boulder, while a later attempt in the mid-20th century deployed a powerful rotary plough known as a Gyrotiller. Every attempt to budge the giant rock failed, preserving both the physical boulder and its flood-prevention legend.
