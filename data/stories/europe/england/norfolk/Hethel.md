#title
The Legend of the Hethel Thorn

#summary
The ancient Hethel Thorn in Norfolk is said to have grown from the staff of Joseph of Arimathea and served as a secret meeting place for rebel peasants during the reign of King John.

#details
Standing in a meadow north-west of All Saints Church in the village of Hethel, the Hethel Old Thorn is recognized as one of the oldest and largest hawthorns in Britain, with historical records dating back to the early 13th century when it was already noted as a boundary marker. Over the centuries, local folklore attached rich legends to the gnarled, weather-beaten tree. Locally referred to as the "Witch of Hethel" due to its curiously convoluted branches and crown of mistletoe, tradition claims that the thorn miraculously sprouted from the staff of Joseph of Arimathea. Furthermore, historical folklore maintains that the sprawling tree served as a clandestine gathering place for rebel peasants during the reign of King John.

#location
The Hethel Thorn stands in a meadow north-west of All Saints Church in the Norfolk village of Hethel (grid reference TG171006).

#title
The Wandering Airman of Hethel

#summary
Following his death from combat wounds at a nearby medical facility, the spirit of a USAAF bomber crew member continually walks the nocturnal path back to his wartime airfield. Local residents report encountering his phantom figure roaming between the hospital grounds and the old base.

#details
During the Second World War, a young crew member serving aboard a USAAF Liberator heavy bomber stationed at Hethel airfield suffered severe, life-threatening wounds during a combat mission. Evacuated from the base, he was transferred for emergency medical care to the military hospital at Morley. Despite medical intervention, the airman succumbed to his injuries and passed away. In the years following the war, local folklore and resident accounts maintain that his restless spirit never truly left his post; at night, the phantom airman is said to walk the lonely rural route between the hospital site and his former station at Hethel.

#location
RAF Hethel is located in the parish of Hethel in south Norfolk, England, historically designated as USAAF Station 114 and situated at the site now occupied by Lotus Cars.

#title
The Witch of Hethel Thorn

#summary
An ancient hawthorn tree standing for centuries in the parish of Bracon Ash and Hethel has long been tied to local folklore and nicknamed the "Witch of Hethel," serving as a gathering spot for traditional May Day festivities. Local tradition and historical accounts have revered the gnarled tree as both a pagan site and a legendary boundary marker steeped in magical associations.

#details
For over seven centuries, an immense and gnarled hawthorn tree—now recognized as England's smallest nature reserve managed by the Norfolk Wildlife Trust—has stood in a meadow near the church in the joint parishes of Bracon Ash and Hethel. Known locally as the "Witch of Hethel," the ancient tree accumulated numerous legends linking it to pagan fertility symbols, ancient witchcraft beliefs, and historic gatherings. In past centuries, local youths maintained a tradition of "going a-Maying" at the tree, harvesting its blossoms to present to their girlfriends. Historians and arborists have documented its remarkable survival and its status as a focal point of local folklore, bridging medieval insurrection tales from the reign of King John with deeply rooted rural customs regarding witchcraft and magical trees.

#location
The ancient Hethel Thorn is located in a meadow near the church within the parish of Hethel and Bracon Ash, south of Norwich in Norfolk, England.
