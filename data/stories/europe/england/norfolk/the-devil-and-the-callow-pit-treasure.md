#title
The Devil and the Callow Pit Treasure

#summary
Two men attempting to salvage a chest of gold from Callow Pit are thwarted by the devil after boasting that Satan himself could not take it from them. The ensuing supernatural struggle causes the treasure to sink back into the depths forever, leaving behind only the chest's iron ring.

#details
Many years ago, two adventurous men took advantage of unusually low water levels at Callow Pit—located near the border of the Norfolk parishes of Southwood, Moulton, and Cantley—to haul up an ancient iron chest filled with gold. Using a platform of ladders across the pit, they inserted a staff through the ring on the lid and successfully raised the prize. As they celebrated, one of the men arrogantly proclaimed that not even the devil himself could take the treasure from them. Instantly, the pit was enveloped in a sulphurous cloud of steam, and a monstrous black hand emerged from the water to seize the chest. A fierce tug-of-war ensued between the men and the devil until the chest broke apart under the immense strain, sinking back into the water never to be seen again. The thwarted men escaped with nothing more than the heavy iron ring, which was subsequently fastened to the door of Southwood Church. Additionally, local folklore holds that a headless horseman rides at midnight from Callow Pit toward Cantley Spong.

#location
Callow Pit is situated on the parish boundaries near Southwood, Moulton, and Cantley in Norfolk.
