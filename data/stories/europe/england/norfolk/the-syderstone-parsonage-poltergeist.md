#title
The Syderstone Parsonage Poltergeist

#summary
In 1833, the rector of Syderstone and multiple visiting clergymen were terrorized by inexplicable moaning, clashing, and hammering noises throughout the local parsonage. Local accounts attributed the haunting to the troubled spirit of a disgraced former vicar.

#details
In 1833, Reverend J. Stewart, the Rector of Syderstone, found his home—known locally as the "House of Mystery"—plagued by bizarre and terrifying disturbances. Household staff were so frightened by eerie sounds, ranging from low moans and clashing glass to the rattling of iron, that some fled in terror. On one particular night, four local priests and a surgeon stayed overnight to investigate and witnessed loud, anvil-like hammer blows and scratching noises that persisted from late evening until after sunrise, leaving all witnesses completely bewildered. Reverend Stewart traced the haunting back some 60 years to a former vicar, Reverend Mantle, who had lived a life of heavy drinking and debauchery before dying in a miserable state right before the mysterious noises first began.

#location
The former parsonage in the village of Syderstone, located near Fakenham in Norfolk.
