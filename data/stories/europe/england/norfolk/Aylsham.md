#title
The Ghostly Coach of Sir Thomas Boleyn

#summary
Condemned to a thousand-year penance for failing to save his children, Sir Thomas Boleyn's phantom coach nightly races across twelve Norfolk bridges. Driven by headless horses, the condemned nobleman carries his severed head through the dark countryside.

#details
According to Norfolk folklore, Sir Thomas Boleyn was cursed after the executions of his children, Anne and George Boleyn, during the reign of Henry VIII. As punishment for his ambition and failure to protect them, Thomas is condemned to drive a ghostly coach drawn by four headless horses across twelve bridges before cockcrow, a penance he must repeat for a thousand years. Witnesses in the local countryside have long reported hearing the thunderous rush of phantom wheels and catching sight of the terrifying carriage, with Thomas holding his severed head under his arm and flames pouring from his mouth as it crosses through the parish of Blickling and neighboring Aylsham.

#location
Blickling Hall and its surrounding historic estate are situated just northwest of the market town of Aylsham in Norfolk, England.

#title
The Haunting of the Black Boys Inn

#summary
The original owner of an ancient coaching inn, slain by a soldier of Oliver Cromwell during the Civil War, was buried on the premises and returned to haunt the building for over a century.

#details
During the turbulent years of the English Civil War, the original owner of the Black Boys Inn in Aylsham became embroiled in a fatal altercation with one of Oliver Cromwell's men and lost his life. Following his violent demise, his remains were buried within the grounds of the inn. Throughout the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, his restless phantom was reported haunting the historic building, reminding visitors of his untimely end.

#location
The Black Boys Inn is located in the Market Place of Aylsham, Norfolk, UK.
