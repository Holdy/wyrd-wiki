#title
The Demonic Curse of Margaret Wade

#summary
In 1611, a Barton Bendish woman was brought before the Norfolk Quarter Sessions for publicly wishing that the devil would carry away a local man.

#details
In 1611, community tensions in the village of Barton Bendish boiled over when Margaret Wade engaged in a bitter dispute with a local resident named Mr. Lynghoke. During the confrontation, she unleashed a torrent of abuse, labeling him a "Rascall, Jack, knave" alongside other severe imprecations. Most damningly, she publicly prayed that "the devile myght fetch him awaye," invoking demonic forces against him. Her aggressive verbal malediction led to formal articles of misbehaviour being filed against her at the Norfolk Quarter Sessions, mirroring the terrifying accusations of malediction and cursing that frequently accompanied early modern witchcraft trials.

#title
The Malicious Cursing of Margaret Wood

#summary
In 1628, a Barton Bendish resident was officially hauled before the Norfolk magistracy for her notorious reputation as a malignant curser and evil-tongued woman.

#details
Village life in Barton Bendish was frequently disrupted by severe interpersonal conflicts and verbal assaults that mirrored witchcraft-adjacent fears of malediction. In 1628, Margaret Wood of Barton Bendish was formally presented to the Norfolk magistracy by her neighbors as "a great curser & banner & A very evill tonged woman." Her persistent cursing and malicious tongue were viewed by local authorities and villagers as a direct threat to community harmony, capturing the historical reality of how women accused of malevolent speech and cursing were targeted by early modern English courts.

#location
The historic parish of Barton Bendish is situated in the West Norfolk district of Norfolk, England.
