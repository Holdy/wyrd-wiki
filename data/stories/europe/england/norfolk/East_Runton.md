#title
The Phantom Light of Runton Mill

#summary
A mysterious ghostly light is frequently seen crossing the fields from the old windmill in East Runton to vanish into a nearby woodland copse where human remains were once discovered.

#details
Historical accounts and local tradition record a strange phenomenon where an anomalous light issues from the area around the old Runton Mill, traverses the surrounding fields, and disappears into a wooded copse or fir-spinney. According to local lore, the phantom light sinks into the ground precisely where human bones had previously been uncovered. While some outside antiquarians suggested the light might merely be the reflection of the distant Cromer lighthouse on coastal vapor, local witnesses rejected this explanation, maintaining the wandering light was connected to the unquiet resting place of the dead.

#location
The old Runton Mill and Broomhill Plantation located just south of East Runton in Norfolk.

#upstream-source
*A History of Norfolk* by Walter Rye (published by E. Stock, 1887, pp. 227, 290).

#title
The Smuggler's Grave and Ghostly Light of East Runton

#summary
During road-widening works in 1906, human remains believed to belong to a smuggler were unearthed just west of East Runton, linking to long-standing local accounts of a phantom light that haunted the fields near the village's corn mill.

#details
Just outside the village of East Runton along the coast road, human skeletal remains were uncovered four feet below the surface during road-widening operations. Local tradition put forward various identities for the skeleton, with some villagers claiming it was a smuggler slain during an attempted escape from excise men, and others suggesting it was a murdered watch pedlar targeted for robbery. The remains were subsequently removed for Christian burial. This discovery became intertwined in regional folklore with accounts of a mysterious ghostly light recorded by antiquarian Walter Rye in his 1887 book *A History of Norfolk*. The phantom light was said to issue from the old Runton corn mill, travel across the fields, and sink into the earth at the exact roadside spot where human bones had once been found.

#location
Just west of East Runton along the A149 coast road near the old Runton corn mill (approximate grid reference TG195426).

Walter Rye: A History of Norfolk (Elliot Stock, 1887).
