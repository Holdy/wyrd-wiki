    The Enigmatic Epitaph of Christopher Burraway

    Summary

    A local landowner's tombstone bore a startling and scandalous epitaph claiming his wife was simultaneously his sister, mistress, mother, and wife, sparking decades of whispered village legend.

    Details

    According to the long-standing local legend surrounding St Mary the Virgin Church in Martham, Christopher Burraway married an older woman named Alice, unaware that she was actually his mother—who had abandoned him as an infant following an incestuous family scandal. Years later, Alice allegedly discovered the truth when she caught sight of a distinctive birthmark on his shoulder while he was changing clothes. Overcome by shock and horror at the realization that she had unknowingly married her own son, Alice fell gravely ill and died in his chamber, while Christopher passed away the following year under the weight of the tragic revelation. Their gravestones eventually featured the enigmatic and scandalous inscription: "And there lyes Alice, who by hir Life was my Sister, my Mistres, My Mother, and my Wife." Though genealogical records later revealed a more complicated web of multiple marriages rather than literal incest, the dramatic tale cemented itself as one of Norfolk's most famous and macabre local legends.
