#title
The Christmas Eve Apparition at Temple Bar

#summary
A late-night winter traveler walking home to Earsham witnessed a large black hound emerge from a private estate, cross the road, and vanish directly through a rectory wall without leaving any footprints in the snow.

#details
Late one night shortly before Christmas, a local man was walking the final mile from Bungay back to Earsham under a clear, moonlit sky with thick snow covering the ground. As he approached the row of cottages known as Temple Bar, he was suddenly overcome by a chilling sensation and a prickling of his skin. He watched as a large black dog emerged through the fence of a property called 'The Elms', crossed the road directly in front of him, and vanished straight through the wall of the opposite Rectory. Shocked by the apparition, he inspected the snow and discovered a complete absence of paw prints or any other marks, prompting him to flee in terror to his relative's home in the village.

#location
The incident took place near the Temple Bar cottages in Earsham, situated opposite 'The Elms' and the village rectory.

#title
The Legend of Aslac the Foundling

#summary
According to local tradition, an infant orphan left abandoned at the church style grew up under the care of the parish to become a renowned standard-bearer for King Edward III.

#details
Local tradition held within the parish of Aslacton tells the tale of a helpless infant found wrapped in swaddling clothes and left at the style of St Michael's Church. Taken in and reared by the local community, the orphan was named Aslac after the village. As he matured, he distinguished himself as a man of great renown and prowess, eventually serving as standard-bearer to King Edward III and marrying the daughter of Sir Oliver Calthorp of Burnham. For centuries, this legend was commemorated in the chancel window of St Michael's Church, which featured the image of an infant in a cradle surrounded by the heraldic arms of Aslac and connected families.

#location
St Michael's Church, situated in the village of Aslacton in South Norfolk, England.

#title
The Phantom Black Dog of Manor Farm

#summary
A young farm boy encountered a monstrous, phantom black dog that vanished through a closed gate in a meadow at Manor Farm in Aslacton.

#details
During the early twentieth century, twelve-year-old C. Skillings was staying with his grandparents at Manor Farm in Aslacton. While approaching the top field with an empty wagon, his horse suddenly shied in terror. Skillings looked up to see an extremely large, rough-coated black dog with a massive head and foaming, open jaws sprinting across the meadow. To his utter shock, the spectral beast passed straight through a closed gate and vanished entirely. When he recounted the terrifying experience to his grandfather, he was warned that it was an omen of bad luck and impending family death—a prophecy that seemed to come true when his other grandparents died soon after, leaving him permanently spooked and unwilling to ever cross that particular field alone again.

#location
The top field of Manor Farm in Aslacton, Norfolk (grid reference TM154908).
