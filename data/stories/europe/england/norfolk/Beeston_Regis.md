#title
The Farmer and the Hooded Spectre

#summary
A furious Norfolk farmer was continually terrorized by a hooded grey ghost that leapt from behind a glacial boulder to spook his horses at sunset. To lay the spirit to rest, he requested that the very boulder be placed atop his grave upon his death.

#details
In Beeston Regis, a farmer named James Reynolds frequently drove his horse and cart along a pathway near the ruins of Beeston Priory. At sunset, a hooded grey ghost would repeatedly leap out from behind one of two large glacial erratic boulders guarding the gateway to frighten the horses and attempt to grab their reins. Unfazed by the supernatural harassment, Reynolds vowed to stop the haunting. Upon his death in 1941, he arranged for the boulder to be hauled and placed directly over his final resting place at All Saints' Church, believing that burying the ghost's hiding place under stone would permanently trap and quiet the spirit.

#location
James Reynolds' grave is located in the churchyard of All Saints' Church in Beeston Regis, Norfolk.

#title
The Farmer and the Hooded Spectre of Beeston Regis

#summary
A local farmer, routinely startled by a hooded grey ghost leaping from behind two glacial granite boulders near Beeston Priory, requested that one of the stones be placed over his grave to lay the entity to rest.

#details
According to local folklore in Beeston Regis, Norfolk, a farmer named James Reynolds frequently drove his horse and cart past a pathway bordered by two large glacial granite boulders near the ruins of Beeston Priory. Around sunset, a hooded grey ghost—suspected to be the spirit of an old monk—would routinely leap out from behind one of the stones and attempt to grab the horse's reins before vanishing into thin air. Though unintimidated by the apparition, Reynolds grew determined to put a stop to its mischief. He left strict instructions that upon his death, the boulder associated with the haunting should be placed directly on top of his grave. When James Reynolds died in 1941, his wish was carried out, and the heavy granite stone now serves as his grave marker in the churchyard.

#location
The churchyard of All Saints Church in Beeston Regis, Norfolk.

#title
The Hound of Beeston Heath

#summary
A terrifying demon dog known as Old Shuck rises from the coast at Beeston Regis and prowls towards Overstrand, leaving scorched earth and the smell of brimstone in its wake.

#details
According to long-standing East Anglian folklore, a monstrous, coal-black hellhound with blazing saucer eyes—commonly referred to as Old Shuck or Black Shuck—emerges from the sea or Beeston Heath near Beeston Regis. On stormy nights and at twilight, this spectral beast prowls along the coast towards Overstrand churchyard. Witnesses and traditional accounts note that the creature is both visible and tangible, and wherever it appears or vanishes, the ground is found scorched and heavily impregnated with the smell of brimstone.

#location
Beeston Bump is a prominent glacial hill rising above the coast of Beeston Regis, Norfolk.

#title
The Spectral Hound of Beeston Heath

#summary
A terrifying headless spectral dog with flaming saucer eyes rises from the sea at Beeston to prowl along Shock's Lane toward Overstrand churchyard every evening at twilight.

#details
Old Shuck, a monstrous black dog described as either headless with glowing saucer eyes or wearing a white handkerchief tied over the place where its head should be, emerges from the sea near Beeston Heath. Accompanied by the scent of brimstone and scorched earth, the phantom hound races along the coastal path known as Shock's Lane, maintaining its eerie patrol before passing under the stone wall of Overstrand churchyard at twilight as a portent of coastal storms.

#location
Beeston Bump is a prominent glacial hill rising on the North Norfolk coast in Beeston Regis.

#upstream-source
James Hooper, 'Demon Dogs of Norfolk & Suffolk' (Eastern Daily Press, 1894); Rev. J. Gunn, 'Proverbs etc...in...Irstead' (Norfolk Archaeology, Vol. 2, 1849); R. Chambers (ed.), The Book of Days (W. & R. Chambers, 1864).
