    The Good Sword of Winfarthing

    Summary

    According to medieval legend, a knight who sought sanctuary in St Mary's Church after killing his rival left behind a sword that later gained supernatural powers, including the ability to rid women of abusive husbands.

    Details

    In medieval times, a dispute between two knights over a woman resulted in one killing the other. Fearing retribution, the victorious knight fled and sought sanctuary at St Mary's Church in Winfarthing, where he claimed benefit of clergy before eventually escaping into exile. He left behind his sword, which over time came to be regarded as a holy relic and a major draw for pilgrims, famously known as the "Good Sword of Winfarthing." Recorded by Thomas Becon in 1563, the sword was believed to possess powerful supernatural properties: a wife wishing to rid herself of an abusive husband could achieve her goal by praying and placing a candle before the sword every Sunday for a whole year without missing a single week. Additionally, people sought the sword's miraculous aid to recover lost items or find horses that had wandered off or been stolen. The relic vanished during the Reformation and its whereabouts remain unknown, but the legend is still commemorated by a 1957 stained-glass window inside the church.
