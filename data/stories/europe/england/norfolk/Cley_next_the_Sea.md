#title
The Chain of Cley Hill

#summary
Walking home from Cley along the coastal road late one summer night, a local woman was stalked up Cley Hill by the phantom sound of dragging chains belonging to the legendary Black Shuck.

#details
In the summer around 1968, a woman was walking home from Cley toward Blakeney along the A149 road around midnight. As she reached the bottom of Cley Hill, she heard the distinct, light dragging and tinkling sound of a chain following closely behind her in the dark. Though she could see nothing, the unseen presence trailed her all the way up the hill to the streetlit crossroads at the top, before veering down Back Lane. Recognizing the classic manifestations of East Anglia's spectral black dog, Black Shuck, she fled in terror down the High Street.

#location
The A149 road ascending from Cley next the Sea toward Cley Hill and Back Lane in north Norfolk.

#title
The Lost Man

#summary
A retired soldier on an early morning fishing trip becomes hopelessly disoriented in a dense marshland fog and is attacked by a terrifying, clawed entity on Meadow Lane.

#details
In November 1921, retired soldier Jonathan Barkley ventured out early for a fishing trip along the marshes near Blakeney and Cley. Trapped by an unnatural, choking fog, he found himself unable to navigate the winding waterways until he spotted a glowing lantern carried by someone wading through water far too deep to touch. Terrified, he fled the marsh and reached solid ground, stumbling onto Meadow Lane in Cley next the Sea. There, a jittery, black-veiled figure holding a lantern approached him and slashed his side with a clawed hand before Barkley lost consciousness and was later discovered outside The George pub.

#location
Meadow Lane in Cley next the Sea, Norfolk, where the protagonist was attacked by the mysterious lantern-bearing entity.

#title
The Phantom Chain of Cley Hill

#summary
A woman walking home along the A149 road in Cley next the Sea late at night was stalked by the invisible, chain-rattling spectral hellhound known as Black Shuck.

#details
In the summer of around 1968, a woman walking home from Cley to Blakeney along the A149 road reached the bottom of Cley Hill around midnight. In the stillness of the dark, she began to hear the chilling, light dragging and tinkling sound of a chain. Although she could see nothing, the unseen presence closely followed her step-for-step as she climbed the hill. Arriving at the street-lit crossroads at the top near Back Lane, she stopped and waited for it to pass, but saw only empty space before hearing the rattling chain turn down Back Lane toward the marsh banks. Realising she had encountered Black Shuck—the demonic black dog of East Anglian folklore—she fled in terror down the High Street.

#location
The encounter took place along the A149 road at the foot of Cley Hill and the crossroads leading to Back Lane in Cley next the Sea, Norfolk.

#title
The Unseen Hound of Cley Hill

#summary
Walking home from Cley along the A149 at midnight, a woman was stalked by an invisible, chain-rattling spectral hound that followed her up Cley Hill before turning down Back Lane.

#details
Around midnight during the summer of 1968, a woman walking home from Cley to Blakeney along the A149 road heard the light dragging and tinkling of a chain near the bottom of Cley Hill. Although she could see nothing, the unseen presence closely followed her up the hill. Upon reaching the lit crossroads at the top, she waited to observe whatever was making the noise, but nothing materialized. She then heard the phantom entity pass away down Back Lane toward the marsh banks, recognizing the classic signs of Black Shuck and fleeing home down the High Street.
