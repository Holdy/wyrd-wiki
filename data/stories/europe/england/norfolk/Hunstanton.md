#title
The Ghostly Carpet of Hunstanton Hall

#summary
Following the destruction and distribution of her cherished Persian rug, an 18th-century matriarch returned from the dead to haunt her ancestral estate. The vengeful spirit of Dame Armine Le Strange continues to pace the corridors of Hunstanton Hall in lingering fury.

#details
In the mid-18th century, Dame Armine Le Strange of Hunstanton Hall deeply treasured an exquisite Persian carpet gifted by the Shah of Persia. On her deathbed, she made her gambler son Nicholas promise that no harm would ever come to the rug, warning that she would rise from the grave to haunt the hall if he broke her vow. Nicholas dutifully locked the rug inside a wooden crate, which remained hidden and forgotten in the attic for almost a century. However, when a later mistress of the house, Emmeline, discovered the chest during renovations, she decided to have the antique carpet cut into pieces and distributed as rugs to the poor villagers of Old Hunstanton. Almost immediately, the angry, grey-clad apparition of Dame Armine began pacing the corridors of the hall. Although the terrified family frantically gathered the fragments and had them sewn back together, the damage was done, and the matriarch's phantom presence has haunted the estate ever since.

#location
Hunstanton Hall is a historic moated country house located in the village of Old Hunstanton, Norfolk.

#title
The Grey Lady of Hunstanton Hall

#summary
Dame Armine le Strange, an 18th-century heiress, haunts Hunstanton Hall after her treasured Persian carpet—a gift from the Shah of Persia—was cut up into smaller rugs by a later resident.

#details
In the mid-18th century, Dame Armine le Strange lived at the ancestral Hunstanton Hall in Norfolk. Among her most prized possessions was a magnificent Persian carpet gifted to her by the Shah of Persia. On her deathbed in 1768, aware of her son's reckless gambling, she made him swear never to part with her cherished carpet, warning that failure to keep it safe would prompt her return to haunt the estate. Nicholas honored the promise, and the carpet remained stored away in a wooden crate in the attic for decades. 

Years later, during Victorian-era renovations, Emmeline Austin (wife of the estate's owner) discovered the forgotten carpet and decided to cut it into pieces to distribute as rugs for the poor villagers of Hunstanton. Shortly after, the ghostly apparition of a tall, grey-clad woman began pacing the corridors of the Hall. Although the surviving pieces of the carpet were hastily recovered and brought back, the matriarch's spirit remained unsettled, and subsequent generations of residents and visitors have reported sightings of the grey lady roaming the grand staircase and peering from the windows of Hunstanton Hall.

#location
Hunstanton Hall, located in the village of Old Hunstanton, Norfolk.

#title
The Phantom Hound of the Links

#summary
A spectral dog has been reported haunting the fairways of the Old Hunstanton golf course. Local folklore and regional surveys explicitly distinguish this apparition from the infamous Black Shuck.

#details
Visitors and players navigating the links at Old Hunstanton have periodically encountered a ghostly canine appearing across the course. While East Anglian folklore is dominated by tales of the demonic Black Shuck, local paranormal investigators and collectors have documented this phantom hound as an independent local presence rather than the legendary coastal fiend.

#location
The sightings occur across the grounds of the Old Hunstanton Golf Club situated in Old Hunstanton, Norfolk.
