#title
The Giant's Effigy of Walpole St. Peter

#summary
A weathered stone carving on the exterior wall of a Norfolk marshland church is locally believed to be the effigy of the legendary giant Tom Hickathrift, who famously battled the Devil.

#details
Local tradition in the Fenland and Marshland villages attributes several extraordinary feats to the folklore giant Tom Hickathrift. On the exterior wall of St. Peter's Church, at the junction of the north aisle and chancel, sits a small, heavily weathered stone figure—identified by architectural historians as an ancient Roman caryatid, but stubbornly regarded by local residents as an effigy of Tom. According to the legend, Tom hurled a heavy stone all the way from the Marshland Smeeth straight through the church wall. In a related traditional account, Tom and the Devil squared off in the churchyard for a game of football or a wrestling match, during which Satan lobbed a stone ball that missed Tom and punched clean through the stonework of the church.

#location
The exterior wall of St. Peter's Church in Walpole St. Peter, Norfolk (grid reference TF502168).
