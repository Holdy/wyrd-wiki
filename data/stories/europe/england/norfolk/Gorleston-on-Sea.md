#title
The Coastguard's Phantom Hound

#summary
In April 1972, a coastguard on duty early in the morning witnessed a large black hound suddenly appear and vanish on the beach north of the Gorleston rescue headquarters.

#details
On April 19th, 1972, at 4:45 AM, coastguard officer Graham Grant was on night duty at the Gorleston rescue headquarters. He spotted a large black hound-type dog running and stopping on the beach about a quarter of a mile north of the lookout, as if searching for someone. After watching it for a couple of minutes, the creature vanished entirely into thin air right before his eyes on a stretch of beach that had been recently flattened by bulldozers, leaving nowhere for an animal to hide. Unfamiliar with local folklore at the time, Grant later learned from a colleague that he had encountered the legendary Black Shuck.

#location
The beach approximately a quarter of a mile north of the former Gorleston coastguard rescue headquarters in Gorleston-on-Sea.

#title
The Haunting of Pier House

#summary
Residents of a historic quayside home in Gorleston-on-Sea experienced terrifying ghostly activity, ranging from unexplained noises and physical touches to a malevolent entity targeting the homeowner in her dreams. Paranormal investigators were called in to cleanse the property and free the resident from a dark spiritual attachment.

#details
Daniel and his fiancé Claire Meakins sought help from paranormal investigators Barri Ghai, Jayne Harris, and Ian Lawman after being plagued by persistent whispers, strange smells, growling, and heavy footsteps at their home, Pier House on Quay Road. Historically associated with 20th-century harbour master Captain Sutton, the house harbored a heavy, oppressive atmosphere that deterred visiting family members. During the investigation, the team captured unexplained noises and physical interactions where clothing was tugged, while clairvoyant Ian Lawman worked to sever a dangerous, suffocating dark entity that had been attaching itself to Claire during her sleep.

#location
Pier House is situated on Quay Road in Gorleston-on-Sea, Norfolk.

#title
The Phantom of the Lily Pit

#summary
A deep pond along the Gorleston to Beccles road is haunted by a midnight phantom pony and trap, tied to local legends of tragic drownings and coaching accidents.

#details
Close to the side of the main Gorleston to Beccles road (now the A143) lies a deep pond known as the Lily Pit. At midnight, a phantom pony and trap are said to thunder along the road and plunge directly into the water. Three distinct legends attempt to explain the haunting: that a mail-coach missed the road and vanished into the pit forever; that a farm-hand's beloved drowned in the pool after an elopement, leading the young man to hang himself from a nearby tree; or that the pit was the ancient well of a chieftain named 'Old Canoba'. These dark tales are anchored by grim real-world events, such as an 1888 incident where a local man named James Keable lost his way in the fog and drowned in the pit, and the 1892 discovery of an ancient British burial skeleton resting on paved flints at the site.

#location
The Lily Pit pond, situated just west of the former Otter's Farm alongside the Gorleston to Beccles road (A143) at grid reference TG518049 in Gorleston-on-Sea.
