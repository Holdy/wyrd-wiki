    The Origin of Jack Valentine and the First Love Letter

    Summary

    On Valentine's Eve, the elusive folkloric figure Jack Valentine visits homes to leave gifts and play pranks, a tradition rooted in the historic first Valentine's letter sent from Topcroft. This enduring custom blends seasonal magic with the legacy of medieval romance in Norfolk.

    Details

    Long before modern commercialized Valentine's Day cards, the county of Norfolk celebrated a unique folklore surrounding a mysterious gift-giver known as Jack Valentine. On the evening of February 13th, Jack Valentine would silently approach houses under the cover of night, knocking on doors and leaving treats, sweets, or parcels on doorsteps before vanishing into thin air. In some variations of the legend, a mischievous counterpart named Snatch Valentine would play pranks—leaving empty boxes, attaching strings to yank gifts away as children reached for them, or chalking false letters on doorsteps. This rich regional tradition is famously connected to Topcroft, where in 1477 Margery Brews wrote the world's earliest known surviving Valentine's letter at Topcroft Hall to her betrothed, John Paston, addressing him as her "right well-beloved Valentine."
