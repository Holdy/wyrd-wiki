    The Battle of Preleston

    Summary

    The Norfolk village of Billingford derived its original Domesday name, Preleston, from a legendary, ancient battle fought there during the Roman period. In later centuries, inhabitants settled by the river ford, giving rise to its modern name meaning "the dwelling at the ford by the low meadows."

    Details

    According to local tradition and historical topographical accounts, the Norfolk village of Billingford was originally recorded in the Domesday Book as Preleston, which translates to the "Town of the Battle." This ancient designation was believed to stem from a remarkable and fierce battle fought on the site while the Romans possessed the land. Centuries later, during the reign of Henry III, local inhabitants began establishing their homes by the pass and ford across the river leading into Suffolk. Consequently, the village acquired the name Billingford—signifying the dwelling at the ford by the low meadows—thus preserving both its warlike prehistoric origins and its geography in local folklore.
