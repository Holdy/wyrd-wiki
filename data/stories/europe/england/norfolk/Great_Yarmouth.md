#title
Specters of the Tolhouse

#summary
Tolhouse Gaol in Great Yarmouth is plagued by the tormented spirits of those imprisoned and executed during Matthew Hopkins' 1645 witch trials. Visitors and staff frequently report physical encounters with unseen hands, extreme temperature drops, and bloodcurdling screams echoing through the historic building.

#details
Originally constructed in the 12th century, Tolhouse Gaol served as Great Yarmouth's primary penal institution for centuries. Its darkest chapter occurred in 1645 when the notorious witch-finder general Matthew Hopkins arrived to conduct witch trials, leading to the imprisonment and hanging execution of five accused individuals in the dismal holding area known as "the hold." Today, centuries of suffering, torture, and death have left a heavy supernatural imprint on the stone building. Witnesses inside report full-bodied apparitions pacing the corridors, sudden and freezing temperature drops, and being physically grabbed, pushed, or touched by unseen entities, accompanied by the disembodied echoes of terrified screams.

#location
Tolhouse Gaol is located at 12 Tolhouse Street in Great Yarmouth, Norfolk (NR30 2SQ).

#title
The Demonic Hound of Old Scarfe

#summary
A depraved 13th-century baron was transformed by the Devil into a fiery-eyed, chained hellhound that haunts Southtown Road and the vaults of a local inn. To this day, the spectral beast terrorizes benighted travelers with sorrowful moans and clanking chains.

#details
Baron Rudolph Scarfe was a wicked and depraved German man notorious in the 13th century for his evil deeds who sought asylum in England, settling at Burgh Castle and terrorizing the people of Great Yarmouth. When Scarfe was eventually killed, the Devil transformed him into a black hellhound with glowing red eyes and heavy chains, sending him back to earth. Although an attempt was made to exorcise the spectre—dooming the evil spirit to remain in a vault under the Duke's Head Inn until the river should cease to run under Yarmouth bridge—Old Scarfe continues to rush up and down Southtown Road on dark winter nights, making sorrowful moanings and dragging a clanking chain behind him.

#location
The Duke's Head Inn is located on Southtown Road in Great Yarmouth, Norfolk.

#title
The Execution of Elizabeth Bradwell

#summary
In 1645, beach-dweller Elizabeth Bradwell and four other women were condemned as witches in Great Yarmouth during the witch-finder general's reign and buried in an unmarked pit at St Nicholas Churchyard.

#details
In September 1645, amidst the turmoil of the English Civil War and the witch-hunting campaign of Matthew Hopkins, an impoverished beach-comber named Elizabeth Bradwell who lived in an upturned boat on the dunes near the Yarmouth-Caister border was accused of bewitching a town councillor's sick child. Following brutal interrogation and sleep deprivation in the Tollhouse jail, she was convicted alongside four other women—Alice Clesswell, Bridgit Howard, Maria Blackborne, and Elizabeth Dudgeon. They were hanged on September 29, 1645, near the Jellicoe Road area on the Yarmouth-Caister border, and their bodies were buried together in an unmarked grave on the north side of St Nicholas Churchyard in Great Yarmouth.

#location
St Nicholas Churchyard and the Jellicoe Road on the Yarmouth-Caister border, Great Yarmouth, Norfolk.

#title
The Ghostly Knights of St Nicholas Churchyard

#summary
In October 1922, residents of Great Yarmouth gathered outside St Nicholas Church after witnesses reported seeing three spectral knights in shining armour riding horses across the graveyard.

#details
In October 1922, a wave of eerie fascination gripped Great Yarmouth following a startling report made to the local police. A woman came forward stating that she had witnessed three phantoms clad in shining armour riding horses directly across the grounds of St Nicholas Churchyard. As word of the nocturnal encounter spread rapidly through the town, hundreds of curious townspeople gathered night after night along the church railings, hoping to catch a glimpse of the supernatural horsemen. Despite informal bets and challenges inviting brave individuals to camp out in the graveyard to keep watch for the knights, no one dared take up the offer, and the mysterious riders passed permanently into local folklore.

#location
St Nicholas Church graveyard, Church Plain, Great Yarmouth, Norfolk.
