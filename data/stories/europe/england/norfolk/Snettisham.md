#title
The Snettisham Ghost Mystery

#summary
In October 1893, a London woman named Mrs. Goodeve was visited by a series of spectral apparitions that led her on a 200-mile journey to St Mary's Church in Snettisham to help resolve an old legal dispute.

#details
While staying at a friend's house near Bristol in October 1893, Mrs. Goodeve was awakened at night by the ghost of a former resident, Mrs. Seagrim, who instructed her to prepare for a second visitation. The following night, Mrs. Seagrim reappeared alongside two male spirits—one of whom identified himself as Henry Barnard, stating he was buried in the Snettisham churchyard. The spirits instructed Mrs. Goodeve to travel over 200 miles to the village of Snettisham, verify specific marriage and death dates in the parish register, stay at the home of church clerk John Bishop (whose child was buried in the local churchyard), and meet them at 1:15 A.M. inside St Mary's Church near the grave of Robert Cobb. Following these instructions precisely, Mrs. Goodeve entered the dark church at night, where the three spirits appeared to her to give instructions on correcting an illegal real estate transaction involving Cobb Hall, concluding her bizarre mission by delivering a white rose from Barnard's grave to his daughter.

#location
St Mary's Church and churchyard in Snettisham, Norfolk.

#upstream-source
Ghost Hunters by James M. Deem (Avon, 1992)

#title
The Snettisham Ghost Who Asked for a Favour

#summary
In 1893, a reluctant psychic named Mrs Goodeve was visited in the night by spirits who instructed her to travel to Snettisham and deliver a posthumous message. She faithfully followed their detailed nocturnal instructions to meet at St Mary's Church and pass a final token to the deceased man's daughter.

#details
While visiting friends in October 1893, Mrs Goodeve—a capable woman recognized by psychical researchers as a reluctant psychic—was visited by the apparition of a sad-eyed woman wrapped in a shawl, followed by a spirit identifying himself as Henry Barnard, who was buried in Snettisham. Henry gave precise prophecies about her upcoming train journey and meeting a local parish clerk, and instructed Mrs Goodeve to visit St Mary's Church in Snettisham at 1:15 AM to stand by the grave of Robert Cobb. Upon arriving in Norfolk where all the spirits' predictions came true, Mrs Goodeve was locked into the church at night, waited by Robert Cobb's stone, and received instructions to pick a white rose from Barnard's grave to deliver alongside a confidential message to his surviving daughter at Cobb Hall.

#location
St Mary's Church in Snettisham, Norfolk, houses the historic interior grave of Robert Cobb and the churchyard plot where the midnight spectral encounter took place.
