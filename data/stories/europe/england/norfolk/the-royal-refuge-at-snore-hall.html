    The Royal Refuge at Snore Hall

    Summary

    Local tradition maintains that King Charles I sought refuge and held a secret council at Snore Hall in Fordham during his flight in the English Civil War, utilizing the manor's hidden priest hole to evade capture.

    Details

    Snore Hall, a historic brick and timber-framed manor house situated in the Norfolk village of Fordham, is steeped in local legend regarding the English Civil War. In May 1646, as the Royalist cause collapsed and Parliamentary forces pursued him, King Charles I reportedly sought shelter at the isolated hall. According to local accounts and historical records, the King stayed at the property and convened a secret council. During his time at Snore Hall, the monarch allegedly made use of the building's ingenious 16th-century priest hole—a concealed two-tier hiding space built into the heavy chimney breast featuring a connecting trapdoor—to elude capture before continuing his escape.
