#title
The Guardian Hound of Bawburgh

#summary
While cycling home late at night, a young man encountered a colossal spectral hound sitting by a village signpost before an unlit vehicle seemingly crashed into it and vanished. The supernatural encounter was later believed by the witness to have miraculously saved his life.

#details
Late one moonlit night, Mr. E. Ramsey of Bawburgh was cycling home from a darts match in Norwich. As he approached his home village, he spotted the largest hound he had ever seen sitting by the village signpost, its eyes shining like glowing coals. Though nervous, he cycled past the motionless beast, but soon heard heavy paws beating the road behind him. The giant dog sprinted past him so closely that he could smell its rank odor, before abruptly stopping and standing aggressively in the middle of the road near a spinney. Terrified, Ramsey dismounted and sought cover behind his bicycle. At that exact moment, an unlit vehicle roared out of the spinney, careered erratically, and appeared to smash straight into the hound, sending Ramsey tumbling into the hedge. When he picked himself up, the dog was still standing unharmed before turning and vanishing completely into thin air. Ramsey later associated the appearance of the ghostly hound—which he believed saved him from being struck by the runaway vehicle—with the tragic death of his wife two years later.

#location
The sighting took place near the village signpost and adjacent spinney along the road entering Bawburgh, Norfolk.

#title
The Legend of Saint Walstan

#summary
Born into nobility in Bawburgh, Prince Walstan renounced his wealth at age twelve to work as a humble farm labourer, inspiring centuries of folklore about his miraculous oxen and holy wells.

#details
Born around 975 AD in Bawburgh, Walstan was the son of noble parents with royal connections. At the age of twelve, he chose to renounce his privileged background, leaving home to walk seven miles to Taverham to work as a farm labourer. Throughout his life, he remained deeply pious and charitable, giving away his wages and even his shoes to the needy. When the farmer’s wife forced him to work barefoot among thorns as punishment for his generosity, the thorns miraculously emitted a pleasant scent rather than scratching him. Nearing the end of his life, Walstan received a vision foretelling his death and instructed that upon his passing, his body should be placed on a cart pulled by two oxen, allowing the animals to wander freely to determine his burial place. Following his death on May 30, 1016, the oxen journeyed through the countryside, causing springs of water to gush forth wherever they paused, culminating in the valley below Bawburgh Church where St. Walstan's Well now stands. The oxen then climbed the hill to the church, where Walstan was buried, establishing Bawburgh as a major medieval pilgrimage site.

#location
St. Mary and St. Walstan’s Church is located on Church Street in the village of Bawburgh, Norfolk.

#title
The Phantom Hound of Bawburgh

#summary
While cycling home late at night, a young man encountered a colossal spectral hound with glowing eyes that mysteriously vanished after an unlit vehicle seemingly crashed through it. The witness later believed the ghostly beast had saved him from a fatal accident.

#details
Late one moonlit night, Mr. E. Ramsey was cycling home to Bawburgh after attending a darts match in Norwich. As he approached his home village, he spotted an enormous hound sitting by the roadside signpost, its eyes shining like coals of fire. Though nervous, he passed safely, but half a mile further down the road, he heard the heavy beating of paws approaching from behind. The massive beast overtook him so closely that he could smell its rank odor before stopping aggressively in the middle of the road ahead. Just as Ramsey dismounted in terror behind his bicycle, an unlit vehicle careered out of a nearby spinney and seemed to crash straight through the hound. Ramsey fell into the hedge in shock as the vehicle sped past. When he picked himself up, he was astonished to see the dog completely unharmed; it then turned and vanished into thin air. Although folklore dictated that seeing the ghostly hound foreshadowed death—an omen he later associated with his wife's passing two years later—Ramsey came to believe the apparition had saved his life by occupying the exact spot where the speeding vehicle would have struck him.

#location
The encounter took place by the village signpost and a nearby spinney on the approach road into Bawburgh, Norfolk.
