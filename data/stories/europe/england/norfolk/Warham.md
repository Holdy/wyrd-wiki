#title
The Fiddler of Fiddler's Hill

#summary
An itinerant violinist and his dog ventured into a secret subterranean passage to investigate reports of a ghostly monk, only for his music to abruptly cease beneath a roadside burial mound from which he never returned.

#details
According to local tradition, a fiddler and his canine companion attempted to explore a legendary underground tunnel said to link Binham Priory and Walsingham in order to track down a phantom black-hooded monk. As the musician walked underground playing his violin, villagers followed his progress above ground by listening to the travelling strains of his music. When the procession reached the ancient mound at the crossroads near Warham, the music suddenly stopped. The fiddler failed to emerge, and his dog eventually scrambled back to the surface shivering and whining. That same night, a violent thunderstorm battered the area, and the tunnel entrance mysteriously collapsed. Years later, road workers excavating the mound discovered human and animal skeletal remains.

#location
Fiddler's Hill is a round barrow located at the crossroads of the Wighton and Stiffkey road and the Binham and Warham road near Warham, Norfolk.

#title
The Fiddler of Fiddler's Hill

#summary
A local fiddler and his dog entered a subterranean monk's tunnel beneath an ancient Norfolk mound, but only the terrified dog returned while the musician vanished forever. Alongside his disappearance, the apparition of a black monk continues to haunt the site.

#details
According to local legend, a secret subterranean tunnel was said to run between Binham Priory and Walsingham, above which the ghost of a black monk would wander nightly, searching in vain. When an old fiddler named Jimmy Griggs and his dog Trap heard tales of the tunnel, they decided to investigate the entrance located at an ancient Bronze Age barrow known as Fiddler's Hill. As Jimmy walked into the subterranean passage, he played his violin so villagers could follow his progress along the green bank. However, when they reached the mound, the music abruptly stopped. The dog shot out of the dark opening shivering and terrified with its tail between its legs, but the fiddler was never seen again. Following a violent storm that night which caved in and filled the entrance, mysterious music has occasionally been heard echoing around the mound at midnight.

#location
Fiddler's Hill is a 4,000-year-old Bronze Age burial mound located in the angle of a crossroads between Binham and Warham in Norfolk.
