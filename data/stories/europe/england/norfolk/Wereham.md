#title
The Festivities and Well-Worship of St. Margaret's Well

#summary
In pre-Reformation times, villagers gathered at St. Margaret's Well in Wereham on St. Margaret's Day to partake in music, dancing, cakes, and ale, offering vows in a traditional practice known as well-worship.

#details
Historical accounts document that to the west of St. Margaret's Church in Wereham, a sacred spring known as St. Margaret's Well was a focal point of local devotion and celebration before the English Reformation. On St. Margaret's Day, celebrated annually on July 20th, the local community would assemble at the well to engage in "well-worship." Participants brought alms and offerings, made solemn vows, and diverted themselves with music, dancing, cakes, and ale. Furthermore, traditional weather-lore dictated that rain would invariably fall on this day, a phenomenon locally termed "Margaret's flood." Although the medieval well was later modified with an obelisk in the 19th century and eventually paved over near Margaret's Hill, the date continues to be commemorated by the village church's annual festival.

#location
St. Margaret's Church and the historic site of St. Margaret's Well located in the village of Wereham, West Norfolk.
