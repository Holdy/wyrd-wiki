#title
The Treasure of Callow Pit

#summary
Two adventurous men attempting to haul a chest of gold from a deep pit near Cantley lost their prize to the Devil, leaving behind only the iron ring from the chest which was affixed to a local church door.

#details
On the boundary of the parishes of Southwood and Moulton, near Cantley in Norfolk, sits a deep, tree-shrouded hollow known as Callow Pit (or Caller Pit), which village tradition whispered contained an iron chest filled with gold. Many years ago, two adventurous men took advantage of an unusually low water level to try to retrieve the treasure. Constructing a platform of ladders across the pool, they managed to insert a staff through the ring on the chest lid and heave it to the surface, placing the staff onto their shoulders to carry it away. 

In a moment of supreme hubris, one of the men shouted triumphantly, "We've got it safe, and the devil himself can't get it from us!" Instantly, a thick cloud of sulphurous steam enveloped the pit, and a black hand and arm surged from the water to grab the chest. A fierce tug-of-war ensued between the men and the unseen entity until the chest could no longer bear the strain and broke apart, sinking back beneath the waters forever. The only remnant the thwarted men walked away with was the iron ring from the lid, which was subsequently fastened to the door of Southwood Church. To this day, local folklore adds that the area is patrolled by a headless horseman riding at midnight from Callow Pit toward Cantley Spong.

#location
Callow Pit, situated on the parish boundary between Southwood and Moulton near Cantley, Norfolk, UK.
