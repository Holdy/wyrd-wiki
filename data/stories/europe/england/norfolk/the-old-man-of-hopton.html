The Old Man of Hopton

Summary

A spectral pedestrian known as the Old Man of Hopton repeatedly materializes to cross the A47 dual carriageway in front of startled drivers, sometimes vanishing or appearing semi-transparent before vehicles pass right through him. Motorists have reported encountering this eerie, heavy-booted figure since at least the 1950s.

Details

For decades, motorists driving along the A47 (formerly the A12) through Hopton-on-Sea have reported uncanny encounters with a stocky, hunched apparition clad in a shapeless coat and heavy boots. Known locally as the Old Man of Hopton, this spectral pedestrian steps out into the mist-shrouded carriageway with unnatural deliberation before vanishing into thin air.

One of the most notable accounts occurred in November 1980, when Frank Colby of the British Transport Police was driving with his wife on the Hopton bypass. He slammed on his brakes as a glowing, misty figure plodded across the road ahead, observing that the white road markings were clearly visible right through the apparition's body—though his wife, sitting right beside him, saw nothing at all. Earlier sightings date back to January 1957, when a Lowestoft resident named Roger Hammersley witnessed a tall man in a fawn overcoat cross the road and disappear instantly. In another incident in November 1981, a driver named Andrew Cutajar braked desperately to avoid a misty form with long straggly hair, skidding on the verge as his car passed straight through the phantom. Local tradition and researchers have connected the recurring apparition to William Balls, a dedicated local postman who tragically collapsed and died on duty in 1899 near the road, forever tethering his ghost to the misty coastal route near the ruined St Margaret's Church.
