#title
The Stolen Brass of Sir Adam de Clifton

#summary
In 1680, a corrupt sexton in Methwold stole and sold a priceless memorial brass of Sir Adam de Clifton to a tinker, leaving only scattered fragments that languished in a parish chest for nearly two centuries.

#details
According to local tradition at St George's Church in Methwold, the parish suffered a scandalous loss in 1680 when a rogue sexton illicitly removed the historical memorial brass of Sir Adam de Clifton and sold it to a traveling tinker. Authorities eventually tracked down the tinker, but much of the brass had already been broken up or lost. The recovered fragments were placed inside the parish chest, where they remained neglected and forgotten for two hundred years. It was not until 1860 that the new incumbent, John Denny Gedge, gathered the surviving pieces and had them mounted on the wall of the north aisle, where Sir Adam's incomplete brass effigy can still be seen today.

#location
The north aisle of St George's Church on Crown Street in Methwold, Norfolk.
