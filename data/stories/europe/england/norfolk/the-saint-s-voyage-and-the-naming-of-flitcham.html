    The Saint's Voyage and the Naming of Flitcham

    Summary

    According to local tradition, the Norfolk village of Flitcham takes its name from Saint Felix, who sailed up the River Babingley in the seventh century to establish an early Christian sanctuary.

    Details

    While etymological records suggest that Flitcham's name stems from the Old English for a homestead where flitches of bacon were cured, local folklore preserves a far more sacred origin. In the seventh century, Saint Felix, the apostle and first bishop of the East Angles, journeyed into the region. Legend holds that he navigated his vessel up the winding waters of the River Babingley after arriving on the Norfolk coast, making his way inland to found a religious settlement and church. The settlement became known locally as Felix-ham—the homestead of Felix—which gradually corrupted over the centuries into Flitcham. This foundational legend is celebrated in the village's history and depicted on its village sign, where the saint is remembered sailing upstream into the heart of the parish.
