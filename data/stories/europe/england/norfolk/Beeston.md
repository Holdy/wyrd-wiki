#title
Farmer Reynolds and the Hooded Spectre of Beeston Regis

#summary
A frustrated Norfolk farmer repeatedly harassed by a grey-hooded ghost at sunset ensured his final resting place would crush the spirit's hiding spot by having his grave marker replaced with the very glacial erratic the spectre used for cover.

#details
At Beeston Regis, farmer James Reynolds frequently drove his horse and cart past All Saints Church, where ancient glacial erratic stones stood near the gateway. At sunset, a hooded apparition dressed in grey robes would repeatedly leap out from behind one of the stones to frighten his horses and attempt to grab their reins. Rather than being intimidated, Reynolds was infuriated by the disturbances. Before his death in 1941, he gave strict instructions for his grave to be marked by the large stone the ghost used as a hiding place, believing the heavy stone's weight and removal of its cover would permanently trap or put the spirit to rest. Sixteen years later, the stone was temporarily moved so his wife, Ann Elizabeth, could be buried alongside him before being placed back over their shared resting place.

#location
All Saints Church in Beeston Regis, Norfolk.

#title
The Ghost Dog of Beeston Bump

#summary
A terrifying spectral hound known as Old Shuck patrols the North Norfolk coast between Beeston and Overstrand every evening, heralded by glowing saucer-shaped eyes and smelling of brimstone. This phantom dog serves as both a fearful premonition of coastal storms and a guardian of the crumbling cliff paths.

#details
In Norfolk folklore, a gigantic black ghost dog called Old Shuck—also known as Shock—regularly haunts the coastal expanse between Beeston and Overstrand. Unlike purely ethereal spirits, the hound is reported to be tangible and material, possessing massive glowing red or saucer-like eyes and occasionally appearing headless or wearing a handkerchief over its missing head. According to accounts dating back to the 19th century, including recollections by writer Amelia Opie, the spectral hound starts its journey at twilight from Beeston, rising from Beeston Bump or Beeston Heath, and prowls along the treacherous cliff paths and lanes toward Overstrand churchyard. Fishermen and locals regard sightings of the beast not only as an ominous warning of misfortune or death, but also as a portent of fierce incoming sea storms.

#location
Beeston Bump is a prominent, glaciated coastal hill located in Beeston Regis, Norfolk.

#title
The Ghostly Guardian of Beeston Regis

#summary
A furious Norfolk farmer repeatedly menaced by a hooded spectre near a ruined priory vowed to stop the haunting by having a massive glacial stone placed over his grave after his death. To this day, his stone-capped tomb at All Saints' Church marks his final resting place.

#details
Farmer James Reynolds regularly drove his horses past the historic gateway of Beeston Regis Priory, where a hooded ghost in grey robes would leap out from behind a large glacial erratic stone at sunset, attempting to grab the horses' reins and terrifying the animals. Refusing to be intimidated by the apparition, Reynolds vowed to put an end to the haunting. When he died in 1941, a four-foot-long, two-foot-high glacial stone was placed atop his grave at All Saints' Church in accordance with his wishes, symbolically removing the ghost's hiding spot and using the weight of the stone to keep the spirit from roaming between worlds.

#location
All Saints' Church in Beeston Regis, Norfolk, houses the grave of James Reynolds beneath a large glacial stone in its churchyard.

#title
The Legend of Organ Beck

#summary
A mysterious water-filled quarry pit in Beeston Regis is said to conceal a church pump-organ in its depths. Local belief holds that the pool is entirely bottomless, connecting directly to Satan's lair.

#details
Organ Beck is a deep pool situated on the site of a coastal caravan park in Beeston Regis, occupying an old brickworks quarry pit near the cliffs. According to local folklore, the pond takes its peculiar name from the local church's original pump-organ, which allegedly came to rest somewhere in its murky water. More sinister tales suggest that the pit has no bed at all, forming a bottomless abyss that leads straight down to Satan's lair.

#location
Organ Beck is a water-filled quarry pit located within the coastal caravan park at Beeston Regis in North Norfolk.
