#title
The Green Lady of Costessey Hall

#summary
Legend holds that the ruins of Costessey Hall are haunted by a benevolent Green Lady who guarded the historic estate and its secret Catholic heritage. According to local tradition, her spirit was bound to the hall's Thornbury Tower, casting a curse on anyone who attempted to fully demolish it.

#details
Costessey Hall was a magnificent Gothic mansion built by the Jerningham family, who were staunch Catholics that concealed priests and maintained secret places of worship during periods of religious persecution. After being used for military training during the First World War, the hall fell into disrepair and was largely torn down, leaving behind ivy-clad ruins and towers. Local residents and former estate servants long recounted sightings of the Green Lady, a protective spirit whose presence was tied to the history of the house. When demolition contractors attempted to blast away Thornbury Tower in the 1950s, the structure stubbornly resisted destruction, eventually sinking into the ground before partially collapsing during a storm in the 1960s, fulfilling the dark local tradition that the spirit would haunt those who dismantled her domain.

#location
The remaining ivy-clad ruins of Costessey Hall and the former site of Thornbury Tower stand near the 18th fairway of Costessey Park Golf Course in Costessey, Norfolk.

#upstream-source
Ernest Gage, *Costessey Hall: A Retrospect of the Jernegans, Jeringhams and Stafford Jerninghams of Costessey Hall, Norfolk* (1991).

#title
The Green Lady of Costessey Hall

#summary
A spectral woman dressed in green guards the ivy-clad ruins and belfry of a historic Norfolk estate, acting as an echo of its tumultuous past. Witnesses report her lingering presence around the remaining structures of the once-grand Gothic hall.

#details
Costessey Hall, a magnificent Gothic mansion built by the Jerningham family, met its demise after being requisitioned for military training during World War One, leaving behind only scattered ruins and a belfry. Amidst this architectural loss, local lore speaks of the Green Lady, a restless female spirit who watches over the grounds. Believed by some to embody the protective spirit of Catholic residents who once sheltered persecuted priests in secret attic chapels and priest holes, the apparition is said to haunt the surviving structures, including the ill-fated Thornbury Tower before its collapse. To this day, locals recount encounters with this elusive guardian of the estate.

#location
The ivy-clad ruins and belfry block of Costessey Hall, situated near Costessey Park Golf Course in Costessey, Norfolk.

#upstream-source
*Costessey Hall: A Retrospect of the Jernegans, Jeringhams and Stafford Jerninghams of Costessey Hall, Norfolk* by Ernest Gage (1991).

#title
The Shuck of Old Costessey

#summary
A mysterious spectral black dog, known as a Shuck, was reported roaming around a village pub in Old Costessey. The unsettling apparition added to the rich folklore of ghostly hounds in East Anglia.

#details
In 2005, residents and visitors in the Norfolk village of Old Costessey reported encounters with a strange, giant black dog. The phantom creature, identified as a Shuck, was seen wandering the streets and countryside near The Heart of Costessy Pub. Though no hostile interactions or specific behaviors were recorded, the frequency of sightings generated widespread local intrigue.

#location
The apparition was reported in the area surrounding The Heart of Costessy Pub in Old Costessey, Norfolk.
