#title
The Witch's Leg at St Mary's Church

#summary
In the ruined medieval church of East Somerton near Scratby stands an ancient oak tree said to have sprouted from the wooden leg of a witch buried alive within its walls.

#details
Dating back to the 15th century, St Mary’s Church fell into disuse in the 17th century and was gradually reclaimed by nature, leaving behind glassless windows, tumbling walls, and an open-air tower. The most striking feature inside the ruined nave is a slender oak tree known locally as "The Witch's Leg." According to local legend, during the height of England's witch trials, a suspected witch was buried alive on the grounds. In her final hours of suffering, she enchanted her wooden leg so that an oak tree would sprout from it and tear down the church above her. Tradition holds that anyone who walks around the tree three times will release the witch's trapped spirit, though it is said that ghostly monks haunt the ruins to prevent visitors from breaking the spell.

#location
The ruins of St Mary's Church are located in the rural woodland of East Somerton, just northwest of Scratby in Norfolk, England.
