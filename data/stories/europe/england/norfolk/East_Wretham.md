#title
Devil's Mere

#summary
Langmere on East Wretham Heath was long regarded as the Devil's domain, where shepherds and cattlemen risked misfortune or ill if they drove their animals onto its island.

#details
Langmere is a fluctuating mere situated on East Wretham Heath in Breckland, characterised by water levels that rise and fall unpredictably as underground chalk caverns fill and drain. According to local folklore, the mere and its central island were considered the domain of the Devil. Shepherds and cattlemen were warned never to drive their livestock onto the island when the water level dropped, for fear of incurring severe misfortune or ill. Furthermore, locals strictly avoided hunting fowl or taking fish from its waters, believing that trespassing in the Devil's lair would invite unholy retribution.

#location
Langmere (grid reference TL906884) is a fluctuating mere located on East Wretham Heath.

#title
The Devil's Mere

#summary
Langmere, a remote Breckland lake in East Wretham, was feared as the Devil's domain where driving livestock onto its temporary island brought certain misfortune. Locals also strictly avoided fishing in its waters or hunting fowl around its shores.

#details
Langmere is a small lake located within East Wretham amongst the traditional Breckland meres. When water levels drop during dry periods, a small island in the center of the lake becomes accessible via a narrow isthmus connecting it to the shore. Local folklore long warned that any shepherd or cattleman who drove their animals onto this island would be cursed with severe misfortune and ill luck. Considered to be under the dominion of the Devil, no one in the parish would ever dare to fish in the mere or hunt wildfowl on its waters.

#location
Langmere is a small lake and nature spot located in the parish of East Wretham, Norfolk (grid reference TL906884).
