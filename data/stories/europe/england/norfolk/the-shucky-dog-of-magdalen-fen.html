The Shucky Dog of Magdalen Fen

Summary

During a severe winter in Wiggenhall St. Mary Magdalen, two men found their acquaintance dead of sheer terror in his cottage after noticing dog paw prints leading up to his door with none leading away, linked to the legend of the phantom Shucky Dog.

Details

In about 1865, during a severe winter in Wiggenhall St. Mary Magdalen, two local men visited the cottage of their acquaintance Abe Mindham in Magdalen Fen to check on him because he had not been seen for several days. Walking through heavy snow, they noticed dog paw prints leading right up to Mindham's door, but none leading away from it. Inside, they discovered his dead body on the floor, cold and rigid, with his eyes wide and staring in absolute horror. Local tradition held that Magdalen's Shucky Dog—a great black phantom beast said to gallop across the fen fields on moonless nights, accompanied only by the rattling of a broken chain—had approached his door, causing Mindham to die of sheer fright when he opened it.
