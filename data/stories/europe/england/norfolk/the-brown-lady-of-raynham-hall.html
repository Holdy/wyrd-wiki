The Brown Lady of Raynham Hall

Summary

According to legend, Raynham Hall in Norfolk is haunted by the spirit of Lady Dorothy Walpole, an eighteenth-century noblewoman who famously manifests as an ethereal figure in a brown brocade dress.

Details

Lady Dorothy Walpole, sister of Great Britain's first Prime Minister Robert Walpole, was married to Charles, 2nd Viscount Townshend. Local legend holds that after her husband discovered her infidelity, she was locked away in her rooms at Raynham Hall until her death in 1726. Over the following centuries, the apparition—dubbed the "Brown Lady"—was witnessed by numerous guests and residents, including King George IV, Colonel Loftus, and Captain Frederick Marryat, who reported being confronted by a figure with empty, dark eye-sockets before firing a revolver through its phantom form. The haunting achieved global renown in September 1936 when photographers Captain Hubert Provand and Indre Shira captured what is claimed to be a photograph of the ghost descending the hall's main staircase.
