    The Vanished Church and the Lost Shrine of Langham

    Summary

    In the Norfolk village of Langham, the medieval church of St Mary disappeared entirely following the Reformation, leaving behind persistent local traditions of its mysterious shrine dedicated to Our Lady of Tofte. Alongside the vanished church, ancient records and oral history link the area to the "Langham Madonna," a thirteenth-century wooden statuette whispered to be a hidden survivor of the region's sacred past.

    Details

    Long before modern times, the small agricultural parish of Langham was divided into two manors and featured two medieval churches standing only a few hundred meters apart: St Andrew's, which still crowns a ridge in the centre of the village, and St Mary's, which served Langham Parva. By the early seventeenth century, St Mary's had been completely demolished and ruined, leaving no visible above-ground remains except for the corner of an empty field off the Binham Road. Throughout late medieval wills, parishioners frequently left gifts for the repair and upkeep of "Our Lady of Tofte," a revered chapel and devotional image associated with the parish. Centuries later, historians and museum curators identified a thirteenth-century wooden statuette of the Virgin and Child in the Victoria and Albert Museum—known as the Langham Madonna—whose provenance and unique physical fittings strongly connect it to Langham and local legends of miraculous statues surviving the upheaval of the English Reformation.
