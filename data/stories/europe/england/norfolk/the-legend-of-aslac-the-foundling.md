#title
The Legend of Aslac the Foundling

#summary
According to local tradition, an infant orphan left abandoned at the church style grew up under the care of the parish to become a renowned standard-bearer for King Edward III.

#details
Local tradition held within the parish of Aslacton tells the tale of a helpless infant found wrapped in swaddling clothes and left at the style of St Michael's Church. Taken in and reared by the local community, the orphan was named Aslac after the village. As he matured, he distinguished himself as a man of great renown and prowess, eventually serving as standard-bearer to King Edward III and marrying the daughter of Sir Oliver Calthorp of Burnham. For centuries, this legend was commemorated in the chancel window of St Michael's Church, which featured the image of an infant in a cradle surrounded by the heraldic arms of Aslac and connected families.

#location
St Michael's Church, situated in the village of Aslacton in South Norfolk, England.
