#title
The Collapsed Tower and the Survivor's Font

#summary
When the heavy stone tower of All Saints' Church collapsed through the roof, it smashed directly into the baptismal font where the infant Horatio Nelson had been christened. After surviving the disaster, the damaged font was cast out into the rectory garden as a planter before eventually being restored to the church.

#details
In the small Norfolk village of Burnham Thorpe, All Saints' Church served as the local parish church where Edmund Nelson was rector. In September 1758, his sickly newborn son, future naval hero Horatio Nelson, was hastily baptized at the church's stone font. According to local historical accounts and village lore, the church subsequently fell into severe disrepair, culminating in a dramatic structural disaster when the heavy stone tower collapsed, crashing straight down through the roof and landing on the font, breaking its pedestal. Long after the wreckage was cleared, the damaged stone bowl was hauled out into the rectory garden and repurposed as a garden planter. It sat there for generations before being rediscovered, repaired, and triumphantly reinstated inside the church.

#location
All Saints' Church is located on Church Lane in the village of Burnham Thorpe, Norfolk.
