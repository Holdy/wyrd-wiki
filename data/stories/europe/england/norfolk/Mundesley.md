#title
The Long Coastguardsman of Mundesley and Bacton Cliffs

#summary
A betrayed 18th-century coastguardsman, hacked to pieces by smugglers on the North Norfolk coast after being double-crossed, eternally patrols the dark cliffs between Bacton and Mundesley.

#details
During the late 18th century, the lawless, isolated North Norfolk coastline was heavily targeted by smugglers bringing in illicit luxuries such as lace, silk, brandy, and tea. A coastguardsman tasked with watching this treacherous stretch believed he had made a breakthrough when a local informer promised to tip him off about an incoming smuggling ship. Instead, the informer double-crossed him, leading the coastguardsman straight into an ambush by a ruthless gang of smugglers. Believing his backup troops were right behind him, the coastguardsman charged forward, only to be overpowered, hacked to pieces, and flung over the cliff edge into the sea. Bound to the spot of his violent death, his ghost—referred to in historical records as the Long Coastguardsman—has haunted the lonely cliffs between Bacton and Mundesley for centuries, striding purposefully through the dark, screaming into the wind, and reliving his final patrol.

#location
The lonely cliff-edge and coastal path between Bacton and Mundesley in Norfolk, UK.

#upstream-source
