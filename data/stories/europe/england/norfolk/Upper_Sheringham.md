#title
The Mermaid of All Saints Church

#summary
Lured from the sea by the sound of church bells and singing, a mermaid dragged herself across the land to Upper Sheringham, where she was initially turned away before sneaking back inside to remain carved in wood forever.

#details
Drawn from her watery depths by the sweet sound of human voices singing and church bells ringing during a service, a mermaid left the sea off Sheringham and laboriously hauled herself more than a mile across the pebble beach and rough terrain up to All Saints Church in Upper Sheringham. When she reached the church door and asked for entry, the horrified beadle slammed the door in her face, exclaiming that they could have no mermaids inside. However, after the congregation dispersed, the determined sea nymph managed to slip back into the church unnoticed. Immortalised for posterity, she can still be found today carved into the bench end of the 15th-century pews closest to the north door.

#location
All Saints Church, Upper Sheringham, Norfolk (Grid reference TG144418).

#upstream-source
A. C. Erroll, A History of the Parishes of Sheringham & Beeston Regis.

#title
The Sheringham Mermaid

#summary
Drawn inland by the sound of church singing, a mermaid was barred from entering a village church before managing to sneak inside. Her presence is commemorated to this day by a carved wooden bench end.

#details
Lured by the sweet symphony of hymns, a mermaid dragged herself over a mile inland from the seashore up to All Saints Church in Upper Sheringham. When she reached the church doors, the beadle rudely slammed them in her face, declaring that sea creatures had no souls and were unwelcome in God's house. Undeterred, the mermaid managed to slip past the church officials and crept into the back of the building. Her enduring spirit is immortalized by a carved wooden bench end located near the north door.

#location
All Saints Church is located in Upper Sheringham, Norfolk (grid reference TG144418).
