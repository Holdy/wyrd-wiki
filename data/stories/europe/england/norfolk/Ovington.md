#title
The Breakfast-Triggered Bomber of Ovington

#summary
During World War II, a German bomber attacking RAF Watton was accidentally brought down over Ovington by an experimental cable defense system triggered by breakfasting gunners, leading to its dramatic capture by a local farmer and farm labourer armed with a shotgun.

#details
On the morning of February 18, 1941, a Heinkel He 111 bomber of the German Luftwaffe strafed RAF Watton before flying into a forest of steel cables deployed by a Parachute and Cable (PAC) defense system. The PAC rockets had been fired accidentally when the crew of gunpost "F Freddy"—located just behind the local aerolite garage—scrambled off the roof where they were eating breakfast to avoid enemy fire. Heavily damaged by the steel cables, the aircraft crash-landed in a wheat field in Ovington. Local farmer George Dudley Bowes and farm labourer Frank Wilfred Warnes immediately rushed across the fields to investigate. Armed with a 12-bore shotgun and a pitchfork, Bowes fired a warning shot into the air, prompting the five startled German crew members to surrender. For their bravery in apprehending the armed airmen, Bowes and Warnes received formal commendations from King George VI in the London Gazette. The pilot, Heinrich Busch, survived capture and was transferred to a prisoner of war camp in Canada, though his life later ended tragically in 1944 when he was executed for his role in the killing of a suspected informer within the camp.

#location
The incident centered around a wheat field in Ovington, Norfolk, located near the southern boundary of RAF Watton.
