    The Miraculous Construction of the Holy House and the Slipper Chapel

    Summary

    In 1061, angels miraculously built a replica of the house of Nazareth after a Saxon noblewoman's carpenters failed, leading centuries later to the establishment of the Slipper Chapel in Houghton St Giles where pilgrims discarded their shoes to walk the final holy mile. Countless miraculous healings followed as travelers journeyed barefoot to the shrine.

    Details

    In 1061, a virtuous Saxon lady of the manor named Richeldis de Faverches prayed to the Virgin Mary asking how she could best honor her. In response, Mary appeared to her in visions and transported her spiritually to Nazareth, showing her the Holy House where the Annunciation took place and asking her to build an exact replica in Walsingham. Richeldis marked out a site in a local meadow with the help of heavenly dew, but when master carpenters began construction, their measurements mysteriously failed, and no pieces would fit together. Distressed by the setback, Richeldis spent the entire night in fervent prayer. Overnight, angels descended upon the site, completing the construction flawlessly and positioning the building 200 feet away next to a spring of heavenly healing water. Centuries later in 1325, the Slipper Chapel was built in the nearby hamlet of Houghton St Giles, marking the spot where medieval monarchs and pilgrims left their shoes behind to walk the final holy mile barefoot to the shrine.
