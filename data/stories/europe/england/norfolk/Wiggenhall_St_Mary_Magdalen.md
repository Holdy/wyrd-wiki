#title
The Shucky Dog of Magdalen Fen

#summary
During a severe winter in Wiggenhall St. Mary Magdalen, two men found their acquaintance dead of sheer terror in his cottage after noticing dog paw prints leading up to his door with none leading away, linked to the legend of the phantom Shucky Dog.

#details
In about 1865, during a severe winter in Wiggenhall St. Mary Magdalen, two local men visited the cottage of their acquaintance Abe Mindham in Magdalen Fen to check on him because he had not been seen for several days. Walking through heavy snow, they noticed dog paw prints leading right up to Mindham's door, but none leading away from it. Inside, they discovered his dead body on the floor, cold and rigid, with his eyes wide and staring in absolute horror. Local tradition held that Magdalen's Shucky Dog—a great black phantom beast said to gallop across the fen fields on moonless nights, accompanied only by the rattling of a broken chain—had approached his door, causing Mindham to die of sheer fright when he opened it.

#location
A cottage in Magdalen Fen within the parish of Wiggenhall St. Mary Magdalen, Norfolk.

#title
The Shucky Dog of Magdalen Fen

#summary
Two villagers checking on an isolated fen resident during a harsh winter discovered him dead of sheer terror after finding solitary paw prints leading right up to his cottage door.

#details
During a severe winter around 1865, two men walked out to the remote cottage of Abe Mindham in Magdalen Fen to check on his welfare. Upon arriving in the heavy snow, they noticed a single set of dog paw prints leading directly to his front door, with no prints leading back away from it. Entering the dwelling, they found Mindham's body lying on the floor, cold and rigid, with his eyes frozen in an expression of absolute horror. Drawing upon local folklore of the Shucky Dog—a massive black hound said to gallop across the fen fields on moonless nights dragging a broken chain—they concluded that Mindham had opened his door to the spectral beast and died instantly of fright.

#location
Abe Mindham's cottage situated in Magdalen Fen within the parish of Wiggenhall St Mary Magdalen, Norfolk.
