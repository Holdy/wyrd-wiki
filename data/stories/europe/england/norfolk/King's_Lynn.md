#title
The Haunting of the Exorcist's House

#summary
Following the death of its eccentric former occupant, a 17th-century cottage in King's Lynn became haunted by the apparition of his late wife. Subsequent residents of the historic home have reported sighting her ghost in the living room.

#details
Situated next to St Nicholas' Chapel at 7 Chapel Lane, a 17th-century building known locally as the "Exorcist's House" has long held a reputation for the supernatural. For many years, the property was home to the eccentric writer and broadcaster F. R. Buckley, who cultivated a local reputation as an expert on the occult and black magic. After Buckley passed away in 1976, new occupants reported encounters with a ghost in the cottage's living room, which was believed to be the spirit of Buckley's second wife, who had died in America on the exact same day. Earlier inhabitants also reported sightings of a mysterious male figure within the house.

#location
The story takes place at The Exorcist's House, located at 7 Chapel Lane in King's Lynn, directly adjacent to St Nicholas' Chapel.

#title
The Witch's Heart of Tuesday Market Place

#summary
When a woman was burned at the stake for witchcraft in 1590, her heart allegedly burst from her chest, struck a building in King's Lynn's Tuesday Market Place, and bounced away toward the River Ouse.

#details
In 1590, a local woman named Margaret Read was accused and convicted of witchcraft in the historic port town of King's Lynn. Her sentence was brutal: she was condemned to be burned alive at the stake in the public Tuesday Market Place. According to local folklore, as the flames consumed her body, her heart burst from her chest with supernatural force. The blazing organ flew through the air and struck the brickwork of a 16th-century building on the north side of the square, leaving an imprint on the wall before falling to the cobbles and bouncing down toward the River Ouse, where the water bubbled and churned as the heart sank beneath the surface. To this day, a diamond shape enclosing a carved heart remains visible in the red brickwork of buildings 15 and 16, marking the spot where the legend says the witch's heart hit the wall.

#location
The exterior brickwork of buildings 15 and 16 on the north side of the Tuesday Market Place in King's Lynn, Norfolk.
