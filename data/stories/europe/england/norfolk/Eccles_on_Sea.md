#title
The Lost Church and Risen Skeletons of Eccles-on-Sea

#summary
Swallowed by the North Sea, the medieval village and church of Eccles-on-Sea occasionally reappear after violent storms, exposing ancient graves where sea-bleached skeletons are washed clean by the tide.

#details
The ancient village of Eccles-on-Sea and its 12th-century St Mary’s Church were gradually engulfed by the advancing North Sea over centuries, leaving only the church tower standing as a solitary landmark known as the "Lonely Sentinel" on the foreshore. After severe gales and scouring tides, the shifting sands would periodically reveal the ruins of the church, medieval wells, cart tracks, and the old churchyard. Local accounts and historical records—such as those recounted by author Lilias Rider Haggard—noted the eerie spectacle of north-easterly gales stripping away the sand to expose the long outlines of graves, with clay-embedded skeletons and hollow-eyed skulls gazing up at the sky. Alongside the uncovered graveyard, maritime lore also told of phantom bells tolling from beneath the waves near the lost church before storms. Although the tower finally collapsed during a fierce gale in January 1895 and is now buried deep beneath the sand and protective sea defenses, the legend of the lost church and its restless dead remains a potent piece of Norfolk folklore.

#location
The foreshore and sand dunes where St Mary’s Church once stood in Eccles-on-Sea, Norfolk.
