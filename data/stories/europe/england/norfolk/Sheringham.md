#title
The Phantom Hound of Sheringham Road

#summary
On a clear summer evening, a young Sheringham farmer watched the legendary Black Shuck cross a country road in front of him and pass straight through a closed five-barred gate.

#details
During a 1933 BBC radio broadcast titled *The Dark Shore*, listeners were presented with accounts of East Anglian folklore, including testimony from a young farmer living in Sheringham, Norfolk. He reported that while out on a clear summer evening, he saw the notorious Black Shuck cross the road directly in front of him. To his astonishment, the gigantic spectral hound did not break stride or jump the barrier, but instead walked straight through a closed five-barred gate as if it were made of mist.

#location
A country road on the outskirts of Sheringham, Norfolk, marked by a traditional five-barred field gate.

#upstream-source
G. McEwan: 'Mystery Animals of Britain and Ireland' (Hale, 1986); James Wentworth Day: 'Black Shuck', in East Anglian Magazine, Vol. 21, No. 11 (Sept. 1962).

#title
The Sheringham Mermaid

#summary
A mermaid drawn ashore by the sound of church singing was initially turned away by the church beadle, but eventually crept inside to take her place on a 15th-century pew end.

#details
Legend has it that a mermaid was drawn from the sea at Lower Sheringham, traveling more than a mile inland to All Saints Church in Upper Sheringham after hearing heavenly singing. When she arrived while a church service was in full swing, the church beadle was horrified and slammed the door in her face, declaring that mermaids had no place in the house of God. Undeterred by the beadle's rejection, the sea princess quietly crept into the church once she was able and settled on the bench end of the pew closest to the north door, where her likeness remains carved in wood to this day.

#location
All Saints Church in Upper Sheringham, Norfolk, UK.
