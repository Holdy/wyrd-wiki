#title
Elizabeth Fyson, the Witch of Holme Hale

#summary
Elizabeth Fyson was an elderly Norfolk doctress and reputed witch living near Ashill who married a much younger miller at age eighty, only to lose her life savings to his failed milling ventures.

#details
Elizabeth Fyson lived in Holme Hale, just two miles north of Ashill, Norfolk. Celebrated throughout the region as a skilled herbalist and doctress who successfully treated dropsy and other ailments, she also carried a formidable local reputation as a witch. In December 1799, at the age of eighty-two, she married twenty-four-year-old miller Richard Parfrey—who had been born and baptized in neighboring Ashill. Their unusual union caused a sensation, celebrated with great festivity and punch-giving across the community. However, Richard proceeded to spend her accumulated savings of £500 to £1,000 on building a windmill and attempting a watermill. When these ventures failed, Elizabeth's money was entirely gone, and she spent her final years in poverty and want, passing away in 1803 at the age of eighty-five. Nineteenth-century memoirs and letters published in *Notes and Queries* preserved memories of her eccentric habits—such as oiling her shoes while still on her feet—and her enduring local legacy as a woman believed to possess witchcraft.

#location
The villages of Ashill and Holme Hale in Norfolk, United Kingdom, where Elizabeth Fyson lived and her husband Richard Parfrey was born and baptized.
