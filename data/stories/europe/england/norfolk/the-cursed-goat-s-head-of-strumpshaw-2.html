The Cursed Goat's Head of Strumpshaw

Summary

A mounted goat's head brought to a Norfolk pub in 1908 unleashed decades of poltergeist activity, tragic accidents, and bizarre hauntings that persisted even after repeated attempts to destroy or banish the relic.

Details

In 1908, Mrs. Newton, the wife of the landlord at The Goat Inn in Strumpshaw, bought a magnificent white goat from an itinerant pedlar. After the goat was slaughtered, its preserved head—complete with long horns and piercing black-and-hazel eyes—was mounted behind the bar. Decades later, under landlord Frank Walpole, the pub became plagued by inexplicable phenomena, including mirrors flying off walls, a piano playing by itself, phantom figures, and a fatal car crash suffered by a teenager who had touched the head. Believing the relic was cursed or haunted, Walpole repeatedly removed it, throwing it into the River Yare and burying it in a gravel pit, but the head continually resurfaced or caused new misfortunes wherever it went, cementing its chilling legacy in local folklore.
