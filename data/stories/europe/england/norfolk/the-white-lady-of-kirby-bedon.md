#title
The White Lady of Kirby Bedon

#summary
A ghostly rider, the White Lady, is said to guard two churches in Kirby Bedon, Norfolk, riding a white horse along an ancient ley line.

#details
In the village of Kirby Bedon, Norfolk, two churches stand side by side: St. Andrew's and the ruins of St. Mary's. Both are reputed to be haunted by the same ghostly figure—a White Lady riding a white horse. This spectral rider is believed to guard the ley lines that intersect at these churches, which are thought to carry supernatural energy.

The legend of the White Lady is part of a broader folklore tradition where such apparitions are often associated with tragic events and unrequited love. The ghostly figure is described as a very tall woman in white, riding slowly around the churchyards. The story does not specify who the White Lady is or what she guards, but it hints at a connection to the medieval shroud brasses found in St. Andrew's Church, which depict William Dussyng and his wife in their winding sheets.

#location
The two churches of Kirby Bedon, Norfolk, are located on either side of the road that runs through the village.
