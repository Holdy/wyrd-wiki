#title
The Burnham Market Poisoners

#summary
In the 1830s, two adulterous neighbors in Burnham Market conspired to murder their rivals using arsenic-laced food, a crime linked to local witchcraft associations that allegedly left their ghosts haunting North Street.

#details
In the 1830s in the north Norfolk village of Burnham Market, neighbors Frances "Fanny" Billing and Catherine "Kate" Frary became notorious for their scandalous affairs and local gossip. Kate Frary, who was known to associate with local fortune-tellers and witches, joined forces with Fanny in a deadly murder plot. After Fanny's husband discovered her affair with Peter Taylor, the women began eliminating those in their way using arsenic. They poisoned Kate's husband, Robert, and baby Harriet Southgate with tainted drinks and food. Shortly after, they laced a supper of dumplings and gravy with arsenic for Mary Taylor, the wife of Fanny's lover. Mary died in agony, and an investigation by the local pharmacist revealed her stomach was riddled with arsenic. Arrested and tried at the Norwich Assizes, Fanny and Kate were found guilty and hanged before a massive crowd in August 1834. To this day, local residents claim that North Street in Burnham Market remains haunted by the vengeful ghosts of the two women.

#location
North Street in the village of Burnham Market, Norfolk, marks the former neighborhood setting where the conspirators lived and carried out their crimes.

The Dumpling Poisoners of Burnham Market

#summary
Two 19th-century women committed a series of murders by slipping arsenic into food and drink. Following their public execution, their vengeful spirits were said to haunt North Street in Burnham Market.

#details

Ever since, locals have reported that the spirits of the two poisoners continue to haunt North Street, leaving an eerie atmosphere behind.

#title
The Poisoners of North Street

#summary
In the 1830s, two Norfolk women murdered family members and neighbors using arsenic, giving rise to local folklore that their vengeful spirits still haunt Burnham Market.

#details
During the 1830s in the north Norfolk village of Burnham Market (then called Burnham Westgate), two neighbors, Frances "Fanny" Billing and Catherine "Kate" Frary, became notorious for a series of murders. Amid local gossip regarding illicit affairs, Catherine's husband Robert and a neighbor's baby fell violently ill and died after consuming poisoned food and drink. Shortly after, Mary Taylor—whose husband was romantically involved with Fanny—was killed after arsenic was slipped into her evening dumplings and gravy. A coroner's inquest and medical examination confirmed lethal levels of arsenic in the victims' stomachs. Billing and Frary were arrested, tried, found guilty, and publicly executed in Norwich in 1835 before massive crowds. Their dark legacy persisted in local lore, with modern villagers claiming that the two female poisoners haunt North Street, making the blood of anyone walking the area run cold.

#location
North Street in the village of Burnham Market, Norfolk, is said to be haunted by the spirits of the two executed women.
