#title
The Geese and Turkey Drovers of Ashby St Mary

#summary
In the churchyard of St Mary the Virgin in Ashby St Mary, unique 19th-century headstones commemorate local farmers George and Ann Basey, preserving the regional folk tradition of driving massive flocks of turkeys and geese on foot all the way to London markets.

#details
At St Mary the Virgin Church in the Norfolk village of Ashby St Mary, two unusual headstones stand immediately beside the footpath leading to the south porch. They mark the graves of local farmers George Basey (who died in 1876) and his wife Ann Basey (who died in 1868). Uniquely, the stone reliefs on their memorials depict the couple surrounded by their respective flocks—George with his turkeys and Ann with her geese. 

These carvings preserve a remarkable piece of local folk history and rural tradition. In the 19th century and earlier, East Anglia was famed for the domestication and rearing of poultry. Every autumn leading up to Christmas, drovers from villages like Ashby St Mary would gather massive droves of turkeys and geese and drive them on foot along the roads all the way to Smithfield Market in London. Travelling at a pace of about ten miles a day, these skilled drovers guided the birds in great waddling processions to ensure they arrived in prime condition for the London Christmas tables, where Norfolk poultry commanded the highest prices. The enduring local memory of this arduous practice and the Baseys' trade is celebrated today, with the image of Ann Basey feeding her geese immortalized on the Ashby St Mary village sign.

#location
The churchyard of St Mary the Virgin Church on the village street in Ashby St Mary, Norfolk, UK.
