#title
The Battle Pit of Stanford

#summary
A local tradition recorded in the early 20th century held that a desolate pit near Stanford was the site of a fierce historic battle against the Danes. Ordnance Survey maps from the period marked the exact location along the road to Thetford.

#details
In 1909, an elderly man from the neighbouring village of Thompson shared a local legend with folklorist W. G. Clarke regarding a depression or pit situated roughly half a mile from the old Cock Inn in Stanford. According to the oral tradition handed down in the area, this pit marked the scene of a desperate engagement fought during the ancient wars between the English and the Danes. Historical Ordnance Survey maps up to 1905 officially recorded a pit at this location along the road heading toward Thetford. The surrounding village of Stanford was later evacuated and subsumed into the Stanford Training Area during the Second World War, leaving the ancient landscape deserted.

#location
A pit located approximately half a mile southeast of the former Cock Inn site along the road toward Thetford within the deserted parish of Stanford, Norfolk (around grid reference TL855938).
