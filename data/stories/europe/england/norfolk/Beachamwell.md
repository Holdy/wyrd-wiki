#title
Diana and Her Spectral Hounds

#summary
The isolated ruins of All Saints church are said to be haunted by the ghostly apparition of Diana accompanied by supernatural hounds. This haunting stems from local folklore connecting the site to ancient pagan deities and phantom packs roaming the countryside.

#details
According to local tradition, the crumbling ruins of All Saints church in Beachamwell are haunted by the apparition of the goddess Diana and her pack of dogs. This eerie belief developed from 19th-century antiquarian speculations that pagan temples dedicated to the sun and various goddesses once stood in the area. Consequently, the surrounding plantations and church ruins became reputed to be haunted by spectral hounds known as Bel's dogs.

#location
The key location is the ruins of All Saints Church, situated in Beachamwell, Norfolk (OS Grid Reference TF749046).

#title
The Devil's Ditch and Hangour Hill

#summary
According to local legend, the ancient linear earthwork known as the Bichamditch was dug by the Devil, who created the nearby round barrow of Hangour Hill by scraping the leftover earth from his spade.

#details
In the parish of Beachamwell, Norfolk, a long prehistoric or early Saxon linear earthwork known as the Bichamditch—frequently referred to locally as the Devil's Ditch—cuts across the countryside. According to enduring folk tradition, the ditch was excavated by the Devil himself using supernatural powers. When he finished digging the massive trench, he cleaned his spade by scraping the excess soil onto a single spot, thereby forming the prominent round barrow known as Hangour Hill. Local lore further maintains that hidden deep beneath the earth of the mound lies a pair of mysterious silver gates.

#location
Hangour Hill is a round barrow located at grid reference TF752087 in the parish of Beachamwell, Norfolk.

#title
The Devil's Spadeful at Hangour Hill

#summary
The Devil created Hangour Hill by shaking excess earth from his spade after digging the ancient Bichamditch. Beneath this barrow, a mysterious pair of silver gates is said to be hidden.

#details
According to local tradition, the Devil took up a spade and carved out the linear earthwork known as the Bichamditch, or Devil's Ditch, in Beachamwell. Upon finishing his excavation, he shook the leftover earth from his spade, which formed the prominent round barrow known as Hangour Hill. Folklore adds that deep beneath this earthen mound, a hidden pair of silver gates remains buried out of sight.

#location
Hangour Hill is a round barrow located at grid reference TF752087 in Beachamwell, Norfolk.
