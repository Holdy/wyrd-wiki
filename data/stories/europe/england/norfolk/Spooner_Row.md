#title
The Hanging of Dinah Bunting

#summary
According to local Norfolk folklore, Dinah's Lane in Spooner Row derives its name from a woman named Dinah Bunting who was hanged from a tree for theft.

#details
Local folklore in the Norfolk village of Spooner Row tells the grim tale of Dinah Bunting, a local woman after whom Dinah's Lane (historically also known as Wick Lane or Bunting Lane) is traditionally believed to be named. Situated near the medieval moated site of Wick Hall, the country lane is associated with oral traditions recorded by local historians and the Wymondham Heritage Society, which state that Dinah was condemned and hanged from a tree alongside the lane for the crime of stealing.

#location
Dinah's Lane, running near the site of Wick Hall in Spooner Row, Norfolk.

Information supplied by Wymondham Heritage Society.

#title
The Phantom of Dinah's Lane

#summary
Local folklore recalls the tragic fate of Dinah Bunting, a woman who was hanged from a tree for theft and whose name endures along a quiet lane in Spooner Row. Her lingering spirit is said to haunt the route where she met her execution.

#details
According to traditional folklore preserved by the Wymondham Heritage Society, Dinah Bunting was accused of stealing and condemned to death. As punishment, she was hanged from a tree in the area that is now known as Dinah's Lane in Spooner Row. Over generations, locals have passed down tales of her execution and reports of her ghostly presence lingering among the trees along the lane.

#location
Dinah's Lane is a specific rural track located within the village of Spooner Row in Norfolk.
