The Lantern Man of Thurlton Marsh

Summary

In August 1809, a Norfolk wherryman named Joseph Bexfield ignored warnings from his companions and left the White Horse Inn in Thurlton, only to be lured to his death in the marshes by a mysterious glowing Lantern Man. His body was recovered three days later and buried in the village churchyard.

Details

On the night of August 11, 1809, Joseph Bexfield, a wherryman who sailed his barge between Norwich and Yarmouth, was drinking with fellow sailors at the White Horse Inn at Thurlton Staithe. Glancing outside into the pitch-dark evening, Joseph spotted a bobbing light out on Thurlton Marsh and noticed a small figure scurrying about with a lantern. Assuming someone was tampering with the moored wherrys, he prepared to go investigate. His companions urgently begged him to stay, warning him of the Lantern Men—malevolent spirits that lure unwary travellers into treacherous quagmires and watery graves. Brushing off their warnings, Joseph headed out into the night to retrieve a package he had left behind for his wife. He never returned alive. Three days later, his waterlogged body washed ashore near his usual mooring. To this day, locals recount how the deceptive lights of the marsh claimed him, and his grave remains in the churchyard of All Saints' Church in Thurlton.
