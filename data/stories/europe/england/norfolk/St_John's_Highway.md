#title
The Legend of Tom Hickathrift and the Giant's Grave

#summary
In the Norfolk fenland, local folklore commemorates Tom Hickathrift, an immensely strong legendary hero said to be buried beneath an ancient earthen mound at the crossroads known as Hickathrift's Corner. Generations of fen dwellers maintained that this site held the giant's remains alongside his formidable weapons, protected by ancient tradition and stone markers.

#details
Long before the Norman Conquest, the marshlands of western Norfolk—specifically the Smeeth between King's Lynn and Wisbech—were home to Tom Hickathrift, a man of gigantic stature and astonishing strength who defended the common grazing lands of the Seven Towns of Marshland. According to local tradition, at the centre of the Smeeth where the roads cross at Hickathrift's Corner, there once stood an earthen burial mound known as the "Giant's Grave." Fen residents related that beneath this mound lay either a slain ogre or Tom himself, buried alongside the cart wheel and axletree that served as his traditional weapons. Atop the mound stood an ancient stone cross affectionately dubbed "Hickathrift's Candlestick" or "Collar-stud." Nearby depressions in Hickathrift's Field were pointed out as the giant's bath and feeding bowl, while local lore even spoke of mysterious slates bearing a protective curse warning any who dared to threaten or besiege the fen. Though the mound was later leveled and the surviving cross fragment eventually incorporated into the base of the village sign, the legend of Tom Hickathrift remains deeply rooted in the landscape of Marshland St. James and St John's Highway.

#location
Hickathrift's Corner and Hickathrift's Field are located at the crossroads in the center of Marshland St. James, just south of St John's Highway in West Norfolk.
