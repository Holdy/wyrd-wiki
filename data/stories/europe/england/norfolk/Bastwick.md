#title
The Devil's Bride of Bastwick

#summary
On her wedding night in 1741, a young bride was snatched from the doors of Bastwick by the devil in a skeleton-driven coach that ultimately plunged into the river at Potter Heigham Bridge.

#details
In 1741, Lady Evelyn married Sir Godfrey Haslitt, a union made possible by a love potion that her mother, Lady Carew, had obtained from a witch. On the night of their wedding feast, a terrifying coach driven by a skeleton thundered up to the hall doors in Bastwick. The devil himself leapt out, grabbed the bride, and forced her inside before speeding away into the darkness. Sir Godfrey and his wedding guests frantically mounted their horses and gave chase. As the demonic carriage attempted to cross Potter Heigham Bridge, it burst into blazing flames and vanished completely into the water beneath, leaving neither wreckage nor trace behind.

#location
Bastwick Hall in the Norfolk Broads and Potter Heigham Bridge spanning the River Thurne.

#title
The Devil's Wedding Toll

#summary
On her wedding night in Bastwick, a young bride is abducted by the devil in a fiery, skeleton-driven coach as payment for a magical love potion.

#details
Lady Carew enlisted a local witch to help her daughter, Evelyn, win the hand of the wealthy bachelor Sir Godfrey Haslitt of Bastwick. The witch provided a potent love potion in exchange for a solemn vow that whatever she later demanded would be granted. Following Sir Godfrey and Evelyn's wedding on May 31, 1741, and their return to Bastwick for the reception, a skeleton-driven coach thundered up to the hall doors at midnight. The devil leapt from the vehicle, seized the bride, and sped off toward Potter Heigham. Though pursued by wedding guests, the coach burst into flames and plunged into the river at Potter Heigham bridge, claiming Lady Evelyn as the witch's ultimate price.

#title
The Phantom Coach of Bastwick

#summary
Following a bargain struck with a witch for a love potion, a newlywed bride is abducted from her wedding reception in Bastwick by the devil in a skeleton-driven coach. The fiery carriage ultimately plunges into the water at Potter Heigham bridge.

#details
In 1741, Lady Carew sought the help of a local witch to ensure her daughter, Lady Evelyn, could win the affections of the wealthy bachelor Sir Godfrey Haslitt of Bastwick. In exchange for the potent love potion, Lady Carew swore that whatever the witch later requested would be granted. The potion worked, and Sir Godfrey and Evelyn were married on May 31, 1741. However, when the wedding party returned to Bastwick for the evening celebration, a skeleton-driven coach thundered up to the hall doors at midnight. The devil leapt out, seized the bride, and whisked her away toward Potter Heigham. As the coach reached Potter Heigham bridge, it burst into flames and plunged into the river with Lady Evelyn still inside, fulfilling the witch's grim price. It is said that on every anniversary of May 31st, the sound of hoofbeats and screeching wheels can be heard before a fiery phantom coach appears over the bridge and plunges into the water.

#location
Potter Heigham Bridge is an ancient stone bridge crossing the River Thurne in Norfolk where the phantom coach famously plunges into the water.

#title
The Witch's Wicked Love Potion

#summary
To secure a wealthy bachelor's hand in marriage for her daughter, an ambitious mother enlisted a local witch to brew a potent love potion, triggering a horrifying supernatural reckoning on the wedding night.

#details
In 1741, Lady Carew of Potter Heigham was determined that her daughter, Evelyn, should marry the astoundingly wealthy Sir Godfrey Haslitt of Bastwick. When Sir Godfrey showed no interest in Evelyn, Lady Carew turned to a local witch who brewed a powerful love potion. Refusing monetary payment, the sorceress demanded instead that if the potion worked, Lady Carew must grant her whatever she subsequently asked for. 

The charmed liquid was secretly slipped into Sir Godfrey's drink during a hunting party in King's Lynn. Instantly, Sir Godfrey fell madly in love, and he and Evelyn were married on May 31, 1741, in Norwich before returning to Bastwick for the wedding reception. However, as the clock struck midnight, the doors flew open to reveal a grinning skeleton. The spectral figure seized the bride in its boney arms, flung her into a carriage, and galloped off toward Potter Heigham. The wedding party gave chase, but as the coach reached Potter Heigham Bridge, it burst into flames and plunged into the river, claiming Lady Evelyn as the dreadful payment demanded by the witch.

#location
Sir Godfrey Haslitt's estate was located in the village of Bastwick within the civil parish of Repps with Bastwick in Norfolk, England.
