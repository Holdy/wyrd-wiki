#title
The Golden Gates of Hautbois Castle

#summary
During a period of medieval unrest, the owner of Hautbois Castle cast the estate's golden gates into a nearby bottomless pond for safekeeping, where they are said to remain hidden to this day.

#details
In the marshes beside the River Bure within the parish of Coltishall lie the sparse earthwork remains of Hautbois Castle, originally built in 1312. Local tradition relates that when trouble threatened the manor, the owner had the castle's solid golden gates removed and dropped into a nearby pond until the danger passed. Because the pool was reputed to be bottomless, the precious gates could never be retrieved and continue to rest in the depths of what is now known as Golden Gates Pond.

#location
Golden Gates Pond, located beside the Great Hautbois Road (grid reference TG261207) in the parish of Coltishall, Norfolk.

'The Marlpit' Community News Magazine, Aug-Sept 2002.

#title
The Phantom Hound of Coltishall Bridge

#summary
A massive, silent black hound known as Black Shuck—often considered a manifestation of the devil—terrorises the bridge and high street of Coltishall as an ominous spectral presence. Two RAF officers famously encountered the phantom beast before it mysteriously vanished into thin air on the village cobbles.

#details
For centuries, the Norfolk village of Coltishall has been haunted by legends of Black Shuck, a terrifying phantom hound viewed by locals as either the devil's dog or the devil himself. Described as a silent beast as large as a calf with blazing red eyes, Shuck is frequently sighted prowling Coltishall Bridge at the upper reaches of the River Bure. In addition to serving as a foreboding omen of death, the creature startled two Royal Air Force officers when it appeared directly on the cobbles of Coltishall High Street before completely vanishing into thin air.

#location
Coltishall Bridge spans the River Bure in the Norfolk village of Coltishall, UK.

#title
The Phantom Hound of Coltishall Bridge

#summary
Multiple residents and travellers have reported encountering a massive, silent black dog with glowing eyes lurking around Coltishall Bridge after dark. Over the decades, terrified witnesses have watched the monstrous phantom hound materialize, cross their path, and vanish into thin air.

#details
For generations, local folklore has linked the terrifying phantom hound Black Shuck with Coltishall Bridge over the River Bure. Throughout the 20th century, several specific encounters were recorded at this location. In the 1930s, a middle-aged couple stopping to light a cigarette watched a noiseless dog as big as a calf pass within a foot of them. In the early 1950s, a young couple walking from Coltishall Island encountered a pony-sized black dog that looked at them before vanishing as it walked. Later in the early 1960s, two RAF officers driving back to RAF Coltishall were forced to brake sharply when a massive, labrador-like hound roughly 53 inches tall crossed the road toward Coltishall High Street before abruptly disappearing.

#location
Coltishall Bridge crosses the upper reaches of the River Bure in the village of Coltishall, Norfolk.

#title
The Phantom Hound of Coltishall Bridge

#summary
Black Shuck, the legendary ghostly black hound of East Anglia, is frequently sighted as a silent, massive beast near Coltishall Bridge and vanishing into thin air on Coltishall High Street.

#details
Black Shuck, the terrifying spectral hound of East Anglia, is said to haunt the upper reaches of the River Bure at Coltishall Bridge, appearing variously as a headless beast or a silent creature as big as a calf. Sightings of the phantom dog persisted well into the 20th century, notably when two RAF officers in the 1960s were stunned to encounter a large black dog on the cobbles of Coltishall High Street, only for the apparition to completely vanish into thin air.
