#title
Owd Rugman of Attlebridge

#summary
A shaggy phantom hound known as Owd Rugman haunted the rural lanes and villages of Attlebridge and Lenwade during the late nineteenth century. Local residents reported numerous frightening encounters with this spectral beast as it roamed the countryside.

#details
During the late 19th century, sightings of a rare local phantom hound named Owd Rugman reached a peak in the Attlebridge area. Described as a shaggy, rough-coated spectral dog with a distinct inland habitat across Attlebridge, Lenwade, and Lyng, this ghostly creature terrorized travelers navigating the dark rural tracks and lanes of the Norfolk countryside.

#location
St. Andrew's Church (TG130170) in Attlebridge sits near the River Wensum within the rural area haunted by the spectral hound.

#title
Owd Rugman's Prowl

#summary
A giant, silent-footed spectral dog with a single fiery eye in the center of its head roams the dark lanes and isolated footpaths of Attlebridge. Encountering this terrifying phantom serves as a fatal omen that death will occur before the year is out.

#details
Referred to locally as Owd Rugman (or Owd Rugusan), this elusive regional hell hound is said to frequent the lonely lanes and field footpaths around Attlebridge, Lenwade, and Lyng. Described as a massive, shaggy black beast, its footfalls make absolutely no sound, though its eerie howls can freeze a traveler's blood. Its most distinctive and frightening feature is its single fiery eye positioned directly in the middle of its head, resembling a Cyclops. According to local folklore, anyone unfortunate enough to catch sight of Owd Rugman receives an infallible warning that they will die before the end of the year, leading locals to shut their eyes tightly at the sound of any suspicious howling in the night.

#location
The dark country lanes and isolated field footpaths around the village of Attlebridge in Norfolk, UK.

#upstream-source
William A. Dutt, *Highways and Byways in East Anglia* (1901) / *The Norfolk Broads* (1903).

#title
The Silver Bells of the River Wensum

#summary
During the English Civil War, two silver bells were removed from St. Andrew's Church and submerged in the nearby river Wensum to conceal them from advancing Parliamentarian forces. To this day, the hidden treasure remains lost beneath the riverbed.

#details
As Oliver Cromwell's troops marched through Norfolk during the English Civil War, local clergy and parishioners feared for the safety of their church valuables. To prevent the two precious silver bells of St. Andrew's Church from being seized and melted down, they were spirited away from the church tower. Under the cover of secrecy, the bells were taken down to the banks of the nearby River Wensum and buried deep within its muddy bed near Attlebridge. Though the threat of Cromwell's forces eventually passed, the exact resting place of the submerged bells was forgotten, leaving them hidden beneath the flowing water.

#location
St. Andrew's Church and the adjacent stretch of the River Wensum in Attlebridge, Norfolk.
