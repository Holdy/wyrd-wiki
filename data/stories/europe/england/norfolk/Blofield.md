#title
The Duelling Knights of Blofield

#summary
Once a year, the ghostly apparitions of Sir Thomas Boleyn and Sir Thomas Paston are said to clash along the rural footpath between Blofield and Braydeston wielding burning swords. Accompanied by the spectral presence of Lady Anne Boleyn, this legendary feud reenacts itself in the Norfolk countryside.

#details
Rooted in the history of prominent Norfolk families from the 15th and 16th centuries, local folklore tells of a bitter, enduring feud between the Boleyn and Paston households. According to the legend, the ghosts of Sir Thomas Boleyn and Sir Thomas Paston return on a specific night each year to fight a fiery duel. The two knights clash with burning swords along the rural pathway connecting St Andrew and St Peter's Church in Blofield to St Michael's Church in neighboring Braydeston. Adding to the eerie spectacle, Lady Anne Boleyn is reportedly seen watching the combat from a carriage drawn by headless horses.

#location
The rural footpath connecting St Andrew and St Peter's Church in Blofield to St Michael's Church in Braydeston, Norfolk.

#title
The Spectral Duel of Blofield

#summary
Sir Thomas Boleyn and Sir Thomas Paston return to Blofield each year to engage in a fierce nocturnal duel with burning swords, watched over by Anne Boleyn from her carriage.

#details
As part of his enduring spectral penance across Norfolk, the headless ghost of Sir Thomas Boleyn—cursed for failing to defend his children Anne and George from execution—makes a yearly appearance in Blofield. Here, he meets Sir Thomas Paston for a violent midnight duel upon the ancient path running between Blofield's parish church and St Michael’s at Braydeston. The two knights clash with burning swords while Anne Boleyn watches from a coach pulled by headless horses, awaiting the moment they can ride away into the night.

#location
The spectral duel takes place along the historic path connecting St Andrew and St Peter’s Church in Blofield to St Michael’s at Braydeston.
