#title
Sir Thomas Boleyn’s Cursed Bridge Crossing

#summary
Cursed for failing to prevent the execution of his children, Sir Thomas Boleyn is doomed to drive a spectral carriage across twelve bridges between Wroxham and Blickling every anniversary of Anne's death.

#details
Sir Thomas Boleyn, father of the ill-fated Queen Anne Boleyn and Lord George Boleyn, stood by passively as both of his children were condemned and executed on charges including treason and witchcraft in 1536. According to local Norfolk folklore, his betrayal and cowardice brought upon him a severe supernatural curse. On the anniversary of Anne's execution each year, Thomas is condemned to drive a ghostly carriage drawn by headless horses across twelve bridges along the route between Wroxham and Blickling Hall. Struggling to guide the phantom team while holding his own severed head in his lap, he relives his eternal punishment across the Norfolk landscape.

#location
The road and bridges spanning between Wroxham and Blickling Hall in Norfolk, UK.

Alison Weir, The Lady in the Tower (Random House, 2009).

#title
The Curse of Langley Cross

#summary
A cursed stone cross brought disaster to a noble estate when it was moved from its original site near the abbey. True to the prophecy, Langley Hall was struck by fire shortly after the relocation.

#details
Langley Cross carried a traditional curse stating that if it were ever removed from its original site near the abbey, Langley Hall would be gutted by fire. In 1801, Lady Beauchamp disregarded the folklore and had the cross moved into the park grounds to mark a parish meeting-place. True to the warning, a destructive fire broke out near the estate around the same time, burning down the predecessor of Abbey Farm and causing a turret of the Hall to catch fire.

#location
Langley Cross stood near the abbey precincts at the corner of a wood known as The Thicks in Langley, Norfolk.

#title
The White Lady of Kirby Bedon

#summary
A ghostly rider, the White Lady, is said to guard two churches in Kirby Bedon, Norfolk, riding a white horse along an ancient ley line.

#details
In the village of Kirby Bedon, Norfolk, two churches stand side by side: St. Andrew's and the ruins of St. Mary's. Both are reputed to be haunted by the same ghostly figure—a White Lady riding a white horse. This spectral rider is believed to guard the ley lines that intersect at these churches, which are thought to carry supernatural energy.

The legend of the White Lady is part of a broader folklore tradition where such apparitions are often associated with tragic events and unrequited love. The ghostly figure is described as a very tall woman in white, riding slowly around the churchyards. The story does not specify who the White Lady is or what she guards, but it hints at a connection to the medieval shroud brasses found in St. Andrew's Church, which depict William Dussyng and his wife in their winding sheets.

#location
The two churches of Kirby Bedon, Norfolk, are located on either side of the road that runs through the village.
