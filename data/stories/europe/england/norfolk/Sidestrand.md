#title
The Garden of Sleep at Sidestrand

#summary
Perched on the eroding cliffs of Sidestrand, an old church graveyard known as the "Garden of Sleep" inspired Victorian poet Clement Scott before the relentless North Sea finally claimed its remaining tower and graves into the waves.

#details
In the late 19th century, Victorian journalist and writer Clement Scott visited the small Norfolk village of Sidestrand and stumbled upon the cliffside churchyard of St Michael and All Angels. Mesmerized by the sea of bright red poppies growing amongst the graves right on the precipice of the cliff, Scott dubbed the location "the Garden of Sleep" and immortalized it in a famous poem of the same name. Because the fragile glacial cliffs were rapidly eroding due to marine erosion, the main body of the church had already been dismantled and rebuilt further inland, leaving only the ancient tower and the clifftop graveyard behind. Local folklore and travellers long haunted the site, romanticising the peaceful resting place of the dead suspended between sky and sea. Despite efforts to preserve it, the relentless power of the North Sea proved unstoppable; in 1916, during a fierce storm, the solitary church tower finally toppled off the crumbling cliff edge and fell into the sea, taking parts of the graveyard with it into the depths below.

#location
The clifftop site of the former St Michael and All Angels Church tower and graveyard in Sidestrand, Norfolk, overlooking the North Sea.

#title
The Resident Hound of St. Martin's

#summary
For decades, the spectral black dog known as Black Shuck made the abandoned ruins of St. Martin's Church in Sidestrand its permanent home. Glowing with fiery eyes, the legendary beast haunted the clifftop churchyard until restoration efforts disturbed its lair in the early twentieth century.

#details
Local tradition holds that the terrifying East Anglian spectral hound, Black Shuck—described as a massive, coal-black dog with burning eyes—spent forty years residing within the abandoned ruins of St. Martin's Church in Sidestrand. Long after the church was abandoned to the elements on the eroding Norfolk cliffs, the ghostly hell-hound used the ruin as its sanctuary, remaining there until restoration work commenced in 1911 prior to the church's ultimate collapse into the sea.

#location
The ruins of St. Martin's Church (known as the Garden of Sleep) located on the clifftop in Sidestrand, Norfolk.
