#title
The Depopulation of Wallington and the Fate of St Margaret's Tower

#summary
According to local legend, the 17th-century judge Sir Francis Gawdy cleared an entire Norfolk village and dismantled its parish church to improve the view from his newly rebuilt hall.

#details
In the parish of Runcton Holme lies the historic estate of Wallington Hall. Centuries ago, the site was home to a thriving small settlement and the parish church of St Margaret of Antioch. In 1605, Sir Francis Gawdy—a prominent and formidable judge—rebuilt Wallington Hall. According to local legend and historical lore, Gawdy desired an unobstructed, picturesque landscape around his grand manor and ruthlessly forced out the local villagers, effectively erasing the medieval settlement of Wallington. As part of this sweeping depopulation, he partially demolished St Margaret’s Church, leaving only its stone tower standing amidst the parklands to serve as an ornamental view from the hall. Over the generations, tales persisted of the harsh judge's actions, alongside rumors that hidden wealth and buried treasure lay concealed within the estate's landscaped grounds.

#location
Wallington Hall and the ruined tower of St Margaret of Antioch, located within the parish of Runcton Holme, Norfolk, UK.
