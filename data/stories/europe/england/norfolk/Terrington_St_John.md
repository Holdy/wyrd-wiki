#title
The Legend of Hickathrift's Candlestick

#summary
Fenland folklore tells of the giant Tom Hickathrift, whose legendary exploits and burial are marked by ancient stone crosses and earthworks across the Marshland, including a mysterious stone cross known as 'Hickathrift's Candlestick' at the village church in Terrington St John.

#details
According to local tradition and historical records, the Norfolk fenland hero and giant Tom Hickathrift was closely associated with several remarkable landmarks in Terrington St John and the surrounding Marshland Smeeth. An earthen mound once stood in a field south of the village crossroads—known as the 'Giant's Grave'—where local people maintained the tradition that the giant lay buried alongside his cartwheel and axletree. Upon this mound once stood an ancient stone cross. Over time, fragments of this stone cross and related markers were relocated, with a notable portion coming to rest just outside the north door of the Church of St John the Baptist in Terrington St John, where it became known as 'Hickathrift's Candlestick'. Throughout the centuries, antiquarians and villagers pointed to these stones, wash-bowls, and earthworks as tangible proof of the mighty giant who once ruled the fenlands.

#location
The stone cross known as 'Hickathrift's Candlestick' stands just outside the north door of the Church of St John the Baptist in Terrington St John, Norfolk.
