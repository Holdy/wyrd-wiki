#title
The Blood-Soaked Child of Cromer Churchyard

#summary
While clearing debris in the ruined churchyard of St. Peter and St. Paul, a caretaker was slashed across the throat by the terrifying apparition of a murdered child wearing blood-soaked white clothes. The spectral figure vanished instantly, leaving behind a chilling sighing sound in the air.

#details
During the period when the chancel of the church of St. Peter and St. Paul in Cromer lay in ruins, a caretaker was working on-site to clear away debris and overgrowth. Without warning, he was confronted by a horrifying apparition of a murdered child dressed in white garments that appeared entirely soaked in blood. The ghostly figure inflicted a mysterious and violent gash across the caretaker's throat before disappearing in a flash, leaving the worker shaken as a heavy sigh echoed in his ears.

#location
The churchyard of St. Peter and St. Paul (Cromer Parish Church) located on Church Street in Cromer, Norfolk.

Walter Rye, *Cromer Past and Present* (1889)

#title
The Blood-Stained Child of Cromer Churchyard

#summary
While crossing the ruined chancel of the town's parish church at night, a laborer was confronted by a small white-clad figure that grew to his height before revealing a bleeding, slit throat and vanishing into thin air. 

#details
In the late 19th century, a workman hired to clear away rubbish from the ruined chancel of St Peter and St Paul’s Church in Cromer took a shortcut across the old churchyard path after dark. As he walked through the eerie ruins, a small child-like figure dressed in white suddenly rose from the ground directly in front of him. To his horror, the figure grew taller until its face was level with his own, at which point a deep gash opened across its throat. A torrent of blood poured down its white clothing before the apparition vanished in a flash, leaving behind only a faint sighing sound.

#location
The churchyard of St Peter and St Paul’s Church, situated on Church Street in the coastal town of Cromer, Norfolk.

#upstream-source
Walter Rye, Cromer Past and Present (1899).

#title
The Faithful Phantom of Cromer Pier

#summary
A ghostly black dog haunts the shoreline near Cromer Pier, eternally gazing out to sea and vanishing into thin air when approached.

#details
Along the coast in Cromer, local folklore tells of a spectral black dog that haunts the beach near the town's famous pier. The loyal phantom is often seen sitting where the waves break on the sand, staring out toward the open water near the site of the swallowed medieval town of Shipden, waiting faithfully for an owner who never returned from the sea. Whenever curious onlookers or visitors attempt to approach the hound, it abruptly dissolves into thin air. Furthermore, local tradition associates the area with the legendary Black Shuck, whose ghostly tracks are said to run through Mill Lane and into the grounds of nearby Cromer Hall.

#location
The beach and shoreline immediately adjacent to Cromer Pier in Cromer, Norfolk.
