The Ghostly Smuggler of Weybourne

Summary

A phantom smuggler who drowned while trying to evade customs officers is said to haunt the pebble beach and village of Weybourne, his lonely whistling still heard on moonlit nights.

Details

According to local folklore, a smuggler named John Smythe frequented the shores of Weybourne during the era when the north Norfolk coast was a hotspot for landing illicit goods. One night, while his crew was operating on the pebble beach, Smythe left to meet the landlady of a local inn, promising to return promptly. In his absence, customs officers discovered the operation and closed in on the area. Warned of the danger, Smythe's fellow smugglers rowed back out to sea to wait for him offshore. Having lost track of time, Smythe returned too late and found himself cornered by the officers. Desperate to escape, he waded into the surf—unable to swim—expecting his crew to come back and rescue him. Instead, the crew assumed he had been captured and abandoned him, leaving Smythe to drown. To this day, locals report hearing a haunting whistle drifting across the village during full moons, followed by the appearance of his spirit on the shingle beach.
