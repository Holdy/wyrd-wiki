#title
The Fiddler of Blakeney Guildhall

#summary
A daring fiddler and his dog who ventured into a secret tunnel beneath Blakeney Guildhall to map its route vanished without a trace when his music abruptly stopped. Only his terrified dog emerged, leaving behind a haunted hill and ghostly strains of violin music on dark nights.

#details
According to local tradition, a network of underground smugglers' tunnels once connected Blakeney Guildhall to Binham Priory. Reluctant to explore the dark, subterranean depths themselves, the villagers offered a fee to a local fiddler to walk the tunnel while playing his violin so his underground progress could be tracked from above. Accompanied by his little dog and holding a lantern on a rod, the fiddler descended into the hole beneath the Guildhall. As he walked underground playing lively tunes, crowds of villagers and officials followed his music along the route. Suddenly, the music stopped completely and never resumed. Hours later, while waiting at the other end near the priory, only the fiddler's traumatized dog emerged, shivering and whining, while the fiddler himself was never seen again. A mound of earth known as Fiddler's Hill was raised to mark his disappearance, and on dark nights, locals still report hearing a solitary violin playing across the fields between Blakeney and Binham.

#location
Blakeney Guildhall is situated near the quayside in the coastal village of Blakeney in North Norfolk, England.

#title
The Fiddler of Blakeney Tunnels

#summary
A local musician who ventured into the subterranean smuggler tunnels beneath Blakeney Guildhall playing his instrument vanished without a trace. His mysterious disappearance is now permanently commemorated on the village sign.

#details
During the Middle Ages, Blakeney served as a prosperous port and a notorious hub for smugglers, giving rise to a secret network of underground tunnels beneath the village. According to local legend, a brave fiddler volunteered to explore these dark passageways leading from Blakeney Guildhall while playing his fiddle so villagers could track his movement overhead. As the crowd listened, the music abruptly stopped. Neither the fiddler nor his dog ever emerged from the depths, though decades later human skeletons were uncovered in the tunnels between Blakeney and Binham.

#location
Blakeney Guildhall is situated next to Mariner's Hill on the east side of Blakeney quay in North Norfolk.

#title
The Phantom Hound of Back Lane

#summary
A terrified local woman was stalked through the quiet village lanes by the glowing-eyed demon dog known as Black Shuck. As she heard the light dragging and tinkling of a chain behind her in the dark, she realized the legendary beast was following her every step.

#details
In the coastal village of Blakeney, witnesses have long reported harrowing encounters with Black Shuck, the demonic devil dog of East Anglian folklore. On one occasion, a woman walking home at night heard the unsettling, light dragging sound and tinkling of an unseen chain behind her. Unable to see anything physical making the noise, she realized she was being stalked by the spirit. Recognizing the classic signs of the phantom hound—whose name derives from the Old English word for demon or evil spirit—she fled in terror as the invisible entity continued to shadow her along Back Lane.

#location
Back Lane in the coastal village of Blakeney, Norfolk.

#title
The Tinkling Chain of Cley Hill

#summary
Walking home along the A149 from Cley to Blakeney at midnight, a woman is stalked up a hill by an invisible presence dragging a chain. She hears the unseen entity pass down Back Lane before fleeing down the High Street.

#details
During the summer of around 1968, a woman was walking home from Cley to Blakeney along the A149 road at approximately midnight. As she reached the bottom of Cley Hill (also referred to as Howe Hill), she heard the light dragging and tinkling of a chain behind her. Although she could see nothing in the dark, whatever was making the noise closely followed her all the way up the slope. Upon reaching the street-lit crossroads at the top, she stopped and waited to see what would pass, yet still spotted nothing. She then heard the entity turn and move away down Back Lane. Recognizing the local folklore that Black Shuck frequented this lane before heading across the marsh banks, she panicked and fled all the way home down the High Street.

#location
The encounter occurred along the A149 road ascending Cley Hill towards the crossroads and Back Lane in Blakeney.
