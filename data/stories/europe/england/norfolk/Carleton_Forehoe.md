#title
The Legend of the Forehoe Hills

#summary
The parish of Carleton Forehoe takes its distinctive name from four ancient Bronze Age barrows known as the Forehoe Hills, which local tradition maintains were artificially raised as the assembly place for the Saxon hundred court.

#details
For centuries, local tradition and historical accounts have associated the hamlet of Carleton Forehoe with the Saxon hundred of Forehoe, which derived its name from four prominent earthen barrows located within a wooded area near the parish. Known as the Forehoe Hills (derived from the Old English "four howes"), these Bronze Age bowl barrows served as the medieval moot or meeting place where the local hundred court administered justice and public administration. According to local lore and early topographical records, these mounds were recognized as ancient artificial structures around which local assemblies, courts, and community gatherings convened from the Saxon period onward.

#location
The Forehoe Hills barrows are located in Forehoe Wood near Carleton Forehoe, Norfolk (National Grid Reference TG 081 054).
