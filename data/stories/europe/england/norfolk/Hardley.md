#title
The Haunted Cross-Stone of Hardley

#summary
At the lonely crossroads of Lower Hardley Road and Cross Stone Road, the spectral apparition of an old woman clad in red is said to materialize at midnight and sit upon an ancient stone marker. 

#details
Situated near the village of Hardley in Norfolk, at the intersection of local roads and a dirt track, stands a small, shaped stone block historically known as a cross-stone or boundary marker. According to local folklore first collected in the late 19th century by local folklorist William Blythe Gerish, on certain nights of the year an old woman dressed entirely in red appears at midnight to sit upon the stone. The spot's ghostly reputation is even older than Gerish's records; Bryant's 1826 map of Norfolk explicitly designated the crossroads as 'Haunted Gap'. 

#location
At the junction of Lower Hardley Road and Cross Stone Road, approximately half a mile south-west of St Margaret's Church in Hardley, Norfolk.

#upstream-source
W. B. Gerish: 'Norfolk Folklore Notes' (unpublished, 1890-93), p.88.

#title
The Phantom Dog of Church Lane

#summary
A witness walking along Church Lane in Hardley at dawn encountered a large, shaggy grey dog that vanished mysteriously into the barley fields.

#details
At around half past four on a midsummer morning, a person walking back from a friend's house near the church in Hardley encountered a large, charcoal-grey dog with matted, shaggy hair. The creature—roughly the size of an elongated Rottweiler—darted rapidly across Church Lane between tall grain fields and vanished into the barley without a trace. Though dismissed by acquaintances as a ghost story, the witness was profoundly shaken and noted that within a year of the encounter, their younger sister developed cancer.

#location
Church Lane next to the church in the village of Hardley, Norfolk.

#title
The Woman in Red at Hardley Crossroads

#summary
At a lonely crossroads in Hardley, the apparition of an old woman dressed in red appears at midnight to sit upon a historic stone marker. The site's supernatural reputation dates back centuries, earning it the historic map designation of 'Haunted Gap'.

#details
Near Hardley, at the junction where three roads and a dirt track meet about half a mile south-west of the village church, stands a small medieval cross-stone. According to local folklore first documented in the late 19th century, a spectral old woman clad in red materialises at midnight on certain nights to sit silently upon the stone. The area's eerie reputation predates formal collection, as Bryant's 1826 map of Norfolk explicitly named the spot 'Haunted Gap'.

#location
The historic cross-stone located at the road junction approximately half a mile south-west of Hardley church in Norfolk.
