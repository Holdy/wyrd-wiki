#title
The Deserted Village of the Black Death

#summary
According to local legend, the medieval settlement and church of Saxlingham Thorpe was completely abandoned and its inhabitants dispersed after being ravaged by the Black Death.

#details
Long ago, the thriving community of Saxlingham Thorpe stood surrounding its medieval parish church, which was dedicated to the Assumption of St Mary. Local tradition maintains that the hamlet met a tragic end when the Black Death swept through the region, devastating the population and forcing any survivors to flee or disperse. Though historians point out that poor soil quality and shifting economic prospects also encouraged villagers to relocate to neighboring areas, the enduring folk legend attributes the total abandonment of the medieval settlement directly to the plague. By the late seventeenth century, the church was officially abandoned and declared ruined, its stone and materials repurposed elsewhere, leaving behind an isolated and atmospheric ruin encircled by trees.

#location
The ruined church of St Mary in the historic parish of Saxlingham Thorpe, situated just south of Saxlingham Nethergate in Norfolk.
