#title
The Foundling and the Grasshopper of Gresham

#summary
According to local legend, the founder of the ancient Gresham family was discovered as an abandoned newborn in a Norfolk field after a boy was led to the spot by a grasshopper. This miraculous finding gave rise to both the village's name and the family's iconic grasshopper heraldry.

#details
Long ago in the parish of Gresham, a local legend took root regarding the origins of the prominent Gresham family. According to the account, the founding ancestor, a child named Roger de Gresham, was abandoned as a helpless newborn baby left out among the long grass in a rural Norfolk field. A young boy walking through the countryside heard a distinct and persistent sound—a grasshopper chirping loudly nearby. Intrigued, the boy followed the insect's calls until he reached the patch of grass where he discovered the infant. The child was rescued and raised, and the miraculous event became enduring folklore in the village. While scholars suggest the family's famous grasshopper symbol may be a heraldic pun on the Anglo-Saxon word for grass ("gres"), the legend of the grasshopper guiding the boy to the abandoned foundling remains a cherished part of local history and explains the origin of the village's name.

#location
The grassy fields and parish lands surrounding the village of Gresham in North Norfolk, where the infant was historically said to have been discovered.

#title
The Shrieking Pits of Gresham Cross

#summary
A spectral mother dressed in white haunts the ancient woodland pits near Gresham Cross, weeping and wailing as she searches endlessly for her murdered baby.

#details
According to local legend, a jealous husband became convinced that his infant child was not his own. He brutally murdered the baby and buried it in a pit west of the church, before returning home to kill his wife as well. Ever since, her ghostly figure has been seen haunting the shallow pits in the wooded slopes near Gresham Cross, pacing between the depressions in the ground and crying out in grief for her lost child.

#location
The wooded slopes and shallow pits located near Gresham Cross in Norfolk.

#title
The Spectral Hound of Gresham Cross

#summary
Cyclists returning from Gresham in the late 1930s encountered an enormous, silent black dog emerging suddenly from heavy sea mist near the Old Stone Cross.

#details
In the late 1930s, two individuals cycling from Gresham back to West Runton at dusk experienced a terrifying encounter in thick sea mist. As they approached the Old Stone Cross on the parish boundary, an enormous black dog—at least twice the size of a large Labrador, with its mouth open and tongue lolling—suddenly materialized out of the fog. Making no sound whatsoever, the phantom hound ran right beside them on the road before vanishing into the mist, leaving the cyclists convinced they had witnessed Old Shuck.

#location
The encounter took place near the Old Stone Cross (Gresham Cross) at the parish boundary between Gresham and Aylmerton (TG18073879).
