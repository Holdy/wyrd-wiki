#title
The Mystery of the Swanton Novers Rectory

#summary
In 1919, the rectory in Swanton Novers was besieged by inexplicable showers of water and inflammable liquids dripping from its ceilings, leading to a sensational investigation and accusations against a young servant girl.

#details
In August 1919, Swanton Novers Rectory—then home to the Reverend H. Guy and his family—became the center of a bizarre poltergeist-like mystery when various liquids, including petrol, paraffin, methylated spirits, and water, began mysteriously dripping from every ceiling in the house. Despite examining the plaster and laths and shutting off the home's petrol lighting plant, experts could find no rational source for the ooze. The phenomenon attracted widespread attention until illusionist Mr. Oswald Williams and his wife intervened; Mrs. Williams subsequently claimed she witnessed the rector's 15-year-old servant girl, Mabel Phillips, throwing salted water toward the ceiling through a floor hole. Although Mabel allegedly confessed before later vehemently denying it and accusing Mrs. Williams of assault, the baffling disturbances and subsequent legal disputes deeply divided the local community.

#location
Swanton Novers Rectory is situated in the rural village of Swanton Novers, located in the North Norfolk district of Norfolk, England.

#title
The Oily Rectory of Swanton Novers

#summary
In the late summer of 1919, Swanton Novers Rectory gained national notoriety when flammable liquids mysteriously began raining from its ceilings, baffling experts before being exposed as a servant's hoax.

#details
In August and September 1919, strange and unprecedented phenomena gripped Swanton Novers Rectory, the home of the rector, the Reverend Hugh Guy. Bizarre hissing sounds preceded sudden cascades of petrol, paraffin, sandalwood oil, and methylated spirits that soaked floors and seeped down from the ceilings during daytime hours. The relentless flooding forced the rector and his wife to abandon the parsonage and seek refuge elsewhere. National newspapers seized upon the event as a sensation, attracting surveyors, mineralogists, journalists, and even famous illusionist Nevil Maskelyne to investigate. While some speculated that the Victorian parsonage had been built directly over a genuine oil well, others suspected poltergeist activity. The mystery came to an abrupt end when it was revealed to be a clever hoax orchestrated by the household's 15-year-old servant girl, Mabel Williams, who had used a garden syringe and hidden caches of fuel to manufacture the supernatural manifestations.

#location
Swanton Novers Rectory was a Victorian parsonage situated in the village of Swanton Novers in North Norfolk.
