#title
The Curse of the Dirty Bottles

#summary
An innkeeper and a later workman both died suddenly after attempting to move a collection of bottles left in a pub window, cementing a local legend that touching them invokes a fatal curse. Out of fear, the bottles have remained completely untouched and in place for centuries.

#details
In 1725, the innkeeper of Ye Old Cross Inn in Alnwick was tidying up and moving a collection of wine bottles resting on a window sill when he suddenly collapsed and died. His distraught widow declared that a deadly curse was tied to the bottles, warning that anyone who dared move or touch them again would share his fate. Fearing the superstition, the staff left the bottles right where they sat. Approximately a century later, a workman scoffed at the old warning while carrying out renovations and attempted to move them; he finished his work, stepped outside, and also dropped dead. True to the legend, the dust-covered bottles have been permanently sealed behind a protective secondary window to prevent anyone from ever trying to move them again.

#location
The Dirty Bottles pub (historically Ye Old Cross Inn) located on Narrowgate in the town of Alnwick, Northumberland.

#title
William Winter's Gibbet

#summary
The story revolves around a notorious criminal named William Winter who was executed in 1791. His body was hung on a gibbet near Elsdon, and locals have reported sightings of his ghost wandering the area.

#details
In the late 18th century, William Winter, along with two female accomplices, committed a brutal murder of an innocent woman named Margaret Crozier. The trio was apprehended and subsequently hanged in Newcastle for their crimes. However, while the bodies of the women were taken by physicians, William's body was left to hang unattended.

His corpse was eventually moved to a gibbet located two and a half miles from Elsdon, where it stood as a grim reminder of his heinous deed. Over time, the gibbet has been repaired and replaced due to weather damage. Locals have reported eerie sightings of William Winter's ghost wandering around the gibbet late at night.

#location
The key location is the gibbet located two and a half miles from Elsdon in Northumberland National Park.
