    Calgarth Hall skulls

    Summary

    Wealthy magistrate Myles Phillipson framed farmers Kraster and Dorothy Cook for theft to get their land, and as they were sentenced to hang Dorothy cursed his family. Two grinning skulls then appeared on the Phillipsons' staircase and returned with terrible screams however often they were buried, burned or smashed.

    Details

    Kraster and Dorothy Cook were respected farmers who refused to sell their small plot of land to Myles Phillipson, a wealthy and powerful magistrate. Phillipson invited them to a banquet, hid a silver cup among their belongings, then had them arrested for theft, a capital crime, and sentenced them both to death. As the sentence was passed, Dorothy cursed him: his family would never prosper, they would lose all their land, and the Cooks would haunt Calgarth's walls. "Never will ye be rid of us," she declared. After the hanging, two grinning skulls appeared at the head of the Phillipsons' staircase. When they were buried far away, screams woke the house that night and the skulls were back by morning, and burning and smashing them did no better. The curse came true: the Phillipsons lost their land and money, and Calgarth passed to other owners.
