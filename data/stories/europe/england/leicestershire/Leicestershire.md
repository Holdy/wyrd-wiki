#title
The Cursed Shoes of Papillon Hall

#summary
A Spanish mistress of a former 18th-century owner placed a dying curse on her silver and green slippers, ensuring that severe misfortune and paranormal disturbances would plague anyone who dared remove them from the hall. Every time the footwear was taken away, subsequent owners or caretakers suffered fatal accidents, fires, and hauntings until the shoes were promptly returned.

#details
Papillon Hall was built in 1624 near Lubenham, Leicestershire, and later inherited by David "Old Pamp" Papillon, a man reputed to be in league with the Devil. Before his marriage in 1717, Pamp kept a Spanish mistress who lived at the hall and reportedly walked along its rooftop leads. Before she died in 1715, she placed a curse on her green and silver brocade slippers, vowing that disaster would strike if her shoes ever left the building. 

True to her word, whenever subsequent owners sold the hall, they were bound by deed to keep the slippers on the premises. Violations of this rule invariably brought swift retribution: when the shoes were sent away to exhibitions, solicitors, or museums, the household experienced violent spectral noises, severe fires, lightning striking their prized animals, and fatal accidents befalling owners and servants alike. Even when American servicemen broke open the cabinet containing the shoes during World War II and took them as souvenirs, the culprits quickly died, returning the cursed footwear to the hall. 

#location
Papillon Hall formerly stood on raised ground near the village of Lubenham in Leicestershire, and the cursed slippers are now preserved nearby in the Market Harborough Museum.

#title
The Jaywalking Nun of Shady Lane

#summary
A couple driving through Evington reported encountering a ghostly nun appearing directly in front of their car on Shady Lane, leaving skid marks but no collision.

#details
On a quiet summer evening, a newlywed couple traveling along the B582 route from Earl Shilton to Evington Village encountered a figure dressed as a nun standing on Shady Lane between the small bridge over the brook and the entrance to Shady Lane Arboretum. The couple braked sharply, expecting impact, but the figure disappeared into a hedge without any physical evidence. Witnesses later noted skid marks on the road suggesting other vehicles had also braked suddenly at the same location.

#location
Shady Lane, Evington, Leicestershire, specifically between the small bridge over the brook and the entrance to Shady Lane Arboretum.
