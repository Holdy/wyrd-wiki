#title
Sally in the Woods

#summary
The road near Bath, known as Sally in the Wood, is believed to be haunted by the ghost of a murdered girl named Sally. The area is notorious for eerie occurrences and an unsettling atmosphere.

#details
Sally in the Wood is a rural lane located just outside Bath, stretching between Bathford and Farleigh Wick. It is widely regarded as one of the most haunted locations in the world. The road's eerie reputation stems from tales of a murdered young girl whose spirit is said to haunt the area, causing crashes and other supernatural events.

One of the most chilling aspects of this legend is that birds in the surrounding trees are reportedly unable to sing, adding to the unsettling atmosphere. Some people refuse to travel along the road at night due to these ghostly tales. Various theories exist about the origins of Sally's name, including stories of a murdered girl, a spooky crash involving a young couple, and connections to the English Civil War.

#location
The road known as Sally in the Wood is located between Bathford and Farleigh Wick just outside Bath.

#title
The Dragon's Retribution of Dinder

#summary
When Bishop Jocelin slew a terrorizing dragon in the 13th century, the dying beast cursed the Somerset village of Dinder to face its wrath every fifty years unless the defeat was perpetually remembered. To keep the curse at bay, villagers established a tradition of reenacting the slaying every half-century.

#details
In the early 1300s, the Somerset village of Dinder and surrounding settlements were plagued by a ferocious dragon residing on the nearby hill of Worminster Sleight. Bishop Jocelin confronted the beast, commanding his men to stay back while he single-handedly struck it down. As the monster breathed its dying breath, it laid a curse upon the village, swearing that if the inhabitants ever forgot about it, the dragon would return. To appease this hex and prevent the creature's return, every fifty years since the beast's defeat the village has re-enacted the slaying.

#location
The village of Dinder lies approximately three miles east of Wells in Somerset, situated near the hill of Worminster Sleight.
