The Dragon's Retribution of Dinder

Summary

When Bishop Jocelin slew a terrorizing dragon in the 13th century, the dying beast cursed the Somerset village of Dinder to face its wrath every fifty years unless the defeat was perpetually remembered. To keep the curse at bay, villagers established a tradition of reenacting the slaying every half-century.

Details

In the early 1300s, the Somerset village of Dinder and surrounding settlements were plagued by a ferocious dragon residing on the nearby hill of Worminster Sleight. Bishop Jocelin confronted the beast, commanding his men to stay back while he single-handedly struck it down. As the monster breathed its dying breath, it laid a curse upon the village, swearing that if the inhabitants ever forgot about it, the dragon would return. To appease this hex and prevent the creature's return, every fifty years since the beast's defeat the village has re-enacted the slaying.
