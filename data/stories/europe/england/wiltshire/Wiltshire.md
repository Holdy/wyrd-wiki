#title
The Curse of the Locked Church Door

#summary
Local folklore dictates that anyone who locks the door of Odstock church will face a sudden and untimely death, a curse placed after authorities imprisoned and executed an innocent gypsy man and barred access to his grave. For nearly two hundred years the church door remained unlocked to avoid the fatal penalty.

#details
In 1801, a young gypsy man was accused of horse theft. To save his son, a man named Joseph Scamp took the blame upon himself, but the local authorities showed no mercy and had him hanged. His people commemorated him annually with a graveside gathering. Angered by the celebrations, the local authorities retaliated by locking the church door and ripping up a rose planted at Joseph's resting place. In response, the gypsies laid a deadly curse on the church: anyone who dared lock its door would be condemned to a premature death, a fate reportedly proven by the sudden demise of two overly enthusiastic church wardens. To protect future parishioners, the rector ultimately threw the church key into the nearby River Ebble.

#location
St Mary's Church in the village of Odstock, just south of Salisbury in Wiltshire.

#title
The Ghost of Dore

#summary
A vicar encounters a headless ghost on a moonlit road near Aldbourne, Wiltshire.

#details
In October 1890, Reverend H.C. Lambert was walking from Aldbourne to Baydon under the light of a full moon. As he approached the top of a hill, he saw a short man standing in the middle of the road, seemingly engaged in conversation with someone at a gate. The figure did not move or speak as the vicar got closer. When he reached the figure, he realized it had no head. The vicar spoke to the ghost, asking if he could help, but received no response. As he reached out to touch the ghost's shirt, his hand passed through vapor. The sound of approaching wheels caused him to look away, and when he looked back, the ghost was gone.

The vicar later learned that a man named Dore had committed suicide by hanging himself in a nearby copse known as "Dor’s Grave." Two other people also reported seeing the headless ghost on the same road. The first was an old soldier who saw a short slight man carrying a ladder, and the second was a lady who saw the form inside the hedge and watched it dissolve into air.

#location
The top of a hill near Aldbourne where the vicar encountered the headless ghost.
