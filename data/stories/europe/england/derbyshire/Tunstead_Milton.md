#title
Dickie of Tunstead

#summary
Dickie, a skull on the windowsill at Tunstead Farm, was treated as a guardian, and taking it away even briefly was said to bring screaming and unrest. When the railway was planned across the farm, the works kept sinking into the marsh until the line was rerouted over what became "Dickie's Bridge".

#details
For centuries a skull known as Dickie sat on the windowsill of a lonely farmhouse at Tunstead. Locals saw it as a guardian that warned of burglars or illness and woke the household in emergencies. In 1809 John Hutchinson met the owner, Adam Fox, who said the skull had been there at least 200 years and that whenever it was taken away, even briefly, "There was no peace! no rest! It must be replaced!" When the London and North Western Railway tried to build its line across the farm, the works repeatedly collapsed and sank into the marsh, so the route was moved and a new bridge built, known ever after as "Dickie's Bridge". One story claims Dickie was once thrown into a reservoir and had to be retrieved because the fish started dying. In 1940 historian William Bunting claimed a surgeon had identified it as a young woman's skull, possibly from the Iron Age, and around 1977 a new owner is said to have secretly buried it before moving away soon after.

#title
Flagg Hall skull

#summary
Flagg Hall is said to be home to another of Derbyshire's screaming skulls. If the skull is ever removed, the house is said to echo with cries and moans.

#details
Flagg Hall is described as the home of another Derbyshire screaming skull. Nothing is recorded of whose it was, but the warning is a familiar one: if the skull is taken away, the building echoes with cries and moans.
