#title
The Bride of National Road DN7

#summary
Following a fatal accident on her wedding day in 1936, a young bride's spirit haunts national road DN7 near Găești, appearing as a white-clad apparition near her memorial. Motorists frequently report encountering her ghost on the straight stretch of highway where she perished.

#details
On September 24, 1936, a young woman named Margareta Ștefănescu tragically died in a car crash on national road DN7 on her wedding day. In her memory, a monument known as the Bride's Trinity (or Margareta's Trinity) was erected near the town of Găești. Over the decades, locals and drivers have reported numerous ghostly encounters with the silhouette of a woman dressed in white standing near the trinity and along the roadside. Despite the road being straight with maximum visibility, the location has gained a dark reputation for frequent fatal accidents, which locals attribute to the heartbroken bride's restless spirit haunting the highway.

#location
The Bride's Trinity is located alongside national road DN7 just outside the town of Găești in Dâmbovița County, Romania.

#title
The Heart of the Moroi

#summary
After a local pensioner died in an accident, his spirit allegedly possessed and began draining the life force of a young woman in the village, prompting a group of men to perform a midnight exhumation and ritual exorcism.

#details
In 2003, in the village of Marotinu de Sus in Dolj County, southwest Romania, a 76-year-old man named Petre Toma died after falling from his horse-drawn wagon. A few weeks later, a young mother living nearby fell gravely ill, screaming that Toma was on top of her and eating her alive. Convinced she was possessed by a restless *moroi*—an undead spirit draining her life force—shepherd Mircea Mitrica and several other villagers broke into the local cemetery at midnight. They exhumed Toma's body, cut into his chest with a pitchfork to remove his heart, and burned the organ at a crossroads. They then mixed the ashes into tea and had the sick woman drink it, after which she miraculously recovered, though the men were later caught and fined for desecrating the grave.

#location
The local cemetery at the edge of the village of Marotinu de Sus in Dolj County, southwest Romania.

#title
The Unfinished Curse of Chiajna

#summary
Built in the late 18th century but abandoned before it could be consecrated due to an Ottoman siege and plague, the massive structure became trapped in a multi-century curse marked by ghostly chimes and tragic deaths. Locals maintain that its heavy bronze bell, cast into the nearby river, still rings on nights of the full moon.

#details
Commissioned during the Phanariote era, the grand Chiajna Monastery was designed to be one of Wallachia’s premier places of worship. However, before religious rites could consecrate the building, Ottoman forces mistook the towering neoclassical structure for a military fortress and bombarded it. The unfinished sanctuary was subsequently abandoned during a devastating outbreak of the bubonic plague, where many sick individuals suffered and perished within its thick brick walls. Over the ensuing centuries, the ruins became a nexus of misfortune, marked by mysterious disappearances, tragic executions, and structural destruction during earthquakes. According to enduring folklore, phantom bell chimes echo from the depths of the Dâmbovița River on full moon nights, and shadowy apparitions continue to haunt the crumbling arches.

#location
The ruined Chiajna Monastery stands in the Giulești-Sârbi neighborhood on the western edge of Bucharest, Romania.
