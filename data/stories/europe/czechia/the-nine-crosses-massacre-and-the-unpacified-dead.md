#title
The Nine Crosses Massacre and the Unpacified Dead

#summary
A 16th-century wedding party was ambushed and killed at a forest crossroads due to a jilted lover's revenge, leading to centuries of ghostly hauntings and accidents triggered when the traditional centennial reconsecration of the site's crosses lapsed.

#details
In 1540, a young woman from Lesní Hluboké was forced into an arranged marriage by her father, breaking a promise she had made to a foreign merchant who had previously healed with her family's aid. Upon discovering the wedding, the jilted merchant lay in wait with an accomplice at a forest crossroads near the village to ambush the procession. In the chaos of the gunfire, the bride, the groom, the father, and several members of the wedding party were killed, and the attackers also perished. Benedictine monks subsequently erected nine wooden crosses at the site, returning every one hundred years to reconsecrate them and pacify the souls of the dead. Because the centennial ritual lapsed in 1987 when the monastic community was absent, the restless spirits—particularly a white-shrouded ghost bride—began haunting modern motorists near the highway exit.

#location
The Nine Crosses (Devět křížů) monument and historic crossroads stand immediately adjacent to Exit 168 of the D1 motorway near the village of Lesní Hluboké in the South Moravian Region of Czechia.
