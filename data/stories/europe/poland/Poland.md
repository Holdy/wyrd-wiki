#title
The Demonic Dwarf of Wrocław

#summary
An errant grandson, possessed by a malevolent demonic dwarf residing in a nearby church, savagely murdered his grandmother beneath the stone archway connecting two historic tenement houses.

#details
In the city of Wrocław, two slender medieval tenement houses known as the Hansel and Gretel buildings are joined by an overhanging stone archway inscribed with the Latin phrase "Death is the gate to life." According to local folklore, this passage was the site of a brutal murder committed by a young man who had fallen completely under the control of an evil entity. Possessed by a demonic dwarf that inhabited nearby St. Elizabeth's Church, the grandson flew into a devilish frenzy and butchered his grandmother. To this day, local legend maintains that the agonizing shrieks of the victim can still be heard lingering around the archway after dark.

#location
The key location is the overhanging archway connecting the Hansel and Gretel tenement houses (Jaś i Małgosia) immediately adjacent to St. Elizabeth's Church in the Old Town of Wrocław, Poland.

#title
The Haunting of Małachowo's Road

#summary
The vengeful spirit of a cruel local count haunts motorists traveling through his former domain, frequently causing dangerous car accidents.

#details
Centuries ago, the village of Małachowo was ruled by a tyrannical and exploitative count who tormented his subjects. His life ended abruptly when his carriage sank into deep mud, causing him to drown. Instead of finding peace, his ghost returned to haunt the village roads, startling drivers and causing numerous vehicular accidents. Consequently, the settlement was renamed Małachowo-Złych Miejsc—meaning "Małachowo of Bad Places"—to warn travelers passing through the area.

#location
The village of Małachowo-Złych Miejsc is located in west-central Poland near the city of Gniezno.

#title
The White Lady of Czocha Castle

#summary
After betraying her family to enemy forces in the 15th century, a woman named Gertrude was cursed by her brother to wander the halls of Czocha Castle for eternity, barred even from the gates of hell.

#details
In the 15th century, Czocha Castle was home to a brother and sister named Gertrude. Following a bitter dispute between the siblings, Gertrude sought ruthless revenge by conspiring with the invading Hussites, betraying her family and attempting to help the enemy conquer the stronghold. Her plot ultimately failed, and her furious brother ordered her execution. Before she was beheaded, he placed a powerful curse upon her soul, condemning her to roam the castle grounds forever. Because of this hex, Gertrude cannot even enter the gates of hell, leaving her spirit endlessly trapped within the fortress. Visitors and staff at the historic castle have reported encountering her apparition—known as the White Lady—wandering the corridors and rooms of the estate.

#location
Czocha Castle is located in the village of Sucha, near Leśniańskie Lake in southwestern Poland.
