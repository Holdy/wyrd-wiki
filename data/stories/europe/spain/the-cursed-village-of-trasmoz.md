#title
The Cursed Village of Trasmoz

#summary
Nestled in the foothills of Spain's Moncayo mountains, the medieval village and castle of Trasmoz were officially cursed by the Catholic Church in 1511 via a papal decree that remains active to this day.

#details
During the Middle Ages, Trasmoz was a thriving, secular fiefdom independent of the nearby Veruela Abbey, which generated intense friction over taxes and water rights. To mask illegal coin-forging operations inside Trasmoz Castle, counterfeiters spread rumors that witches and sorcerers gathered there at night. Seizing on these witchcraft allegations and angered when King Ferdinand II ruled in favor of Trasmoz in a dispute over diverted water, the Church sought vengeance. With the explicit sanction of Pope Julius II, the archbishop officially cursed the entire village in 1511 by chanting a powerful penitential psalm as a spiritual weapon. Following the curse, Trasmoz suffered a devastating decline, including a fire that reduced its 12th-century castle to ruins in 1520 and a massive drop in population over the centuries. Because the curse was pronounced with papal authority, only a reigning Pope holds the power to lift it, making Trasmoz the only officially cursed town in Spain.

#location
Trasmoz Castle and village, situated in the foothills of the Moncayo mountain range in the province of Zaragoza, Aragon, Spain.
