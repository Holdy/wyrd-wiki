#title
The Cursed Village of Trasmoz

#summary
Nestled in the foothills of Spain's Moncayo mountains, the medieval village and castle of Trasmoz were officially cursed by the Catholic Church in 1511 via a papal decree that remains active to this day.

#details
During the Middle Ages, Trasmoz was a thriving, secular fiefdom independent of the nearby Veruela Abbey, which generated intense friction over taxes and water rights. To mask illegal coin-forging operations inside Trasmoz Castle, counterfeiters spread rumors that witches and sorcerers gathered there at night. Seizing on these witchcraft allegations and angered when King Ferdinand II ruled in favor of Trasmoz in a dispute over diverted water, the Church sought vengeance. With the explicit sanction of Pope Julius II, the archbishop officially cursed the entire village in 1511 by chanting a powerful penitential psalm as a spiritual weapon. Following the curse, Trasmoz suffered a devastating decline, including a fire that reduced its 12th-century castle to ruins in 1520 and a massive drop in population over the centuries. Because the curse was pronounced with papal authority, only a reigning Pope holds the power to lift it, making Trasmoz the only officially cursed town in Spain.

#location
Trasmoz Castle and village, situated in the foothills of the Moncayo mountain range in the province of Zaragoza, Aragon, Spain.

#title
The Ghost Girl on the Arrabassada Road

#summary
A ghostly young woman in a white dress is frequently picked up by drivers on a treacherous mountain pass before vanishing into thin air once the danger has passed.

#details
Drivers navigating the winding Arrabassada Road in Barcelona have long reported encountering a young female hitchhiker wearing a white dress near the sharp bend known as *Revolt de la Paella*. After being welcomed into a passing car, she grows noticeably anxious as the vehicle nears the dangerous curve, urgently warning the driver to slow down to avoid a crash. Once the bend is safely negotiated, she inexplicably vanishes from the moving vehicle. According to local legend, she is the spirit of a tragic traffic accident victim who spends her afterlife trying to prevent others from sharing her fatal fate.

#location
The haunting takes place at the *Revolt de la Paella* sharp bend on the Carretera de l'Arrabassada (BP-1417) connecting Barcelona and Sant Cugat del Vallès in Spain.

#title
The Haunting and Possession of Estefanía Gutiérrez Lázaro

#summary
Following a disrupted school seance with a Ouija board, a teenage girl in Madrid began experiencing severe hallucinations and violent seizures before her mysterious death, after which her family's apartment became the site of documented paranormal disturbances and alleged demonic possession.

#details
In 1990, eighteen-year-old Estefanía Gutiérrez Lázaro participated in a school Ouija board session to contact a deceased friend. When a teacher interrupted and broke the board, Estefanía reportedly inhaled a strange grey smoke. Immediately following the incident, she suffered from intense insomnia, hallucinations, and terrifying visions of faceless shadow entities, culminating in violent fits and a sudden death under suspicious medical circumstances. Following her passing, paranormal activity escalated dramatically within her family's apartment: furniture moved independently, crucifixes were turned upside down, and disembodied voices echoed through the walls. The terror became so severe that in November 1992, the family fled into the cold rain and called emergency services. Responding police officers, led by Inspector José Negri, documented doors opening on their own, unexplained drops in temperature, and physical phenomena that were officially recorded in a police report as an inexplicable paranormal occurrence.

#location
The events took place inside an apartment located at Calle Luis Marín No. 8 in the Vallecas district of Madrid, Spain.
