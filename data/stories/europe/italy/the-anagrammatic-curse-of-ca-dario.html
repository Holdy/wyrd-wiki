The Anagrammatic Curse of Ca’ Dario

Summary

Built in the late 15th century by Giovanni Dario, the Venetian palazzo bears an inscription whose Latin anagram foreshadows ruin, binding generations of subsequent owners to financial collapse, murder, or suicide.

Details

Commissioned in 1479 by diplomat Giovanni Dario as a tribute to Venice, the palace was later inherited by his daughter Marietta and her husband Vincenzo Barbaro. According to local lore, a hidden anagram within the building's Latin facade inscription—Joannes Darivs Genio—rearranges to read Sub ruina insidiosa genero (“I create under an insidious ruin”). True to the omen, almost every subsequent owner or resident over the centuries has met a tragic fate, including bankruptcy, violent attacks, mysterious accidents, and suicide. Even prospective buyers and visitors have reportedly faced severe misfortune or fatal illness shortly after interacting with the property.
