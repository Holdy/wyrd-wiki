#title
The Anagrammatic Curse of Ca’ Dario

#summary
Built in the late 15th century by Giovanni Dario, the Venetian palazzo bears an inscription whose Latin anagram foreshadows ruin, binding generations of subsequent owners to financial collapse, murder, or suicide.

#details
Commissioned in 1479 by diplomat Giovanni Dario as a tribute to Venice, the palace was later inherited by his daughter Marietta and her husband Vincenzo Barbaro. According to local lore, a hidden anagram within the building's Latin facade inscription—*Joannes Darivs Genio*—rearranges to read *Sub ruina insidiosa genero* (“I create under an insidious ruin”). True to the omen, almost every subsequent owner or resident over the centuries has met a tragic fate, including bankruptcy, violent attacks, mysterious accidents, and suicide. Even prospective buyers and visitors have reportedly faced severe misfortune or fatal illness shortly after interacting with the property.

#location
Ca’ Dario is located at number 353 in the Dorsoduro district of Venice, facing the Grand Canal near the Church of Santa Maria della Salute.

#title
The Phantom of Via Bagnera

#summary
The restless spirit of Italy's first serial killer is said to haunt a narrow Milanese street where he once committed his gruesome murders. Visitors walking down this historic alleyway can experience sudden, chilling drops in temperature as his phantom presence paces the pavement.

#details
During the 19th century, Antonio Boggia—historically recognized as Italy's first serial killer, known as the "Monster of Milan"—operated out of a home and basement situated on the narrow street of Via Bagnera, where he brutally murdered his victims. Following his arrest and subsequent execution, his dark deeds left a permanent mark on the alleyway. Today, local lore maintains that Boggia's restless spirit continues to roam the pavement, making the street feel unnaturally and inexplicably cold to anyone passing through where his crimes took place.

#location
Via Bagnera is a narrow street located in central Milan, situated between Via Torino, the Basilica of Sant'Ambrogio, and the Duomo.

#title
The Possessed Novices of Lucedio

#summary
During a medieval gathering of witches, the Devil possessed the young novices of a nearby convent and used them to corrupt the monks of the Principato di Lucedio into a century of dark rituals.

#details
According to local Italian folklore, the Prince of Darkness materialized during a witch's sabbath at the Darola cemetery near Vercelli. Eager to take over the peaceful Cistercian abbey of Santa Maria di Lucedio, the entity possessed the young novices of a convent in Trino Vercellese. These possessed novices infiltrated the monastery and seduced the monks, plunging the sacred foundation into a century of perdition, torture, and black magic. The demonic reign only ended centuries later, and legend maintains that the demonic presence was ultimately captured and sealed in a secret crypt beneath the abbey church, guarded eternally by four pious monks whose mummified bodies remain seated on chairs inside.

#location
The Principato di Lucedio is located in the Piedmont region of northern Italy, situated in the countryside north of Vercelli.
