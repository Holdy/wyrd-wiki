#title
The Curse of St. Peter

#summary
Those who dare to disturb or excavate the sacred tombs beneath St. Peter's Basilica invoke an ancient curse, bringing sudden illness and death to the offenders.

#details
According to legend dating back to Pope Gregory I in the sixth century, the tombs of the Apostles are protected by a fearsome curse that strikes anyone who attempts to open or move their contents. This dread was realized in July 1629 when architect Gian Lorenzo Bernini excavated beneath St. Peter's Basilica to erect his magnificent bronze baldachin. When architect Nicolò Alamanni ordered ancient sarcophagi uncovered during the digging to be opened and destroyed, he fell violently ill and died within days. His death was soon followed by a papal chaplain and a scribe who also succumbed to mysterious illnesses, cementing the belief among Romans that the ancient curse remained lethal and active.

#location
St. Peter's Basilica in Vatican City, specifically beneath the main altar and baldachin in the Vatican Grottos.

#title
The Exorcism at the Swiss Guardhouse

#summary
During Pope Benedict XVI's pontificate, an elderly cardinal performed an emergency exorcism on a possessed young woman inside the guardhouse of the Swiss Guards at Vatican City.

#details
A young Italian woman attended a papal Mass held by Pope Benedict XVI in St. Peter's Square. During the gathering, she was suddenly overtaken by a demonic presence, manifesting visible signs of possession and causing great distress among her companions. As they sought assistance near the colonnade, Cardinal Ivan Dias happened to approach. Recognizing the spiritual emergency, he asked for a private space to pray for her soul, and the young guards offered the nearby guardhouse of the Swiss Guards. Inside the secure guardhouse, Cardinal Dias conducted intense prayers of deliverance over the course of twenty minutes, after which the young woman was seemingly freed from the entity and calmly departed.

#location
The guardhouse of the Swiss Guards, located just outside the colonnade of St. Peter's Square in Vatican City.
