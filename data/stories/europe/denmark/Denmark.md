#title
The Curse of the Wooden Man of Egeskov Castle

#summary
Generations of owners have believed that moving a mysterious wooden doll resting in the attic rafters of a historic Danish castle will cause the entire building to sink into its moat on Christmas Eve. To keep the impending doom at bay, the family appeases the figure each year with a traditional bowl of rice pudding.

#details
Hidden among the dusty rafters beneath the spire of Egeskov Castle on the island of Funen sits an ancient, child-sized wooden doll resting on an old pillow. According to longstanding legend, if the doll is ever moved from its designated spot, the magnificent Renaissance castle will plunge straight down into its surrounding moat on Christmas Eve. Fearing the fulfillment of this destructive curse, earlier generations of residents famously refused to stay at the manor during the Christmas holidays. Modern occupants have mitigated the risk by adopting a holiday ritual traditionally reserved for household elves, leaving out a bowl of sweet rice pudding to keep the wooden figure content and the castle safely above water.

#location
Egeskov Castle is situated on the island of Funen (Fyn) in southern Denmark, near the town of Kværndrup.

#title
The Glowing Sentry of Amaliegade

#summary
Witnesses near a historic Copenhagen intersection have reported seeing a luminous, spectral soldier standing watch after dark. The apparition is said to haunt the street corner where old skeletal remains were once unearthed during construction.

#details
In the historic district of Copenhagen near Amaliegade and Sankt Annæ Plads, past excavations for new buildings uncovered human skeletons buried beneath the streets. Following these discoveries, locals and visitors reported strange nocturnal phenomena, including glowing figures and phantom wagons. Most notably, on the street corner where Sankt Annæ Plads meets Amaliegade, people have repeatedly encountered the glowing phantom of a sentry standing guard in the night.

#location
The corner of Sankt Annæ Plads and Amaliegade in Copenhagen, Denmark.

#title
The Køge House Cross

#summary
Between 1608 and 1615, a wealthy merchant's household in Denmark endured years of demonic haunting and spirit possession that culminated in a devastating witch trial.

#details
Beginning in 1608, merchant Hans Bartskær and his wife Anne experienced a series of supernatural disturbances in their home in Køge, Denmark, which they termed the *Køge Huskors* (House Cross). The haunting began with the phantom sound of a clucking hen and escalated into terrifying manifestations of the Devil in various shapes, including a toad on tall legs. Their foster son suffered violent physical convulsions while claiming Satan was actively tormenting him, and household servants were driven away by the pervasive demonic presence. Convinced that their neighbor, Johanne Thomes, had used sorcery to send the Devil into their dwelling, the family initiated legal action against her. During the ensuing 1612 witch trial, Johanne confessed under duress and implicated multiple other women, ultimately resulting in at least fifteen women being burned at the stake.

#location
The former home site of merchant Hans Bartskær in downtown Køge, Denmark, commemorated by the historical Køge Huskors Memorial plaque.
