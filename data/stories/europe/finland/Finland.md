#title
The Curse of Ämmänhauta

#summary
According to local folklore in Vesilahti, a pact made with an 11th-century witch established a permanent curse upon anyone who passes her forest tomb without tossing a branch upon it. Passing travelers and villagers have faithfully adhered to this ritual across generations to avoid invoking her wrath.

#details
In the 11th-century village of Vesilahti, a local witch made a treaty with the villagers regarding her final resting place. Knowing that her practice of witchcraft would disqualify her from being buried in consecrated church ground, she stipulated that if she passed away while being transported there, she must be buried right where she fell in the forest. She placed a curse upon the site, decreeing that anyone who walks past her tomb without placing a branch or twig upon it will face the power of her curse. For centuries, locals, school children, and travelers have maintained the tradition of tossing branches onto the grave to appease the witch and escape her hex.

#location
Ämmänhauta (The Witch's Tomb) is located in the forest in the municipality of Vesilahti, Finland.

#title
The Haunting of the Pyhäkangas Corpse Road

#summary
Travelers along the historic corpse road in Central Finland encounter the lingering spirits of past villagers whose memories are carved into sacred memorial trees. Visitors walking the path experience unexplainable chills and ghostly digital anomalies near these ancient forest markers.

#details
Beginning in the early 17th century, mourners in Saarijärvi transported deceased villagers along a remote 15-kilometer corpse road to reach the local church. To prevent the souls of the dead from wandering back home along the path, travelers carved memorial markings—known as *karsikko*—into the bark of pine trees along the route. Over the centuries, locals reported that the corpse road and its surrounding pruned forest became haunted by the restless spirits of those carried to their graves, such as historical figures Heikki Niilonpoika and Mikko Iivanainen. Modern visitors walking the historic trail still report an eerie, cemetery-like silence, with unexplained atmospheric phenomena and mysterious video anomalies captured near the carved memorial trees.

#location
The historic Pyhäkangas corpse road and pruned forest (*Pyhäkankaan karsikko*) are located off Häkkiläntie 1475 in Saarijärvi, Central Finland.

#title
The Martin Croft Devil

#summary
In January 1885, a Finnish croft in Ylöjärvi suffered a violent sixteen-day haunting centered around a young maid, during which objects flew through the air, furniture moved spontaneously, and an invisible entity terrorized the inhabitants. The extraordinary case was subsequently documented in extensive court records and witness testimonies.

#details
On January 12, 1885, strange disturbances broke out at the croft of Efraim Martin in Ylöjärvi, Finland. The phenomena—which included flying knives, doors refusing to stay closed, heavy clay accumulating from nowhere, and locked drawers emptying themselves without the binding ties breaking—appeared to center around the household's 13-year-old maid, Emma Lindroos. As word spread, curious visitors and local authorities flocked to the remote cottage, witnessing objects such as candlesticks and bread poles move autonomously through the air. The haunting culminated in a legal trial where fifteen witnesses testified under oath regarding seventy-eight inexplicable occurrences before the disturbances abruptly ceased on January 27.

#location
Efraim Martin's former croft located in the rural parish and village of Ylöjärvi, near Keijärvi in Finland.
