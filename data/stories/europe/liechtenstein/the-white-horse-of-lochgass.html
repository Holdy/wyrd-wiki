The White Horse of Lochgass

Summary

A notorious farmer who stole a saddled white horse on Christmas Eve discovered the animal was actually the devil in disguise before crashing to his death. Cursed for his sins, his spirit was condemned to haunt a Vaduz street as a phantom white horse until a protective cross was raised.

Details

In the town of Vaduz, a greedy farmer known for stealing property and livestock went out on Christmas Eve and stole a white saddled horse tied near a church across the Rhine. As he rode the animal back toward his home along the Lochgass, the beast began galloping at a terrifying, unnatural speed. Upon reaching the Lochgass road, the horse stopped abruptly, catapulting the farmer to the ground and breaking his neck. As he lay dying, he realized that the horse was the devil itself, which had taken the form of the animal. Condemned for his lifelong wickedness, the farmer's spirit was forced to endlessly trot up and down the Lochgass as a white horse until a sacred cross was eventually erected in the street to end the haunting.
