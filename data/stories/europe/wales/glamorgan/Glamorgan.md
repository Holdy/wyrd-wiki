#title
The Curse of the Swansea Devil

#summary
An embittered architect placed a grinning wooden carving of Satan on his newly built office building directly opposite a rival's church, prophesying that his devil would remain laughing when the holy building was eventually destroyed. Decades later, during the Second World War, the church was burned down in a bombing raid while the devil's building stood completely unscathed.

#details
In the 1890s, plans were drawn up to completely rebuild St. Mary's Church in the heart of Swansea. The prestigious project was put out to tender, and a local architect submitted designs alongside those of the renowned English architect Sir Arthur Blomfield. When Blomfield’s designs were chosen by the committee, the rejected local architect took the decision as a personal insult and harbored a bitter grudge. 

Some years later, a row of cottages directly opposite the church became available. The scorned architect purchased the land, tore down the cottages, and erected a red-brick building to house brewery offices. Seeking vengeance, he carved a three-foot wooden effigy of Satan—affectionately dubbed "Old Nick" or the Swansea Devil—and mounted it on the building so that it leered directly across the street at the church. Embittered by his professional rejection, he laid a vindictive curse and prophecy upon the site: "When your church is destroyed and burnt to the ground, my devil will remain laughing."

Decades passed, and during the fierce Swansea Blitz of February 1941, the strategic port city suffered heavy enemy bombing. St. Mary's Church took direct hits and was completely gutted by fire, fulfilling the first part of the curse. Miraculously, the brewery offices bearing the wooden devil survived the wartime destruction without a scratch, leaving Old Nick to overlook and mock the ruined masonry of his rival's creation.

#location
St. Mary's Church and the adjacent Quadrant Shopping Centre in Swansea city centre, South Wales.

#title
The Phantom Woman of Llangennith

#summary
A phantom woman dressed in white is frequently seen on the main street of Llangennith, Gower.

#details
In Llangennith, a small village in the Gower Peninsula, South Wales, locals and visitors alike have reported sightings of a spectral figure—a woman dressed entirely in white. This ghostly apparition has been spotted numerous times on the main street, adding to the eerie atmosphere of this otherwise picturesque location.

#location
The main street of Llangennith is where the phantom woman is frequently seen.
