#title
The Cursing Well of Penrhos

#summary
A well near the village of Penrhos was believed to have both healing and cursing powers. Visitors could cure cancer by bathing in its waters or afflict enemies with the disease by uttering curses and dropping pins around the well.

#details
The well near Penrhos, Anglesey, was renowned for its dual powers: it could heal cancer if one bathed in its waters, but it also held the power to curse. By the mid-nineteenth century, the well's popularity led to significant damage to the surrounding property, prompting the landowner to drain and destroy it.

#location
The well is located near Penrhos village on Anglesey

#title
The Ghost Rock

#summary
A 15ft tall stone known as the "Ghost Rock" or "Bogey Rock" is said to be haunted by a headless highwayman who was decapitated in a fight with a soldier.

#details
The Ghost Rock, also known as Carreg Y Bwgan, stands alongside Porthdafarch Road near Holyhead on Anglesey's Holy Island. This 15ft-tall stone is shrouded in mystery and local lore, often described as resembling a cloaked sorcerer. It is reputed to be haunted by the Devil and is associated with various unsettling tales.

One of the most chilling stories involves a headless highwayman who lurks behind the rock, waiting to be summoned. According to legend, if someone walks around the stone three times on any night in August, they will encounter the ghost of this vengeful man with a nearly severed head. The highwayman is said to have operated from this spot, preying on travelers between Holyhead and Ireland until he met his end in a fight with a soldier.

The rock's enigmatic presence has sparked numerous myths and legends over time. Some believe that walking around the stone anti-clockwise 12 times at midnight will summon a spectre, while others claim that circling it with eyes closed might transport you inside the stone itself.

#location
The Ghost Rock stands alongside Porthdafarch Road near Holyhead on Anglesey's Holy Island.
