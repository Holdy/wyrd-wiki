#title
Dafydd's skull

#summary
A maid at Ffagnallt Hall threw a skull she had been told not to touch into the horse pond, and that night the household found her in the pond clutching it and crying that the Dead Man's curse was upon her. The skull is said to be that of Dafydd ap Owain Gwynedd, poisoned at the hall for a reward, who swore the house would be plagued while his head was away.

#details
Ffagnallt Hall, a 15th-century manor near Rhes-y-cae on Halkyn Mountain, keeps part of a skull in a case. The story was first told in 1861 by Margaret Butler Clough in "The Vengeance of the Skull". A maid named Anne was told never to dust a bone on the bookcase; convinced it housed a malicious sprite, she threw it into the horse pond. That night a shriek woke the household, and they found her in the pond clutching the skull and crying that the Dead Man's curse had come upon her. The skull was put back and she slowly recovered. Clough said the skull belonged to Dafydd ap Owain Gwynedd, a fugitive poisoned at the hall by his cousin's husband for a reward, whose head was then displayed above the gates of Chester. Dafydd had cursed the household to be plagued while his head was absent, until it returned to the traitor's threshold. The traitor was trampled to death by cattle, and his heir brought the skull back to the hall, where it remains. The real Dafydd ap Owain Gwynedd, however, was never a hunted fugitive and died in England in 1203.
