#title
The Curse of Miramare Castle

#summary
Built by Archduke Ferdinand Maximilian of Austria as a romantic seaside sanctuary, Miramare Castle became bound to a generational curse that brought violent ends, madness, and tragedy to its owners and subsequent occupants. Anyone who takes residence or stays too long within its walls is marked for misfortune.

#details
Commissioned in the mid-19th century by Archduke Ferdinand Maximilian of the Austrian Habsburg-Lorraine dynasty for his wife, Charlotte (Carlota) of Belgium, Miramare Castle was erected upon a stark limestone cliff overlooking the Adriatic Sea. Shortly after moving into their dream home, Maximilian accepted the ill-fated crown of Mexico, leading to his capture and execution by a firing squad in 1867. His wife, Carlota, suffered a catastrophic psychological collapse and spent decades in secluded madness. Over subsequent generations, the curse extended outward to various royal residents, guests, and military commanders who stayed at the castle—including Crown Prince Rudolf, Empress Elisabeth of Austria, and Archduke Franz Ferdinand, all of whose lives ended in assassinations, suicides, or tragic downfalls.

#location
Miramare Castle stands on the rocky promontory of Grignano along the Gulf of Trieste, just northwest of Trieste, Italy.

#title
The Vienna Possession of 1583

#summary
A sixteen-year-old girl underwent extensive exorcisms at a Jesuit chapel in Vienna after allegedly being possessed by more than 12,000 demons. Following the extraction of the entities, her grandmother was accused of witchcraft, tortured, and executed.

#details
In 1583, a sixteen-year-old girl from the village of Manx near Vienna began suffering from severe cramps and seizures, leading local authorities to determine she was possessed by evil spirits. She was sent to Vienna and placed in the Jesuit chapel of St. Barbara for daily exorcisms. Over eight weeks, priests reportedly succeeded in expelling 12,652 demons, whose overwhelming presence made the girl's body so heavy that horses pulling her transport cart sweated profusely. Under pressure from her interrogators, the girl claimed her seventy-year-old grandmother, Elisabeth Pleinarcher, had caused the possession by giving her a bewitched apple containing the devil. Pleinarcher was arrested, tortured until she confessed to witchcraft and consorting with animal-shaped demons, and burned alive at the Richplatz in Vienna.

#location
The exorcisms were performed at the historic Jesuit chapel of St. Barbara in Vienna, Austria.

#title
The Woman in Black of Pinzgauer Bundesstrasse

#summary
Since 1981, motorists driving along an Austrian highway have reported encountering a mysterious hitchhiking woman in black who issues a chilling warning before vanishing. Believed to be the spirit of a tragic traffic victim, she forewarns drivers that they would have crashed had they not stopped for her.

#details
Motorists traveling along the Pinzgauer Bundesstrasse in the Salzburg region of Austria have frequently reported seeing a young woman dressed entirely in black standing by the roadside, attempting to flag down rides. According to recurring accounts dating back to 1981, once a driver picks her up and she is inside the vehicle, she turns to them and says, "If you hadn’t stopped and picked me up, you would have had an accident," immediately after which she vanishes into thin air. Local lore associates her with the ghost of a 23-year-old waitress from St. Veit who lost her life in a horrific road crash nearby in 1980, when her car ran off the road and straight into the path of a speeding train on the parallel tracks.

#location
The Pinzgauer Bundesstrasse is a federal highway running through the Pinzgau and Pongau regions in the Salzburg region of Austria.
