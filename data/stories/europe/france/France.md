#title
The Curse of Lourmarin Castle

#summary
Following the eviction of transient occupants from a ruined Renaissance fortress by its new owner, a revengeful curse was said to have been laid upon anyone who would subsequently inhabit or restore the site, linked to strange cabalistic graffiti found inside. This dark legend gained further notoriety after a series of tragic misfortunes struck those connected to the building, including the untimely death of author Albert Camus.

#details
In 1921, Robert Laurent-Vibert, an industrialist from Lyon, purchased the dilapidated Château de Lourmarin in the south of France and began extensive restoration works. To clear the building, he forced out the travelers and groups who had been squatting within its ancient towers and chimneys. According to popular legend, before departing, these occupants left behind cryptic cabalistic symbols and engravings on the plaster walls and pronounced a severe curse intended to strike down anyone who ever tried to claim or govern the property. Just a few years later, in 1925, Laurent-Vibert died an accidental death, feeding the growing local superstition. The mystery of the "curse" deepened over the decades, reinforced by strange etchings in the tower's "blue room" depicting figures, a large cross, and a perilous boat, and was further cemented in the public imagination following the tragic fatal car crash of Nobel Prize-winning author Albert Camus in January 1960, who had deep ties to Lourmarin. Modern historical research has since revealed that the mysterious wall markings are actually 16th-century ex-voto carvings left by persecuted Protestants or refugees during the Wars of Religion, but the captivating legend of the castle's dark curse remains a persistent part of regional folklore.

#location
The Château de Lourmarin is situated at the foot of the Luberon mountains in the village of Lourmarin within the Vaucluse department of the Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur region in southeastern France.

#title
The Demonic Possession of the Ursuline Nuns of Loudun

#summary
In 1632, an entire community of Ursuline nuns at a convent in Loudun, France, fell victim to violent mass demonic possession, allegedly orchestrated by a local parish priest. The ensuing public exorcisms and witchcraft trials gripped the region and ended in a tragic execution.

#details
Beginning in 1632, Prioress Jeanne des Anges and over two dozen Ursuline nuns at the newly established convent in Loudun, France, began experiencing severe convulsions, speaking in tongues, and uttering blasphemies. The possessed nuns accused Father Urbain Grandier, a popular and controversial parish priest of St-Pierre-du-Marché and canon at the Church of Sainte-Croix, of using black magic and demonic pacts to possess them with evil spirits such as Asmodai. Although Grandier had never previously met many of the nuns, local political enemies and church authorities capitalized on the panic amidst tensions over town fortifications and a recent plague outbreak. Grandier was arrested, tortured, and forced to participate in humiliating public exorcisms alongside the writhing nuns at the Church of St. Croix. He maintained his innocence throughout, but was convicted of sorcery by ecclesiastical and secular judges and burned alive at the stake in the public square in 1634. Public exorcisms and claims of demonic manifestations continued for several years until the mother superior's afflictions finally subsided.

#location
The events centered around the Ursuline convent and the Church of St. Croix in the historic town of Loudun, located in the Poitou region of France.

#title
The White Lady of Palavas-les-Flots

#summary
Four young travelers picking up a hitchhiker in a white raincoat near a bridge witness her suddenly vanish from the moving vehicle after she warns them of a dangerous bend.

#details
On May 20, 1981, four young friends drove to the coastal town of Palavas-les-Flots for an evening out. Around midnight, as they headed back toward Montpellier, they spotted a hitchhiker on the side of the road just before the Pont des Quatre Canals. The woman, appearing to be in her fifties and wearing a white raincoat and scarf, silently climbed into the car and sat in the back. A kilometer further down the road, as the vehicle approached a sharp, dangerous turn, she abruptly shouted, "Watch out for the bend! Watch out for the bend!" Startled, the driver slowed down and negotiated the curve safely. Moments later, the terrified passengers watched in disbelief as the woman vanished completely into thin air while the car was traveling at 90 km/h with all doors closed. The shaken friends reported the incident to the police, who confirmed through separate interviews that all four witnesses gave identical, sincere accounts of the phantom passenger.

#location
The encounter occurred on the roadway just before the Pont des Quatre Canals in the coastal commune of Palavas-les-Flots in southern France.
