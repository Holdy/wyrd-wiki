#title
The Deacon of Dark River

#summary
A deceased deacon rides his horse on a winter night to pick up his unsuspecting fiancée, revealing his skeletal head during their journey back to his grave. When they reach the churchyard, he attempts to pull her into an open grave before she manages to escape.

#details
A deacon living at the Myrká farm in Iceland's Hörgárdalur valley was deeply in love with a housemaid named Guðrún who lived across the Hörgá river at Bægisá. While attempting to cross the river to visit her, the bridge broke due to melting ice and the deacon hit his head and drowned. Unaware of his death, Guðrún eagerly awaited him on Christmas Eve when he arrived on horseback to take her to a party. As they rode together along the path toward the river, the deacon's hat slipped forward in the moonlight, revealing his bare skull with a white spot on the back. He recited a chilling poem about death riding, but Guðrún maintained a terrified silence until they reached the lychgate at Myrká. There, the deacon asked her to wait while he moved his horse, but as she glanced toward the graveyard, she saw an open grave. The ghost tried to drag her inside, but she escaped after he ripped away the empty sleeve of her coat, leaving her haunted until a sorcerer eventually exorcised the spirit.

#location
The story centers around the Myrká graveyard and the church at Ytri-Bægisá in the Hörgárdalur valley of northern Iceland.

#title
The Possession of Sangeeta Persaud at Canal Number Two Polder

#summary
Following sudden and extreme behavioral changes attributed to demonic possession, a fourteen-year-old girl was subjected to day-long prayers and exorcism rites at a local church. Despite efforts by her pastor and church members to cast out the evil spirits, she was later hospitalized and tragically passed away.

#details
In March 2010, fourteen-year-old Sangeeta Persaud (also known as Sheena) of Canal Number Two Polder began exhibiting alarming and abnormal behavior, including screaming, grunting like a pig, and barking like a dog. Convinced that she had been taken over by evil spirits, her family sought help from Pastor Ewart Cummings of the Christ Ambassador Church. The pastor initially prayed at her home, but as the manifestations persisted, Sangeeta was brought to the church itself. There, unceasing prayers and religious interventions were performed throughout the day by church members to free her from the alleged demonic possession. Once she became calm, she was rushed to the West Demerara Regional Hospital, where she unfortunately succumbed. The case subsequently sparked widespread public attention and an official inquiry by the Ethnic Relations Commission to address the circumstances surrounding the tragic event.

#location
The Christ Ambassador Church is located in the agricultural community of Canal Number Two Polder on the West Bank of Demerara in Guyana.

#title
The Sels-Móri or Ghost of Þorgarður

#summary
A vengeful spirit cursed a family to torment their female descendants across nine generations after a housewife swept away the ransom money that would have spared the ghost's life. Following the family wherever they moved, the entity drove several generations of women to madness and paranoia.

#details
The legend begins at a farm near the Elliðaá river, close to Reykjavík, where a farm worker named Þorgarður was rumored to have an affair with the farmer's wife. When the farmer was found dead in the river under suspicious circumstances following a storm, Þorgarður was immediately blamed and sentenced to hanging. Unable to pay the fine to redeem his life, Þorgarður begged a kind statesman named Jón for help. Jón began counting out the ransom money, but his wife, Guðrún, swept the coins away into her apron, declaring, "Let each one suffer for his actions." 

Furious, Þorgarður cursed them, vowing that his farewell would follow her and her family to the ninth generation. After Þorgarður was executed, his spirit returned as an ancestral *fylgja* ghost known as Sels-Móri. True to his word, the entity latched onto Guðrún and relentlessly haunted the female lineage of the family through multiple generations. As descendants moved to locations like Skildinganes, Bessastaðir, and across Hellisheiði, the malevolent entity followed them, causing psychological torment, sudden distress, and severe madness in successive female descendants until the curse finally expired at the ninth generation.

#location
The farm of Sel in Seltjarnarnes, east of Reykjavík, Iceland, near the Elliðaá salmon river.
