#title
The Curse of the Mir Castle Orchard

#summary
When Prince Nikolai Svyatopolk-Mirsky ordered a blooming apple orchard to be cut down to dig a decorative pond, a local wise woman cursed the waters, leading to his untimely death and a lingering toll of lives. To this day, locals believe the lake demands a life every year as a consequence of the destroyed spring blossoms.

#details
At the end of the 19th century, Prince Nikolai Svyatopolk-Mirsky acquired the historic Mir Castle and decided to remodel the surrounding landscape. In May 1898, he ordered his peasants to clear away a flourishing, blossom-laden apple orchard to make space for a large artificial lake. According to popular belief, destroying a blooming tree is a grave sin that destroys an innocent soul, and the local peasants fiercely resisted the order, warning the Prince of dark repercussions. Ignoring their pleas, the Prince pushed forward with the construction. An old local woman whose relatives had tended the trees pronounced a severe curse upon the site, declaring that the disrupted earth would exact a bloody toll. Not long after, Prince Nikolai died suddenly under mysterious circumstances, his body discovered floating lifelessly in the very waters he had created. The curse outlived him, and down through the decades, folklore maintains that the lake claims at least one human life every single year.

#location
The castle pond and park grounds are located immediately adjacent to Mir Castle in the urban village of Mir, Karelichy District, Grodno Region, Belarus.
