The Curse of Ragnhild Tregagås

Summary

In fourteenth-century Norway, a woman named Ragnhild Tregagås placed a malicious marital curse on her former lover and his new bride, invoking dark forces to induce impotence and tear the marriage apart.

Details

In 1324, during a wedding in Fusa, Norway, a woman named Ragnhild Tregagås sought vengeance against her former lover, Bård, who had married another woman named Bergljot. Acting out of jealousy, Ragnhild crept into the newlyweds' bedroom and laid a curse by concealing five loaves of bread, five peas, and a nearby sword in their bed. She invoked spirits and valkyries in a ritualistic charm intended to render Bård impotent and fill the couple with mutual hatred and envy. The curse succeeded, causing the couple to divorce shortly thereafter. When Ragnhild's boasting of her sorcery reached church authorities, she was arrested and brought before Bishop Audfinn Sigurdsson in Bergen in 1325, resulting in medieval Norway's earliest documented witchcraft trial.
