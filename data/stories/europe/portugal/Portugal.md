#title
The Curse of Santa Engrácia

#summary
Condemned to death for a desecration he did not commit, an innocent man placed a dying curse on a Lisbon church, dooming its reconstruction to centuries of collapse, financial ruin, and unending delay. This engineering nightmare ultimately gave birth to the enduring Portuguese idiom "Works of Santa Engrácia."

#details
In 1630, after the interior of the Church of Santa Engrácia was desecrated, the local religious brotherhood falsely accused an innocent man named Simão Pires de Solis. Despite having a solid alibi, Solis was tortured, dragged through the streets of Lisbon, and burned to death at the stake. Although the true culprit confessed a year later and cleared Solis's name, it was too late. Before his death, Solis cursed the church's rebuilding project, vowing that it would never be finished and would remain in a state of eternal limbo. True to his dying words, subsequent reconstruction efforts were repeatedly stalled by political and financial crises, culminating in a severe 1681 storm that caused the newly built chancel to completely collapse. The roofless skeleton of the church sat abandoned for nearly two hundred years, spawning the national phrase *Obras de Santa Engrácia* ("Works of Santa Engrácia") as a synonym for any endless, unending project. Construction finally resumed and was completed in the 20th century, transforming the cursed structure into Portugal's National Pantheon.

#location
The Church of Santa Engrácia (National Pantheon) is located in the historic Alfama neighborhood of Lisbon, Portugal.

#title
The Legend of Teresa Fidalgo

#summary
A young female hitchhiker picked up by motorists on a remote mountain road warns of her fatal accident before vanishing into thin air.

#details
Motorists driving along the dark, forested roads of the Sintra mountains in Portugal frequently encounter a pale young woman standing by the roadside seeking a ride. After getting into the vehicle, she behaves with growing anxiety and unease. As they approach a specific curve ahead, she points forward and reveals that she died there in a tragic car crash years prior, before screaming in terror and disappearing from the backseat.

#location
The events take place along Estrada do Vale dos Anjos in Colares, situated within the Sintra mountain range in Portugal.

#title
The Tragic Burning of Arminda de Jesus

#summary
In February 1933, superstitious villagers in the hamlet of Oliveira, Soalhães, accused a 32-year-old woman named Arminda de Jesus of being possessed by the devil and burned her alive to purge the perceived demons.

#details
In the winter of 1933, within the remote hamlet of Oliveira in the parish of Soalhães, Portugal, a horrific tragedy rooted in religious superstition and mass hysteria took place. A 32-year-old egg vendor named Arminda de Jesus was targeted by local villagers who became convinced that she was possessed by the devil. Swept up in fervent belief and manipulated by fanaticism, four men dragged Arminda between two stone houses in the hamlet and threw her into a massive bonfire, believing they were casting out evil spirits. The brutal murder shocked the nation, later inspiring Bernardo Santareno’s famous 1959 play *O Crime da Aldeia Velha* and remaining deeply embedded in local folklore as the ultimate dark legend of the region.

#location
The events took place in the hamlet of Oliveira, within the civil parish of Soalhães in the municipality of Marco de Canaveses, Portugal.
