#title
The Cow Clout Stone

#summary
A flat stone on the farm of Arvie preserves the miraculous impressions of a kneeling man's prayer, a horse's hoof, and a cow's foot as a lasting memorial against a ruthless landlord's seizure of distressed livestock. 

#details
On the farm of Arvie in the parish of Parton, there lies a flat rock approximately three feet in diameter known locally as the "Cow Clout" Stone. According to tradition recorded in the mid-19th century, a harsh landlord sought to clear severe rent arrears by forcibly driving away the hypothecated stock amidst fierce resistance from the tenants. While one desperate tenant knelt in prayer upon the stone to plead for relief against his oppressors, the seized cattle were driven over the rock, followed closely by a mounted officer. The rock miraculously retained the impressions of the man's knee and foot—complete with the knot of his garter—alongside the hoofprint of the horse and the foot of a cow, remaining as an enduring memorial to posterity of the cruel deed.

#location
The Cow Clout Stone is located on the farm of Arvie (near Ken-Ervie) in the parish of Parton, Kirkcudbrightshire (OS Grid Reference NX 6694 7336).

#title
The Headless Woman of Buckland Bridge

#summary
A farmer and his farmhand narrowly escape a violent encounter after encountering a headless woman on Buckland Bridge, who warns them of hidden robbers in the Glen.

#details
In the early 1900s, a farmer from Monkland was returning home to Kirkcudbright with a young farmhand near midnight. As they crossed Buckland Bridge over Buckland Burn, the farmer's pony suddenly panicked, nearly throwing him from the saddle. The farmhand then spotted a figure standing near the bridge—clearly a woman with no head. The farmer confirmed the sighting, and they immediately took an alternative route home. Later, they learned that men had been hiding in Buckland Glen intending to rob the farmer, an encounter the headless woman's warning had prevented.

#location
Buckland Bridge over Buckland Burn in Buckland Glen, Kirkcudbrightshire, Scotland.
