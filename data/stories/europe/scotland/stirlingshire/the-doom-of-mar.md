#title
The Doom of Mar

#summary
After John Erskine dismantled Cambuskenneth Abbey to build his grand Stirling mansion, an enraged abbot placed a multi-generational curse on his family, foretelling blindness, ruin, and the extinction of their line. True to the prophecy, subsequent generations suffered blinded children and tragic political downfall before the bloodline finally died out.

#details
In the 16th century, John Erskine, the Earl of Mar, decided to construct a lavish townhouse—known today as Mar's Wark—at the head of Broad Street in Stirling. To hasten and grandly fund its construction, he ordered the systematic dismantling of the nearby historic Cambuskenneth Abbey, stripping away its hallowed stone and timber. 

According to local tradition, the last abbot of the ruined monastery was incandescent with rage at the sacrilege. Standing before the desecrated holy site, he pronounced a sweeping, multi-part curse upon Erskine and his descendants, which became known as the "Doom of Mar." The abbot's prophecy declared that the Erskine family would face profound ruin: three of the Earl's descendants would be born completely blind, their grand house would never be finished, and the noble line would ultimately suffer total extinction. 

As the decades unfolded, the terrifying components of the curse appeared to systematically materialize. The construction of Mar's Wark was indeed halted forever, leaving behind an evocative ruin. Later, generations of the family experienced heavy political downfalls, and true to the abbot's dark words, multiple children in the family line were born blind. The family's tragic trajectory culminated centuries later when the direct line finally met its end, sealing the legacy of the abbey's revenge.

#location
Mar's Wark looms over Broad Street in the historic old town of Stirling, Stirlingshire.
