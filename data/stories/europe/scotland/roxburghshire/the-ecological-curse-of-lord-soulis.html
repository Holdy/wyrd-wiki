The Ecological Curse of Lord Soulis

Summary

Following the gruesome execution of the wicked sorcerer Lord Soulis on a desolate border ridge, a lasting blight fell upon the landscape, ensuring that certain plants would never grow where his cauldron boiled. This environmental curse remains eternally bound to the site of his punishment.

Details

In the fourteenth century, the infamous William de Soulis ruled Hermitage Castle with unspeakable cruelty, practicing black arts alongside his demonic familiar Redcap. Protected by sorcery from ordinary steel and rope, he could not be slain by normal means. Driven to desperation by his tyranny, the local people of Liddesdale seized him and carried him up to the ancient stone circle on Ninestane Rig. There, wrapping him in a sheet of lead, they suspended a giant cauldron over a roaring fire between the standing stones and boiled him to death. As a lasting testament to his dark end and execution, a curse settled upon the ground itself: it was declared that upon the very spot where the pot was boiled, neither the spreat rush nor the deer-hair grass shall ever grow.
