The Curse of Lady Margaret Sinclair

Summary

Following the devastating loss of Caithness clansmen at the Battle of Flodden, Lady Margaret Sinclair became bound by an enduring curse of eternal grief. Her unyielding sorrow transformed her into a spirit guard, haunting the castle ruins and mist-laden peatbogs of the far north.

Details

In 1513, three hundred men from Caithness marched south under the banner of William Sinclair, 2nd Earl of Caithness, to participate in the fateful Battle of Flodden, where King James IV and most of the Scottish nobility were slain. Only a single soldier boy returned to tell the tale. Left behind at Castle Sinclair-Girnigoe, the Earl's wife, Lady Margaret Sinclair, was consumed by overwhelming sorrow. Over the centuries, her tragic grief hardened into an enduring legend and curse, binding her spirit as an eternal guard to the crumbling fortress and surrounding myres to lament the ravages of human conflict and environmental destruction.
