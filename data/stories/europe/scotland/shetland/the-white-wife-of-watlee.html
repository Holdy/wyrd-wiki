The White Wife of Watlee

Summary

The White Wife of Watlee is a legendary ghost figure on the island of Unst in Shetland, Scotland. She is said to appear to single men driving alone on a remote stretch of road near Loch Watlee.

Details

The White Wife of Watlee is a well-known figure in Unst folklore and has even inspired a local ale named after her. The ghostly apparition is said to appear to single men driving alone on a remote stretch of road near Loch Watlee. One notable encounter involved Steven Spence, an Unst fiddler, who had a close brush with the spectral figure while driving home one dark January night about 20 years ago.

Spence was driving down the Watlee Brae, just before the Watlee Burn, when he noticed what he thought was moonlight inside his van. Upon looking around, he saw the White Wife sitting in the seat next to him. She was transparent, grey, and had a smile that Spence described as unforgettable. The apparition vanished moments later.

Another encounter involved Alan Hunter, who also saw an old grey woman appear beside him in his car. He later learned that an elderly female relative had died in Uyeasound at the exact same time. Both men's experiences highlight the enduring legend of the White Wife and her association with the road near Loch Watlee.
