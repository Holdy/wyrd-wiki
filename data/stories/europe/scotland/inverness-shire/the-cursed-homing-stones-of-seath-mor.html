    The Cursed Homing Stones of Seath Mòr

    Summary

    The 14th-century grave of Clan Shaw chief Seath Mòr in Inverness-shire is topped with five protective stones carrying a lethal curse that strikes anyone who tampers with them or removes them from the tomb.

    Details

    Deep within the Rothiemurchus estate in Inverness-shire lies the historic kirkyard of Saint Kaldas, home to the burial site of Seath Mòr Sgòrchiaclach, a 14th-century chief of Clan Shaw. Resting on top of his tomb are five cylindrical granite boulders known as "homing stones," which tradition says represent the warriors who survived alongside him at the famous Battle of the Clans at Perth in 1396. According to local folklore, these stones are fiercely protected by a supernatural guardian spirit, and a terrible curse befalls anyone who dares to touch, move, or throw them away. Numerous cautionary tales warn of the deadly consequences: a footman who once tossed one of the stones into the nearby River Spey was found drowned three days later, and a journalist who lifted a stone over his head died in a car accident just hours afterward. Due to the lingering dread of the curse and ongoing attempts by thrill-seekers to test their fate, an iron cage was erected over the tomb in 1983 to keep the stones permanently secured.
