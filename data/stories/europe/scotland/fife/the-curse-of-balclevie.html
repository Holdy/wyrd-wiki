The Curse of Balclevie

Summary

Angered by the destruction of her village to improve the estate's view, a local woman cursed the Anstruther family to hold Elie House for only six generations. True to the prophecy, the family's fortunes eventually waned and they were forced to sell the estate.

Details

When Lady Janet Anstruther and her husband undertook extensive renovations and landscaping around Elie House in the 18th century, she reportedly complained that the nearby rustic village of Balclevie disrupted her view of the distant loch. Driven by this aristocratic desire for an unobstructed landscape, the cottages of the small village were cast down. Incensed by the high-handed eviction and destruction of their homes, a local resident laid a curse upon the family, declaring that the Anstruthers would remain at Elie House for only six generations. Centuries later, after enduring a decline in family fortunes, the cash-strapped sixth generation was indeed forced to sell the ancestral estate in 1853, bringing the old legend to fulfillment.
