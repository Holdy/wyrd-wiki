#title
The Boghall Brownie

#summary
After being mistakenly offered warm blankets during a harsh winter, a loyal farm brownie curses the garments and abandons the homestead forever, leading to its permanent decline in prosperity.

#details
At Boghall Farm, a diligent brownie worked tirelessly every night, cleaning and tending to the property while the household slept, asking for nothing more than a bowl of porridge and fresh milk. One exceptionally bitter winter, the well-meaning mistress of the farm placed warm blankets on his straw bed in the barn out of concern for his comfort. Offended by this gift—as providing clothing or bedding banishes brownies—the spirit recited a rhyming verse leaving his curse upon the blankets and departed instantaneously. Following his departure, the farm never recovered its former happiness or fortune.

#location
Boghall Farm is located near the town of Biggar in South Lanarkshire, Scotland.

#upstream-source
George Douglas, Scottish Fairy and Folk Tales (1892)

#title
The Woman of Glespin

#summary
A tramp named Elizabeth Campbell died on a road near Glespin in 1832, leading to local tales of her ghost haunting the area where she collapsed.

#details
In 1832, a tramp named Elizabeth Campbell collapsed on a road near Glespin, South Lanarkshire, while traveling from Glasgow. Believed to be carrying cholera, she was hastily buried in Windrow Woods without proper rites. Her grave, marked by a simple headstone reading "Eliz. Cambel," became the center of a local haunting legend, with residents avoiding the woods after dark and parents warning children about the "baby brought by the doctor from Windrow Wood."

#location
The road near Glespin, Douglas, South Lanarkshire, where Elizabeth Campbell collapsed and died in 1832.

Hutchison, J.D., *Douglasdale: Its History and Traditions* (1940)
