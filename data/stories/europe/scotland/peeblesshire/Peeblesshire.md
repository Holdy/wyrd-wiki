#title
The Man in the Tweed Bonnet

#summary
A man dressed in old-fashioned clothing, including a tweed bonnet with no visible face, is seen standing by the roadside on the A75 near Peeblesshire. He vanishes as quickly as he appears.

#details
The A75, known as Scotland's Ghost Road, is infamous for its eerie encounters. One such tale involves a man in a tweed bonnet with an eerily featureless face standing by the roadside. This spectral figure, reminiscent of a bygone era, has been reported multiple times, leaving witnesses baffled and unsettled.

#location
The A75 is a stretch of road that runs through Peeblesshire.

#title
The Prophecy of Merlin's Grave

#summary
An ancient conditional prophecy foretold that Scotland and England would not be united under one monarch until the River Tweed and the Powsail Burn met at Merlin's grave. This long-delayed union condition was miraculously fulfilled centuries later on the exact day King James VI and I was crowned.

#details
According to local tradition rooted in the dark-age legends of the seer Lailoken (often identified as Merlin), the wild prophet was buried near the confluence of the Powsail Burn and the River Tweed in Drumelzier after meeting his famous 'triple death'. Over generations, a curious prophetic curse or conditional rhyme became attached to his resting place: “When Tweed and Powsail meet at Merlin's grave, Scotland and England shall one monarch have.” For centuries, the waters flowed separately, keeping the crowns divided. However, on the exact day that James VI of Scotland was crowned as King of England, the River Tweed mysteriously burst its banks, swelling out to meet the Powsail Burn precisely at Merlin's grave and finally fulfilling the historic prophecy.

#location
Merlin's Grave is located beside the Powsail Burn just west of Drumelzier Kirk in the Tweed Valley, Scottish Borders.
