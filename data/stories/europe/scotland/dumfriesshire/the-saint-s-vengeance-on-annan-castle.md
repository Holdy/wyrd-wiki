#title
The Saint's Vengeance on Annan Castle

#summary
After a nobleman broke his solemn promise to spare a condemned prisoner, an outraged Irish saint placed a devastating curse upon the household. This hex caused a catastrophic flood to destroy their ancestral stronghold and compelled future descendants to seek out his tomb to lift the burden.

#details
In the 12th century, the Irish bishop St Malachy stayed as a guest of Robert the Bruce II at the family's motte-and-bailey stronghold in Annan, Dumfriesshire. Upon learning that a local robber was scheduled for execution, St Malachy pleaded with Bruce to show mercy, and the lord gave his word that the man's life would be spared. However, as the saint was leaving the town, he discovered that the robber had been hanged anyway in defiance of the promise. Enraged by the deceit, St Malachy placed a curse on the Bruce household, prophesying that misfortune would plague them. Shortly thereafter, a severe flood swept away a large portion of the castle's motte and destroyed the fortifications, forcing the family to abandon Annan and relocate their headquarters to Lochmaben. Generations later, future Scottish king Robert the Bruce even travelled to St Malachy's enshrined tomb in Burgundy, hoping to finally lift the ancient curse.

#location
The site of the historic Annan motte-and-bailey castle is located by the River Annan in the town of Annan, Dumfriesshire.
