#title
The Bloodsucking Chieftain of Slaghtaverty

#summary
A tyrannical 5th-century warlord terrorized his subjects with bloodlust, returning repeatedly from the grave after execution until vanquished by magical means. Buried upside down beneath a heavy stone cairn, his resting place remains marked to this day.

#details
Abhartach was a brutal chieftain who ruled a small kingdom in what is now County Londonderry. Feared by his people for his dark powers and cruelty, he was eventually overthrown and slain by a neighboring chieftain named Cathain. However, death could not hold him; Abhartach rose from his grave demanding a bowl of blood from his terrified subjects. Though Cathain killed him a second time, the tyrant returned once more. Seeking guidance, Cathain learned from a holy figure that Abhartach was an undead creature who could only be permanently subdued by a sword made of yew wood and burial upside down underneath a massive stone. Cathain carried out these instructions, trapping the tyrant beneath the earth at Slaghtaverty.

#location
The grave of Abhartach, known as the Slaghtaverty Dolmen, is located in the townland of Slaghtaverty near Garvagh in County Londonderry, Northern Ireland.

#upstream-source
Dr Geoffrey Keating, *Foras Feasa ar Eireann* (A General History of Ireland); Patrick Weston Joyce, *The Origin and History of Irish Names of Places*.

#title
The Ghosts of Maggie Murphy's Lane

#summary
Maggie Murphy, her lover, and her enraged father are said to haunt Maggie Murphy's Lane following their tragic deaths in the 1880s. Witnesses report spectral figures and chilling phenomena along the road.

#details
According to the Derry Journal, Maggie Murphy and her lover—a farmhand from an unsuitable background—died under mysterious circumstances in the 1880s, alongside Maggie's father. Their ghosts are believed to linger on Maggie Murphy's Lane, which runs from Kilfennan to Nelson Drive. Locals claim to hear wailing and see shadowy figures, prompting many to cross the street rather than walk past the lane. The haunting was documented in Peter McCartney's *Parade of Phantoms*.

#location
Maggie Murphy's Lane, connecting Kilfennan to Nelson Drive in Derry, Northern Ireland.

#upstream-source
Peter McCartney's *Parade of Phantoms*
