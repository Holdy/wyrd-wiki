#title
The Curse Of Macha

#summary
Macha, a goddess in human form, is forced to race against horses while pregnant and dies giving birth. She curses the men of Ulster to be weak during their greatest need.

#details
At Emain Macha, the royal site of the ancient kings and queens of Ulster, King Conor McNessa hosts a great feast with important guests, including Crunniuc and his companion, who is actually the goddess Macha in disguise. Crunniuc boasts about Macha's speed, leading the king to demand she race against his prized horses. Despite being heavily pregnant, Macha agrees to save Crunniuc's life. She wins the race but goes into labor and gives birth to twins at the finish line, dying shortly after. Before her death, Macha blesses the men of Ulster with great strength but curses them to be weak as a woman in childbirth during their greatest need.

#location
The story is set at Emain Macha, also known as Navan Fort, which can be found on a map near Armagh.

#title
The Green Lady of Vicar's Hill

#summary
A young woman, Bellina Prior, is accused of killing a child and later returns as a ghost to haunt Vicar's Hill in Armagh.

#details
Vicar’s Hill in Armagh was once a fashionable address for merchants, professionals, and vicars. However, its reputation changed dramatically after the tragic events at No. 6 in 1888. Bellina Prior, just 21 years old and daughter of a respected military commander, was accused of killing Annie Slavin, whose body was found in a kitchen boiler. Despite her protests of innocence, witnesses recalled her crying, “I have killed the child.” Found guilty but insane, she was committed to an asylum and later released under the condition that she never return to Armagh.

After Bellina’s death, stories circulated about her ghost haunting Vicar's Hill. Locals reported icy chills, whispers, and scratching at windows. Some claimed to see a pale face through the letterbox or a spectral figure in green. Children were warned of the Green Lady, and various encounters with her ghostly presence were reported over the years.

#location
Vicar’s Hill is one of Armagh’s most elegant streets, located near St Patrick’s Cathedral.
