McCabe's Curse on Nassau

Summary

A bitter prisoner named McCabe cast a vengeful, generation-spanning curse upon the town of Nassau in 1814, decreeing that its citizens' graves would forever be desecrated and their town plagued. This poetic malediction was preserved and later unearthed by journalists, etching itself into local lore.

Details

In the year 1814, a prisoner named McCabe was held in Nassau, New Providence. Deeply aggrieved and filled with malice toward the authorities and townspeople, he penned a fierce and poetic curse upon the settlement before his demise. The curse decreed that the inhabitants would never rest in peace, wishing that their graves would be rooted up by hogs and their community subjected to continuous misfortune across generations. Long after McCabe's time, the text of the curse was brought to light by an American journalist named William Drysdale, who lived in Nassau, cementing the legend within Bahamian historical folklore.
