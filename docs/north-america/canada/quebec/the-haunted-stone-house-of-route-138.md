#title
The Haunted Stone House of Route 138

#summary
Built in the late 18th century on land granted in 1746, the decaying stone house ruins along Route 138 near Laiterie Charlevoix became the subject of local folklore claiming it was haunted or used by the devil for a witches' sabbath.

#details
Situated along Route 138 near the Charlevoix Dairy in Baie-Saint-Paul, an old stone house built between the English Conquest of 1759 and 1800 stood without foundations directly on the ground. After being inhabited until the late 19th century, the building was abandoned and gradually fell into ruins, with trees eventually growing inside its empty shell. Over generations, mischievous local imagination transformed the eerie, crumbling structure into a reputed haunted house where villagers whispered that the devil himself might hold his sabbaths. Historians note that while these accounts are pure fantasy, the open-air ruin remains a striking testament to the region's early stone architecture.

#location
The ruins of the historic stone house are located along Route 138, near the Laiterie Charlevoix in Baie-Saint-Paul, Quebec.
