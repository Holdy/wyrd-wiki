    The White Lady of Montmorency Falls

    Summary

    Following the death of her fiancé during the 1759 Battle of Montmorency, a grieving young woman donned her wedding gown and threw herself into the falls, where her ghostly white figure is still said to haunt the mist.

    Details

    Set against the backdrop of the 1759 Siege of Quebec during the Seven Years' War, local folklore on the Côte-de-Beaupré tells of a young woman named Mathilde who was eagerly anticipating her wedding to her beloved, Louis Tessier, having spent the summer tailoring her white bridal gown. When British forces launched attacks along the north shore, culminating in the Battle of Montmorency on July 31, local militia men including Louis joined the French defense. Though the battle resulted in a French victory, Louis was among the casualties who never returned from the front lines. Overcome by profound grief upon learning of his death, Mathilde dressed in her wedding gown and veil, walked to the cliffs of the Montmorency Falls where she and Louis used to spend time, and plunged into the churning waters below. Ever since, locals and visitors have reported sightings of a slender female silhouette in a white wedding dress roaming the mist near the falls—particularly on moonlit nights—while the secondary waterfall nearby, known as the Chute de la Dame Blanche, preserves her tragic memory.
