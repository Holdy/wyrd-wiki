The Poltergeist of Brooks Avenue

Summary

In late 1951, a Chilliwack chicken farmer and her teenage niece were driven to exhaustion by weeks of violent exterior bangings and mysterious disturbances that attracted massive crowds of onlookers.

Details

Beginning in November 1951, 38-year-old chicken farmer Anna Duryba and her 14-year-old niece, Kathryn, found themselves tormented by weeks of strange noises and broken windows at their home on Brooks Avenue. Heavy, rapid bangings echoed against the outer walls of the house on a regular basis, sometimes occurring dozens of times in a single week. The disturbances were severe enough to prompt Kathryn to temporarily relocate to Vancouver while her aunt tried to cope with the chaos.

Local figures weighed in on the mystery, with Reverend William Clarke publicly diagnosing that a poltergeist was at large. Despite repeatedly telling reporters that she did not believe in ghosts, Anna found herself exhausted not only by the unexplained noises and broken glass, but also by the hordes of curious townspeople who flocked to her property to gawk at the house, creating severe parking and traffic problems for the neighborhood.
