#title
The Resident Ghost of the Greenwood Courthouse Jail

#summary
In the basement jail of Greenwood's historic 1903 Courthouse, a prisoner named Charlie took his life in 1915, and his spirit is said to still haunt the solitary confinement cell.

#details
Constructed between 1902 and 1903 during the height of the local mining boom, the Greenwood Courthouse served as the Supreme Court of Yale and later became the city's municipal hall. Down in its stone basement, three original holding cells were built to house prisoners awaiting trial or transport. According to local historical records and walking tour accounts, the first cell—designated for solitary confinement—is haunted by a resident ghost known as "Charlie," an inmate who hanged himself within the cell on July 6, 1915. Visitors and ghost tour guides exploring the dimly lit basement holding cells frequently report eerie phenomena, shadowy figures near the old coal bin entranceway, and an overwhelming sense of a lingering presence.

#location
The basement jail of the Greenwood Courthouse (City Hall) located on South Government Street in Greenwood, British Columbia.
