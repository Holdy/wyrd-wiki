    The Phantoms of Stave Falls

    Summary

    Staff and visitors at a historic hydroelectric powerhouse in Mission have reported chilling encounters with disembodied voices, shadow figures, and phantom footsteps echoing through the cavernous plant. Eerie occurrences include tour guides bumping into invisible presences and employees spotting unexplained reflections in empty windows.

    Details

    Built between 1909 and 1912 on the edge of Stave Lake, the Stave Falls Powerhouse once served as British Columbia's largest hydroelectric power source before becoming a designated National Historic Site. Over the decades, staff members and visitors have experienced numerous inexplicable phenomena after hours and during tours. Employees frequently report hearing loud bangs, disembodied echoes, and phantom footsteps bouncing across the industrial floors. On one occasion, a tour guide stepped backward and physically bumped into someone, only to turn around and find nobody there. Another employee was left startled after catching the clear reflection of a dark-haired man standing right behind her in a window when she was completely alone in the room.
