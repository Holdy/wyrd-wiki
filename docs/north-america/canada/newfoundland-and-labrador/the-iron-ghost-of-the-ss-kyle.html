The Iron Ghost of the SS Kyle

Summary

The historic sealing steamer SS Kyle broke its moorings during a 1967 winter storm and drifted silently across Conception Bay to run aground at Harbour Grace, becoming an enduring maritime landmark.

Details

Built in Newcastle-on-Tyne, England, and launched in 1913, the 1,055-ton steamship SS Kyle served for decades as a vital link along the Newfoundland coast and as a rugged sealing vessel on the icy northern waters. On February 4, 1967, during a massive storm, the ship broke its moorings in St. John's and drifted driverless across the bay in what local lore recalls as a ghost-drift, finally running aground on a bed of mussels at the Riverhead in Harbour Grace. Stranded and battered by decades of weather, the rusting iron hulk remains upright in the mud, often referred to as a ghost ship or iron ghost that evokes the memories of its bygone sealing crews.
