    The Restless Ghost of St. Paul’s

    Summary

    An unfulfilled deathbed wish to be buried in a secluded coastal cove leads a deceased settler named Old Martin to haunt his former neighbours in St. Paul's, Newfoundland. When severe winter weather prevents his requested burial, his spirit returns on Boxing Day, relentlessly wailing and demanding to be moved from his temporary garden grave.

    Details

    On the west coast of Newfoundland at St. Paul's Bay, an aging, reclusive settler named Old Martin fell gravely ill. Before passing away, he made his neighbour promise to bury him in a specific spot in a cove around the south head, warning that if his wish was ignored, he would never rest in peace and would haunt the settlement. Because violent winter storms made reaching the cove impossible, the villagers temporarily buried Martin in his garden with the intention of moving him in the spring.

    On Boxing Day, while villagers gathered at a home for a festive dance, the local dogs began howling frantically. A chilling series of raps echoed at the door, followed by a ghostly light and a despairing voice wailing, "I can't lay here; oh! I can't lay here." When a local man opened the door to confront the noise, he saw the apparition of Old Martin outside. The terrifying sight paralyzed his arm, and the haunting continued with mysterious knocks, freezing drafts, and ghostly disruptions night after night until the residents eventually relocated their settlement closer to the coast.
