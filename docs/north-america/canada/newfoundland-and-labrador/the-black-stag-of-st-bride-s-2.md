#title
The Black Stag of St. Bride's

#summary
In 1804, a Newfoundland man and later his family met mysterious, fatal ends on the winter road between Point Lance and St. Bride's after repeatedly encountering a spectral black caribou stag that was impervious to gunfire.

#details
In the winter of 1804, a hardworking young man named Thomas Conway set out by horse and sled from St. Bride's to deliver winter provisions to his father in Point Lance. While traveling along the snowy cross-country trail, he encountered an impressive caribou stag with coal-black fur standing in a roadside clearing. Attempting to hunt it for fresh meat, Thomas fired at the animal three times at close range, but each shot had no effect and left the stag completely unconcerned. When the creature turned and stared at him with unsettling, unnatural eyes, overwhelming dread seized Thomas, causing him to flee in terror toward Point Lance. Later that evening, as he drove back toward St. Bride's in the dark, his pony froze in terror near the same clearing. The following morning, searchers found Thomas dead on his sled at the clearing with his powder spilled across his face, entirely uninjured and with no signs of struggle. Sometime later, his wife Emily and infant son—traveling the same route to have the baby christened—along with two companions, also perished near the clearing, their bodies found scattered in the snow after fleeing an invisible terror.

#location
The key events take place along the coastal winter road and forest clearing situated between the communities of Point Lance and St. Bride's on the Cape Shore of Placentia Bay, Newfoundland and Labrador.
