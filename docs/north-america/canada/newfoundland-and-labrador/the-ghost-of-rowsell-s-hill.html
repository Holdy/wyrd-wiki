    The Ghost of Rowsell's Hill

    Summary

    Rowsell's Hill is said to be haunted by the spirit of Thomas Rowsell, a settler who was ambushed and beheaded by Beothuk warriors in 1789. Over the generations, locals have reported ghostly phenomena and eerie encounters tied to the historic tragedy at the brook.

    Details

    In 1789, Thomas Rowsell operated a salmon weir on the brooks of New Bay while trapping in the region. Regarded as an aggressive frontiersman and Indian killer by the native population, he was eventually targeted for revenge. While working unattended at his salmon weir, he was ambushed by Beothuk warriors, beheaded, and killed. In the years that followed, the hill and brook overlooking the site became deeply rooted in local folklore, generating ghost stories, phantom sightings, and traditional cautionary tales about the lingering spiritual unrest tied to the spot of his violent death.
