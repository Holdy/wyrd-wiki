    The Goudie Witch of Burlington

    Summary

    In Burlington, Newfoundland, the spirit of a feared local woman is said to haunt her isolated burial site, which miraculously survived a devastating town fire and now produces cursed blueberries that cause painful, fruit-flavored boils.

    Details

    In the community of Burlington, located in Newfoundland's Green Bay region, residents have long passed down the legend of the Goudie Witch. Described as a formidable and widely feared figure who lived to an extraordinary age, she was not buried in the communal cemetery after her death. Out of fear of her lingering malevolence, the townspeople buried her separately and enclosed the gravesite with a white white picket fence to contain her spirit. Years later, when a massive fire swept through Burlington and nearly leveled the entire settlement, the flames miraculously halted at the boundary of her burial ground, leaving it completely untouched. In the wake of the fire, unusually large wild blueberries began to thrive over the grave. Local lore sternly warns against eating these berries, telling of a hapless local man who ate them and woke up covered in hideous boils that tasted precisely of blueberries, drawing scavenging crows to peck at his skin.
