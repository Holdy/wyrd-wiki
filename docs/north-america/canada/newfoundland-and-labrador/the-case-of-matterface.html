The Case of Matterface

Summary

An English clerk who died in St. Lawrence in 1835 was preserved in a barrel of rum that was later unknowingly raided by locals, and his ghost now haunts Turpin's Island where his remains were buried.

Details

Around 1835, an English clerk named Matterface working at Newman and Company's plantation in St. Lawrence passed away. Because embalming wasn't possible, his body was preserved inside a barrel of rum intended to be shipped back to England for burial. However, locals unaware of the barrel's true contents drank the tainted liquor. The barrel—with Matterface still inside—was eventually buried in the cove on Turpin's Island in Little St. Lawrence. Folklore claims that Matterface's ghost haunts the island; years later, cousins camping in a tent on the island heard mysterious footsteps circling them before their campfire was suddenly snuffed out.
