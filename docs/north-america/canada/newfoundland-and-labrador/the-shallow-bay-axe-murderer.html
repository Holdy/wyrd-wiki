    The Shallow Bay Axe Murderer

    Summary

    In 1809, a brutal double axe murder at Shallow Bay near Cow Head led to the execution of trapper John Pelley, whose grim legacy evolved into local folklore where parents warn misbehaving children that his ghost will come for them.

    Details

    In April 1809, Sarah Singleton grew alarmed when her brother, trapper Richard Cross, failed to return from checking his trap lines north toward Shallow Bay, near Cow Head. Accompanied by John Payne, Sarah undertook a grueling thirty-mile trek along the coast until they reached a remote cabin occupied by the notoriously ill-tempered trapper John Pelley. Pelley acted evasively and claimed he had not seen Cross. The following morning, Sarah discovered her brother's familiar handmade mittens and snowshoes stashed behind the cabin, realizing Pelley had murdered him. After hiking back to Rocky Harbour to assemble a rescue party, Sarah and John returned to confront Pelley, bound him, and extracted a confession. Pelley admitted to murdering both Richard Cross and Joseph Rendells with a hatchet following trivial disputes. Pelley was transported to St. John's, tried, convicted, and publicly hanged in September 1809. Over the generations, the horrifying true crime morphed into local Newfoundland folklore, inspiring theatrical plays and cautionary tales that Pelly's ghost haunts the area. The actual axe used in the double homicide is preserved at Cow Head's community museum.
