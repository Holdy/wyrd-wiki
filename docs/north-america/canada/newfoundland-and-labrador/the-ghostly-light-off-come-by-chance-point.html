    The Ghostly Light Off Come-By-Chance Point

    Summary

    In the early twentieth century, residents of Placentia Bay frequently witnessed an eerie red glow rising from the water off Come-By-Chance Point during storms, believed to mark the site where three sailors tragically drowned.

    Details

    Recounted by author Charles Jamieson in a 1928 issue of the Newfoundland Quarterly, the legend dates back to a boyhood memory from around 1913. Jamieson and an elderly local resident stood watching the water as a dull red glow appeared to rise directly out of the depths of the sea off Come-By-Chance Point. The old man explained the origin of the phenomenon: years prior, three men in an open boat departed Sound Island in high spirits, singing as they sailed toward Come-By-Chance. A sudden, violent storm overtook them, overturning their vessel and drowning all three. According to local folklore, whenever a strong wind blows in harsh weather, a strange red light rises at the exact spot of the tragedy, often accompanied by faint, eerie cries and groans. Additionally, an inland swamp just behind Come-By-Chance Point held a parallel reputation for mysterious "Jack-o'-lantern" lights that would lure travelers astray.
