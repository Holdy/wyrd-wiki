The Ghost of the Bay of Dead Men

Summary

Local legend holds that Trepassey—historically named La Baie des Trépassés or the Bay of Dead Men—is haunted by the souls of shipwreck victims, including a British aristocrat who washed ashore after a fateful storm.

Details

Derived from the French trépassés meaning "dead men" or "souls," Trepassey Bay earned its ominous moniker from centuries of devastating shipwrecks along its rocky coast. Among the enduring folklore of the bay is the legend of Lady Mary Alice Tucker, a noblewoman who fled Georgian London with an Irish sailor named Patrick Molloy in 1770 aboard a brig called the Fanny. When a white squall shattered their ship near the Grand Banks, the couple washed ashore near Trepassey. In local ghost lore, the bay remains perpetually haunted by the restless spirits of those claimed by its freezing waters, where phantom figures and unearthly cries are said to echo along the shore.
