    The Ghostly Voyage of Ellen Dower

    Summary

    While her husband was sealing miles away on an ice-locked schooner, a Conche woman seemingly fell into a deathlike trance while her apparition travelled across the frozen ocean to visit him in his cabin. When she revived at home, her husband independently reported the exact same supernatural encounter, confirming she had searched his sea chest for forgotten property deeds.

    Details

    In March 1872, Edward "Ned" Dower sailed from the small fishing community of Conche on Newfoundland's Great Northern Peninsula aboard the schooner Elsie for the annual seal hunt. Unbeknownst to him, a box containing crucial financial papers and property deeds had been accidentally left inside his sea chest. Back home, Ellen Dower grew overwhelmingly anxious about the missing documents. On March 25th, Ellen collapsed into a catatonic, deathlike trance at home, her body growing ice-cold with no detectable pulse as family and neighbors gathered for a wake. At that exact same time, far out at sea amidst the ice floes, Ned Dower was resting in his cabin aboard the Elsie when he heard familiar rustling and smelled his wife's perfume. To his utter shock, the apparition of Ellen appeared in his room, silently pulled the sea chest out from under his bunk, opened the document box to inspect the papers, and vanished. Convinced this was a death token warning of her passing, Ned and his crew prepared to return home in mourning. However, back in Conche the following day, Ellen suddenly regained consciousness, bolted upright, and whispered of her impossible journey across the ice and rocks to find the Elsie and ensure the papers were safe. When the Elsie finally tied to the wharf, both husband and wife corroborated every detail of the uncanny event.
