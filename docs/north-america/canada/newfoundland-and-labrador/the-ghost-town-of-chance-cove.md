#title
The Ghost Town of Chance Cove

#summary
Following the tragic shipwreck of the *Anglo-Saxon* in 1863, the isolated fishing village of Chance Cove was allegedly abandoned overnight after its residents were driven out by the nightly hauntings and screams of the drowned.

#details
Chance Cove was a small, isolated fishing settlement of about fifty people on the southern shore of the Avalon Peninsula. Rumours long dogged the community that its residents lured ships onto the jagged coastline by lighting false fires, subsequently plundering cargo and murdering survivors. In April 1863, the steamship *Anglo-Saxon* struck the shore nearby, resulting in the drowning of over 230 people, many of whose bodies washed ashore and were buried on nearby banks. According to local legend, the ghosts of the drowned began surfacing every night to torment the villagers, with activity peaking around the anniversary on April 27. The nightly apparition of spectral wails, phantom ship bells, and haunting cries from the shore became so terrifying that the entire population fled the village overnight, leaving their homes fully furnished with laundry still drying on the lines. A delivery man visiting the village found every house empty and silent. Later fishermen who attempted to inhabit the abandoned settlement were equally spooked by supernatural occurrences and eventually burned the remaining structures. Today, visitors to Chance Cove Provincial Park continue to report hearing disembodied cries, ships' bells, and phantom footsteps in the mist.

#location
Chance Cove Provincial Park on the southern shore of the Avalon Peninsula, Newfoundland and Labrador.
