The Blood-Stained Wharf of Salmon Cove

Summary

Following the brutal 1833 murder of planter John Snow at his Salmon Cove fishing stage, the site became steeped in dark local folklore and ghostly legends.

Details

On August 31, 1833, John Snow, a prominent planter in Salmon Cove, mysteriously vanished after a stormy domestic dispute. Local authorities launched an intense investigation that was grimly confirmed when pools of dried blood were discovered on John Snow's fishing stage along the Salmon Cove shore. His servant Arthur Spring and cousin Tobias Mandeville were arrested and confessed that Snow had been murdered and his body thrown into the Atlantic at the instigation of his wife, Catherine Snow. Catherine was later convicted and executed in St. John's as the last woman hanged in Newfoundland, though she maintained her innocence until her death. In the years following the murder, locals whispered of eerie noises, phantom sightings, and supernatural unease lingering around the Salmon Cove wharf where the blood was first found.
