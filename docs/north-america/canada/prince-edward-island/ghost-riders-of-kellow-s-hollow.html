    Ghost Riders of Kellow's Hollow

    Summary

    After early settler Jack Connaway was fatally thrown from his horse near Kellow's Hollow bridge in Cornwall, Prince Edward Island, residents subsequently reported sighting his ghost alongside a procession of white-robed phantoms vanishing into a massive local pine tree.

    Details

    Jack Connaway, one of the earliest settlers of the Cornwall area, lived near the site where East Wiltshire School stands today. One night while riding home from town, he attempted to cross the Kellow's Hollow bridge when a bloodcurdling scream shattered the night air and startled his horse. The horse reared and threw Jack against the bridge rail, killing him instantly—an event witnessed by James Kellow. Following the tragedy, locals claimed Jack's restless spirit haunted the bridge. The most striking account came from his brother, Michael Connaway, who was riding home along the same road under a full moon. As Michael approached the bridge, his horse abruptly halted; he saw a cluster of glowing lights transform into twenty white-robed figures wearing white skull caps. Leading them was the spectral form of Jack Connaway, who drove the ghostly procession toward a great hollow pine tree near the bridge, where all of them vanished into its trunk.
