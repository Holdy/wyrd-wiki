The Phantoms and Poltergeists of Fulford Place

Summary

Built at the turn of the 20th century by a wealthy patent-medicine tycoon, the grand Edwardian mansion of Fulford Place in Brockville is renowned for ghostly footsteps, moving objects, and spirit encounters.

Details

Fulford Place was constructed between 1899 and 1901 by George Taylor Fulford, who amassed a massive fortune from his patent medicine, "Pink Pills for Pale People." Following the family's occupancy and the mansion's eventual transition into a historic museum maintained by the Ontario Heritage Trust, staff, volunteers, and visitors began reporting persistent paranormal activity. Caretakers over the decades noted mysterious balls of light, books violently flying off library shelves, and sudden drops in temperature. Modern ghost-hunting groups and paranormal investigators visiting the estate have recorded disembodied voices, spirit names, and cold sensations using specialized equipment like spirit boxes and the Estes method, cementing the mansion's reputation as one of eastern Ontario's most active haunted sites.
