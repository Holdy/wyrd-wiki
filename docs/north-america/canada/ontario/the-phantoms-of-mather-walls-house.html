The Phantoms of Mather-Walls House

Summary

Built in 1889 in Kenora, Ontario, the historic Queen Anne-style Mather-Walls House is widely reported to be haunted by unexplained footsteps, self-playing pianos, and moving furniture.

Details

The 130-year-old Mather-Walls House, overlooking Portage Bay in the Keewatin neighbourhood of Kenora, was originally constructed for David Mather by his father John Mather. Over the decades, caretakers, museum staff, and visitors have reported numerous chilling paranormal experiences. Workers have frequently heard disembodied footsteps marching across the upper floors and stairwells, doors opening and closing by themselves, and sudden blasts of cold air in the breezeway. Most famously, former historical society president Arend Visser and student workers witnessed an antique piano playing by itself in the music room while out of tune, and a heavy rocking chair spontaneously beginning to rock back and forth with no breeze present. Paranormal investigators and psychics visiting the heritage site have also claimed to sense the lingering presences of past residents.
