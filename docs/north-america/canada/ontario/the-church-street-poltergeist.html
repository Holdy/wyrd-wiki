The Church Street Poltergeist

Summary

In February 1970, an apartment at 237 Church Street in St. Catharines became the site of a heavily documented poltergeist outbreak centered around an eleven-year-old boy, which was witnessed by multiple police officers and clergymen.

Details

In early February 1970, Niagara Regional Police officers responded to a domestic dispute call at Apartment 1 of 237 Church Street in St. Catharines. Upon arriving, officers found the apartment in total disarray with furniture and household objects displaced by unseen forces. Over the course of several weeks, responding officers—including Bob Crawford and Bill Weir—alongside local clergy, witnessed extreme paranormal phenomena. Bowling trophies flew off shelves one by one, a kitchen wall clock unplugged itself and fell without making a sound, a heavy chest of drawers slid across the room, and beds and chesterfields levitated while people were sitting on them. The epicenter of the disturbances was the family's eleven-year-old son, Peter, who was occasionally pinned to the floor by heavy furniture or targeted by invisible entities before the family was advised to temporarily relocate.
