The Haunting of Oak Hill

Summary

Built in 1860 by Welland Canal pioneer William Hamilton Merritt, the historic Oak Hill estate in St. Catharines has long held a reputation for hauntings, marked by unexplained noises, phantom odours, and eerie phenomena in its historic rooms and underground tunnels.

Details

Following the destruction of his previous home by an arsonist fire, William Hamilton Merritt constructed Oak Hill around 1860 at the corner of St. Paul Street West and Yates Street in St. Catharines. Merritt was a prominent merchant and the driving force behind the construction of the first Welland Canal, as well as an abolitionist associated with the Underground Railroad, featuring basement tunnels leading down toward Twelve Mile Creek. After Merritt's death, the estate passed through various private owners and served as a military convalescent home and an inn before being purchased in 1938 to house the broadcasting studios of CKTB radio. Over the decades, staff and visitors have reported a steady stream of unexplained noises, strange drafts, phantom odours, and anomalous presences throughout the grand old home.
