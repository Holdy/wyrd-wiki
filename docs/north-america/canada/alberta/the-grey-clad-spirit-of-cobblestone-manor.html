    The Grey-Clad Spirit of Cobblestone Manor

    Summary

    Built by a reclusive craftsman as a tribute to a romance that never materialized, the historic stone-faced Cobblestone Manor in Cardston is said to be haunted by a grey-clad phantom roaming its staircases and rooftop parapet.

    Details

    In 1913, Belgian finishing carpenter Henry Hoet purchased an early pioneer log cabin in Cardston and spent the next sixteen years meticulously expanding it into an architectural masterpiece. Hauling river rocks from nearby Lee's Creek and utilizing rare hardwoods left over from the construction of the monumental Cardston Alberta Temple, Hoet crafted elaborate honeycombed ceilings, custom bookshelves, and imported Italian stained-glass windows. Local legend maintains that Hoet built this grand dwelling as a labor of love for his sweetheart, who tragically never came to Canada, leaving him a lifelong bachelor. In the decades following, visitors, residents, and security personnel have reported paranormal occurrences within the residence, most notably sightings of a grey-clad ghost wandering through the historic rooms, ascending the wooden staircase, and standing upon the rooftop parapet.
