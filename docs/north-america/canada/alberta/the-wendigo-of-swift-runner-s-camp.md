#title
The Wendigo of Swift Runner's Camp

#summary
During the harsh winter of 1878–1879 at his isolated hunting camp south of Athabasca, a Cree man named Swift Runner became possessed by the malevolent windigo spirit, resulting in a horrific tragedy that entered regional folklore.

#details
In the winter of 1878–1879, a Cree guide named Swift Runner (Ka-Ki-Si-Kutchin) took his family—consisting of his wife, mother, brother, and six children—into the wilderness to a winter camp located south of Athabasca, Alberta. When Swift Runner later returned alone, an investigation by the North-West Mounted Police uncovered a horrifying scene at the camp, where the remains of his family members had been scattered. Swift Runner confessed that a monstrous, supernatural half-beast known in Algonquin folklore as the windigo had entered his mind, filling him with an insatiable hunger and compelling him to slaughter and consume his own relatives. This grim event became one of the most infamous real-world accounts of windigo spirit possession in Canadian history, leading to Swift Runner's trial and execution.

#location
Swift Runner's winter camp was located in the forested wilderness situated south of Athabasca, Alberta.
