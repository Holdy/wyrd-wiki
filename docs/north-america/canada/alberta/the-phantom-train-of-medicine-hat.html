The Phantom Train of Medicine Hat

Summary

In 1908, railway workers twice encountered a glowing phantom train on the tracks near Ross Creek coulee, a haunting premonition that preceded a fatal collision.

Details

In June 1908, CPR engineer Bob Twohey and fireman Gus Day were heading east from the Medicine Hat station toward Dunmore. As their train rounded a cutbank in the Ross Creek coulee, an oncoming train with a brilliant headlight appeared, gliding laterally off the tracks before vanishing into thin air while the spectral crew waved a greeting. Shaken by the eerie encounter, Twohey sought a fortune teller who predicted his death within a month, prompting him to take time off. Days later, fireman Gus Day experienced the exact same apparition along a new engineer, J. Nicholson. On July 8, 1908, Nicholson was operating the same locomotive alongside fireman H. Thompson, while Bob Twohey—having returned to work—was at the throttle of an inbound train from Lethbridge. The two real trains collided head-on near the exact spot of the phantom sightings, killing Twohey, Nicholson, and several crew members and passengers.
