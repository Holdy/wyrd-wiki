The Burnt Mines of Storr Pits

Summary

Following suspected arson and a series of devastating fires and explosions in the 1830s, Stellarton’s earliest coal mine became permanently abandoned and steeped in local lore as the "Burnt Mines."

Details

Opened in 1827 by the General Mining Association on the west bank of the East River, the Storr Pits were Stellarton's very first coal mine. In 1832, a malicious fire broke out underground, killing 14 horses and resulting in an investigation that concluded the blaze was deliberately set by locals opposed to the mining monopoly. After repeated subsequent explosions and fires in 1834, 1836, and 1839, the workings were permanently sealed and abandoned, earning the ominous moniker "The Burnt Mines" as haunting symbols of early industrial turmoil and tragedy.
