    The Great Amherst Poltergeist

    Summary

    In 1878, an 18-year-old woman living in a modest cottage became the center of a notorious poltergeist disturbance involving flying objects, spontaneous fires, and menacing physical attacks. Observers across North America documented the bizarre manifestations that plagued her for over a year.

    Details

    In the late summer of 1878, eighteen-year-old Esther Cox was living with her sister Olive Teed and her family in a house on Princess Street in Amherst, Nova Scotia. Following a severe personal trauma, Esther began experiencing violent and inexplicable phenomena. Her bed shuddered, objects flew across the room of their own accord, and scratching sounds echoed through the house. When local doctors, clergymen, and curious visitors arrived to investigate, they witnessed bedclothes shifting, objects hovering, and chilling messages—such as "Esther Cox, You Are Mine to Kill"—scratched into the wall above her bed. Despite fleeing to other towns and staying with various families, the supernatural disturbances followed Esther wherever she went. American actor Walter Hubbell later moved into the Teed home as a lodger to investigate, documenting the phenomena and briefly touring with Esther before public skepticism, fires attributed to the haunting, and subsequent legal trouble brought the spectacle to a close.
