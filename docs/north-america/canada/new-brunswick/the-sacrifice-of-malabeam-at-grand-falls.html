    The Sacrifice of Malabeam at Grand Falls

    Summary

    A young Wolastoqiyik woman named Malabeam sacrificed her life to protect her people by luring an enemy invasion party over the massive waterfall on the Saint John River.

    Details

    According to regional oral history and accounts compiled in L’Histoire de Madawaska, a war party of invading Mohawks attacked villages along the Wolastoq (Saint John River) and captured two women—Nécomah and Malabeam—to serve as guides downriver toward the major village of Aucpaque. Knowing that the invaders would destroy her people if they successfully navigated past the cataract, Malabeam agreed to guide them by night. As they approached the roaring 23-metre drop at Grand Falls, Malabeam kept the warriors calm until they were trapped in the deadly current. She plunged over the waterfall alongside the canoes, sacrificing her life to save her village. In commemoration of her heroic act, the Wolastoqiyik name for Grand Falls is Chik-un-ik’-a-bik, meaning "The Destroyer."
