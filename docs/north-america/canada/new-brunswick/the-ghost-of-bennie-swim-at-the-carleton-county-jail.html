    The Ghost of Bennie Swim at the Carleton County Jail

    Summary

    Following his botched, twice-performed execution in 1922, the ghost of Bennie Swim is said to haunt the historic Carleton County Jail in Woodstock, where water taps turn on by themselves and a frost-free circle mysteriously forms on a high window in winter.

    Details

    In October 1922, twenty-year-old Bennie Swim was condemned to death and hanged at the Carleton County Jail in Woodstock, New Brunswick, for a double murder. The execution became a notorious local scandal because it was severely bungled: after being dropped through the scaffold trapdoor, Swim was cut down too soon and was discovered to still have a pulse, necessitating a second hanging an hour later. Long after the jail closed and the building transitioned into office and storage spaces, former inmates, staff, and visitors reported unexplainable paranormal phenomena. Witnesses described hearing unexplained noises, water taps turning themselves on and off, and a distinct circle of frost clearing on a high window during freezing winter months, allowing the lingering spirit of Bennie Swim to seemingly gaze out across the town.
