The Spirits of McRaven House

Summary

Built in 1797 by a notorious highwayman, Vicksburg's McRaven House is haunted by multiple spirits, including a young bride who died in childbirth and soldiers from its days as a Civil War field hospital.

Details

The McRaven House was originally constructed in 1797 by highwayman Andrew Glass, who used the isolated structure to hide his ill-gotten loot from travelers along the Natchez Trace. Following Glass's death, the home passed through several owners. In 1836, Sheriff Stephen Howard's 15-year-old wife, Mary Elizabeth, died in the middle bedroom while giving birth. Her personal belongings, including her wedding shawl, remain in the house, and her apparition is frequently sighted roaming the rooms. During the Civil War, the house served as a Confederate field hospital, enduring cannon blasts before its owner, John H. Bobb, was fatally shot nearby. Later residents, including the Murray family, also left their mark, and ghostly phenomena ranging from disembodied footsteps to shadow figures continue to be reported by modern visitors.
