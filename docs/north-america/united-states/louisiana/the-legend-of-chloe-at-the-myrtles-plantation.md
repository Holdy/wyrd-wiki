#title
The Legend of Chloe at the Myrtles Plantation

#summary
According to local Louisiana folklore, an enslaved woman named Chloe poisoned her master's family in a desperate bid to win back favor, resulting in her execution and a haunting that has endured for generations.

#details
In the nineteenth century at the Myrtles Plantation just outside St. Francisville, Louisiana, an enslaved woman named Chloe served as governess for Judge Clark Woodruffe and his family. Unhappy about being forced into the judge's unwanted romantic advances and terrified of being cast out to work in the grueling fields, Chloe took to eavesdropping on the household. When the judge caught her listening at a door, he punished her brutally by severing one of her ears, forcing her to wear a green turban to hide the wound. Desperate to regain her standing, Chloe decided to bake a birthday cake laced with poisonous oleander leaves, intending to make the family only slightly ill so she could heroically nurse them back to health. Tragically, the poison was too strong, killing the judge's wife and two daughters. Terrified that the judge would retaliate against all the enslaved residents, a group of her peers caught Chloe, lynched her, and threw her weighted body into the Mississippi River. Ever since, visitors and staff have reported sightings of a spectral woman in a green turban wandering the historic halls and lifting mosquito netting from beds, making the estate one of the most famous haunted landmarks in the American South.

#location
The Myrtles Plantation, located just outside St. Francisville, Louisiana.
