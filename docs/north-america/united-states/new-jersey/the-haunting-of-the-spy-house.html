The Haunting of the Spy House

Summary

The historic Seabrook-Wilson Homestead along the Sandy Hook Bay is plagued by reports of phantom children, old sailors, and rocking chairs moving on their own in locked rooms.

Details

The Seabrook-Wilson Homestead, widely known as the Spy House, is a weather-beaten wooden building dating back to around 1650 located on Port Monmouth Road in the Port Monmouth section of Middletown, New Jersey. Local legend claims that during the Revolutionary War, patriot innkeeper Thomas Seabrook used the tavern to get British soldiers drunk and extract military secrets. While historians debate the veracity of wartime espionage, generations of visitors and park rangers have reported eerie encounters with spectral inhabitants. Witnesses have described seeing a young boy in colonial attire peering from an upstairs window before backing away into an empty, locked room. Other visitors looking into the darkened building have witnessed an old man reading in a rocking chair that rocked back and forth entirely on its own inside the boarded-up homestead.
