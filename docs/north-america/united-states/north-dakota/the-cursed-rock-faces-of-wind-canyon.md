#title
The Cursed Rock Faces of Wind Canyon

#summary
After a trapper and his family refused to abandon their home in the Dakota Badlands, an indigenous shaman cursed them to remain there forever. Consequently, their likenesses were etched into the rocky bluffs overlooking the Little Missouri River for eternity.

#details
In the early 1800s, a solitary trapper living along the Little Missouri River fell in love with a French widow, brought her to his isolated homestead, and started a family in what is now the North Dakota Badlands. As tensions mounted between encroaching settlers and local indigenous tribes, a shaman visited the family and demanded they leave their land. When the trapper firmly refused, declaring they would never leave their home, the shaman chanted a curse ensuring the white family would be trapped there forever. Shortly thereafter, a smallpox outbreak swept the area, claiming the lives of the wife, son, and trapper. Over the years, wind and water sculpted the soft rock bluffs along Wind Canyon into human-like faces that watch the sunset, fulfilling the shaman's curse.

#location
The rock faces are located along the Wind Canyon trail in the South Unit of Theodore Roosevelt National Park in western North Dakota, overlooking the Little Missouri River.
