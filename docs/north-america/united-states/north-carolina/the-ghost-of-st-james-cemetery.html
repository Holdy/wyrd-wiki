The Ghost of St. James Cemetery

Summary

Mistakenly pronounced dead following an argument in 1810, Samuel Jocelyn was buried alive at a Wilmington church cemetery, where his grieving ghost is said to still linger near his grave.

Details

In 1810, following a violent argument with his wife at a hunting lodge, a young man named Samuel Jocelyn rode out into the Honey Island Swamp in the middle of winter, where he was found cold, stiff, and unresponsive the next day. Believed dead, he was quickly buried in the historic cemetery behind St. James Episcopal Church in Wilmington. Three days later, Jocelyn reportedly appeared in the dreams of two friends, begging them to dig him up. When his friends finally relented and exhumed the body, they discovered him lying in a dried pool of blood with his fingers completely clawed down to the bone from desperately trying to escape his casket. Local accounts and ghost tour guides state that his shadowy apparition continues to haunt the church grounds where he met his tragic fate.
