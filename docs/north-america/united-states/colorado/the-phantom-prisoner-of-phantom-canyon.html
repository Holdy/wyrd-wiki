The Phantom Prisoner of Phantom Canyon

Summary

Train passengers traveling through a rugged mountain gorge in the 1890s repeatedly reported seeing an executed convict walking along the tracks. Even after the railway was converted into a public road, spectral figures continued to haunt the route.

Details

During the 1890s, when the narrow-gauge Florence and Cripple Creek Railroad was operating through a remote mountain pass, train passengers were frequently startled by a grim apparition. Along the tracks, travelers would spot a man wearing a prison uniform, walking as though nothing was amiss. The deeply unnerving part of these encounters was that the man had been executed at the state prison in Cañon City just days prior to being seen wandering the canyon. Today, as motorists traverse the winding route known as Phantom Canyon Road, reports persist of ghostly encounters involving not only the executed prisoner but also the spirits of past miners and railroad workers.
