The Ghostly Performer of the Central City Opera House

Summary

The historic Central City Opera House is reportedly haunted by the spirit of Mike Dougherty, a 19th-century miner and stage performer who startles backstage visitors with sudden cold spots, hair-ruffling, and the lingering scent of alcohol.

Details

Built in 1878 in the historic mining hub of Central City, the Central City Opera House hosted countless entertainment troupes during Colorado's gold rush. Among them was Mike Dougherty, a miner who became a beloved local stage performer in 1865 before tragically drinking himself to death. Decades later, long after the theater went dark or during quiet hours, backstage visitors and staff have repeatedly reported being startled by a strong, fleeting odor of alcohol, stout nudges on the shoulder, and their hair being lightly ruffled from behind in empty corridors. Strange flickering orbs of light and ghostly footsteps on the balconies have also been documented by visitors exploring the historic landmark.
