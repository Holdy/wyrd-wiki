Robert the Doll

Summary

A handmade doll given to a young boy in Key West at the turn of the 20th century became the center of a chilling local legend involving unexplained movements, giggling, and lifelong paranormal activity.

Details

In 1904, four-year-old Robert Eugene "Gene" Otto received a life-sized, sailor-suited doll from a household servant. The boy named the doll after himself, and the two became inseparable companions. Throughout his childhood, Gene frequently blamed the doll for mischief and household disruptions, claiming, "I didn't do it. Robert did it." As Gene grew into an artist and eventually passed away in 1974, subsequent residents of the Otto home reported hearing phantom footsteps in the attic, mysterious giggling, and watching the doll shift position and change facial expressions on its own. In 1994, the doll was donated to the Fort East Martello Museum in Key West, Florida, where staff and visitors continue to report electronic malfunctions, unexplained occurrences, and misfortune befalling those who disrespect the doll without asking permission.
