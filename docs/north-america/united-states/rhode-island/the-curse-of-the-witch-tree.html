The Curse of the Witch Tree

Summary

A rural Rhode Island crossroads is said to be cursed by a suspected witch buried beneath an ancient oak tree, bringing misfortune and strange occurrences to those who pass. Drivers who circle the intersection multiple times are believed to summon the restless spirit.

Details

In the town of Smithfield, an old oak tree sprouted at the intersection of Mann School Road and Colwell Road in the early 19th century. As the rural roads were paved and traffic increased over the decades, motorists frequently suffered unusual accidents and fender-benders near the tree. Local folklore emerged claiming that an unremembered outcast or witch had been buried at the isolated spot, with the oak tree planted intentionally over her grave to trap her corpse. The enduring curse of the Witch Tree inspired legends that anyone who drives around the island and tree thirteen times will awaken the dormant witch. Even after the original tree was removed due to decay and replaced by a modern island with a guardrail, the local legend persists.
