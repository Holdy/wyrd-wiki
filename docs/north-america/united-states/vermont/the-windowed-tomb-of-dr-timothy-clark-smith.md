#title
The Windowed Tomb of Dr. Timothy Clark Smith

#summary
Plagued by a paralyzing fear of premature burial, a globe-trotting Vermont doctor had a custom underground crypt built with a glass viewing window so he could monitor his status in the afterlife.

#details
Dr. Timothy Clark Smith (1821–1893) was a worldly physician who served as a surgeon in the Russian army and a U.S. Consul in Odessa. Living during an era before modern embalming when mistaken death and premature burials were legitimate concerns, Smith developed an intense phobia of waking up inside a coffin under the earth. Following his sudden death at a hotel in Middlebury, Vermont, in February 1893, his son Harrison supervised the construction of a bizarre and specialized burial vault at Evergreen Cemetery in New Haven, Vermont. The brick crypt was built beneath a grassy mound, featuring stone steps leading to a subterranean chamber and a vertical cement shaft rising from the doctor's face to a 14-by-14-inch glass plate at the surface. Local legend immediately attached itself to the "tomb with a view," with generations of visitors whispering that Smith was entombed alongside a hammer, a chisel, or a hand-held bell so he could claw his way out or signal the living if he ever awoke.

#location
Evergreen Cemetery off Town Hill Road in New Haven, Vermont.
