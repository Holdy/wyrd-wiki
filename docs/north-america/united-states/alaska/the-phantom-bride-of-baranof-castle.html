The Phantom Bride of Baranof Castle

Summary

Following the 1867 Alaska purchase, a heartbroken Russian governor's daughter who allegedly died on her wedding night became a legendary apparition haunting a historic rocky promontory in Sitka.

Details

Following the transfer of Alaska to the United States in 1867, a persistent ghost story emerged regarding Castle Hill in Sitka, where a Russian governor's two-story wooden and brick building known as the castle once stood before burning down in 1894. According to local folklore, a young woman—variously described as a governor's daughter or a Russian princess—was forced into an unwanted arranged marriage. On her wedding night, amidst the celebrations, she vanished and was later found dead in a drawing-room. Visitors and soldiers stationed at the abandoned site reported encountering her sorrowful apparition draped in a dark robe, wandering the halls and cupola while leaving the faint scent of wild roses in her wake.
