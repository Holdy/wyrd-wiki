#title
The Curse of Portlock

#summary
Settlers and cannery workers in the remote Alaskan bay of Portlock were driven away by a decades-long curse of violent attacks, mysterious disappearances, and a monstrous presence haunting the settlement. Following years of mutilated bodies, eerie nocturnal screams, and a devastating fire at the local cannery, the entire community was forced to abandon the area by 1950.

#details
Originally visited by explorers in the 18th century who found abandoned native settlements and fled from sickness and terrifying mountain cries, Portlock Bay on the Kenai Peninsula later became home to a bustling salmon cannery and community of Russian-Alutiiq settlers in 1921. Despite strict rules, curfews, and armed guards established to protect against Nantiinaq—a legendary cannibalistic giant said to rule the surrounding forest—workers began turning up mutilated or disappearing entirely. After multiple violent incursions into the cannery building, including a devastating fire, and over fifteen recorded disappearances, the remaining residents fled overnight in 1950, leaving the area a desolate ghost town widely regarded as cursed ground.

#location
The historic Portlock cannery site is located on the southern edge of the Kenai Peninsula along the shores of Port Chatham bay in Alaska.
