The Haunted Tales and Curses of Euharlee

Summary

In the historic northwest Georgia town of Euharlee, visitors and landowners report chilling encounters with restless spirits at Mount Zion Cemetery's 19th-century graves and the town's 1886 covered bridge.

Details

Nestled along Euharlee Creek, the rural town of Euharlee carries scars from the Civil War when General William T. Sherman's troops camped on the land now known as Tilley Mill. Adjacent to the mill ruins lies Mount Zion Cemetery, where property owners and surveyors have repeatedly experienced a strange, unsettling phenomenon: an invisible force—described as a white cloth sweeping across their brows—whenever they tend to the tombstone of Leddy Rogers. A short distance away in the center of town stands the 1886 Euharlee Covered Bridge, which local folklore ties to an ancient witch's curse. Legends recount tragic figures haunting the bridge, including the sounds of a groaning hangman's rope and phantom footsteps, phenomena reinforced during overnight paranormal vigils when inexplicable boot and hoof prints appeared across guarded sand-covered floors.
