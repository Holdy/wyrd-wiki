The Ghostly Belle of the Wayside Inn

Summary

Room 9 of Longfellow's Wayside Inn in Sudbury is said to be haunted by Jerusha Howe, a 19th-century innkeeper's sister who died of a broken heart after her English fiancé failed to return.

Details

Jerusha Howe lived during the early 19th century and managed Howe's Tavern (later known as Longfellow's Wayside Inn) alongside her brother Lyman. Revered as the "belle of Sudbury" for her refinement, musical talents, and grace, Jerusha reportedly fell in love with a visitor from England who promised to return for her but never did. She never married, spent much of her life confined to her room above the old kitchen, and eventually passed away from tuberculosis in 1842. Ever since, guests staying in Room 9—often nicknamed the Hobgoblin Room—have reported paranormal occurrences, including phantom footsteps half-running and half-floating across the room, cold spots, and encounters with the spirit of Jerusha sitting by the fireplace or near her old chair. Visitors continue the tradition of leaving handwritten notes tucked into the room's ceiling joists and hidden drawers for the spectral belle.
