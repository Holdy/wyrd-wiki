The Black Angel of Oakland Cemetery

Summary

An 8.5-foot-tall bronze angel statue erected in an Iowa City graveyard in the early 20th century became the center of dark local folklore due to its eerie, oxidized black discoloration and tragic origins. According to local superstition, touching or kissing the monument brings swift death or terrible curses.

Details

In 1912, Teresa Dolezal Feldevert commissioned a monumental bronze angel from artist Mario Korbel to stand watch over the graves of her son, Edward, and her late husband, Nicholas, in Oakland Cemetery in Iowa City, Iowa. Originally cast in bronze, the statue gradually oxidized over the decades into a dark, greenish-black hue. As the monument darkened, generations of local students and thrill-seekers spun sinister legends to explain its morbid appearance, claiming that Teresa had been a witch or that the statue turned black due to hidden sins. Numerous taboos and curses arose around the monument, with local folklore warning that anyone who kisses or touches the statue will be struck dead, that virgins kissed before it will break its curse, and that young women who approach it in the moonlight will die within months.
