#title
The Tragic Apparitions of the Lemp Mansion

#summary
The historic Lemp Mansion in St. Louis is haunted by members of the wealthy brewing family and other lingering spirits following a legacy of tragic suicides and family turmoil.

#details
Beginning in the late 19th century, the Lemp family built an immense beer empire in St. Louis and transformed a 33-room Victorian mansion into their family home. However, the family was struck by a series of devastating tragedies, including the sudden death of favorite son Frederick and four family suicides—most notably patriarch William Lemp Sr., his son William Lemp Jr., sister Elsa Lemp, and brother Charles Lemp—all occurring via gunshot within the estate. Following the end of the brewing dynasty and decades as a declining boarding house, visitors and staff reported enduring paranormal phenomena, such as phantom footsteps, slamming doors, poltergeist activity, and spectral sightings. Notable ghosts include the "Monkey Face Boy" confined to the attic, the apparition of William Lemp Jr. peering over bathroom stalls, and the unmistakable scent of lavender linked to Lillian Lemp.

#location
The Lemp Mansion is located at 3322 De Menil Place in St. Louis, Missouri.
