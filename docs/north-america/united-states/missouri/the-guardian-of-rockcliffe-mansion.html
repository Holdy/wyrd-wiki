The Guardian of Rockcliffe Mansion

Summary

Perched high above the Mississippi River in Hannibal, Missouri, the grand Rockcliffe Mansion is said to be haunted by its original builder, lumber baron John J. Cruikshank, whose phantom footsteps still echo up the servant's staircase.

Details

Built between 1898 and 1900 by wealthy lumber baron John J. Cruikshank, Rockcliffe Mansion is a massive 30-room Gilded Age estate that once hosted Mark Twain in 1902. After Cruikshank died in his bed in 1924, his family abruptly abandoned the home, leaving it fully furnished, vacant, and deteriorating for over four decades while local children dared each other to explore its ruins. Following its eventual restoration and conversion into a bed and breakfast, caretakers, guests, and owners reported numerous ghostly phenomena. Most famously, witnesses hear the heavy boot steps of Cruikshank climbing the servant's staircase around 2:00 a.m. just as he did in life, while his indentation has occasionally been found rumpling the bed linens in his former bedroom.
