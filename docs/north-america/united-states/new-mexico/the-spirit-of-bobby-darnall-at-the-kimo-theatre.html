The Spirit of Bobby Darnall at the KiMo Theatre

Summary

The historic KiMo Theatre in Albuquerque is said to be haunted by a young boy named Bobby Darnall, who tragically perished in a 1951 boiler explosion.

Details

Opened in September 1927 and designed in the flamboyant Pueblo Deco architectural style, the KiMo Theatre became a beloved cultural landmark in downtown Albuquerque. On August 2, 1951, tragedy struck when a water heater exploded beneath the lobby concession stand. Six-year-old Bobby Darnall, who had been sitting in the balcony and ran down to the lobby after being frightened by a film, was killed in the blast. Since the disaster, Bobby's mischievous spirit has haunted the theatre. He is known to play pranks on performers and technical crew members, tripping people or causing unexplained commotions. To maintain peace during productions, cast members routinely leave offerings of candy, toys, and doughnuts on the water pipe behind the stage. According to local lore, whenever these offerings are neglected or removed, productions are plagued by immediate lighting, sound, and technical disasters until the treats are restored.
