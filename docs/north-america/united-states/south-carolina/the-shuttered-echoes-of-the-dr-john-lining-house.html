The Shuttered Echoes of the Dr. John Lining House

Summary

A historic Charleston home built during the colonial era is believed to be haunted by a woman traumatized by the Yamasee Indian uprising, whose spirit turns off lamps on the third floor.

Details

The Dr. John Lining House is one of Charleston’s oldest surviving structures, constructed between 1690 and 1715 on high ground near King Street. According to family history and deed records, the early 18th-century female owner survived the terrifying Yamasee War of 1715, while her family outside the city walls perished in brutal fashion. Her mind left shattered by the trauma, her presence allegedly lingers in the building's upper rooms. Decades later, when a descendant lived in the third-floor children's quarters, his bedside lamp would repeatedly and inexplicably switch itself off precisely at 10:00 p.m., even when plugged in and fully functional.
