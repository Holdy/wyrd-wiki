The Spirits of the Columbian House

Summary

Built in 1828 as a frontier stagecoach stop and inn in Waterville, Ohio, the historic Columbian House is said to be haunted by the souls of early pioneers and travelers who passed through its doors.

Details

Constructed by town founder John Pray along North River Road, the prominent yellow building has served as a hotel, jail, and restaurant over its nearly two-century history. Its storied past even attracted industrialist Henry Ford, who held a grand ball on the third floor and reportedly attempted to purchase the building. For more than a century, residents and visitors have shared ghost stories about unexplained phenomena, doors locking on their own, and the distinct feeling of unseen souls roaming the halls of the former stagecoach stop.
