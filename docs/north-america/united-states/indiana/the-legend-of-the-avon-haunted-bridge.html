The Legend of the Avon Haunted Bridge

Summary

Motorists driving under a historic 1906 railroad span in Avon, Indiana, are traditionally expected to honk their horns to muffle the phantom screams of a grieving mother or a construction worker buried in wet cement. The soaring masonry arch bridge has collected numerous tragic folklore tales since its early-twentieth-century construction.

Details

Built in 1906 for the Big Four Railroad (and still utilized by CSX today), the massive double-tracked masonry arch bridge on County Road 625 East in Avon, Hendricks County, has inspired persistent ghost stories for over a century. Several distinct legends attempt to explain the haunting presence: one tale tells of a drunken railroad worker who slipped during construction and was accidentally buried alive in the wet cement, whose moans can allegedly be heard whenever a train crosses overhead. Another widely repeated local legend claims that a young mother walking the railroad tracks with her sick baby slipped and fell from the bridge to their deaths, with her wailing cries for her infant echoing at night. A third account attributes the noises to four workers who fell into White Lick Creek below. To this day, local drivers customarily honk their horns when passing beneath the bridge's stone arches to quiet the restless spirits.
