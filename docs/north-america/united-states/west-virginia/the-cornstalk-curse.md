#title
The Cornstalk Curse

#summary
Following his treacherous murder by American soldiers at a frontier fort, the Shawnee leader Chief Cornstalk placed a two-century curse upon the surrounding land, dooming it to repeated tragedy and disaster. Over the following generations, the blighted region suffered numerous catastrophic events, including historic mining disasters, structural collapses, and severe storms.

#details
In 1777, the respected Shawnee Chief Cornstalk visited Fort Randolph at Point Pleasant to negotiate peace, but was detained as a hostage by American soldiers. When a soldier was killed outside by hostile warriors, an enraged mob broke into the quarters and brutally murdered Cornstalk, his son, and their companions. As he lay dying, Cornstalk pronounced a curse upon the land, wishing that the Great Spirit would blight the region and paralyze the strength of its people. For the next two hundred years, the area was plagued by a succession of historic tragedies, industrial accidents, and freak disasters—culminating in the collapse of the Silver Bridge in 1967 before finally waning around the bicentennial of his death.

#location
Tu-Endie-Wei Park is situated at the confluence of the Ohio and Kanawha rivers in Point Pleasant, West Virginia, marking the historic site of Fort Randolph where Chief Cornstalk was killed and buried.
