The Lady in White of Chatham Manor

Summary

A young Englishwoman sent away by her father to prevent her from eloping with her true love instead encountered George Washington at a Virginia plantation, inspiring a centuries-old ghostly legend. Her apparition is said to return every seven years on the anniversary of her death.

Details

In the late 18th century, a wealthy English girl was sent by her disapproving father to stay at Chatham Manor in Virginia to break off her relationship with a gentleman he deemed beneath her station. Unbeknownst to her father, the young man followed her to America and arranged to elope with her. However, a servant overheard their plans and informed another prominent guest at the manor: General George Washington. On the night of the intended elopement, as the young woman climbed out her bedroom window, she was met not by her lover, but by Washington, who ensured she was shipped back to England to marry someone matching her social class. Heartbroken and unable to forget her true love, she died on June 21, 1790, and reportedly vowed to return. Legend holds that her glowing, spectral figure in a colonial-style gown walks the river path at Chatham Manor every seventh year on June 21 between midday and midnight.
