#title
The Legend of Witches Pond

#summary
Local folklore holds that an 18th-century woman accused of witchcraft was executed and thrown into a Stafford County swamp, where her restless spirit and eerie phenomena continue to manifest. Visitors report hearing unearthly shrieks in the woods and seeing a shadowy figure hovering above the water.

#details
For generations, residents in Widewater, Virginia, have whispered about Witches Pond, a murky body of water hidden in the woods near a colonial-era cemetery off U.S. 1 and Telegraph Road. According to regional legend, an 18th-century woman named Edith was accused of witchcraft, put to death, and tossed into the water. Lore claims the pond turns blood red in the spring and that Edith's restless spirit haunts the surrounding trails. Over the years, thrill-seekers and visitors have reported hearing blood-curdling shrieks that grow closer in the dark, witnessing a shadowy figure hovering just above the water, and feeling an oppressive presence. While the supernatural stories are largely local myth, the woods hold a genuine grim history: in November 1998, the skeletal remains of an unidentified woman were discovered just 400 feet from the pond, cementing the area's eerie reputation in both folklore and real-world mystery.

#location
Witches Pond is located in the wooded area behind the Brent Family Cemetery off U.S. 1 and Telegraph Road in Widewater, Stafford County, Virginia.
