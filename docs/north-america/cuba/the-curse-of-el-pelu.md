#title
The Curse of El Pelú

#summary
After being abused and expelled from Cuba's first city by local residents, a destitute Spanish beggar cursed the settlement so that all future plans and ambitious ideas would inevitably crumble. Passed down through generations, this enduring curse is still blamed by locals for any misfortune or unfulfilled endeavor in the region.

#details
In the late 19th century, a destitute Spanish immigrant named Vicente Rodríguez—known widely as "El Pelú" due to his unkempt hair, beard, and ragged clothing—arrived in Baracoa after losing his fortune. He wandered the streets as a beggar, occasionally sustained by charitable locals. However, many residents grew fearful of him and began treating him with cruelty, ultimately stoning him, humiliating him, and forcing local authorities to banish him from the city. As he was being forced onto the dock to board a boat back to his homeland, he turned back and pronounced a bitter curse upon the settlement: that while many great plans and ideas would continually be generated in Baracoa, they would all fall apart and nothing would ever be successfully fulfilled. Generations of inhabitants have since blamed this delayed curse for both everyday setbacks and major hardships plaguing the town.

#location
The historic city of Baracoa, located in the easternmost province of Guantánamo, Cuba.
