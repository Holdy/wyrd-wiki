    The Seventy-One Spirits of the Schoolyard Date Tree

    Summary

    Adolescent schoolgirls in a Niger town are repeatedly seized and thrown into violent trances by invisible spirits seeking retribution for their displaced woodland homes. These terrifying possessions disrupt classrooms as the entities demand justice for the trees destroyed during the school's construction.

    Details

    In the town of Dogondoutchi, Niger, local residents and teachers bear witness to recurring outbreaks of mass spirit possession among female secondary students. According to local accounts, a prominent date tree standing within the schoolyard serves as the dwelling place for seventy-one aljanu (spirits), presided over by a powerful female entity named Salamatou. When the school was originally built, the clearing of surrounding bushland and cutting of trees destroyed the sacred topography and ancient homes of these spirits. Stripped of their refuges, the displaced entities target vulnerable students, suddenly plunging them into violent trances and shaking seizures. During episodes of possession, the spirits speak through the students, voicing their grief, anger, and demands for justice against human encroachment on their natural habitats.
