#title
The Curse of Ikoka Village

#summary
Over three centuries ago, Oba Ewuakpe of the Benin Kingdom placed a bitter curse on his mother's ancestral home of Ikoka, declaring that only women would ever prosper there while its men would languish in poverty. Today, the isolated village remains under the shadow of this royal decree, though many residents argue that government neglect and a lack of access roads are the true causes of its hardship.

#details
Around 1700 A.D., Prince Idova ascended the throne as Oba Ewuakpe of the Benin Kingdom, a reign marked by deep turbulence. Following the death of his mother, Queen Ewebonoza, a delegation from her maternal home of Ikoka village travelled to the royal court to break the news to the king. When the Oba saw the messengers wearing white garments, he flew into a rage, believing they were celebrating his mother's death, and had them killed—leaving only one survivor to carry the message back. Years later, when the Oba faced a severe revolt from his own subjects and lost his throne, he fled to Ikoka seeking refuge among his mother's kinsmen. Remembering the slaughter of their people, the village elders refused to help him unless he humbled himself by sweeping the village road and drinking pond water. Offended by the demand, the furious monarch abandoned the village, casting a final, powerful curse declaring that only the women of Ikoka would ever thrive while the men would forever wallow in poverty.

#location
The village of Ikoka is located in the Ovia South-West Local Government Area of Edo State, Nigeria, near Udo.
