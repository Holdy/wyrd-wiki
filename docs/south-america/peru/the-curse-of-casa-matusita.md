#title
The Curse of Casa Matusita

#summary
A notorious building in downtown Lima is plagued by centuries-old legends of witchcraft, mysterious tragedies, and a hex that drives anyone who stays on its upper floor to madness. According to lore, the property was originally cursed by a Persian woman condemned by the Spanish Inquisition.

#details
Standing at a busy intersection in the historic center of Lima, Casa Matusita has inspired generations of urban legends regarding supernatural occurrences and dark sorcery. One prominent legend traces the building's curse back to the mid-18th century, when a Persian immigrant named Parvaneh Dervaspa settled on the site, was accused of witchcraft by the Holy Inquisition, and was burned at the stake after confessing under torture. Before her death, or as a consequence of her dark pacts, she allegedly cast an enduring curse upon the land and any structure built upon it. Over the decades, other sinister stories accumulated around the house, including tales of tragic mass poisonings, familicides, and psychological breakdowns. Most famously, television personalities and daredevils who bet they could survive a night on the building's second floor allegedly fled within hours, suffering severe mental derangement or psychiatric hospitalization.

#location
Casa Matusita is located at the intersection of Avenida Inca Garcilaso de la Vega and Avenida España in the Historic Centre of Lima, Peru.
