The Guardian of the Divided Road

Summary

Fearing a lingering curse and the release of trapped spirits, road builders chose to split a Guyana highway into two lanes rather than cut down an ancient silk cotton tree. Locals believe the tree acts as a prison for malevolent entities, ensuring that severe misfortune or illness befalls anyone who dares to harm or disturb it.

Details

Deep in Guyanese folklore, the giant silk cotton tree is widely known as a "Jumbie Tree" or "Dutchman tree," rumored to be haunted by vengeful spirits and ancient entities left behind from the colonial era. According to tradition, these trees serve as vessels or prisons for supernatural forces. The folklore dictates that anyone who attempts to fell or damage the tree will unleash these trapped jumbies, bringing a severe curse and madness upon themselves and the surrounding community.

So deeply ingrained is this belief that when the main public highway was being constructed through Mahaicony, planners and builders refused to remove the massive silk cotton tree standing directly in the path. Rather than risk the wrath of the curse, the highway was explicitly engineered to split into two separate lanes, allowing the sacred tree to remain entirely undisturbed in the middle of the divide to this day.
