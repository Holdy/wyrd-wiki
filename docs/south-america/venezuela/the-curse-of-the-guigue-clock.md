#title
The Curse of the Güigüe Clock

#summary
A magnificent four-faced clock, cursed by a dying dictator in Venezuela, brings mysterious and untimely deaths to every watchmaker who dares to repair its stopped hands. Over generations, anyone who successfully restarts the mechanism meets a fatal end precisely aligned with the time the clock displays.

#details
Originally commissioned in the 19th century by President Antonio Guzmán Blanco for his coffee haciendas, the elaborate four-faced clock was later moved to the estate of the dictator General Juan Vicente Gómez. As Gómez's health failed and he faced losing his power and possessions, he placed a dark curse upon the clock so that its hands would stop forever, halting at 11:45 p.m.—the exact moment of his death. Over the decades, the clock was relocated to Plaza Bolívar and Plaza Ávila in front of the parish house in the city of Güigüe. Whenever a bold watchmaker or technician managed to repair the mechanism and get the clock running again, they met a sudden, unexplained death shortly afterward, with their time of passing uncannily matching the hour displayed on the clock face. From early local technicians like Salvatore Consoli and Andrés Mijares to later skeptics and even an international watchmaker named Crónidas Piaget, all succumbed to the relentless curse.

#location
The cursed clock is located in Plaza Bolívar / Plaza Ávila in front of the parish house in the city of Güigüe, Carlos Arvelo Municipality, Carabobo State, Venezuela.
