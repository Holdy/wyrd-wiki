#title
The Vengeful Head of Taira no Masakado

#summary
Following his defeat, the severed head of the rebel samurai Taira no Masakado flew across Japan and became a wrathful spirit, bringing swift and mysterious deaths to anyone who attempts to disturb or disrespect his burial mound.

#details
Taira no Masakado was a powerful provincial samurai who led a rebellion against the emperor in the 10th century before being defeated in battle. His decapitated head was taken to Kyoto to be put on public display, but it refused to decompose, crying out nightly in anger until it finally launched itself into the sky and flew back to the Kantō region, landing at a site in what is now modern Tokyo. A burial mound, known as the *kubizuka*, was built to enshrine the head, but the site became heavily feared because any disturbance, neglect, or disrespect shown to the area unleashes a severe curse. Over the centuries, attempts to demolish or pave over the mound have been repeatedly halted after mysterious accidents, illnesses, and sudden deaths befell the ministers, workers, and occupants involved in the projects. To this day, the small shrine is maintained with deep reverence, surrounded by offerings and stone frog figures, as local businesses and workers take great care never to slight the resting place of the formidable warrior.

#location
The shrine and head-mound (Masakado-zuka) is located in the Ōtemachi financial district of Chiyoda, Tokyo, just a short walk from Tokyo Imperial Palace.
