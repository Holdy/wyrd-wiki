    The Curse of the Sufi Sheikh

    Summary

    When a Khwarazmian ruler wrongfully executed a talented student of the revered Sufi master Najmaddin al-Kubra, the grieving sheikh placed a potent curse on the state that heralded its destruction by Mongol invaders.

    Details

    In medieval Kunya-Urgench, the capital of the Khwarazm Empire, the esteemed philosopher and Sufi master Najmaddin al-Kubra led a community of 360 scholars. After Khwarazmshah Muhammad II falsely accused and executed one of the sheikh's brightest students, Najmaddin al-Kubra placed a devastating curse upon the state. Legend holds that this curse brought about the doom of the realm, summoning the destructive wrath of the advancing Mongol army. When Genghis Khan besieged the city, he offered the holy man safe passage out, but the sheikh refused to flee, ultimately dying in battle with the city's banner clutched in his hands. Today, his legacy and the site of his martyrdom are memorialized at his mausoleum in Kunya-Urgench.
