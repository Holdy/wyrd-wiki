The Curse of Tun Jana Khatib and the Swordfish of Singapore

Summary

An unjustly executed holy man curses the tyrannical ruler of ancient Singapore, unleashing a devastating invasion of swordfish that is temporarily thwarted by a brilliant young boy. Fearing the child's genius, the paranoid ruler orders his execution as well, sealing the doom of the kingdom.

Details

Centuries ago in ancient Singapore (Singapura), a pious missionary named Tun Jana Khatib visited the island and accidentally split a betel palm tree near the royal palace simply by touching it. Mistakenly believing the holy man was displaying dark magic to the queen, the tyrannical ruler Paduka Sri Maharaja ordered his immediate execution at Hujung Pasar (modern-day Kampung Gelam). Before being stabbed to death, Tun Jana Khatib laid a curse on the king and the land, vowing that a cruel ruler would not escape punishment and that a calamity would befall the kingdom. Shortly after, vicious shoals of swordfish swarmed the shores of Singapore, piercing the necks and waists of fishermen and citizens. While the foolish king attempted to form a human shield with his subjects' thighs—causing massive casualties—a seven-year-old boy named Hang Nadim cleverly advised lining the coast with banana tree stems, which trapped the swordfish snouts and saved the populace (naming the area Tanjung Pagar, or "Cape of Stakes"). Terrified that such a brilliant child would eventually usurp the throne, the paranoid ruler had young Hang Nadim executed as well.
