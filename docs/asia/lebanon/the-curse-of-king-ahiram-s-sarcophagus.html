The Curse of King Ahiram's Sarcophagus

Summary

An ancient Phoenician king left a dire curse carved upon his royal tomb to punish any ruler who dared to disturb his resting place by stripping away their judicial scepter and overturning their throne. The curse also forewarned that peace would flee from the city of Byblos if the sanctuary was ever uncovered.

Details

Commissioned by his son Ittobaal around 1000 BC, the limestone sarcophagus of King Ahiram was placed deep within the royal necropolis of the ancient Phoenician city of Byblos. To protect his mortal remains from desecration by future rulers, commanders, or rival governors, a powerful protective warning was engraved along the rim and lid using some of the earliest fully developed Phoenician alphabetic characters. The inscription declared that if anyone were to uncover or tamper with the coffin, their judicial scepter would be stripped away, their royal throne overthrown, and peace and tranquility permanently banished from Byblos. Despite this foreboding curse, ancient tomb robbers eventually breached the burial chamber, though the remarkable carved artifact survived for modern discovery following a rain-triggered collapse of the hillside.
