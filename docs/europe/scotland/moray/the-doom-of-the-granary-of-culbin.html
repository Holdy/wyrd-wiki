The Doom of the Granary of Culbin

Summary

Reckless wickedness and the desecration of the Sabbath by the laird of Culbin brought down a devastating curse of shifting sand that buried a fertile Moray barony. Tenants who stubbornly refused to abandon their homes were ultimately swallowed alive as the creeping dunes overtook them.

Details

Known historically as the "Granary of Moray" for its remarkably rich and reliable soil, the barony of Culbin was utterly transformed in the autumn of 1694 when a massive sand-drift engulfed the community. According to local legend, the catastrophe was a form of divine retribution brought upon the laird, Alexander Kinnaird, for his wicked lifestyle, which allegedly included making a pact with the Devil to gamble with cards and forcing his tenants to harvest crops on the Sabbath. When the great storm struck without warning, it happened so suddenly that a ploughman had to abandon his plough mid-furrow and reapers fled the fields.

Though many families initially escaped, numerous tenants lingered in hope or stubbornly cleared paths to their front doors as the sand mounted. When the drift blocked their access entirely, they broke through their back walls, but this offered only a temporary reprieve. The relentless dunes ultimately overtopped and buried their homes, sealing the entire estate beneath a desert of sand. To this day, folklore maintains that the laird and the Devil remain trapped beneath the largest sand mound, still playing their eternal game of cards.
