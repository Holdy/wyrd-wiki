#title
The Curse of the Locked Church Door

#summary
Local folklore dictates that anyone who locks the door of Odstock church will face a sudden and untimely death, a curse placed after authorities imprisoned and executed an innocent gypsy man and barred access to his grave. For nearly two hundred years the church door remained unlocked to avoid the fatal penalty.

#details
In 1801, a young gypsy man was accused of horse theft. To save his son, a man named Joseph Scamp took the blame upon himself, but the local authorities showed no mercy and had him hanged. His people commemorated him annually with a graveside gathering. Angered by the celebrations, the local authorities retaliated by locking the church door and ripping up a rose planted at Joseph's resting place. In response, the gypsies laid a deadly curse on the church: anyone who dared lock its door would be condemned to a premature death, a fate reportedly proven by the sudden demise of two overly enthusiastic church wardens. To protect future parishioners, the rector ultimately threw the church key into the nearby River Ebble.

#location
St Mary's Church in the village of Odstock, just south of Salisbury in Wiltshire.
