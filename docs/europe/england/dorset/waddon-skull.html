    Waddon skull

    Summary

    A screaming skull kept in a recess on the stairs at Waddon was liable to be fractious and cause disturbances, so it was given to the Dorchester Museum. It was said to be the head of a Black servant, killed by mistake when his master took him for a burglar in the night.

    Details

    Around the middle of the 19th century, a "screaming skull" was kept respectfully in a recess on the stairs at Waddon. It was liable to be fractious and cause disturbances in the house, so it was given to the Dorchester Museum. The story went that it was the head of a Black servant, and it bore the mark of a sword cut: he had gone to his master's room at night, and his master, believing him to be a burglar, killed him by mistake in the bedroom over the dining room. Waddon belonged to the Grove family of Zeals in Wiltshire.
