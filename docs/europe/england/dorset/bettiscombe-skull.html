    Bettiscombe skull

    Summary

    A skull at Bettiscombe Manor is said to belong to an enslaved African man who died determined to be buried in his homeland. Any attempt to bury it anywhere else is said to make the skull scream aloud.

    Details

    According to the legend, the skull belonged to an African slave owned by the master of Bettiscombe Manor. He died wanting to be buried in his homeland, and when that wish was denied his skull took its revenge: any attempt to bury it elsewhere would make it scream aloud. The tale was first set down in John Henry Ingram's 1897 book The Haunted Homes and Family Traditions of Great Britain, which also describes curiosity-seekers visiting the house in 1883 to see the skull for themselves.
