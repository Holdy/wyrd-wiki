    Theophilus Brome's skull

    Summary

    Before his death in 1670, Theophilus Broome left instructions that his skull be kept at his farmhouse in Chilton Cantelo. Attempts to remove it are said to have unleashed poltergeist activity and havoc, so it has stayed at the farm.

    Details

    Theophilus Broome died in 1670, leaving instructions that his skull should be kept at Higher Farm in Chilton Cantelo. Attempts to remove it are said to have brought poltergeist activity and general havoc, and a manuscript kept at the farm holds written accounts from several people who claimed to have witnessed the disturbances. The legend was recorded in 1791 by John Collinson in his History and Antiquities of Somerset. Broome's tomb is in St James's Church, while the skull itself could be seen only by written appointment.
