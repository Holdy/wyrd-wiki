    The Ghostly Coach of Chavenage Manor

    Summary

    Following his death after aiding in the execution of King Charles I, Colonel Nathaniel Stephens' ghost was witnessed departing his ancestral home in a spectral black coach driven by a headless figure that assumed the shape of the martyred king. According to local lore, this ghostly carriage returns to carry away every head of the household upon their passing.

    Details

    During the English Civil War, Colonel Nathaniel Stephens, Lord of the Manor of Chavenage, was persuaded by General Henry Ireton to support the trial and execution of King Charles I. Following this decision, Stephens' daughter laid a curse upon him for bringing disgrace to the family name. Soon after, Stephens fell terminally ill. At the exact moment of his death, a spectral black carriage drawn by horses and driven by a headless coachman materialized at the manor entrance. Stephens' ghost rose from his coffin, bowed to the driver, and climbed aboard. As the coach departed, the headless coachman transformed into the likeness of King Charles I. Tradition maintains that every subsequent lord of the manor who died at Chavenage departed in this same supernatural fashion.
