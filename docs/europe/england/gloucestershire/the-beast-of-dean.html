    The Beast of Dean

    Summary

    In 1802, farmers and residents from the village of Parkend organized an expedition into the surrounding woods to hunt a monstrous, giant wild boar that was reportedly felling trees and flattening fences.

    Details

    In the early 19th century, people living in the Forest of Dean village of Parkend were troubled by rumors and disturbances caused by an immense creature roaming the local woodland. Described as a wild boar of staggering proportions—so large that it allegedly crushed hedges and felled trees in its wake—the beast terrified the local populace. In 1802, a group of local farmers and villagers undertook an expedition to find, capture, or kill the creature, though their search ultimately turned up nothing resembling the monster. Despite finding no physical trace, the legend of the giant beast persisted through generations as a celebrated piece of regional folklore.
