    The Golden Coffin of Notgrove Long Barrow

    Summary

    Local folklore maintains that the ancient Neolithic long barrow in Notgrove hides a magnificent coffin made entirely of solid gold. Despite multiple archaeological excavations, no precious metal was ever discovered within the stone chambers.

    Details

    For generations, local tradition held that the prehistoric mound known as Notgrove Long Barrow concealed a fabulous treasure: a coffin fashioned entirely out of gold. Like many ancient burial mounds across the Cotswolds, the barrow attracted rumors of hidden riches that spurred treasure hunters and antiquarians to investigate. However, when official excavations were carried out—first in 1881 and later between 1934 and 1935 by archaeologist Mrs. E.M. Clifford—no golden coffin or glittering hoard was unearthed. Instead, the chambers yielded only ancient human remains, a flint arrowhead, and a few simple beads. Today, the barrow is recognized as a Neolithic communal tomb rather than a treasury, though the legend of the golden coffin persists in local lore.
