    The Christmas Broad of Leighterton

    Summary

    During the Christmas season in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, the village of Leighterton participated in a Gloucestershire folk custom featuring "The Broad," a horned beast made from a hollowed-out, candle-lit turnip mounted on a pole.

    Details

    In the rural villages of the southern Cotswolds, including Leighterton and Hawkesbury in Gloucestershire, winter wassailing celebrations incorporated a strange and ancient mumming tradition known as "The Broad." While some communities constructed the hobby horse figure using a stuffed bull skin or cardboard, the Leighterton tradition involved a large swede or turnip that was expertly hollowed out with carved facial features—eyes, nose, mouth, and ears—and illuminated from within by a flickering candle. Accompanied by groups of local singers carrying ribbon-decked wassail bowls, the glowing, horned vegetable head was brought through the village to visit homes and inns, blending festive song with uncanny rural folklore.
