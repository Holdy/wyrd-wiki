    The Last Private Battle of Nibley Green

    Summary

    Fought on March 20, 1470, the bloody clash at Nibley Green between the private armies of Lord Lisle and Lord Berkeley culminated in the death of Lord Lisle by a fatal arrow wound, marking the final battle between private feudal armies in England.

    Details

    In March 1470, a bitter and long-running territorial dispute over the manor of Wotton-under-Edge between Thomas, Lord Lisle, and William, Lord Berkeley, reached its violent climax. Lord Lisle challenged Lord Berkeley to settle their feud with their respective private forces. On the morning of March 20, Lord Berkeley's men lay concealed within the skirts of Michaelwood Chase while Lord Lisle's force descended from Shankley Hill onto Nibley Green. Believing he held the tactical advantage, Lisle charged down the slope. However, Berkeley's larger force sprang a tactical trap, emerging from the woods and subjecting Lisle's advancing men to a heavy shower of arrows. During the skirmish, as Lord Lisle lifted the visor of his helmet, he was struck in the mouth by an arrow shot by an archer known as Black Will (or James Hiat) from the Forest of Dean, which exited through his neck. His instant death shattered the morale of his troops, who fled in a chaotic rout. Over one hundred men were killed in the battle, with unclaimed casualties later laid to rest in a mass grave in the churchyard of St Martin's in North Nibley.
