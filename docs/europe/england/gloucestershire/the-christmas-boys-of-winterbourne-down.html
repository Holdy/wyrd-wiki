    The Christmas Boys of Winterbourne Down

    Summary

    Every Boxing Day, a troupe known as the Christmas Boys performs traditional mummers' plays from house to house across the village, keeping centuries of local Gloucestershire folklore alive. Dressed in disguises with blackened faces, the performers enact ritual dramas of battle, death, and miraculous resurrection.

    Details

    Winterbourn Down is steeped in enduring folk traditions, most prominently preserved by the troupe known as the Christmas Boys. Every year on Boxing Day, these performers travel through the village streets executing a traditional Mummers' Play outside local homes. The performance centers on themes of duality, conflict, and resurrection, typically featuring a stylized battle between opposing characters representing good and evil. A central figure in the play is a doctor possessing a magical potion capable of reviving any slain character. Accompanied by traditional music and the Winterbourn Down Border Morris dancers, the participants wear disguises with blackened faces, continuing ancient agrarian customs linked to the seasonal cycle and local folklore.
