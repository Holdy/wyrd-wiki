    The Coffin Way and Cross of Saintbury

    Summary

    Funeral processions approaching Saintbury's ancient St Nicholas Church traditionally rested coffins at the lower crossroads cross to ensure the deceased's spirit departed fully before the steep ascent.

    Details

    In the shadow of the Cotswold escarpment, the tiny hamlet of Saintbury is linked by an ancient corpse way running from the crossroads near Weston-sub-Edge up to the 13th-century St Nicholas Church. According to local folklore and traditional custom, funeral parties carrying coffins up to the isolated hilltop church were required to halt at Saintbury Cross, situated at the crossroads downhill from the building. Here, the bearers would rest the coffin and frequently change their positions in a traditional ritual reversal. This practice was believed to disorient wandering spirits and ensure that the soul successfully detached from the physical body, preventing the deceased from rising from the grave. The path itself formed part of a historic straight alignment connecting ancient pagan sites, earthworks, and the medieval church.
