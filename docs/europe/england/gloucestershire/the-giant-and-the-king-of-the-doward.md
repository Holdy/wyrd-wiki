#title
The Giant and the King of the Doward

#summary
Local legend maintains that King Arthur's Cave near Symonds Yat served as a stronghold for ancient monarchs and once yielded the skeleton of a giant in 1695. The cavern's limestone chambers intertwine deep prehistory with enduring folklore of Vortigern, Uther Pendragon, and hidden royal refuge.

#details
Nestled within the wooded slopes of the Doward near Symonds Yat, King Arthur's Cave is a limestone cavern shrouded in centuries of local folklore and myth. According to tradition, the cave was once fortified by King Vortigern during his conflicts against Anglo-Saxon invaders, before being besieged and captured by Aurelius and Uther Pendragon, the father of King Arthur. Legends also whisper that Arthur and his knights sought refuge within its shadowed stone chambers, concealing ancient treasures within the rock. 

Beyond its Arthurian associations, the cave is the setting for a documented local account from 1695, when a local goatherder reportedly discovered the complete skeleton of a giant human inside the cavern. The physical evidence of this extraordinary find was lost when a Bristolian surgeon named Mr. Pye took the bones aboard a ship bound for Jamaica, only for the vessel to founder and sink at sea. Modern excavations confirm the cave's ancient significance by uncovering Upper Palaeolithic artifacts and woolly mammoth bones, bridging the gap between deep prehistory and regional legend.

#location
King Arthur's Cave is situated in the limestone cliffs of the Doward on the north-western edge of the ridge near Symonds Yat in the Wye Valley.
