    The Fugitive in the Barrel and the Cave of Rocks Wood

    Summary

    During the Catholic restoration under Queen Mary, two parishioners secretly held Protestant services in a woodland cave until their discovery led to a dramatic capture, escape, and execution. Afterwards, the women of the parish took their revenge on the reinstated pro-Catholic parson by driving him out of the village.

    Details

    When Queen Mary restored Catholicism in England, two men of Taynton named Home and Crockett refused to conform and secretly continued holding forbidden Protestant services inside a cave located in Rocks Wood, a site marked by an ancient yew tree. Eventually discovered by the authorities, both men were arrested and imprisoned. Home managed to escape from custody and returned in secret to his home. Shortly thereafter, his wife gave birth, and during the christening feast, the local midwife became suspicious when she observed Home's mother cutting unusually large slices of meat from the joint. Realizing someone else was being fed in secret, she alerted the authorities, who searched the house and discovered Home hiding inside a wooden barrel. He was taken to Newent and burned at the stake in the grounds of Newent Priory. Years later, when Queen Elizabeth I ascended the throne, the women of Taynton parish—with the local men not daring to act—exacted their own form of justice upon the parish priest by tying him back to front onto his horse and driving him out of the parish boundary.
