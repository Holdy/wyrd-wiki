    The Tower of Babel at the Roundhouse

    Summary

    Local legend claimed that a property owner building an archway near Bishop's Cleeve was struck down by divine wrath when his towering stone structure collapsed. Although the tragedy was an accidental masonry collapse, villagers likened his towering ambition to the biblical Tower of Babel.

    Details

    In the high ground above Bishop's Cleeve and Cleeve Hill, a local property owner set out to construct an elaborate stone archway across the gateway of the Roundhouse. Tragically, during construction, the heavy stonework collapsed and crushed him to death. While the practical reality was a fatal building accident, local folklore transformed the tragedy into a moralistic tale. Village legend maintained that he was attempting to build a presumptuous stone tower, likened to the Tower of Babel, and that a supernatural power with divine wrath caused it to crash to the earth as punishment for his hubris.
