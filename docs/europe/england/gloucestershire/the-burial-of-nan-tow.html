    The Burial of Nan Tow

    Summary

    According to local Cotswold folklore, the ancient round barrow known as Nan Tow's Tump marks the grave of a suspected witch who was buried alive and upright by a Duke of Beaufort.

    Details

    Nan Tow's Tump is a prominent Bronze Age round barrow standing approximately 9 feet high and 100 feet in diameter. Local tradition holds that the mound derives its name from a woman named Nan Tow, who was reputed to be a witch. According to the legend, one of the historical Dukes of Beaufort had her buried alive—traditionally placed in an upright position to ensure she could never rise again—either inside her house or beneath the earthwork mound that now bears her name.
