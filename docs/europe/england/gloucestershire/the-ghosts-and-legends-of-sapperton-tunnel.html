    The Ghosts and Legends of Sapperton Tunnel

    Summary

    Deep within the historic Sapperton Tunnel on the Thames and Severn Canal near Coates, canal lore and local legends tell of phantom echoes, dancing shadows, and Peglars Wharf where unruly passengers were once abandoned in total darkness.

    Details

    The Sapperton Tunnel, which carries the disused Thames and Severn Canal beneath the Cotswold hills between Coates and Sapperton, has long been surrounded by eerie local traditions. Carved deep into the solid rock during the canal's construction, an underground cavern known as Peglars Wharf was originally used to store construction materials. According to local folklore, canal boatmen would occasionally leave nagging spouses or troublesome acquaintances stranded at the wharf in pitch blackness for hours or days to teach them a lesson while they retreated to the warmth of nearby coaching inns like the Tunnel Inn in Coates. Over generations, these dark tales transformed into ghostly legends of subterranean hauntings, where phantom voices echo through the damp caverns and unexplained shadows dance along the brickwork and rock walls.
