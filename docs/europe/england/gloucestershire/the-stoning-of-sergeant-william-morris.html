    The Stoning of Sergeant William Morris

    Summary

    In November 1895, Police Sergeant William Morris was fatally stoned by a group of intoxicated men while on patrol in Viney Hill. The violent ambush left his colleague unconscious and resulted in the sergeant's tragic death from a fractured skull and broken neck.

    Details

    On the evening of November 10, 1895, Police Sergeant William Morris and Police Constable Cornelius Harding were carrying out their official duties in the village of Viney Hill within the Forest of Dean. During their patrol, they encountered a group of local colliers—James Morgan, George Morgan, and George Hill—who had spent the day visiting pubs in nearby Blakeney and were heavily intoxicated.

    As the officers confronted the rowdy group, a violent scuffle erupted. The intoxicated men pelted both police officers with heavy stones before fleeing into the night. When Police Constable Harding eventually regained consciousness, he discovered that Sergeant Morris had sustained catastrophic injuries, including a fractured skull and a broken neck, which proved fatal. The three assailants were subsequently apprehended and convicted of manslaughter for their roles in the deadly attack.
