    The Carved Twins of Saul

    Summary

    Two carved figures above the entrance porch of a house in Saul commemorate twin brothers who married sisters and tragically drowned in the River Severn. The memorial stands as a poignant tribute to the immense dangers faced by the watermen of the Gloucestershire village.

    Details

    In the Gloucestershire village of Saul, many homes built by 19th-century sea captains and barge masters feature decorative carvings and incised dates on their exteriors. Among these details, a pair of figures carved above the entrance porch of a house on the village's High Street preserves a tragic local legend. According to the tradition attached to the carvings, the statues represent twin brothers who married a pair of sisters, only for both men to meet their end by drowning in the treacherous tidal currents and mudflats of the nearby River Severn.
