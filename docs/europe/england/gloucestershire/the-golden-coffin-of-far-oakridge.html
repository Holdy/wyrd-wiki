    The Golden Coffin of Far Oakridge

    Summary

    A Bronze Age round barrow located in a triangular field north of Far Oakridge was long believed by locals to contain a buried golden coffin and a Roman general, inspiring generations of fruitless treasure hunts.

    Details

    In 1928, the Reverend R. Jowett Burton documented a long-standing local legend regarding a triangular field north of Far Oakridge known as the "Field of the Golden Coffin." Within this field sat the remains of a Bronze Age round barrow, also cataloged by archaeologists as Bisley 7. According to local folklore, the ancient mound was said to hold a solid golden coffin alongside the remains of a Roman general. This belief sparked multiple vain attempts by treasure seekers over the centuries who dug into and severely disturbed the structure. Subsequent agricultural tenancy records confirmed that the land had long been referred to simply as the "Coffin Field," preserving the enduring legend in Cotswold oral history.
