    The Drowning at Urrist Moat

    Summary

    After one brother drowned the other in a practical joke at a moated homestead in Badgeworth, a runaway hearse during his funeral sparked enduring local legends of haunting.

    Details

    In the 18th and early 19th centuries, a small stone-built house stood within the moated enclosure known as Urrist Moat (also recorded as Hook's Farm) near Little Shurdington in the parish of Badgeworth. The house was inhabited by two eccentric brothers named Hook. According to local accounts preserved from the 19th century, one of the brothers was killed after being drowned in the moat during a practical joke played upon him by the other.

    Following the tragedy, the drowned brother's body was placed in a hearse to be transported to Badgeworth for burial. During the procession, however, the horses suddenly bolted and ran away, overturning the hearse and throwing the coffin to the ground. From that day forward, the moated site gained a dark reputation as being haunted, and the homestead gradually fell into ruin. For decades afterward, local children were cautioned that bubbles rising in the water were the work of "Old Hook," who would stretch out a long stick to pull unsuspecting youngsters down to live at the bottom of the moat with him.
