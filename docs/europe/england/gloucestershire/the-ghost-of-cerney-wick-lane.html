    The Ghost of Cerney Wick Lane

    Summary

    Cyclists returning home after dark along Cerney Wick Lane frequently experienced an overwhelming sense of dread and unseen surveillance, believed by locals to stem from a fatal 19th-century cart accident along the road.

    Details

    During the 1960s, a resident named Roland Wakefield frequently cycled home to South Cerney after dark following bell-ringing practice in Cerney Wick. Whenever he traversed Cerney Wick Lane—particularly near the junction with the Ashton Keynes road—he was gripped by an intense, inexplicable fear that something invisible was watching him and about to jump out from the darkened fields. Fearing ridicule, he kept the experiences to himself for thirty years until reminiscing with his mother, who casually asked if he had encountered the ghost of Wick Lane. She revealed that many local villagers shared the exact same dread and refused to travel the lane alone at night, linking the haunting to an 19th-century carriage accident. Historical records show that in August 1865, 27-year-old local resident William Haskins Allaway died from a severe head injury after his horse turned restless and threw him from his trap along that very stretch of road.
