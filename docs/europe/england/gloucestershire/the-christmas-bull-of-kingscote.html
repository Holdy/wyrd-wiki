    The Christmas Bull of Kingscote

    Summary

    Dating back to approximately 1835, the Cotswold village of Kingscote was recorded as the earliest known home of "The Broad," a traditional winter folk custom featuring a masked bull hobby horse.

    Details

    In the 1830s, the rural Gloucestershire village of Kingscote became the setting for the earliest documented emergence of a striking and unusual traditional folk custom known as "The Broad." Celebrated during the Christmas season as part of local wassailing rounds, the custom centered around a terrifying and dramatic hobby horse representing a bull. The creature—often called the "Christmas Bull"—was constructed from a stuffed bull’s skin or head mounted on a wooden pole, hoisted by a performer who was completely hidden beneath a heavy sackcloth sheet or blanket. Accompanied by villagers carrying ribbon-adorned wassail bowls, the Broad processed through the parish, symbolizing ancient agrarian winter rites that endured across the southern Cotswolds until the outbreak of the First World War.
