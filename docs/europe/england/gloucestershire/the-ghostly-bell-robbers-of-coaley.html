The Ghostly Bell-Robbers of Coaley

Summary

Four young men from Coaley attempted to steal a historic church bell, only to be thwarted by the transparent, furious ghost of the woman who had originally donated it.

Details

In the 18th century, four young men from Coaley sat drinking at the Old Fox pub, looking toward the countryside. Envying the bells of St Peter's Church—situated on the parish border between Frocester and Coaley—they devised a plan to steal one in the dead of night using a donkey and cart. Entering the derelict, roofless church, they managed to lower one of the six heavy bells, cast in 1639 by William Whitmore and embedded with jewels thrown into the molten metal centuries earlier by a pious local woman. As they prepared to load it, an old woman suddenly appeared squatting on the bell, glaring at them in silence. When the men tried to heave the bell, they found it impossibly immovable, and upon closer inspection, they realized they could see the church walls right through the old woman's transparent, spectral body. Terrified by the apparition, the men fled into the night, leaving the bell behind.
