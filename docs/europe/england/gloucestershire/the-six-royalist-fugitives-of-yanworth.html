    The Six Royalist Fugitives of Yanworth

    Summary

    Following the defeat at the Battle of Stow-on-the-Wold in March 1646, six Royalist soldiers fled to Yanworth where they were killed and buried in an unmarked mass grave. Deep pock-mark holes scarring the exterior stonework of St Michael's Church are traditionally said to be the marks left by the musket fire from that fatal encounter.

    Details

    In March 1646, the first English Civil War effectively ended with the crushing defeat of the last Royalist field army at the Battle of Stow-on-the-Wold. Amid the chaos of the rout, six Royalist soldiers managed to break away from the fighting and escape south toward the small village of Yanworth. Their flight ultimately ended in tragedy when they were caught and killed. According to local tradition, the six fugitive soldiers were buried together in a mass grave in unconsecrated ground within the churchyard of St Michael's Church, though the exact location of their burial site has been lost to history. To this day, a series of large, distinct pock-mark holes on the east wall of the church's north transept are pointed to as the grim physical evidence of the musket shots fired during the skirmish.
