    The Cursed Nut-Tree of Longney Church

    Summary

    When the medieval lord of Longney refused to remove a luxuriant nut-tree that blocked the light from his newly built church, Bishop Wulfstan placed a curse upon the tree, causing it to instantly wither away.

    Details

    In the late eleventh century, Elsi, the lord of Longney, constructed a parish church in the village and invited St. Wulfstan, the Bishop of Worcester, to consecrate the sacred building. Upon arrival, it became apparent that a large, luxuriant nut-tree grew so close to the structure that it entirely blocked the natural daylight from reaching the church. When church authorities suggested the tree be cleared for the consecration, Elsi stubbornly declared that he would rather leave the church unconsecrated than lose his prized tree. Displeased by this attachment, Bishop Wulfstan pronounced a curse upon the nut-tree, whereupon it immediately withered and died, clearing the way for the consecration to proceed.
