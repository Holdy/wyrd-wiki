#title
The Miners' Font and Tower of Abenhall Church

#summary
According to local legend, the 15th-century font and tower improvements at St Michael's Church in Abenhall were funded and presented by Forest of Dean freeminers to commemorate their perilous service in the French wars and the Battle of Agincourt.

#details
During the early 15th century, English military expeditions recruited expert freeminers from the Forest of Dean to employ their underground expertise in undermining enemy fortifications during campaigns in France, including the Battle of Agincourt. Local folklore records that out of the 120 miners who marched off to war in 1415, nearly half perished abroad. To commemorate their involvement and give thanks for their survival, the returning miners presented a richly carved stone baptismal font to St Michael's Church in Abenhall around 1450. Furthermore, the spoils and wealth gained from their military adventures reportedly funded structural improvements to the church, including the construction of a new tower. To this day, the octagonal bowl of the font features detailed carvings of pickaxes, shovels, and smiths' tools, while a carved stone positioned over the tower door displays the miners' armorial bearings alongside the head of a miner wearing a traditional Monmouth cap.

#location
St Michael and All Angels Church in the village of Abenhall, Gloucestershire.
