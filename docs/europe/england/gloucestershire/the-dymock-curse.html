    The Dymock Curse

    Summary

    In 1892, a 17th-century lead tablet inscribed with a malignant curse directed at a local resident named Sarah Ellis was discovered hidden in a cupboard in Dymock. According to local legend, the curse drove the unfortunate woman to take her own life, leading to her burial at a nearby crossroads.

    Details

    In 1892, a chilling discovery was made inside a cupboard at Wilton Place in the Gloucestershire village of Dymock when a small lead tablet incised with esoteric symbols representing lunar spirits and a hostile incantation was brought to light. At the top of the tablet, the name "Sarah Ellis" was written backwards as "haras Sille," followed by a dark command directing spirits to banish and torment the victim. The text drew upon occult formulae popular in seventeenth-century grimoires. Although parish records show that women named Sarah Ellis lived in the parish without suffering such a fate, local tradition maintained that she was so distressed by the magical malediction that she died by suicide and was subsequently buried at a crossroads with a stake through her heart. The artifact itself, known as the Dymock Curse, survived the centuries and is preserved in the collections of the Museum of Gloucester.
