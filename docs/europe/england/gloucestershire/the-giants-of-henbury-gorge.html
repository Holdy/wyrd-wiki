    The Giants of Henbury Gorge

    Summary

    According to local legend, two giant brothers named Goram and Vincent competed for the hand of the fair Avona by digging drainage channels through the hills, with Goram carving out the gorge at Henbury before falling asleep in his stone chair.

    Details

    Centuries ago, two mighty giants—brothers named Goram and Vincent—both fell in love with a beautiful woman named Avona. Avona promised her hand in marriage to whichever giant could successfully drain the massive lake stretching between Wiltshire and Bristol. The two brothers chose separate paths to carve their drainage channels through the limestone hills. Goram chose a route through Henbury, while Vincent worked on the south side of Clifton. Goram laboured away, carving out the ravine of the Hazel Brook at Henbury and resting in his stone-winged seat known as Goram's Chair, but he eventually grew tired, drank too much ale, and fell fast asleep. Meanwhile, Vincent diligently completed his channel, creating the Avon Gorge and winning Avona. Upon waking and discovering he had lost, a heartbroken Goram stamped his foot into the rock—leaving the Giant's Footprint on the Blaise Castle Estate—before casting himself into the Severn.
