#title
The Roman Army and the Wrath of Sabrina at Awre

#summary
According to local legend, a Roman army pursuing British druids across the mudflats of the Severn was drowned when the river goddess Sabrina unleashed a sudden, massive tidal bore at the Noose in Awre. Fossilized mammoth and hippo bones occasionally washing up on the riverbanks were long cited by locals as physical proof of the Roman war elephants trapped in the disaster.

#details
As Roman forces pushed westward to crush resisting Celtic tribes, they encountered a vast gathering of druids and Britons assembled on the banks of the Severn. Seeing the enemy across the river during low tide, the Roman infantry and cavalry stepped onto the exposed mudflats to cross near the treacherous sandbank known as the Noose at Awre. However, as they reached the channel, the druids invoked the river goddess Sabrina. Roused by their prayers, Sabrina summoned a towering wall of water—the Severn Bore—which raced up the estuary with ferocious speed. Trapped in the deep silt and rising currents, the Roman force and their war elephants were overwhelmed and swept away by the churning waters, leaving behind ancient fossilized remains buried in the riverbed as enduring testimony to the goddess's wrath.

#location
The Noose sandbank in the River Severn off Awre Point, near the village of Awre in Gloucestershire.
