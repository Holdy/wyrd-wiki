    The Grey Lady of Owlpen Manor

    Summary

    Following her historic stay on the eve of the Battle of Tewkesbury in 1471, the spirit of Queen Margaret of Anjou is said to haunt the upper rooms of Owlpen Manor as a benevolent grey lady. Decades later, evacuee children sleeping in the house reported being visited by this exact noble lady in medieval attire whom they had never met.

    Details

    On the night of May 2, 1471, Queen Margaret of Anjou—the formidable consort of King Henry VI—stayed at Owlpen Manor en route to the fateful Battle of Tewkesbury. Just a day later, her son Prince Edward was killed, plunging the Queen into devastating grief and shattering her hopes for the Lancastrian dynasty. According to local tradition, her restless yet peaceful spirit returned to the manor, frequently appearing in the Great Chamber as a grey lady wearing a fur-trimmed gown and wimple. Centuries later, during the Second World War, young evacuees billeted at the house described being visited in their bedroom by a lovely lady in medieval dress with long sleeves and a peaked veil, completely unaware of the manor's royal phantom.
