    The Giant of Dixton Hill and the Tibblestone

    Summary

    A local giant attempted to hurl a massive boulder from Dixton Hill at pirates sailing up the River Severn, but his foot slipped during the throw, causing the stone to land at Teddington Hands.

    Details

    According to local Gloucestershire folklore, a giant once made his home on the heights of Dixton Hill. Spotting pirates navigating up the River Severn towards Tewkesbury, the giant decided to crush them by tearing a giant stone from the earth and throwing it at their vessels. Just as he released his throw, his foot slipped violently against the side of the hill, spoiling his aim. The heavy rock overshot the river entirely and crashed down at the crossroads of Teddington Hands, where it remains to this day as the prehistoric standing stone known as the Tibblestone. The deep scar left in the turf by the giant's slipping foot is still said to mark the hillside of Dixton Hill.
