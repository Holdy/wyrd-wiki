    The Slayer of the Deerhurst Dragon

    Summary

    A local labourer named John Smith rid the Gloucestershire parish of a monstrous dragon by lulling it with milk before decapitating it with a single stroke of his axe, earning an estate at Walton Hill.

    Details

    Long ago in the parish of Deerhurst (near Apperley and Tewkesbury), a ferocious dragon terrorized the villagers, poisoning them with its fiery breath and devouring their cattle. In response to the terror, the King issued a proclamation promising a grant of land at Walton Hill to anyone brave enough to rid the land of the beast. A local labourer named John Smith devised a clever plan: when the dragon was away from its lair, Smith placed a large quantity of milk at the entrance and hid nearby. Returning and finding the milk, the dragon drank it all and, feeling heavily satiated, lay down in the sun to sleep, ruffling its scales to let the air circulate. Seizing this exact moment, Smith crept up and struck a decisive blow with his large axe between the dragon's unshielded scales on its neck, severing its head in one swift stroke. In reward, Smith received the estate at Walton Hill, and the axe was passed down through generations of the Smith family. Carvings commemorating the beast can still be seen inside Deerhurst Church today.
