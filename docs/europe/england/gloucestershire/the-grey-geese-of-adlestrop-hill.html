    The Grey Geese of Adlestrop Hill

    Summary

    An old woman driving her flock of geese across Adlestrop Hill refused to give alms to a passing witch, who promptly cursed the birds and turned them into stone. These petrified geese remained on the hilltop for generations until they were eventually removed to decorate a nearby estate.

    Details

    According to local tradition on Adlestrop Hill in Gloucestershire, an old woman was driving her flock of geese to pasture when she encountered an old woman who was one of the weird sisters. The beggar demanded alms or money for one of the geese, but the gooseherd stubbornly refused. In retaliation, the witch cast a spell over the entire flock, instantly petrifying the birds into stone. These standing stones became known as the Grey Geese of Adlestrop Hill and stood upon the summit for time immemorial, until the former governor-general Warren Hastings had them uprooted to form rockwork and an artificial cascade in the grounds of Daylesford House.
