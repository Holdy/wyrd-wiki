The Devil's Wheelbarrow and Cam Peak

Summary

Fearing that the Severn estuary had become too godly, the Devil attempted to dam the waters with a heavy wheelbarrow of earth, only to abandon his plan and dump his load at Cam Peak after being tricked by a local cobbler.

Details

Local tradition relates that the Devil, aggrieved by how godly Gloucestershire had become, resolved to dam the Severn estuary by wheeling a massive barrow of earth through Cam. As he pushed the heavy load along, he grew weary and encountered a local cobbler. Hoping to gauge how much further he had to travel, the Devil asked the man the distance to the river. The quick-thinking cobbler immediately recognized the Devil's malicious intent and pointed to a large sack filled with dozens of worn-out boots and shoes, claiming he had worn them all out just walking from the river himself. Believing the estuary was still leagues away and disheartened by the immense distance, the Devil gave up in frustration and upended his wheelbarrow right there, leaving behind the distinctive pointed mound known today as Cam Peak.
