    The Rectory Rector of English Bicknor

    Summary

    During the mid-nineteenth century, the eccentric and formidable rector of English Bicknor maintained order among his parishioners by challenging rowdy pub-goers to physical fights and personally scaling church steeples into his sixties.

    Details

    Serving as the rector of English Bicknor from 1844 to 1877, Reverend John Burdon was renowned throughout the parish for his unorthodox, hands-on approach to both pastoral care and local discipline. When late-night revellers at the village pub (The Bear Inn, situated opposite the rectory) repeatedly disturbed the peace, Burdon did not merely send a polite complaint; instead, he stepped outside, removed his coat, and marched over to challenge the primary troublemaker to settle the dispute physically, bringing a swift and permanent end to the disturbances.

    His physical fearlessness extended to his maintenance of the village church. Even after turning sixty years old, Burdon was frequently found scaling the heights of the 12th-century St Mary's Church steeple to repair the cockerel weather vane with his own hands. Alongside his athletic exploits, he kept a medicinal cupboard for parishioners who could not afford a doctor; among his memorable remedies was treating a man who had accidentally swallowed a pin not with surgery or medicine, but by ordering him a massive, hearty meal to pass it safely through.
