    The Haunting of Wyck Rissington Rectory

    Summary

    When the Cheales family moved into Wyck Rissington Rectory in 1947, they experienced severe poltergeist activity, including heavy footsteps, disembodied screams, and violent nocturnal disturbances. The family ultimately quieted the haunting by ignoring the phenomena and adopting a friendly attitude toward the spirit.

    Details

    In 1947, Canon Harry Cheales, his wife, and their two daughters moved into Wyck Rissington Rectory in Gloucestershire. Choosing to live in the older Elizabethan and Queen Anne wing of the house, the family soon encountered strange phenomena. Within a month, they began hearing slow, measured footsteps—resembling hobnail boots—pacing up the stairs and walking past their bedroom door, though investigations revealed no one there. Local inquiries uncovered that former maids had long avoided a specific room due to frequent sightings of an elderly bearded apparition.

    After three months, the haunting escalated dramatically. An ear-piercing disembodied scream terrorized the children, followed by a violent explosion beneath the master bed that physically lifted the couple, rattled the windows, and brought down ceiling plaster. Seeking guidance, Canon Cheales consulted historical precedents of similar rectories and decided to take a hands-off approach, naming the spirit "Geoffrey" and treating him like an eccentric former resident. As the family maintained this relaxed attitude, the aggressive manifestations faded over time, leaving behind only the occasional sighting of a phantom black cat and an annual, inexplicable sound of a smashing window.
