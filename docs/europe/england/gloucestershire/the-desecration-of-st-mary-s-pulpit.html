    The Desecration of St Mary's Pulpit

    Summary

    In 1682, the notorious rake and politician Thomas Wharton drunkenly broke into a village church and committed an outrageous sacrilege in the pulpit. This infamous act remains the most notorious local legend associated with the parish.

    Details

    In 1682, Thomas Wharton (later the 1st Marquess of Wharton), renowned across the country as a brilliant yet extraordinarily dissolute political figure and hell-raiser, visited Great Barrington in Gloucestershire. During a night of heavy drinking and revelry, Wharton and his companions forced their way into the parish church of St Mary. In an act of shocking irreverence and drunken bravado, Wharton mounted the pulpit and relieved himself inside it. The scandalous act quickly became embedded in local memory and church lore, surviving down the centuries as the most infamous episode in the history of the building.
