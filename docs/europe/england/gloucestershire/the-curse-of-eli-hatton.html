    The Curse of Eli Hatton

    Summary

    After being hanged in a gibbet for murder in 1723, a condemned man named Eli Hatton reportedly placed a curse on Mitcheldean that brought plagues of flies to the town.

    Details

    In the early 18th century, a carpenter named Tom Twibervile was battered to death in his shop in Mitcheldean. Eli Hatton was convicted of the murder and sentenced to death. In 1723, Hatton was hanged at Pingry Tump at Wigpool, a high spot overlooking the town, and his body was left hanging in a gibbet as a grim signpost and warning to others. According to local legend and historical accounts, Hatton placed a curse upon Mitcheldean before his death, which was long blamed for recurring plagues of flies descending upon the settlement.
