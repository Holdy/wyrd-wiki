    The Gibbet of Bourton Hill

    Summary

    Until the middle of the nineteenth century, Bourton Hill served as a grim execution site where the bodies of notorious highwaymen and criminals were displayed in chains. The prominent clump of trees marking the spot became locally renowned as Jones's Gibbet, steeped in dark local lore.

    Details

    Perched high on the Cotswold slopes along the old London to Worcester turnpike road, Bourton-on-the-Hill was frequently plagued by highway robbery during the coaching era. Captured outlaws who preyed upon travellers crossing Bourton Hill and Bourton Downs faced swift and brutal justice. Following their execution, their remains were strung up on a gibbet erected directly on Bourton Hill to serve as a grisly deterrent to other wrongdoers. The cluster of trees surrounding the gallows became widely known in local folklore as Jones's Gibbet, remaining a feared and infamous landmark until the structure was finally dismantled in the mid-19th century.
