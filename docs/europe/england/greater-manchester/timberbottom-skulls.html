    Timberbottom skulls

    Summary

    Two skulls, a man's and a woman's, found in Bradshaw Brook in 1750 were kept at Timberbottom Farm, and every attempt to bury them or throw them away brought moving furniture, loud noises and screaming until they were returned. They later moved to Bradshaw Hall and, after it was demolished, to Turton Tower.

    Details

    In 1750 two human skulls, one male and one female, were reportedly found in Bradshaw Brook near Timberbottom Farm, which became known as the "Skull House". They were kept on the farmhouse mantelpiece, and tradition held that they must stay in the house or its occupants would never be left in peace. Lancashire Legends (1873) records that they were buried several times in Bradshaw Chapel graveyard and thrown into the river, but each time furniture moved, loud noises and screaming broke out until they were dug up and brought back. In 1940 Colonel Henry Hardcastle told Harry Price that his grandfather had buried them in the churchyard, which caused such violent disturbances that they were exhumed and placed on the family Bible at Bradshaw Hall. The female skull was mounted in silver, and when it was sent to a Manchester silversmith for repair the disturbances continued until it came home. After Bradshaw Hall was demolished in the 1950s, the skulls reportedly went to Turton Tower, their last recorded location.
