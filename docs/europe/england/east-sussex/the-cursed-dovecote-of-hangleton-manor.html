The Cursed Dovecote of Hangleton Manor

Summary

A disgruntled monk placed a curse on the 17th-century dovecote of Hangleton Manor due to the mess left by the birds, leaving the structure supposedly haunted by ghost pigeons ever since.

Details

At Hangleton Manor, built in the mid-16th century and once home to the High Sheriff of Sussex, a unique 17th-century dovecote sat in the grounds to house the estate's pigeons. According to local folklore, a monk grew deeply frustrated and angered by the persistent mess and droppings left around by the birds. In retaliation, he placed a fiery curse upon the dovecote. Ever since that day, legend claims the structure has remained haunted, with people reporting the eerie presence of ghost pigeons lingering around the site.
