    Skull House skull

    Summary

    Legend says the skull at Skull House in Appley Bridge belonged to a monk who hid in the chimney from the Roundheads and was smoked out and beheaded. Every attempt to get rid of it has ended badly: one man threw it in the River Douglas, found it back in its place, and later drowned in that same river.

    Details

    Skull House Lane in Appley Bridge takes its name from a cottage called Skull House, which keeps a discoloured human skull. Legend says it belonged to a monk who fled his monastery when the Roundheads were hunting monks and hid in a cubby hole in the chimney breast, until the soldiers lit the fire to smoke him out and beheaded him. The skull was found during renovation work, and the residents' attempts to be rid of it went badly. One man threw it into the River Douglas at the bottom of the lane, came home to find it back in its place, and later drowned in that same river. Another took it as far away as he could, only for it to return, and he was badly hurt falling down the stairs. Others who tried are said to have suffered serious illness or the deaths of loved ones, and the current residents have never tried to move it. Scientific analysis has since shown that the skull is a woman's.
