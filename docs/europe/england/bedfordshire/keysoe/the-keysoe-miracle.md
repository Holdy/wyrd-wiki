#title
The Keysoe Miracle

#summary
In 1718 a mason named William Dickins fell from the spire of St Mary's Church in Keysoe, crying out to God for mercy as he fell, and survived. A stone tablet on the church tower records his deliverance as a miracle.

#details
On 17 April 1718 William Dickins, then 32 years old, was repointing the spire of St Mary the Virgin at Keysoe. He slipped from the ridge of the middle window of the spire, near the south-west pinnacle, and fell. On the way down he struck the battlements of the tower, breaking a leg and a foot, and landed on a long stone with his neck across it.

As he fell he called out "Lord have mercy upon me", and he afterwards gave the credit for his survival to God and to Jesus Christ. Local tradition holds that he was saved by the prayer he spoke in mid-air.

He lived another 41 years and died on 29 November 1759, aged 73. His preservation is recorded on a tablet set into the west face of the church tower, in verse of old-fashioned spelling that thanks God for saving him. In 1780 the wife of the parish clerk recalled that the stone was made at Bedford, that the Dickins brothers were "Meetingers", and that they "Poeted for themselves": the brothers seem to have written the verse.

Dickins was a Baptist, a member of the meeting at Brook End, yet he was buried in the churchyard of St Mary's, close to the tower from which he fell.

#location
St Mary the Virgin, Church Road, south-west of the village of Keysoe, Bedfordshire. The tablet is on the west face of the tower.
