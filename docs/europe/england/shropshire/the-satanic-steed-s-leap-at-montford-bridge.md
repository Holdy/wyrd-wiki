#title
The Satanic Steed's Leap at Montford Bridge

#summary
When the local sheriff tried to trap the outlaw Wild Humphrey Kynaston by dismantling Montford Bridge, Humphrey's devil-gifted steed Beelzebub cleared the gaping chasm in a single bound.

#details
Sir Humphrey Kynaston, a notorious 16th-century highwayman living as an outlaw in Nesscliffe caves, rode a formidable and intelligent black horse named Beelzebub—rumoured to have once been ridden by the devil himself or given to Humphrey through a dark bargain with Old Nick. Exasperated by Humphrey's endless successful robberies and uncanny ability to vanish without a trace, the local sheriff devised a trap at Montford Bridge crossing the River Severn. Under cover of night, lawmen removed the wooden planks from the span of the bridge, leaving a wide, perilous gap intended to plunge the outlaw and his horse into the river below. As Humphrey approached the bridge and sensed the trap, he spurred Beelzebub forward, causing the Satanic steed to leap effortlessly across the forty-foot gap, escaping capture once again.

#location
Montford Bridge carries the road across the River Severn in the village of Montford Bridge, Shropshire.
