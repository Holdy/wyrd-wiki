    The Bottled Terror of Kinlet Church

    Summary

    Following his death, the wrathful spirit of Sir George Blount terrorized the village of Kinlet on a black horse until local clergymen successfully captured and sealed his ghost inside a glass bottle near his tomb.

    Details

    Sir George Blount, a fierce military commander and landowner, returned to his home in Kinlet after campaigning in Scotland. Following tragic personal losses, including his son's death and a bitter family dispute over his daughter's romantic choices, Blount died with a heart full of unresolved fury. Soon after his death, his ghost emerged riding a large black horse, terrorizing the residents of Kinlet and lashing out at estate workers. When Kinlet Hall was rebuilt around 1720, the haunting intensified even further. To put an end to the terror, local clergy performed a rigorous ghost-laying ritual involving candles and intense prayer, successfully shrinking and sealing the wrathful spirit into a small glass bottle. This bottle was placed near Sir George's tomb inside the village church, where it remained a notorious local landmark until the early 20th century.
