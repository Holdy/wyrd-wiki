    Wild Kynaston and his Satanic Steed Beelzebub

    Summary

    The notorious outlaw Sir Humphrey Kynaston evaded the authorities with the aid of his supernatural horse named Beelzebub, whom local legend claimed was either a gift from a wizard or the devil incarnate. Operating from a cliffside lair on Nesscliffe Hill, he robbed the wealthy while protecting local villagers.

    Details

    Following financial ruin and being outlawed in the late fifteenth century, Sir Humphrey Kynaston abandoned his family castle and made his home in a two-roomed sandstone cave carved into Nesscliffe Hill. Alongside his reputation as a Robin Hood-figure who redistributed wealth among the local poor, Kynaston became legendary for his impossible escapes from the Sheriff's men. According to folklore, these supernatural feats were made possible by his jet-black horse, Beelzebub—a beast said to have been ridden by the devil himself or formed when the devil transformed into a steed to serve the outlaw. With Beelzebub, Kynaston famously leaped across gaping river chasms and prison-like gates before retiring safely to his cave hideout.
