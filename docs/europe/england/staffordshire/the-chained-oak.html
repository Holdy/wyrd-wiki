The Chained Oak

Summary

An old beggar curses the Earl of Shrewsbury by declaring that a member of his family will die for every branch that falls from a specific oak tree. To prevent further deaths, the Earl orders the tree's branches to be bound in heavy iron chains.

Details

One night in 1821, the Earl of Shrewsbury was traveling back to Alton Towers in his carriage when an impoverished beggar woman (or old man, depending on the variant) stopped him to beg for alms. The Earl cruelly dismissed the request and told his driver to move on. In retaliation, the beggar placed a dreadful curse upon the noble family, proclaiming that for every branch that fell from a nearby oak tree, a member of the Earl's family would die. That very night, a severe storm struck, a branch broke from the tree, and a member of the Earl's family mysteriously passed away. Terrified that the falling wood would claim more lives, the Earl ordered his servants to bind the remaining limbs of the tree together with heavy iron chains. To this day, the tree stands wrapped in chains in the woods near Alton.
