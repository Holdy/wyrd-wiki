#title
The Floating Face of Barton Broad

#summary
A giant phantom face of a tragic medieval lady is said to manifest on the surface and in the mist of Barton Broad. The apparition has been witnessed across centuries by locals, historical figures, and aviators alike.

#details
According to local folklore, Barton Broad is haunted by a benevolent lady known as Lady Edythe, whose visible presence brings good luck and plentiful fishing. According to family legends passed down through generations, her haunting stems from a tragic medieval incident during the Crusades where a knight accidentally killed his daughter with a crossbow bolt—either by missing her fiancé or by sinking the escaping lovers' boat and drowning them both. Over the centuries, the vision has been recorded by numerous witnesses, including the Bishop of Wentworth in his 1781 poem *Barton Broad*, and by Flight Lieutenant Ronald Jacoby, who flew over the lake in an open-cockpit plane in September 1916 and saw a massive face turn toward him. Under calm, windless conditions with a slowly rising mist, the face can occasionally be witnessed on the early morning of August 5th, floating between 450 and 600 feet in altitude or resting upon the water's surface with closed eyes.
