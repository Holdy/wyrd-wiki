    The Royal Oak of Crostwick Common

    Summary

    An ancient oak tree once stood on the common near St. Peter's Church in Crostwick, carrying a local tradition that a Queen of England once held court beneath its branches before it was struck multiple times by lightning.

    Details

    According to traditional accounts recorded in the 19th century, a remarkably large and ancient oak tree stood upon the common land not far from St. Peter's Church in the Norfolk village of Crostwick. Local legend maintained that an English queen had once assembled and held her royal court beneath its sprawling boughs. Alongside this royal association, the tree was also said to have been struck by thunderbolts on no less than three separate occasions.
