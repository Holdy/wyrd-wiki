    The Salamander of St. German's Church

    Summary

    Carved into a medieval pew end within St. German's Parish Church, a traditional salamander sculpture reflects old folklore regarding creatures immune to fire and sin.

    Details

    St. German's Parish Church in Wiggenhall St Germans contains a collection of ancient, hand-carved wooden pews dating back to the medieval period. Among these intricate carvings is the figure of a salamander. According to ancient mythology and medieval folk belief, salamanders were thought to be completely fireproof and capable of extinguishing flames, symbolizing spiritual resilience against the fiery temptations of sin. Craftsmen and parishioners incorporated such symbolic creatures into church woodwork as a protective emblem against moral corruption and spiritual peril.
