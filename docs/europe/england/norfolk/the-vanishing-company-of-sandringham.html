    The Vanishing Company of Sandringham

    Summary

    During the First World War, a local company of estate workers from West Newton disappeared during battle at Gallipoli, giving rise to a haunting local legend that they had vanished into a mysterious cloud. A memorial window inside the village church commemorates their tragic fate and enduring myth.

    Details

    At the heart of the royal estate village of West Newton, St Peter and St Paul's Church stands as a poignant reminder of a devastating local loss during the First World War. In 1906, Captain Frank Beck—the trusted land agent for the Royal Family—formed the 1/5th Battalion Norfolk Regiment, known as the Sandringham Company, composed entirely of gardeners, farm hands, grooms, and estate workers from West Newton and surrounding villages. On August 12, 1915, during the Gallipoli Campaign at Suvla Bay, the company advanced into battle under heavy machine-gun fire and disappeared behind enemy lines, never to be seen alive again. Because they were officially listed as missing for years, a local legend took root in West Newton that the doomed company had not been killed in combat, but rather had vanished into a strange, mystical cloud and been taken up straight to heaven. Inside the church, an Arts and Crafts memorial window designed by Karl Parsons in 1920 depicts Captain Frank Beck as St George alongside the fiery landscape of Suvla Bay, immortalizing both the tragedy and the enduring folklore of the village.
