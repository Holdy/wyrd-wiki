#title
The Pump Hill Spectre

#summary
A terrifying apparition of a legless, nearly headless sailor was seen roaming from Cart Gap toward Happisburgh before disappearing down a village well, where villagers subsequently discovered the dismembered remains of a murdered man.

#details
During the late 17th or early 18th century, travelers and farmers returning home near Cart Gap encountered a horrifying specter: a figure resembling a hunchbacked sailor without legs, whose nearly severed head hung down its back by a thin strip of flesh, carrying a bundle containing its own severed legs. The ghost drifted through the village streets before being tracked to Pump Hill (also known as Well Corner), where it vanished after throwing its bundle down an old well. The next day, curious locals searched the well and pulled out a sack of legs, followed by the sailor's mutilated torso, cementing the local belief that a gang of smugglers had brutally murdered one of their own and discarded his body there.

#location
The grassy triangle at the junction of Whimpwell Street, School Common Road, and Stalham Road (historically known as Pump Hill or Well Corner) in Happisburgh, Norfolk.
