    The Cursed Foundations of Horsham Priory

    Summary

    When Norman knight Robert Fitzwalter attempted to build a monastery dedicated to St Faith at Kirkescrofte, mysterious forces repeatedly tore down by night what was constructed by day, forcing the abbey to be relocated to Horsham St Faith.

    Details

    Following a pilgrimage to Rome, the Norman lord Robert Fitzwalter and his wife Sybil de Cheney believed they were miraculously saved from robbers by St Faith. In gratitude, they vowed to erect a monastery dedicated to the virgin martyr and chose a site at Horsford known as Kirkescrofte for its construction. However, according to tradition recorded by the antiquary Sir William Dugdale, every stone and wall raised during daylight hours inexplicably collapsed overnight—an obstruction attributed by local lore to divine displeasure or malevolent spirits. Recognizing that St Faith would not permit the monastery to stand there, the founders abandoned Kirkescrofte and successfully re-established the priory at Horsham St Faith instead. Additionally, local legend maintains that an underground passage runs for three-quarters of a mile from the Norman motte and bailey castle at Horsford directly to the remnants of the Benedictine priory at Horsham St Faith.
