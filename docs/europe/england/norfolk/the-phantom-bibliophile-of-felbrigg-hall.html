The Phantom Bibliophile of Felbrigg Hall

Summary

William Windham III, a passionate book collector who died following a library fire rescue in 1810, is said to haunt the Gothic library at Felbrigg Hall in Norfolk, where his apparition appears among his beloved volumes.

Details

Felbrigg Hall, a country house and estate in North Norfolk, is reputed to be haunted by several spirits, the most famous of whom is William Windham III (1750–1810). Known in life as an enthusiastic statesman, sportsman, and fervent bibliophile, Windham met his tragic fate after rushing to rescue valuable books and manuscripts from a burning house on Conduit Street in London in 1809. During the rescue, he suffered a severe hip injury that eventually led to a fatal tumor and his death following surgery in 1810. He was buried in the family vault at Felbrigg Church, just a short distance from the hall.

Ever since, his spectre has frequently been encountered within the estate's Gothic library—often described as standing near a table where his favorite volumes are laid out or relaxing in an armchair by the hearth. Alongside the phantom bibliophile, local lore surrounding the hall includes accounts from former parlour maids who experienced mysterious candle-snuffing outside a vacant chamber accompanied by a disembodied voice, as well as legends of "Mad Windham" driving a phantom coach through the dark surrounding woods.
