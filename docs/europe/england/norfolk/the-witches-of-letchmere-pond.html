    The Witches of Letchmere Pond

    Summary

    Local folklore in North Lopham maintains that Letchmere Pond was once used to duck suspected witches, though subsequent dredging uncovered no physical evidence of the trials.

    Details

    In the Norfolk village of North Lopham, Letchmere Pond was historically vital to the local linen and damask industry, used for retting hemp and flax. Alongside its industrial significance, the pond acquired a dark reputation in local oral tradition. Generations of villagers believed that women accused of witchcraft were brought to the pond and subjected to trial by water, being ducked beneath its surface. However, when the pond was dredged in later years, workers discovered no human remains or grisly relics of these supposed trials, leaving the accounts to persist solely as local legend.
