The Lantern Man of Irstead

Summary

On foggy nights prior to 1810, a malignant spectral light known as Jack o' Lantern haunted Heard's Hole in Irstead, smashing lanterns and harassing travellers until its restless spirit was bound.

Details

Before the Irstead Enclosure in 1810, a ghostly dancing light resembling a candle in a lantern—known as Jack o' Lantern or the Lantern Man—was frequently seen on foggy nights at Heard's Hole in Alder Carr Fen Broad. Legend held that this intelligent, malevolent apparition was the spirit of a criminal named Heard who had drowned there. The spectral light actively attacked people carrying lanterns, knocked a mocking gentleman off his horse in the neighboring parish of Horning, and tormented scoffers by hovering outside their windows. Eventually, villagers sought to lay the spirit to rest; although the ghost kept ahead of scripture-reading men, a boy laid down a pair of pigeons which distracted the entity, allowing the men to successfully bind its spirit.
