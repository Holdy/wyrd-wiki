    The Warring Brothers of Wickhampton

    Summary

    According to local legend, two quarrelsome brothers who fought bitterly over land and inheritance tore out each other's hearts before being struck down and turned to stone by a divine light.

    Details

    In the 13th century, the neighboring Norfolk villages of Halvergate and Wickhampton were owned by Sir Gerbygge, who had two quarrelsome sons named Gilbert and William. Gilbert, the eldest, demanded a greater share of the family's estate, prompting Sir Gerbygge to divide the properties by giving Halvergate to Gilbert and Wickhampton to William. Despite their father's dying prayers for peace, the brothers continued to clash over inheritance and parish boundaries. During a violent confrontation in Wickhampton where Gilbert disputed a field boundary, the feud reached a bloody climax. Blinded by fury, the brothers tore each other's hearts out in front of horrified villagers. A strange, divine light suddenly flashed across the sky, turning their lifeless bodies to stone. Today, inside St Andrew's Church in Wickhampton, 13th-century altar tombs featuring the effigy of Sir William Gerbrygge holding a heart stand as a chilling memorial to the legend.
