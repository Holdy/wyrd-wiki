    The Bloody Water Dykes of Reymerston

    Summary

    Ancient earthwork dykes in Reymerston are said to be the remnants of an old war, with local tradition maintaining that the water in the ditches ran colored with blood during heavy rains.

    Details

    According to oral tradition passed down through generations in the Norfolk countryside, old earthwork dykes located across Reymerston and Hardingham mark the site of a forgotten ancient conflict. Elderly residents of the area recounted tales told by their fathers and grandfathers of these "bloody water dykes," claiming that traces of blood remained embedded in the soil and that during certain rains, the water running through the ditches would turn red. Tradition also linked this violent period of war to the simultaneous ruin of the nearby Southburgh church tower and the moated house known as 'the Moats' near the parish boundary. Today, the medieval moat and fish ponds associated with this history are marked on maps within Reymerston parish.
