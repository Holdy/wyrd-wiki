The Witch's Leg

Summary

A suspected witch buried alive within the ruins of a medieval church in East Somerton enchanted her wooden leg to sprout an oak tree that would destroy the building above. Walking around this tree three times is said to release her spirit, though spectral guardians keep intruders at bay.

Details

Deep in the woodlands of East Somerton lie the romantic ruins of the 15th-century St Mary’s Church, where nature has reclaimed the tumbling stone walls and open-sky tower. According to local legend, during the height of England's witch trials, a suspected witch possessing a wooden leg was brutally buried alive in the church's nave. In her final moments of suffering, she cast a curse, enchanting her wooden appendage to grow into an oak tree that would tear down the sanctuary. Folklore warns that anyone brave enough to walk around the tree three times will awaken and release the witch's spirit, though ghostly figures are believed to haunt the ruins and prevent anyone from doing so.
