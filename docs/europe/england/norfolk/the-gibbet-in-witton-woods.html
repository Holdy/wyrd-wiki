    The Gibbet in Witton Woods

    Summary

    After being executed for the brutal murder of his lover in 1797, William Suffolk's body was suspended in a gibbet in Witton Woods, where his vengeful ghost is said to still haunt the trees.

    Details

    In 1797, a 46-year-old married man named William Suffolk engaged in an affair with a younger woman named Mary Beck, resulting in a secret pregnancy. Fearing exposure, they conspired to hide the child, but Mary's brother eventually discovered the affair and demanded Suffolk end it. When Mary attempted to break off the relationship during a meeting on February 3, 1797, Suffolk flew into a rage, beating her with a large stick and leaving her body by a cart track with her head in a wheel rut. Mary survived long enough to identify Suffolk as her attacker before dying two days later. Suffolk was tried, sentenced to death, and hanged at Norwich Castle on March 25, 1797. Following his execution, his body was placed inside an iron cage suspended from a gibbet post in Witton Woods (also known as Bacton Wood), where it hung for years as a grim public deterrent. Over subsequent generations, locals reported eerie phenomena near the site of the former gibbet, including the sound of creaking chains, horses shying away in terror, and the phantom appearance of a human skull.
