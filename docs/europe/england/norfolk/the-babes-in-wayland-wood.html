The Babes in Wayland Wood

Summary

In the sixteenth century, an avaricious uncle abandoned his orphaned niece and nephew in a Norfolk forest to inherit their fortune, inspiring the dark local legend immortalized on the Watton town sign.

Details

According to local Norfolk folklore, two young orphaned children—a brother and sister—were left in the care of their uncle at Griston Hall. Eager to inherit their late father's wealth, the villainous uncle took the children deep into nearby Wayland Wood in the dead of winter and abandoned them to perish. When the children ultimately succumbed to starvation and the freezing cold, compassionate robins covered their lifeless bodies with leaves. Local tradition holds that the ghostly wails of the murdered children still echo through the ancient trees of the wood, and the historic oak beneath which they supposedly huddled together was struck by lightning in 1879. The tragic account, which later evolved into a popular nursery fable and pantomime, is permanently commemorated on the official town sign of Watton.
