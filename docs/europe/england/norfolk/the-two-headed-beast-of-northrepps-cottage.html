The Two-Headed Beast of Northrepps Cottage

Summary

A terrifying two-headed spectral hound and shape-shifting phantom haunted a sunken tree-arched lane behind a historic Norfolk cottage, while a later owner encountered a massive black dog inside his own cellar. Local smugglers also utilized the legend to ward off unwanted nighttime visitors.

Details

Shuck's Lane—a sunken, tree-arched track running behind Northrepps Cottage, a building established in 1793 as part of the Northrepps estate—gained a fearsome reputation for being haunted by a spectral hound known as Shuck. Unlike other traditional phantom dogs, this particular entity was uniquely described as having two heads and saucer-like eyes, possessing the ability to shape-change into forms such as a rolling wool-pack or a calf. Local smugglers reportedly cultivated and used the terrifying legend to keep people away at night, culminating in a violent clash between smugglers and preventive officers in the early 1820s that resulted in two fatalities. Years later, the proprietor of Northrepps Cottage, Mr. Hoy, experienced a direct physical encounter when he saw a large black dog inside his cellar, only for the creature to mysteriously vanish.
