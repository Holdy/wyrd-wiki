    The Legend of Tom Hickathrift, the Fenland Giant

    Summary

    According to centuries-old Norfolk folklore, the mighty giant Tom Hickathrift defended the common lands of Marshland St James against an invading ogre and local oppressors, armed only with a cart-wheel and axle-tree. His legendary exploits left permanent marks on the Fenland landscape, including an earthen burial mound and wash-basin at the village crossroads.

    Details

    Long before the fens of East Anglia were fully drained, the area known as "The Smeeth" in the Marshland was common pasture grazed by cattle and sheep from the Seven Towns of the region. According to local legend, this area was the birthplace and domain of Tom Hickathrift, a man of monstrous size and superhuman strength who lived before the time of the Normans. Born to a poor labourer's widow, young Tom grew to an extraordinary height of eight feet by age ten, with hands like shoulders of mutton, though his immense strength was initially undiscovered.

    When greedy landlords and later a fierce, man-eating ogre threatened the common rights of the marshland folk and forced travelers to make long detours, Tom stood up to defend his neighbours. Refusing to be intimidated by the ogre's threats, he ripped the axle-tree from a brewer's cart to use as a sword and a heavy cart-wheel as a shield. With these improvised weapons, Tom engaged in a brutal battle against the 12-foot beast of the Smeeth, ultimately slaying the monster and restoring liberties to the local people.

    The legend became deeply rooted in the local geography of Marshland St James. In a field south of the village crossroads, an ancient earthen mound was long pointed out by locals as the giant's grave, alongside hollows known as his wash-basin and feeding-bowl. Tradition also holds that Tom hurled a stone across the fens to choose his final resting place, leading to his traditional grave being venerated at nearby Tilney All Saints churchyard.
