    The Vault Scandal of Ketteringham Church

    Summary

    In 1853, an unholy row erupted in the Norfolk village of Ketteringham when the lord of the manor had coffins removed from beneath St Peter's Church to make room for his own family, sparking a bitter feud between squire and parson that passed into local folklore.

    Details

    In the quiet Norfolk village of Ketteringham, a deep animosity simmered for years between the manor house and the local parish. Sir John Peter Boileau, an antiquarian baronet and lord of the manor who lived at Ketteringham Hall, frequently clashed with the parish vicar, the Reverend W.W. Andrew, who fiercely defended his authority at St Peter's Church located near the entrance to the hall estate. The underlying tensions between the two men culminated in December 1853 in a notorious incident concerning the church vaults. Desiring additional space for his own family's burials, Sir John arranged for coffins previously resting beneath the church to be unceremoniously removed and relocated. This controversial disturbance of the dead sparked a furious parish scandal and a bitter war of words between squire and parson. The intense clash over class, authority, and the sanctity of the resting dead became a legendary piece of local lore reflecting the turbulent relationship between the parish rulers.
