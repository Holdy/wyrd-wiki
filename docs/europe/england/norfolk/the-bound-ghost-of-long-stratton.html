    The Bound Ghost of Long Stratton

    Summary

    According to local folklore collected in the early 20th century, a clergyman named Parson Solly successfully bound a disruptive spirit into the trunk of an ancient oak tree in Long Stratton.

    Details

    In traditional accounts recorded during the early 1900s, villagers passed down stories of a troublesome ghost haunting the parish of Long Stratton. To protect the community from the spirit's hauntings, a local clergyman known as Parson Solly (or Solley) used his spiritual authority to banish and bind the entity into a prominent oak tree within the village. Although the oak tree has since vanished over time, the account was preserved in regional folklore records as a classic example of traditional clerical spirit-binding.
