The Secret Tunnel and Ghost of Foulsham Old Hall

Summary

Foulsham Old Hall is said to be haunted by a white-haired gentleman in a dressing gown, and linked to the parish church by a mysterious underground tunnel that reveals itself during droughts and cave-ins.

Details

Local legend holds that Foulsham Old Hall—historically associated with the Parliamentarian commander Major-General Philip Skippon—harbors a haunting specter. According to local accounts recorded in the 19th century, a poor milkwoman who arrived early one morning to collect milk from the Hall caught sight of a white-haired gentleman wandering the grounds in a dressing gown. Furthermore, tradition long maintained the existence of a subterranean passage running from the Hall's medieval moat all the way to the porch of the village church, a quarter of a mile away. This hidden tunnel was said to be traceable during long dry summers by the parched, discolored grass directly above it. The legend gained startling local credence when parish labourers working in the pasture north of the Hall suddenly fell through the collapsing ground into a brick archway, allowing a local bricklayer to explore sections of the ancient tunnel before being blocked by accumulated earth and debris.
