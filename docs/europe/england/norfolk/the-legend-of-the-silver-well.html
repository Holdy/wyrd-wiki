    The Legend of the Silver Well

    Summary

    Hidden abbey treasure and a devilish struggle are said to account for the shimmering, mineral-rich waters of a historic spring in Fincham. Local tradition maintains that workmen who once unearthed a box of ancient silverware were forced to drop it back into the depths when confronted by the devil.

    Details

    Located in a small woodland known as the High Plantation on the south slope of the Nar valley in Fincham, the Silver Well was a chalybeate spring celebrated for the peculiar silvery appearance of its water. According to local folklore, this strange sheen is tied to lost riches. One version of the legend states that treasure from a dissolved abbey was hidden within the spring during the Reformation. A more dramatic account, recorded by folklorists, relates that workmen repairing the well managed to hoist up a container of old silverware, only to be set upon by the devil during the encounter. Terrified by the demonic confrontation, the men dropped the treasure back into the subterranean depths, where it has remained ever since, permanently tinting the waters.
