The Fugitive King at the Swan

Summary

Following his defeat in the English Civil War, King Charles I sought secret refuge in Downham Market disguised as a clergyman while hiding from Parliamentary pursuers.

Details

In late April and early May of 1646, as the English Civil War drew to a desperate close following his defeat at the Battle of Naseby and his escape from the besieged city of Oxford, King Charles I fled northward across the Fens. Traveling in disguise with his iconic long, curly hair cut short and using the alias "Harry", the fugitive monarch arrived in Downham Market. On May Day in 1646, he sought sanctuary and hid inside an inn that once stood on the site of the modern Swan Inn, desperately attempting to evade capture by Oliver Cromwell's Parliamentary forces before continuing his journey toward the Scottish army camp.
