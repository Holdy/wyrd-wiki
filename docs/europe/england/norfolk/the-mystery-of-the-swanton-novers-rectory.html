The Mystery of the Swanton Novers Rectory

Summary

In 1919, the rectory in Swanton Novers was besieged by inexplicable showers of water and inflammable liquids dripping from its ceilings, leading to a sensational investigation and accusations against a young servant girl.

Details

In August 1919, Swanton Novers Rectory—then home to the Reverend H. Guy and his family—became the center of a bizarre poltergeist-like mystery when various liquids, including petrol, paraffin, methylated spirits, and water, began mysteriously dripping from every ceiling in the house. Despite examining the plaster and laths and shutting off the home's petrol lighting plant, experts could find no rational source for the ooze. The phenomenon attracted widespread attention until illusionist Mr. Oswald Williams and his wife intervened; Mrs. Williams subsequently claimed she witnessed the rector's 15-year-old servant girl, Mabel Phillips, throwing salted water toward the ceiling through a floor hole. Although Mabel allegedly confessed before later vehemently denying it and accusing Mrs. Williams of assault, the baffling disturbances and subsequent legal disputes deeply divided the local community.
