    The Legend of Cutthroat Drift and Clifton's Field

    Summary

    A secluded Norfolk track derives its ominous name from a historic murder, culminating in the execution and gibbeting of a local criminal. The grim tale of greed, betrayal, and the recovery of the murderer's skull became a deeply embedded piece of parish folklore.

    Details

    Cutthroat Drift is a straight green track that winds between Dereham Road and Mattishall Road, earning its dark moniker from an old legend in which a young woman's throat was cut. Years later, a man named Clifton was drinking at the "Great A" inn on Church Lane when he overheard a boastful farmer talking about the large amount of cash he carried. Luring the farmer down a quiet path leading from the church toward the mill on Mattishall road, Clifton murdered him in a field that became known as "Clifton's Field." Clifton was convicted, hanged outside Norwich County Gaol, and his body was displayed in chains on a gibbet near Yaxham. Long afterward, his skull was unearthed by a plough and passed down through local generations before finally being deposited in the Norwich Castle Museum.
