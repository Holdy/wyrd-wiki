    The Remarkable Occurrence at North Walsham

    Summary

    A sexton's long-hidden confession reveals that an actress performing in North Walsham unwittingly used the skull of her murdered first husband as a prop, causing her to scratch herself on the poisoned bone and die from the same toxic substance she had used on him years prior.

    Details

    In 1789, John Flaxman, sexton of North Walsham parish, penned a confidential account detailing a grim tragedy. In 1774, local smuggler Tom Norris returned from Spain with a head wound and a talented young bride who possessed extensive knowledge of herbal poisons. Under the guise of treating his wound, she systematically poisoned him with a concentrated liquid distilled from ants (formic acid) until he died in 1776. Flaxman later exhumed the body and kept the skull as a memento. Twelve years later, the widow—performing under the stage name Mrs. Barry—returned to North Walsham with a theatrical troupe. During a performance of The Fair Penitent at a local barn theatre, she used the preserved skull as a prop, scratched her thumb on the sharp fragment of bone where the lingering poison remained, and quickly succumbed to tetanus and poisoning from her own deadly concoction.
