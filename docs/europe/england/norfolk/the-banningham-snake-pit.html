The Banningham Snake Pit

Summary

According to local legend, Richard Cromwell installed a 14-foot deep snake pit on the Banningham village green during the 17th century where men could test the marital fidelity of their wives.

Details

During the turbulent era of the 17th century, when witch hunts and strict moral codes swept East Anglia under the influence of figures like Matthew Hopkins and Oliver Cromwell, a dark local legend took root in Banningham. It was said that Richard Cromwell, the son of the Lord Protector, commissioned a 14-foot deep pit on the village green and filled it with venomous snakes. According to village lore, any husband harboring suspicions regarding his wife's conduct could throw her into the pit; if she emerged unharmed, her virtue and innocence were proven, whereas failing the ordeal meant a grim fate.
