The Soldiers' Mounds of Bircham Common

Summary

Four ancient barrows on the common were believed by locals to have been hurriedly thrown up by soldiers during wartime, and one of the mounds served as the traditional meeting place for the Smethdon Hundred court.

Details

Local tradition long maintained that the distinctive earthen mounds on Great Bircham Common were not ancient monuments at all, but were instead hastily raised by soldiers during past wars. When antiquarian W. C. Lukis excavated two of these Bronze Age bell barrows in 1842, he discovered an inverted urn containing cremated remains within one, while the other had evidently been robbed in antiquity, yielding only a single pottery fragment. Aside from their military folklore, these prehistoric tumuli held practical importance as the historic site where the court for the Smethdon Hundred would assemble.
