#title
The Brown Lady of Raynham Hall

#summary
Lady Dorothy Walpole haunts her historic Norfolk estate, appearing as an ethereal figure in a brown brocade dress with hollow eye sockets whose apparition was famously captured on film descending the grand staircase.

#details
According to local legend, Lady Dorothy Walpole lived during the 18th century as the second wife of Charles Townshend, 2nd Viscount Townshend. Following marital strife, she was confined to her rooms within the manor until her death from smallpox in 1726. Over the centuries, numerous residents and guests reported encountering her restless spirit. In December 1835, guests including Colonel Loftus saw the brown-robed figure on the corridor landings and noted her chilling, empty eye sockets. In 1836, Captain Frederick Marryat stayed in the most haunted room to debunk the myth, only to be confronted in the dark corridor by the specter grinning maliciously at him before he discharged his revolver straight through her form. The haunting achieved worldwide fame in September 1936 when photographers Hubert Provand and Indre Shira captured an iconic photograph of the veiled, vapoury figure gliding down the manor's main oak staircase.

#location
Raynham Hall is a historic country house situated in the civil parish of Raynham in Norfolk, England, located south of Fakenham.
