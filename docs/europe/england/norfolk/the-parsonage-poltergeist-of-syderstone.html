The Parsonage Poltergeist of Syderstone

Summary

In 1833, the Reverend John Stewart and his family were tormented by a violent poltergeist that produced terrifying scratching noises, moans, and heavy knocks at the parsonage. Despite staying overnight and investigating, local clergy and witnesses could find no rational explanation for the decades-old haunting.

Details

When Reverend John Stewart and his family moved into the Syderstone parsonage near Fakenham in 1833, they soon began referring to their home as 'the House of Mystery'. Almost immediately, inexplicable loud banging, clawing sounds on inner walls, and low moans resembling a whipped soldier or clashing glass began to terrorize the household, causing servants to flee in panic. The noises typically commenced around two o'clock in the morning and continued until daylight, beating to lively tunes or moaning softly during morning and evening hymns.

Seeking answers, the Reverend invited local priests, clergymen, and a surgeon to stay overnight. Witnesses heard clawing animal sounds near Miss Stewart's bed and massive blows struck against the sideboard and walls while hands rested upon them. Historical investigations and contemporary newspaper reports from the Bury and Norwich Post and Norfolk Chronicle revealed that similar violent knockings and groanings had been documented at the parsonage since 1797, preceding even the tenure of the previous troubled curate, William Mantle.
