    The Martyrdom of Margaret in the Little Wood

    Summary

    In May 1170, a young woman named Margaret was murdered in the Little Wood in Hoveton St John, prompting St Benet's Abbey to establish a short-lived cult and shrine in her honor.

    Details

    According to historical accounts and medieval monastic records, including Francis Blomefield's Topographical History of the County of Norfolk, a local girl named Margaret was brutally killed—having her throat cut—in a wooded area known as the Little Wood within the parish of Hoveton St John on May 22, 1170. Seeking to enhance the spiritual prestige of their monastery and capitalize on the lucrative medieval pilgrim trade, the monks of nearby St Benet's Abbey transported her body to be buried under the high altar of the abbey church. They attempted to promote her as a local saint known as St Margaret of Holm. However, despite the abbey's efforts to publicize her martyrdom and draw pilgrims to her shrine, the cult failed to gain widespread appeal or prosperity.
