    The Gruesome Fate of the Rebel Dyer of Felmingham

    Summary

    During the 1381 Peasants' Revolt, local dyer Jack Litester led the Norfolk insurgents before being defeated by the Bishop of Norwich, after which his executed remains were sent back to be publicly displayed at his house in Felmingham.

    Details

    In the summer of 1381, as the Peasants' Revolt swept across England, a dyer named Geoffrey—commonly known as Jack Litester—rose to become the leader of the insurrectionists in Norfolk. Operating from his home in the village of Felmingham, Litester rallied a large force of disgruntled peasants and artisans, marching on Norwich and demanding an end to oppressive taxation and serfdom. However, their campaign was abruptly halted at the Battle of North Walsham by the formidable Bishop of Norwich, Henry le Despenser, who routed the poorly armed rebels. Litester was captured and swiftly condemned to death for treason. He was hanged, drawn, and quartered, and in accordance with the brutal medieval custom of deterrence, his body was divided into four parts. While his other quarters were sent to Norwich, Great Yarmouth, and King's Lynn, one quarter was sent back to Felmingham and displayed at his own house as a grisly warning to the local populace against any future uprisings.
