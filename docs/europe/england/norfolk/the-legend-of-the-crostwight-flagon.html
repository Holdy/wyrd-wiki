    The Legend of the Crostwight Flagon

    Summary

    When a parish priest visited Crostwight Hall to complain about the lack of proper church furnishings, the lord of the manor characteristically poured wine and pushed the communion flagon across the table to him.

    Details

    For centuries, All Saints Church in Crostwight utilized a fine silver parcel-gilt flagon dating back to the Elizabethan era. According to local tradition, the vessel entered the church's possession following a blunt confrontation between the clergy and the local aristocracy. The parish priest called upon the lord of the manor at Crostwight Hall to complain that "the church hath no decent altar furniture." Rather than promising new ecclesiastical items or monetary funds, the lord—who was the patron of the church—reportedly poured two cups of wine, and then pushed the heavy silver flagon across the table towards the priest with the challenge, "Will this do?" The flagon remained in church use for centuries thereafter, and in the 20th century was even safely guarded under a churchwarden's bed behind a chamber pot during turbulent times.
