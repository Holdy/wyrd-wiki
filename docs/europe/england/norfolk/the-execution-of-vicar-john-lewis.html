The Execution of Vicar John Lewis

Summary

John Lewis, the elderly vicar of Brandiston who had served his parish for nearly fifty years, was accused of witchcraft and executed as a wizard during the mid-seventeenth-century witch trials.

Details

John Lewis was presented to the vicarage of Brandiston, Norfolk, on May 6, 1596, and lived there ministering to his parishioners for about half a century. However, during the turbulent years of the English Civil War and the height of the witch-hunting mania propagated by self-styled witch-finders, the elderly clergyman fell under suspicion of sorcery. Subjected to harsh interrogations, Lewis was accused of having familiar spirits or imps, one of which allegedly goaded him into sending a curse that sank a ship at sea. Despite his age and long service, and defending himself courageously at his trial, he was condemned and executed as a wizard, reading the church burial service aloud for himself along the road to the gibbet.
