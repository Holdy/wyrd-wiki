#title
The Cursed Cross of Gayton

#summary
A wayside cross base in Gayton gained a sinister reputation after locals believed that every time the relic was moved, someone would die or be killed.

#details
At the intersection of Lime Kiln Road and Lynn Road in Gayton once stood the ancient pedestal of a wayside cross. Over the decades, the stone base was relocated several times: it was taken into the church to support the font, later moved to serve as a mounting block in the yard of the Crown Inn (where men unsealed its lead-filled mortise hole looking for hidden papers), and eventually transferred to the lawn of Bridge House on Winch Road. According to the village vicar writing in 1889, local superstition held that every relocation of the stone brought about a tragic death, such as a man being crushed by a horse around the time of its move to Bridge House. The superstition was finally considered put to rest when the stone was transported out of the village in 1884 without any misfortune occurring.

#location
At the junction of Lime Kiln Road and Lynn Road in Gayton, Norfolk.
