The Witch of Hethel Thorn

Summary

An ancient hawthorn tree standing for centuries in the parish of Bracon Ash and Hethel has long been tied to local folklore and nicknamed the "Witch of Hethel," serving as a gathering spot for traditional May Day festivities. Local tradition and historical accounts have revered the gnarled tree as both a pagan site and a legendary boundary marker steeped in magical associations.

Details

For over seven centuries, an immense and gnarled hawthorn tree—now recognized as England's smallest nature reserve managed by the Norfolk Wildlife Trust—has stood in a meadow near the church in the joint parishes of Bracon Ash and Hethel. Known locally as the "Witch of Hethel," the ancient tree accumulated numerous legends linking it to pagan fertility symbols, ancient witchcraft beliefs, and historic gatherings. In past centuries, local youths maintained a tradition of "going a-Maying" at the tree, harvesting its blossoms to present to their girlfriends. Historians and arborists have documented its remarkable survival and its status as a focal point of local folklore, bridging medieval insurrection tales from the reign of King John with deeply rooted rural customs regarding witchcraft and magical trees.
