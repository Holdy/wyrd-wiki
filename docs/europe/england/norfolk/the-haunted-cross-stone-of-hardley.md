#title
The Haunted Cross-Stone of Hardley

#summary
At the lonely crossroads of Lower Hardley Road and Cross Stone Road, the spectral apparition of an old woman clad in red is said to materialize at midnight and sit upon an ancient stone marker. 

#details
Situated near the village of Hardley in Norfolk, at the intersection of local roads and a dirt track, stands a small, shaped stone block historically known as a cross-stone or boundary marker. According to local folklore first collected in the late 19th century by local folklorist William Blythe Gerish, on certain nights of the year an old woman dressed entirely in red appears at midnight to sit upon the stone. The spot's ghostly reputation is even older than Gerish's records; Bryant's 1826 map of Norfolk explicitly designated the crossroads as 'Haunted Gap'. 

#location
At the junction of Lower Hardley Road and Cross Stone Road, approximately half a mile south-west of St Margaret's Church in Hardley, Norfolk.
