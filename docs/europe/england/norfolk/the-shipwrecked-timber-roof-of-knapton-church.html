    The Shipwrecked Timber Roof of Knapton Church

    Summary

    According to local tradition, the magnificent carved angel roof of the village church was constructed from timber salvaged from a shipwreck off the nearby coast. This grandiose medieval masterpiece stands in stark contrast to the otherwise plain exterior of the building.

    Details

    The church of St Peter and St Paul in Knapton features an astonishing double-hammerbeam roof dating back to 1503, adorned with nearly 140 carved and painted angels. Because such elaborate and grandiose carpentry was unusual for a relatively simple rural parish church, local folklore long maintained that the fine timber used to build the roof was transported inland after washing ashore from a shipwreck at nearby Mundesley.
