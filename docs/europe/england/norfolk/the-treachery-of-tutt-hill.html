    The Treachery of Tutt Hill

    Summary

    During a Viking siege of Thetford, a local shepherd named Tutt betrayed the town by showing invaders a secret route through the marshes, only to be hanged by them on the very mound that now bears his name.

    Details

    According to local tradition, the Danes were once laying siege to Thetford, but reached a stalemate because the defenders could not escape and the Danes could not breach the town's fortifications at Castle Hill and Red Castle. A local shepherd named Tutt resolved the deadlock by selling out his fellow townspeople, guiding the invaders through a hidden passage in the westward marshes and across a secret river ford. After the Danes successfully captured and plundered the town, they rewarded Tutt's treachery not with wealth, but by hanging him from a prominent Bronze Age bowl barrow just outside the settlement. The mound has since been known as Tutt Hill.
