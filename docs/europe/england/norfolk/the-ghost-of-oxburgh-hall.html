    The Ghost of Oxburgh Hall

    Summary

    Oxburgh Hall is haunted by the spirit of a jilted noblewoman who plunged from an upper window into the moat below. Her phantom continues to roam the historic corridors, North Bedroom, and staircases of the estate.

    Details

    Oxburgh Hall, a 15th-century moated manor house built by Sir Edmund Bedingfield in the village of Oxborough, Norfolk, is the setting for enduring local legends of paranormal activity. According to local tradition, the hall is haunted by the ghost of a jilted lover—variously described as a foreign countess or a Tudor noblewoman—who met a tragic and untimely end. Folklore holds that she plunged from the window of the North Bedroom and drowned in the deep waters of the surrounding moat.

    Generations of visitors and staff have reported eerie encounters within the historic property. The ghost is frequently said to haunt the North Bedroom and the North Staircase, where chilling drafts and sudden, inexplicable drops in temperature are often felt. Eyewitnesses have also reported seeing the silhouette or apparition of a woman in period attire wandering the grounds and near the bridge, keeping alive the haunting legacy of Oxburgh Hall's tragic past.
