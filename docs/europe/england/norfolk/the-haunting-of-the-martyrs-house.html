    The Haunting of the Martyrs' House

    Summary

    Ghost stories and supernatural occurrences have long been reported at the historic Martyrs' House in Little Walsingham, a building closely tied to the tragic executions following the 1537 Walsingham Conspiracy.

    Details

    The Martyrs' House, a Grade II* listed building located on the High Street in Little Walsingham, takes its name from its dark history during the suppression of the monasteries. In 1537, following the failed Walsingham Conspiracy to defend the local abbey, the last sub-prior, Nicholas Mileham, and a layman named Thomas Guisborough were imprisoned in the cellar overnight before being led to Martyrs' Field for execution. Over the years, guests, staff, and residents at the property reported various chilling phenomena. Room 17—a small, oppressive room under the eaves with heavy oak beams and an oval window overlooking the High Street—was particularly reputed to hold a heavy, distressing atmosphere, with visitors and visiting mediums sensing the presence of a terrified Reformation-era monk. Other reported encounters over the decades included ghostly apparitions of nuns and monks walking through corridors or disappearing into walls, phantom lights, items repeatedly going missing, and guests waking to feel an unseen presence sitting on the foot of their beds.
