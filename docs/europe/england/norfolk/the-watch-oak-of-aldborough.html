    The Watch Oak of Aldborough

    Summary

    According to local smuggling folklore, an ancient tree on Hall Lane known as the Watch Oak served as a strategic lookout point where lookouts watched for signals from the coast at Cromer indicating contraband had safely arrived.

    Details

    In the village of Aldborough in Norfolk, local tradition maintains that smuggling gangs active along the coast relied on a prominent landmark to coordinate their illicit operations. Situated on Hall Lane, an ancient tree known as the Watch Oak was utilized by lookouts who would keep watch for signals flashed from the coast at Cromer. These visual cues informed the smugglers that contraband had safely been brought ashore and was ready to be transported inland along Norfolk's network of rural lanes.
