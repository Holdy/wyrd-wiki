#title
The Fairy Cow of South Lopham

#summary
During times of severe hardship and drought, a magical cow would appear in the Norfolk village of South Lopham to provide free milk to the locals before stamping its hoof into a stone slab and vanishing.

#details
According to local legend recorded as early as 1736 by historian Francis Blomefield, a benevolent fairy cow would magically manifest in South Lopham whenever the community faced times of distress or drought, offering her milk freely to anyone in need. When the period of hardship finally ended, the creature would stamp her hoof firmly onto a slab of sandstone in a meadow known as Oxfootpiece, burning the permanent imprint of her hoof into the rock before disappearing. Other variants of the legend attribute the hoofprint to an ordinary cow that was attacked or milked through a sieve by an intoxicated man or witch until she kicked out in pain, or to a rampaging ox with a thorn in its foot. The stone slab itself, known as the Ox-Foot Stone, was historically preserved at Oxfootstone Farm in the parish.

#location
The former site of Oxfootstone Farm in South Lopham, Norfolk.
