    The Treasure of Callow Pit

    Summary

    Two local men attempted to haul a hidden chest of gold from the deep waters of Callow Pit, only to lose it in a struggle against a demonic hand. The iron ring pulled from the chest was later mounted as a church door handle.

    Details

    On the boundary between Moulton St. Mary and Cantley parishes lies Callow Pit, a deep, dark hollow traditionally used as a hiding place by smugglers and outlaws, and said to be haunted at night by a headless horseman. Local legend held that a heavy iron chest of stolen treasure lay submerged in its gloomy waters, visible just beneath the surface. One night, two men from Southwood decided to retrieve the gold and managed to hook an iron ring on the chest. As they strained to pull it ashore, one of them foolishly boasted that not even the Devil himself could take it from them. Instantly, a cloud of sulphurous vapour rolled across the pool, and a long black arm with claw-like fingers erupted from the water to yank the chest back down. In the ensuing tug-of-war, the Devil proved victorious, plunging the treasure back into the depths forever. The two men were left with only the detached iron ring, which they subsequently mounted as a door handle on the parish church.
