The Ghostly Guardian of Beeston Regis

Summary

A furious Norfolk farmer repeatedly menaced by a hooded spectre near a ruined priory vowed to stop the haunting by having a massive glacial stone placed over his grave after his death. To this day, his stone-capped tomb at All Saints' Church marks his final resting place.

Details

Farmer James Reynolds regularly drove his horses past the historic gateway of Beeston Regis Priory, where a hooded ghost in grey robes would leap out from behind a large glacial erratic stone at sunset, attempting to grab the horses' reins and terrifying the animals. Refusing to be intimidated by the apparition, Reynolds vowed to put an end to the haunting. When he died in 1941, a four-foot-long, two-foot-high glacial stone was placed atop his grave at All Saints' Church in accordance with his wishes, symbolically removing the ghost's hiding spot and using the weight of the stone to keep the spirit from roaming between worlds.
