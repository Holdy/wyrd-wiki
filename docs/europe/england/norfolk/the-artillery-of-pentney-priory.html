    The Artillery of Pentney Priory

    Summary

    Local folklore recounts how Oliver Cromwell's soldiers sailed up the River Nar in flat-bottomed boats and planted their cannons on a nearby hill to bombard Pentney Priory into ruins.

    Details

    Pentney Priory was an Augustinian monastery founded in 1075 that stood for nearly five hundred years before falling into decline. While its suppression under Henry VIII began its dissolution, local legend attributes its dramatic final physical destruction to the English Civil War. According to traditional accounts, Oliver Cromwell's men navigated the River Nar in flat-bottomed boats and positioned their heavy guns at the base of a prominent local hill. They proceeded to use the ancient priory buildings for target practice, shattering the monastic stone structures until only the sturdy stone gatehouse remained standing amidst the wreckage.
