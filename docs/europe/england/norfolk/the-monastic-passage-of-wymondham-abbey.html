The Monastic Passage of Wymondham Abbey

Summary

Generations of locals have shared a legend that a secret subterranean tunnel connects the medieval Wymondham Abbey to the historic Green Dragon Inn, built to allow monks to sneak away for ale. The ancient pub and abbey are further haunted by local lore of ghostly apparitions and unexplained nocturnal noises.

Details

Wymondham Abbey, founded as a Benedictine priory in 1107, and the 14th-century Green Dragon Inn are central to one of Norfolk's most enduring local legends. According to tradition, a hidden underground passage stretches between the sacred abbey and the tavern, allegedly constructed so that misbehaving monks could secretly visit the pub for a drink without being seen. Within the timber-framed pub, a specific doorway was long pointed out as the entrance to this subterranean route. Along with the tunnel legend, both buildings are steeped in folklore regarding shadowy figures, phantom footsteps, and ghostly monks roaming the historic streets and rooms.
