    The Witches of Penhill Road

    Summary

    Local tradition and childhood recollections record a mother and daughter living in a wooden shack halfway up Penhill Road who were feared as witches by villagers and children alike, alongside historical accounts of houses left abandoned due to persistent witchcraft superstitions.

    Details

    In the early 20th century, local blacksmith and resident William R. Lebbell recalled that two women—a mother and daughter—lived in a wooden hut situated on the south side of Penhill Road, just off the track leading to Homelea Farm. Children walking past the shack on their way to work in the nearby fields were terrified of the pair, whom villagers whispered possessed witchcraft powers. This folklore of local witchcraft built upon earlier 19th-century accounts, such as a letter published in the Norfolk News in February 1868, which described a house in Great Ellingham standing tenantless and falling into ruin because its last occupant was reputed to be a witch, leaving the property too feared for anyone to inhabit.
