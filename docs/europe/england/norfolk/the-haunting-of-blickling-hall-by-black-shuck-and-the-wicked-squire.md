#title
The Haunting of Blickling Hall by Black Shuck and the Wicked Squire

#summary
Following the death of the indebted and cruel Sir Henry Hobart in 1698, his corpse was repeatedly rejected by consecrated earth and water before a demonic black dog bearing his eyes appeared and was sealed within the hall's turret.

#details
After Sir Henry Hobart died following a duel in 1698, his burial at Blickling church was disrupted when the earth seemingly rejected him, casting his corpse out of its lead casket. When reburied in a copse, the body was once again forced out of the ground. Desperate, the local clergy had the corpse weighted with heavy stones and dropped into ten feet of water in the estate's lake. Shortly after, a monstrous eel was caught, and a supernatural black dog—Black Shuck, possessing the dead man's wicked eyes—manifested at the hall. Unable to be trapped, killed, or poisoned, a London wizard eventually summoned the beast with seven whistles, leading it to the south-east turret of Blickling Hall where the door was permanently bricked up. Over a century later, in the 1860s, residents in the hall's drawing room once again witnessed a mysterious black dog running through the rooms just as the old bricked-up turret was reopened.

#location
Blickling Hall, situated in the village of Blickling near Aylsham in north Norfolk.
