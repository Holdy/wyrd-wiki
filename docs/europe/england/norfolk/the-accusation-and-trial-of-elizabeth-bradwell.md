#title
The Accusation and Trial of Elizabeth Bradwell

#summary
In 1645, an impoverished woman living on the coastal dunes of the Yarmouth-Caister border was accused of witchcraft after a local merchant's child fell ill. Following interrogation and sleep deprivation overseen by Witchfinder General Matthew Hopkins, she was convicted and hanged.

#details
Elizabeth Bradwell lived in an upturned boat on the coastal dunes near the Yarmouth-Caister border after her fiancé drowned and his body washed ashore at Caister. When a local merchant's two-year-old son, John Moulton, fell gravely ill during the winter of 1644–1645, the family and servants blamed Elizabeth after she had previously begged for work and hurled angry words at them when rejected. Amidst the political and religious turmoil of the English Civil War, the town council invited Matthew Hopkins, the self-proclaimed Witchfinder General, to purge the area of witches. Elizabeth was imprisoned in the Tollhouse jail, where she was subjected to sleep deprivation and intense interrogation. Under duress, she confessed to making a diabolical blood pact with the Devil and burying a wax image in the churchyard to cause the child's wasting illness. Despite the physical evidence of the image never being found, her forced confession led to her conviction alongside four other women. On September 29, 1645, she was executed on the gallows near the Yarmouth-Caister border.

#location
The coastal dunes and historical gallows boundary between Great Yarmouth and Caister-on-Sea, Norfolk.
