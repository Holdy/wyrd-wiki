Herolf's Stone

Summary

A large granite block tucked away in Stone Court is said to mark the historic spot where a Danish chieftain or King Harold once stood to issue commands and grant land.

Details

Tucked inside a short alley called Stone Court just off the east side of The Thoroughfare in Harleston rests a ponderous granite block painted black. According to local legend, this stone is named Herolf's Stone, commemorating a Danish chieftain named Herolf who allegedly stood upon it to grant cottages to local guilds, an event said to have given the town its early name of Herolvestone. Alternative traditions claim the rock is the Harold Stone, asserting that King Harold stopped here on his travels and left the impression of his booted foot in a depression at the top. Other accounts suggest Roman origins or that a herald once stood atop the boulder to warn townsfolk of an impending enemy attack.
