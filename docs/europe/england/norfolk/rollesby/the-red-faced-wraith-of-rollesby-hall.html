The Red-Faced Wraith of Rollesby Hall

Summary

Rollesby Hall was said to be haunted on the second Monday of every month by a blood-stained spirit known as "Old Red Face" and his companion. Local folklore and antiquarian accounts offered conflicting origins for the pair, attributing them either to a Danish invader and a Saxon lord's wife or to a Cavalier who murdered his wife with a handsaw.

Details

Rollesby Hall, a mid-16th-century building demolished in the 1950s, was long reputed to be haunted on the second Monday of each month at midnight. According to accounts recorded by antiquaries Antony Norris and Walter Rye, two distinct traditions explained the apparition of "Old Red Face" and his wringing-handed wife. One legend claimed they were a Danish invader who slew a Saxon lord of the manor, followed by the victim's wife. Another legend described them as a Cavalier who cut off his wife's head with a handsaw—splattering blood across his face—and his murdered wife. A local village ballad also preserved the tale of the red-faced Dane roaming the rooms with a blood-dripping sword, closely followed by the dead man's wife.
