The White Lady of St Mary's Church

Summary

A ghostly white lady haunts St Mary's Church in Worstead, having reportedly frightened a skeptic to death on Christmas Eve and later appearing clearly in a 1975 tourist photograph. While some traditions warn that seeing her foretells doom, visitors have also experienced her presence as a comforting and healing influence.

Details

According to local legend, St Mary's Church in Worstead is haunted by a benevolent yet chilling spirit known as the White Lady. On Christmas Eve in 1830, a man staying in the village resolved to test the legend by climbing up into the church belfry. His friends later discovered him gibbering with fright, gasping that he had seen the apparition before immediately dropping dead. Decades later, in August 1975, a tourist named Diane Berthelot was photographed sitting in the church pews. When the film was developed, a ghostly figure in a bonnet and old-fashioned clothing appeared seated directly behind her, though Diane recalled feeling only a warm tingling sensation and believes the spirit possesses healing powers.
