Brother Pacificus of Ranworth Broad

Summary

A medieval monk who survived the massacre of his brethren is said to haunt Ranworth Broad, still rowing toward the church with his dog.

Details

During the 1530s, monks from St Benets Abbey were tasked with mending the ornate screen at St Helens church in Ranworth, a job assigned to Brother Pacificus, who rowed there every morning with his dog. One evening, Pacificus returned to find his fellow monks had been savagely murdered by Henry VIII's soldiers during the dissolution of the monasteries. He remained at the abbey alone until his death, after which locals buried him at St Helens. Early morning risers can still catch a glimpse of his ghostly figure rowing across the Broad toward the church, with his dog standing in the bow.
