    The Legend of the Witch and the Wren

    Summary

    A tyrannical 13th-century Lady of the Manor in Wreningham escaped a knight's fatal blow by transforming into a wren, cementing an old custom of hunting wrens on St. Stephen's Day.

    Details

    According to local tradition recorded in Wreningham, a 13th-century Lady of the Manor exercised oppressive control over the settlement for wicked purposes. A knight attempted to slay her to liberate the community from her tyranny, but at the crucial moment, she used witchcraft to transform into a tiny wren and flew away to evade justice. To commemorate her magical escape, villagers traditionally gathered every St. Stephen's Day to beat the local hedges with sticks and carry the bird in triumph. This local legend also provides the folk etymology behind the naming of Wreningham.
