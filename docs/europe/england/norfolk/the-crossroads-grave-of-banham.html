    The Crossroads Grave of Banham

    Summary

    In 1821, a 70-year-old labourer named Stephen Cutting took his own life and was interred at a rural crossroads in Banham, reviving a dark local toponym dating back to 16th-century maps known as 'Deade Mans Grave'.

    Details

    Following the tragic drowning of 70-year-old labourer Stephen Cutting, local authorities and community custom dictated that his remains could not be consecrated within the traditional parish churchyard. Instead, in accordance with the period's penal practices for suicides, he was buried at a local crossroads in Banham. This roadside interment aligned precisely with a historic spot marked as 'Deade Mans Grave' on 16th-century maps of the parish, cementing the location in local lore as a grim site associated with outcasts and restless dead.
