The Haunting of Flordon Railway Station

Summary

Flordon Railway Station and its former station house are local folklore sites linked to two distinct ghostly phenomena, including a phantom passenger who announces his fatal end and unseen childish touches in the night.

Details

Opened in December 1849 and later closed during the Beeching cuts in the 1960s, Flordon Railway Station in Norfolk gained a local reputation for spectral activity. According to local accounts, when trains formerly slowed to a stop at the station, a phantom passenger would occasionally rise from his seat, walk to the carriage door, and announce, "This is my stop - I died here"—a chilling echo of a historical incident where an insurance salesman tragically lost his life by jumping in front of a train at the location. Years later, in the early 1970s, the disused Station House was occupied by a railway worker's family. The mother repeatedly heard a young child crying upstairs, only to find her own children sound asleep each time she checked. Later that night, before falling asleep, she felt the unmistakable sensation of a small hand gently brushing against her cheek, much like a little child saying goodnight.
