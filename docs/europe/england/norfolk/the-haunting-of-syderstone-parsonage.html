The Haunting of Syderstone Parsonage

Summary

In 1833, the occupants of the parsonage in the Norfolk village of Syderstone were tormented by an inexplicable poltergeist that produced violent hammering and moaning sounds, with local lore linking the haunting to either a dissolute former clergyman or the lingering spirit of Tudor noblewoman Amy Robsart.

Details

In 1833, the Rector of Syderstone, Revd J. Stewart, found his home transformed into a "House of Mystery" plagued by relentless and terrifying disturbances. Household staff and visitors heard chilling sounds ranging from low moans reminiscent of a whipped soldier to deafening hammer blows on an anvil, clashing iron, and rattling glass. The terrified maids were rendered immobile by fear, and local clergy alongside a surgeon stayed overnight to investigate, ultimately attesting that the phenomena were entirely genuine and defied any rational explanation. Local tradition and subsequent historical inquiries attributed the haunting either to Revd Mantle—a former 18th-century vicar of Syderstone who had descended into debauchery and drunkenness—or to Amy Robsart, the daughter of local landowner Sir John Robsart of Syderstone Hall, whose tragic 1560 death at Cumnor Place in Oxfordshire fueled centuries of scandal and ghost stories that reportedly followed her spirit back to her ancestral home and later into the parsonage.
