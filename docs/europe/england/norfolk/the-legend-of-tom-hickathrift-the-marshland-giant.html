    The Legend of Tom Hickathrift, the Marshland Giant

    Summary

    In the fenland marshes of Walsoken and the surrounding Marshland parishes, a gigantic laborer named Tom Hickathrift defended local common lands and vanquished a fierce ogre using only a cart-wheel and axle-tree.

    Details

    According to centuries-old East Anglian folklore, Tom Hickathrift was born in the Norfolk Marshland as the son of a poor labourer. Growing to a monstrous height and possessing superhuman strength by his youth, Tom worked as a drayman driving a brewer's cart across the fen roads connecting King's Lynn and Wisbech through Walsoken and neighboring villages. When a ferocious twelve-foot-high ogre terrorised the region and barred the local pathways, Tom confronted the monster, upended his heavy wagon to use the axle-tree as a sword and the wheel as a shield, and soundly defeated the beast. In a parallel local tradition, when a greedy landlord attempted to unlawfully enclose and seize the vast common pasture known as the Smeeth—which was shared by the Seven Towns of Marshland, including Walsoken, Tilney, and Emneth—Tom rallied the disheartened villagers with his improvised cart-wheel buckler and drove the trespassers from the land. Tom eventually amassed great wealth from the ogre's hoard, built a fine estate, and upon his death, his legendary grave and stone coffin—marked with carvings said to represent his cart-wheel and axle-tree—were established in the region.
