    The Stolen Bells of Thieves' Bridge

    Summary

    On the night before Valentine's Day in 1551, thieves stole the church bells from Tottenhill and attempted to haul them across the countryside, ultimately hiding them beneath a bridge on the Watlington parish boundary when they proved too heavy to transport further. Though the culprits were eventually caught, the crossing became permanently known as Thieves' Bridge.

    Details

    According to local tradition and historical Tudor inventories, on 13 February 1551, criminals broke into the isolated St Botolph's Church at Tottenhill and stripped the heavy bells from the steeple. Finding the metal bells far too heavy to carry away easily, the thieves dragged them a little over a mile and concealed them beneath a small bridge crossing a stream on the Watlington and Tottenhill parish boundary. Although the hiding place was discovered and the robbers apprehended, the bridge, local road, and nearby farm have ever since borne the name Thieves' Bridge in memory of the daring heist.
