#title
The Terror of Tichborne

#summary
Lady Mabella on her deathbed forced a cruel husband to grant land for a yearly charity, placing a generational curse on the family and house if the tradition was ever broken. Centuries later, after the handout was suspended due to rowdy crowds, the family suffered the exact prophetic signifiers of the curse—including seven sons followed by seven daughters—until the dole was hastily revived.

#details
In the 12th century, the arrogant Sir Roger Tichborne agreed to give land for a charitable dole to the poor on Lady Day (March 25th) only on the condition that his pious and dying wife, Lady Mabella, could crawl around the perimeter of the field while a single stick remained burning in his hand. Miraculously, despite her frailty, she crawled twenty-three acres—a field still known today as "The Crawls"—before collapsing. Before she died, she prophesied that if any of her successors ever failed to distribute the dole, a curse would fall upon the family: the estate would fail, the name would die out, the house would fall into ruin, and as a warning sign, a generation of seven sons would be followed by a generation of seven daughters. In 1796, the family stopped the tradition due to unruly vagrants gathering on the estate, prompting the prophecy to fulfill itself through structural collapses, family tragedies, and the exact sequence of seven sons and seven daughters. The panicked family restored the dole in the 1830s, and the traditional distribution of flour continues to this day.

#location
Tichborne House and the surrounding fields near the village of Tichborne, just south of Alresford in Hampshire.
