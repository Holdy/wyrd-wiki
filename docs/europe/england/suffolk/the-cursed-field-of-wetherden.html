The Cursed Field of Wetherden

Summary

In 1556, a Protestant martyr was burned to death on a hillside field near Wetherden, placing a lasting curse upon the land that prevents any crops from ever growing there again.

Details

During the Marian persecutions in 1556, a yeoman named Robert Rosier from Mutton Hall in Wetherden was condemned for his Protestant beliefs at the instigation of Sir John Sulyard, the High Sheriff of Norfolk and Suffolk. Rosier was led to a prominent hillside field alongside the main road between Wetherden and Woolpit—historically known as Brennen or Burning Hill—where he was lashed to a post and burned to death. Some accounts say Rosier chose the hilltop himself so that he could gaze upon his home one last time, while others claim Sulyard selected it to intensify his suffering. Ever since his execution, local tradition has held that the field is under a powerful curse, rendering the soil barren so that no crops have grown there since that fateful day.
