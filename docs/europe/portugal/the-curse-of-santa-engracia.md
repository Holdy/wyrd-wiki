#title
The Curse of Santa Engrácia

#summary
Condemned to death for a desecration he did not commit, an innocent man placed a dying curse on a Lisbon church, dooming its reconstruction to centuries of collapse, financial ruin, and unending delay. This engineering nightmare ultimately gave birth to the enduring Portuguese idiom "Works of Santa Engrácia."

#details
In 1630, after the interior of the Church of Santa Engrácia was desecrated, the local religious brotherhood falsely accused an innocent man named Simão Pires de Solis. Despite having a solid alibi, Solis was tortured, dragged through the streets of Lisbon, and burned to death at the stake. Although the true culprit confessed a year later and cleared Solis's name, it was too late. Before his death, Solis cursed the church's rebuilding project, vowing that it would never be finished and would remain in a state of eternal limbo. True to his dying words, subsequent reconstruction efforts were repeatedly stalled by political and financial crises, culminating in a severe 1681 storm that caused the newly built chancel to completely collapse. The roofless skeleton of the church sat abandoned for nearly two hundred years, spawning the national phrase *Obras de Santa Engrácia* ("Works of Santa Engrácia") as a synonym for any endless, unending project. Construction finally resumed and was completed in the 20th century, transforming the cursed structure into Portugal's National Pantheon.

#location
The Church of Santa Engrácia (National Pantheon) is located in the historic Alfama neighborhood of Lisbon, Portugal.
