#title
The Malcanis of Akershus Fortress

#summary
During the medieval construction of Akershus Castle, a live dog was walled into the foundations as a protective sacrifice, giving rise to a malignant spirit known as Malcanisen. Anyone who encounters the spectral hound's glowing red eyes is doomed to face a tragic death.

#details
According to Norwegian local legend, when Akershus Fortress in Oslo was constructed in the late 13th century, workers bricked up a living dog within the masonry to bring good luck and structural protection. Instead of a benevolent guardian, the entity—dubbed Malcanisen or "the vicious dog"—transformed into a terrifying specter haunting the castle's dark corridors and gates. Around 1550, following a structural collapse in the subterranean hallway, fortress commander Peder Hanssøn Litle ventured into the darkness alone to investigate. He encountered a phantom hound with glowing red eyes, sharp fangs, and a heavy chain around its neck. After a violent struggle in which Peder hurled a burning torch at the beast, he managed to crawl out barely alive. Though he survived the immediate attack, he died mysteriously a month later after being thrown from a horse, cementing the legend that seeing the Malcanis brings a fatal curse.

#location
Akershus Fortress is located on the Akersneset peninsula in central Oslo, Norway.
