#title
The Barren Earth of the Wronged Man

#summary
Condemned for a crime he swore he did not commit, a hanged man cursed his grave so that no grass would grow upon it for a century. True to his dying words, the patch remained stubbornly bare for generations, defying every attempt to cultivate it.

#details
In 1821, a plasterer from Wrexham named John Davies was accused of highway robbery and assault against a man named William Jones. Despite Davies fiercely proclaiming his complete innocence throughout the proceedings, he was convicted and sentenced to death by hanging. Just before his execution, Davies issued a powerful final declaration, swearing before God that he was guiltless and praying that grass would never grow upon his grave for a hundred years as proof of his wrongful execution. He was buried in the churchyard, and true to his solemn prophecy, the soil of his grave remained entirely barren. Generations of locals and groundskeepers watched in awe as grass and weeds refused to take root there, even when turf was repeatedly laid over it. The strange anomaly persisted for over a century, standing as a silent, visible monument to a suspected miscarriage of justice.

#location
The grave is located in the churchyard of St Nicholas' Church in the historic town of Montgomery, Powys.
