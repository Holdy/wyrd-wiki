The Century-Burn Curse of Gräfsnäs Castle

Summary

A mistreated servant reportedly cursed a newly built Swedish noble estate to burn down three times at exact century-long intervals. True to the prophecy, devastating fires consumed the castle in 1634, 1734, and 1834.

Details

During the mid-16th century, Count Sten Eriksson Leijonhufvud established Gräfsnäs Castle beside Lake Anten in Västergötland. Following his death, his widow Ebba Lilliehöök governed the estate harshly, ruling workers and servants with an iron fist. Pushed to the breaking point by this tyranny, a female laborer placed a hex upon the fortress, prophesying that it would suffer three catastrophic fires exactly one hundred years apart and ultimately fall into ruin without peace or prosperity. Decades later, the prophecy manifested with uncanny precision: the castle burned down in 1634, again in 1734, and for the third time in 1834 on the very day the property was purchased with a massive shipment of brandy. The final blaze and the subsequent explosive demolition of its slate roof in 1880 sealed its fate as an open ruin, which is now preserved as a cultural reserve.
